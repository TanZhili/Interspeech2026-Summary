TITLE: AppTek Call-Center Dialogues: A Multi-Accent Long-Form Benchmark for English ASR
PRESENTER: Eugen Beck
ABSTRACT:
Evaluating English ASR systems for conversational AI applications remains difficult, as many publicly available corpora are either pre-segmented into short segments, consist of read or prepared speech, or lack explicit dialect annotations to evaluate robustness for a diverse user base. This work presents the AppTek Call-Center Dialogues corpus, a collection of spontaneous, role-played agent-customer conversations spanning fourteen English accents covering sixteen service-oriented scenarios. The dataset was commissioned specifically for evaluation and none of the audio or text was publicly available prior to release, reducing the risk of overlap with existing large-scale pretraining corpora. We benchmark a set of open-source ASR systems under different segmentation approaches. Results show substantial variation across accents and segmentation methods, indicating that good performance on general American English benchmarks does not necessarily generalize to other accents.

TITLE: Segmental Attention Decoding With Long Form Acoustic Encodings
PRESENTER: Xinwei Li
ABSTRACT:
We address the fundamental incompatibility of attention-based encoder-decoder (AED) models with long-form acoustic encodings. AED trained on segmented utterances learn to encode absolute positions by exploiting limited acoustic context beyond segment boundaries, but fail to generalize when decoding long-form segments where these cues vanish. The model loses ability to order acoustic encodings due to permutation invariance of keys and values in cross-attention. We propose four modifications: 1) injecting explicit absolute positional encodings into cross-attention, 2) long-form training with extended acoustic context to eliminate implicit absolute position encoding, 3) segment concatenation to cover diverse segmentations needed during training, 4) semantic segmentation to align AED-decoded segments with training segments. We show that these modifications close the accuracy gap between continuous and segmented acoustic encodings, enabling auto-regressive use of the attention decoder.

ID: 1542
TITLE: M-LAMA: Multimodal Automated Scoring of Long-form Spoken English
PRESENTER: Minh Dao-Xuan-Quang
ABSTRACT:
Automatic speech assessment (ASA) for real-world exams requires evaluating multi-minute spontaneous responses across acoustic delivery, linguistic quality, and task fulfillment. However, most prior work focuses on short utterances, leaving long-form comprehensive assessment underexplored. We investigate whether reliable scoring of extended speech requires structured multimodal modeling. We propose M-LAMA, a cross-modal framework integrating raw audio, ASR transcripts, and question context through dual encoders and a discourse-aware fusion decoder. A part-based audio structure and question-aware text encoding capture both how and what is spoken, while a three-stage training strategy addresses the bell-curve score distribution of high-stakes exams. Experiments on 87,226 full-length sessions show that structured multimodal alignment substantially improves scoring reliability across five criteria, suggesting that effective long-form ASA requires principled integration of acoustic, linguistic, and contextual evidence.

ID: 1708
TITLE: Attentive Mamba: Channel-wise Local Attention for Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Jen-Tzung Chien
ABSTRACT:
State space models (SSMs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to transformers for speech recognition, but the performance is constrained due to static local aggregation. This paper presents an attentive SSM that integrates a causal convolutional attention where the static convolution is upgraded with a channel-wise local attention. The proposed module first contextualizes queries, keys, and values via causal depth-wise convolutions before applying cross-channel attention over a temporally local window. This architecture is motivated by the inherent structure of speech spectrograms, where distinct frequency channels contain temporally coherent patterns that can benefit the channel-specific modeling prior to their integration. This design facilitates dynamic parameterization of the state transition matrices, leading to the expressive acoustic features. Experimental results confirm the merit of integrating dynamic channel-wise attention into SSM for speech recognition.

ID: 929
TITLE: Attention-Guided Reliability Scaling for Contrastive Decoding in Robust Audio-Visual Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Da-Hee Yang
ABSTRACT:
Large language model (LLM)-based audio-visual speech recognition (AVSR) systems are robust under noise. Contrastive decoding (CD), originally introduced to stabilize LLM generation by contrasting a weaker model against a stronger one at inference time, adjusts predictions without additional training. In this work, we apply CD to AVSR by contrasting audio-only conditioning with full audio-visual conditioning within the same underlying model. However, using a fixed contrastive strength introduces a trade-off across noise levels: stronger intervention helps under severe noise but may over-correct reliable predictions in clean conditions. We propose reliability-aware scaling of CD for AVSR. Instead of using a fixed strength, we adaptively modulate the contrastive influence at each token based on reliability signals derived from attention dynamics and inter-model predictive divergence. Experiments on LRS3 show consistent improvements across clean and low-SNR conditions.

TITLE: Listening with Attention: Entropy-Guided Explainability for Transformer-Based Audio Models
PRESENTER: Ravi Kumar
ABSTRACT:
Transformer-based automatic speech recognition (ASR) models such as Whisper are highly accurate, but their predictions remain difficult to interpret. Existing explainable AI (XAI) methods often lack faithfulness and precise temporal grounding. We propose Listening with Entropy-guided Attention for Faithful eXplainability (LEAF-X), a model-intrinsic XAI framework for transformer-based ASR. LEAF-X combines entropy-guided attention weighting, multi-layer attention rollout, and optional causal ablations to identify low-entropy, high-impact heads and layers, producing sparse token-to-frame attributions. Unlike perturbation-based explainers or raw attention maps, LEAF-X exploits the internal structure of encoder-decoder and speech-augmented decoder-only models to generate explanations that better reflect model computation. Results show that LEAF-X produces more faithful and stable attributions than strong baselines, supporting more transparent and auditable ASR.
