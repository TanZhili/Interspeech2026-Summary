TITLE: PART: Progressive Alignment Representation Training for Multilingual Speech-To-Text with LLMs
PRESENTER: Pei Zhang
ABSTRACT:
Large language models (LLMs) have expanded from text to speech, giving rise to Speech Large Models (SLMs). A key challenge is aligning speech and text representations, which becomes harder in multilingual settings. Existing methods often freeze LLM parameters and train the encoders on multilingual multi-task data, often collapsing language representations into an shared space and hurting performance. We introduce Progressive Alignment Representation Training (PART), a multi-stage and multi-task framework that performs fine-grained alignment across languages and diverse speech tasks. During training, we progressively unfreeze the SLMs parameters, while introducing different types of multilingual tasks stage by stage to enhance multilingual speech-text alignment. Experiments on multiple mainstream benchmarks show that PART surpasses conventional approaches, with analysis confirming its ability to balance language-specific distinctions for robust multilingual speech-text alignment.

ID: 1185
TITLE: Alignment-Aware Continued Pre-training for Multilingual Speech Representation Learning
PRESENTER: Xuyang Wang
ABSTRACT:
Large multilingual self-supervised learning (SSL) models provide strong acoustic representations; they are typically adapted to ASR through direct supervised fine-tuning. This paradigm defers acoustic–symbol alignment entirely to the fine-tuning stage, which may limit cross-lingual generalization under heterogeneous scripts and long-tailed language distributions. We propose an alignment-aware continued pre-training framework on top of multilingual SSL foundation models.Instead of direct fine-tuning, we introduce an intermediate stage that jointly optimizes SSL and CTC objectives to impose textual alignment constraints on representation learning. We analyze the ASR performance of different modeling units introduced in the alignment and introduce a language-aware dual-codebook quantizer to mitigate multilingual competition. Experiments demonstrate consistent gains over direct fine-tuning and continual SSL adaptation, with improved cross-lingual transfer under unified textual spaces.

ID: 2422
TITLE: Synthetic Audio Generation Framework for Air Traffic Control Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Zhe Zhang
ABSTRACT:
Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems, despite achieving remarkable accuracy in general-purpose domains with native speech (L1), struggle in domains like Air Traffic Control (ATC) due to strong channel noise, a presence of non-native (L2) English accents, and data scarcity. We propose a synthetic data generation pipeline with acoustical properties simulations specifically designed to address this lack of real data to improve recognition accuracy in the ATC domain. Our approach leverages a combination of neural generation techniques, including Text-to-Speech, Voice Conversion, L2-to-L1 accent conversion, and a novel controllable L1-to-L2 accent conversion framework built to simulate accented speech. Our experiments with the Whisper model on the ATCO2 corpus demonstrate that fine-tuning with either synthetic data alone, or a mix of real and synthetic data, significantly improves the word error rate over out-of-the-box and real data only baselines respectively.

ID: 2483
TITLE: GigaAM Multilingual: Foundation Model for Underrepresented Languages
PRESENTER: Andrei Kuzmenko
ABSTRACT:
Despite recent scaling successes, multilingual ASR performance remains highly uneven, with long-tail languages suffering from severe data scarcity. This work addresses the challenge of building robust foundation models for underrepresented Central Asian languages (Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Uzbek). We present GigaAM Multilingual, a Conformer encoder pre-trained on 2M hours of audio using a HuBERT-style objective. Crucially, we introduce a cluster-level data balancing strategy during pre-training and a domain-aware sampling method during fine-tuning to mitigate head-language dominance. In controlled comparisons, our approach outperforms strong open pre-trained encoders (Whisper Large v3, Omnilingual-1B) on target languages, achieving significant gains on spontaneous speech while maintaining efficiency. We release the foundation encoder and ASR model, offering a proven recipe for effective multilingual adaptation under realistic data imbalance.

ID: 3469
TITLE: Low-Resource Medical ASR for Rich Transcription in Latvian
PRESENTER: Arturs Znotins
ABSTRACT:
This paper investigates automatic speech recognition (ASR) for rich medical transcription in a low-resource setting. We compare end-to-end ASR models trained to produce formatted reports with a two-stage pipeline combining verbatim transcription and LLM-based post-editing. To address limited training data, we introduce an LLM-driven data curation approach that converts legacy verbatim transcripts into formatted data, and we augment training with LLM-corrected pseudo-labeled data. Our experiments, using 50 hours of manually transcribed medical dictation and an additional 75 hours of LLM-corrected pseudo-labeled audio, show that domain-adapted foundation models significantly improve transcription quality across word error rate, punctuation accuracy, and medical entity recognition. The results demonstrate that high-quality medical ASR with rich transcription is achievable for low-resource languages using limited domain data.

ID: 1133
TITLE: Probing LoRA-to-LoRA Cross-Lingual Transfer for Unseen Low-Resource Conditions in Whisper-Based ASR
PRESENTER: Spandan Dey
ABSTRACT:
Low-resource Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) remains a challenge despite large-scale pre-training, especially for languages with minimal representation. We investigate LoRA-to-LoRA transfer using Whisper, where a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module trained on a donor language, even one under-represented in the base model, initializes adaptation for a low-resource recipient. Unlike full fine-tuning, this approach preserves pretrained parameters, ensuring stable, parameter-efficient cross-lingual transfer. We propose a two-stage donor selection strategy that filters candidates by genealogical relatedness and identifies the optimal donor through orthographic-distributional similarity. Results demonstrate that donor-informed initialization consistently outperforms recipient-only adaptation, yielding relative Word Error Rate (WER) reductions of up to 17%. Our findings indicate that leveraging linguistically and orthographically aligned donors is critical for effective low-resource ASR.

ID: 1007
TITLE: Overcoming Decoder Inconsistencies in Whisper for Dravidian and Low-Resource Languages
PRESENTER: Kumud Tripathi
ABSTRACT:
Multilingual ASR models such as Whisper perform well on high-resource languages but exhibit substantially higher Word Error Rates (WER) for Dravidian languages compared to Indo-Aryan ones. Through linguistic and dataset analysis, we show that Dravidian languages have longer words, higher vocabulary diversity, and lower repetition, resulting in sparse token distributions and frequent character-level substitution errors. Baseline fine-tuning further reveals decoder imbalance between self-attention (linguistic context) and cross-attention (acoustic cues). Although synthetic token-repetition experiments indicate potential gains, they are impractical. Motivated by these observations, we introduce two decoder-level enhancements: Weighted-Attention, which adaptively balances attention sources, and Self-Conditioning, which reinjects intermediate predictions to improve token consistency. Experiments demonstrate consistent WER reductions for low-resource and agglutinative languages.

ID: 2454
TITLE: The Impact of Informal Persian Speech on Low-Resource ASR and Speech Translation
PRESENTER: Hadi Alizadeh
ABSTRACT:
We investigate the impact of informal Persian speech on automatic speech recognition and speech translation in low-resource settings. Persian exhibits substantial variation between formal and informal registers, yet most publicly available datasets focus on formal speech, leaving informal variants underrepresented. To address this gap, we introduce the Toorintan-Persian Informal Dataset, a carefully transcribed and validated collection of informal speech from diverse speakers and contexts. We apply targeted normalization techniques to improve data consistency and fine-tune state-of-the-art models on this dataset. Experiments demonstrate that models trained on informal data generalize effectively across both formal and informal registers and outperform existing baselines in WER and BLEU scores. To facilitate further research, we also release a Persian text normalizer. Both the dataset and associated tools are publicly available on Hugging Face.

ID: 2771
TITLE: BELLA: Efficient Bilevel Learning with LoRA for Multilingual ASR
PRESENTER: Xiaodong Cui
ABSTRACT:
Multilingual automatic speech recognition (ASR) faces challenges due to diverse linguistic structures and resource disparities. We propose an efficient BilEvel Learning with LoRA for Multilingual Speech Recognition (BELLA), a framework that couples a pre-trained ASR encoder with a large language model (LLM) decoder via a trainable bridge to align speech representations with the LLM token space. The decoder incorporates mixture-of-experts LoRA (MoE-LoRA) modules for language specialization, guided by a router. Training is formulated as a bilevel program: the upper level optimizes router and expert LoRA modules for task performance, while the lower level updates the bridge and shared adapter for alignment. We develop an efficient single-loop, value-function-free penalty solver with practical updates. BELLA is parameter-efficient, scalable, and reduces cross-language interference. Experiments on five CoVoST 2 languages show consistent gains over strong multilingual ASR baselines.

ID: 1371
TITLE: Automatic Lyric Transcription for Greek Songs: Scaling and Task Composition Effects in Whisper Adaptation
PRESENTER: Dimitrios Damianos
ABSTRACT:
Automatic Lyric Transcription (ALT) remains substantially more challenging than speech recognition due to melodic variability, rhythmic irregularity, and accompaniment interference. This is heightened in low-resource languages like Greek, where no prior benchmark for ALT exists. We present the first controlled study of Whisper adaptation for Greek ALT, investigating model scaling effects, task composition via multitask training in transcribe-translate ratios, and two-stage speech-to-singing adaptation. We also curate a segment-level aligned singing dataset based on the Greek Audio Dataset (GAD) using source separation and CTC forced alignment. Results show that scaling consistently improves performance, while multitask learning acts as a beneficial regularizer primarily for smaller-capacity models. The 2-stage adaptation in Whisper Large-v3 achieves a Word Error Rate (WER) of 27.2%, a significant improvement over zero-shot baselines, establishing the first Greek ALT benchmark.

TITLE: CrossPhon-Tonal: Streamlining Cross-language Modeling for Forced Alignment in Low-resource Tonal Languages
PRESENTER: Hongchen Wu
ABSTRACT:
Forced alignment is essential for speech research, yet most tonal languages lack language-specific acoustic models for automation and require intensive human labor. While cross-language modeling offers a solution by leveraging foreign acoustic models, it typically overlooks lexical tone (e.g., using English for Tibetan) or relies on scarce phonetic expertise to craft manual mappings (e.g., using Mandarin for Tibetan). CrossPhon-Tonal introduces a novel tone encoding that integrates automated tone mappings into the cross-language modeling pipeline. Tested on six typologically diverse tonal languages, CrossPhon-Tonal presents performance comparable to human expert-generated mappings in cross-language modeling for forced alignment in tonal languages, and matches or outperforms the performance of the state-of-the-art Global English model. This work provides a scalable, automated solution for forced alignment in under-resourced tonal languages without requiring manual phonetic intervention.
