TITLE: Improving Adversarial Robustness in Spoken Language Identification through Self-Defensive Distillation
PRESENTER: Spandan Dey
ABSTRACT:
Robustness against adversarial attacks is majorly explored for security-critical speech applications, such as speaker verification and speech recognition. Concerning their on-growing multilingual expansion, we present one of the first extensive studies on developing adversarial robustness for spoken language identification (LID). We show the vulnerability of state-of-the-art LID models under widely-explored white-box gradient-based adversarial attacks. We then propose a novel adversarial robust LID framework, Self-Defensive Adversarial Re-Training (SDART). It expands the conventional adversarial re-training with adversarial sample mining, teacher-free defensive distillation using dynamic online label smoothing, and linguistic consistency regularization. Across multiple databases and architectures, SDART outperforms several conventional adversarial robustness methods, improving performance on both genuine and adversarially attacked samples.

ID: 975
TITLE: Probing the Layer-wise Geometry of Chinese Dialect Representations in Wav2Vec 2.0
PRESENTER: Zhen Peng
ABSTRACT:
While Wav2Vec 2.0 has achieved significant success in speech representation tasks, the mechanisms by which it encodes dialectal features across different layers remain underexplored. This study applies geometric probing methods to analyze the changes in the representations of five Chinese dialects within the model. Experimental results reveal a three-stage trajectory: (1) lower layers are dominated by fundamental acoustic discrepancies; (2) middle layers exhibit high spatial dispersion, preserving fine-grained phonetic details; and (3) deep layers show significant spatial contraction, though the resulting clustering remains consistent with traditional linguistic taxonomies. Based on these findings, this study suggests that for tasks requiring fine-grained phonetic information, such as accent identification, intermediate-layer features should be prioritized; and deep-layer features are more valuable for broad dialectal classification or phylogenetic analysis.

ID: 2502
TITLE: Robust Language Identification Using Semi-positive Contrastive Learning
PRESENTER: Shubham Sharma
ABSTRACT:
Conventionally, Spoken Language Identification is modeled as a speech-based language identification task, ignoring the importance of text data. This work addresses the cross-corpora generalization issue for the low-resourced spoken language identification (LID) problem. We propose Semi-positive Contrastive Learning (SpCL), a robust framework for LID that addresses domain shifts without requiring explicit domain adaptation or generalization. Our method uses a bi-modal audio-text encoder to map speech waveforms and phoneme-based text captions into a shared embedding space. By introducing a novel semi-positive contrastive loss, SpCL distinguishes strong positive pairs (same language and domain) from semi-positive pairs (same language, different domains) using a controlled weighting scheme. We experimented on 12 Indian languages using multi-domain datasets that demonstrate that SpCL outperforms strong acoustic and phonetic baselines, achieving superior accuracy on both seen and unseen test domains. Furthermore, we conducted ablation studies that confirm the benefits of semipositive weighting and dynamic captions, with Whisper-based audio encoders delivering the best robustness under domain mismatch.

ID: 3333
TITLE: Predict-Then-Adapt: Inferring Coordinates from Speech for Continuous Geo-Conditioned Dialectal ASR
PRESENTER: Pouya Mehralian
ABSTRACT:
Geo-conditioned adaptation can improve dialectal ASR, but most methods assume speaker coordinates are available at inference. We remove this dependency by inferring location from speech at test time and conditioning on the predicted coordinates. We attach a lightweight Coordinate Regression Head (CRH) to a frozen encoder and train it to regress latitude/longitude from utterance-level representations. Inference uses two passes: (i) predict coordinates with geo-conditioning disabled, then (ii) decode with geo-conditioning enabled conditioned on the prediction. CRH adds &lt; 0.1% parameters and increases inference latency by &lt; 3%. On Dutch dialects, CRH achieves 15-25 km mean great-circle error using 10-30 s of speech, and the GLoRIA method is robust to errors in this range. As a result, CRH+GLoRIA outperforms rank-matched LoRA and approaches oracle-coordinate adaptation performance, while preserving GLoRIA interpretability via analyzable coordinate-gated low-rank components.

ID: 458
TITLE: Dialect Bias in Speech Recognition Across 10 Spanish and French Varieties
PRESENTER: Rodrigo Nieto
ABSTRACT:
ASR bias research is largely English-focused and the mechanisms underlying performance disparities remain poorly understood. We address both gaps for Spanish and French. Using a 20-hour, gender-balanced corpus (the first for these languages designed for dialect bias analysis, with country-level stratification and manual transcripts preserving dialectal features), we benchmark seven models and find non-uniform disparities: models favor European varieties in French, while Spanish reveals unexpected patterns with Dominican outperforming Peninsular and with Chilean showing the highest error rates. Lexical analysis links failures to morphosyntactic &quot;correction&quot; and regional discourse markers; acoustic analysis shows that speaker embeddings encode dialect information; a diagnostic fine-tuning experiment localizes these errors to the decoder. Together, these results demonstrate that ASR errors systematically reflect linguistic distance from training distributions.

TITLE: DASR-CPO: Reference-Free Contrastive Preference Optimization for Correcting Mandarin Semantic Drift in Low-Resource Chinese Dialect ASR
PRESENTER: Tao Zhang
ABSTRACT:
Pretrained ASR models such as Whisper-Large-v3 can degrade on low-resource Chinese dialects because a Mandarin-dominant prior favors frequent homophones. This causes Mandarin semantic drift: dialect words are transcribed as phonetically similar but semantically wrong Mandarin forms, often on entity- or keyword-critical spans. LoRA supervised fine-tuning reduces character error rate (CER) but does not explicitly suppress these competitors. We propose DASR-CPO, a reference-free contrastive preference optimization method that teaches the model to prefer dialect-correct spans over mined Mandarin confusions. Preference pairs come from data-driven confusion mining without manual annotation. We localize drift-prone spans by sequence matching and train with a span-level contrastive objective using hard negatives. On MagicData Sichuanese (4.53 h), DASR-CPO reduces CER from 24.85% to 22.58% and improves dialect-entity F1 from 72.82 to 74.15 with zero inference-time overhead.
