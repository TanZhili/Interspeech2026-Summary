TITLE: Decoding the Trade-off: A Large-Scale Analysis of Latency and Stability in LLM-based Speech Translation Cascades
PRESENTER: Shinyoung Sun
ABSTRACT:
Real-time subtitling must balance low latency with stable translations. In segment-level cloud cascades, aggressive endpointing increases request rate and can push utilization beyond service capacity, triggering a backlog avalanche in which queueing delay dominates and &quot;fast&quot; settings become slower in steady state. We present RST, a reproducible KO→EN subtitle pipeline that distinguishes time-to-first-text (latency_first) and time-to-stable-text (latency_stable). On the full FLEURS corpus, we show that aggressive endpointing drives cascades into a backlog regime (median RTF &gt; 1), where latency_stable grows over time. Whisper prompt conditioning further degrades short-chunk streaming ASR by +14-17 pp CER and can leak instructions. Sweeps reveal a clear stability boundary. We define an operational threshold as the most aggressive setting with median RTF ≤1, showing that throughput stability is essential for low-latency LLM-based cascades.

ID: 689
TITLE: Fed-SpeechLLM: Federated Learning Speech Language Models for Multilingual ASR
PRESENTER: Daniele Giuseppe Falavigna
ABSTRACT:
SpeechLLMs show strong promise for multilingual ASR, yet centralized training conflicts with privacy constraints over distributed, linguistically diverse data. This paper presents Fed-SpeechLLM, a federated learning framework for multilingual SpeechLLM-based ASR evaluated on a bilingual English and Italian setting. We study federated optimization under compounded non-IID conditions from acoustic and cross-lingual heterogeneity. Standard FL methods degrade when clients hold language-skewed distributions. Moreover, we propose language-aware gradient aggregation and a sampling clustering-based method to mitigate multilingual imbalance, using selective aggregation of speech encoders and projection layers with a frozen LLM backbone. Results confirm competitive performance against centralized training.

ID: 127
TITLE: Multi-Channel Differential ASR for Robust Wearer Speech Recognition on Smart Glasses
PRESENTER: Yiteng Huang
ABSTRACT:
With the growing adoption of wearable devices such as smart glasses for AI assistants, wearer speech recognition (WSR) is becoming increasingly critical to next-generation human-computer interfaces. However, in real environments, interference from side-talk speech remains a significant challenge to WSR and may cause accumulated errors for downstream tasks such as natural language processing. In this work, we introduce a novel multi-channel differential automatic speech recognition (ASR) method for robust WSR on smart glasses. The proposed system takes differential inputs from different frontends that complement each other to improve the robustness of WSR, including a beamformer, microphone selection, and a lightweight side-talk detection model. Evaluations on both simulated and real datasets demonstrate that the proposed system outperforms the traditional approach, achieving up to an 18.0% relative reduction in word error rate.

ID: 2698
TITLE: ESPnet3: Infrastructure for Scalable Speech and Audio Research in the Foundation Model Era
PRESENTER: Masao Someki
ABSTRACT:
Recent speech research involves increasingly large datasets, complex models, and diverse experimental workflows. However, existing frameworks require substantial engineering effort to support such experiments. We present ESPnet3, a speech and audio research framework built on a modular system architecture with configuration-driven dataset composition and unified Python-based workflows. ESPnet3 introduces a DataOrganizer abstraction for flexible dataset integration and dataset sharding for memory-efficient large-scale training, while allowing recipe-specific logic through lightweight stage overrides. In OWSM pre-training experiments, ESPnet3 reduces per-epoch training time by 21.1 minutes compared to ESPnet2 and achieves &gt;80% GPU utilization in multi-node training. Fine-tuning experiments show that new models and datasets can be integrated with around 46 lines of additional code. ESPnet3 will be publicly released with model checkpoints and training logs.

ID: 3436
TITLE: SCRIBE: Diagnostic Evaluation and Rich Transcription Models for Indic ASR
PRESENTER: Kavya Manohar
ABSTRACT:
Automatic speech recognition replaces typing only when correction costs less than manual entry, a threshold determined by error types, not counts: fixing a misrecognized domain term costs far more than inserting a comma. Word error rate (WER) fails on two fronts: it collapses distinct error categories into a single scalar, and it structurally penalizes agglutinative languages where valid sandhi merges inflate scores. We introduce SCRIBE, a diagnostic framework that provides categorical error decomposition into lexical, punctuation, numeral, and domain-entity rates through sandhi-tolerant alignment with domain vocabulary injection. Human validation confirms SCRIBE aligns with expert judgment where WER does not. We release SCRIBE, an LLM curation pipeline, benchmarks, and open-weight rich transcription models for Hindi, Malayalam, and Kannada.

ID: 363
TITLE: Audio-KWS-Gated Error Memory Retrieval for Incremental ASR Post-Correction
PRESENTER: Taira Ashikawa
ABSTRACT:
ASR has improved, yet rare and domain-specific terms remain challenging. LLMs enable second-pass post-correction without retraining an ASR model. In long-running use, correction logs can be reused to fix recurring errors, but full-history prompting is limited by the LLM context window, and text-only selection is brittle when trigger terms are misrecognized. We propose an incremental post-correction framework with Audio-KWS-gated retrieval, where Audio-KWS detects keywords from audio to select relevant correction records within the context window. We proceed in three steps: (i) LLM-based error analysis extracts correction pairs to update a keyword inventory, (ii) Audio-KWS runs on audio to detect Top-N keywords and retrieve correction records (using phonetic readings as keywords for Japanese), and (iii) the LLM applies record-guided edits. On Earnings-21 and CSJ, our method achieves about 70% prompt reduction (Top-20 English; Top-10 Japanese) while improving WER/CER and Bias-F1.
