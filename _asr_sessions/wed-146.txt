TITLE: From Dispersion to Attraction: Spectral Dynamics of Hallucination Across Whisper Model Scales
PRESENTER: Ivan Viakhirev
ABSTRACT:
Hallucinations in large ASR models present a critical safety risk. In this work, we propose the Spectral Sensitivity Theorem, which predicts a phase transition in deep networks from a dispersive regime (signal decay) to an attractor regime (rank-1 collapse) governed by layer-wise gain and alignment. We validate this theory by analyzing the eigenspectra of activation graphs in Whisper models (Tiny to Large-v3-Turbo) under adversarial stress. Our results are consistent with the theoretical prediction: intermediate models exhibit Structural Disintegration (Regime I), characterized by a 13.4% collapse in Cross-Attention rank. Conversely, large models enter a Compression-Seeking Attractor state (Regime II), where Self-Attention actively compresses rank (-2.34%) and hardens the spectral slope, decoupling the model from acoustic evidence.

ID: 2666
TITLE: Error Diversity and Performance Variability in Zero-Shot Children's Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Abhijit Sinha
ABSTRACT:
Children's speech recognition remains challenging due to acoustic variability and limited labeled data. This study analyzes the behavior of self-supervised learning (SSL) representations for children's speech recognition under a strict zero-shot adult-to-child transfer setting. Layer-wise representations from Wav2Vec2, HuBERT, and Data2Vec are evaluated using hybrid ASR systems trained only on adult speech. Although several layers achieve comparable best-case word error rates, substantial variability exists across representations. Error analysis reveals dataset-dependent error structures, while LLM-based post-recognition correction provides little improvement. Oracle analysis further shows that different layers capture complementary acoustic information, particularly under stronger domain mismatch. These findings indicate that recognition errors are primarily driven by acoustic representation limitations rather than recoverable linguistic inconsistencies.

ID: 3047
TITLE: Gender Bias in ASR: A Controlled Study of Gender Composition Across Training Paradigms
PRESENTER: Seshan S
ABSTRACT:
Gender disparities in ASR are commonly attributed to training data imbalance, often evaluated by fine-tuning pretrained systems on gender-controlled splits. We test whether such fine-tuning reveals a systematic relationship between training composition and gender disparity. Across three pretrained models and 11 splits spanning 0-100% female representation, male and female WERs fluctuate without consistent direction, yielding no systematic composition-disparity relationship. To determine whether the effect exists, we include a from-scratch system as a controlled reference under identical conditions. In contrast, this system exhibits consistent, directional disparity shifts as composition varies, confirming that the effect is real but masked in pretrained models by representations established during large-scale pretraining. These results demonstrate that balancing fine-tuning data alone is insufficient to mitigate gender disparity in pretrained ASR.

ID: 1124
TITLE: Balancing ASR and diarization in end-to-end LLMs for multi-talker speech recognition
PRESENTER: Naijun Zheng
ABSTRACT:
Multi-talker speech recognition is often addressed by combining automatic speech recognition (ASR) and speaker diarization in a pipeline system. Recently, LLM-based approaches have shown promise by jointly modeling semantic and speaker information, but they typically require large-scale multi-talker corpora that are costly to annotate. In this paper, we investigate how to efficiently train an LLM-based system with limited real-recorded data while maintaining high accuracy in speaker attribution. We propose several strategies: (1) a dual-encoder architecture to extract semantic and speaker features, (2) a feature interleaving format to merge these features as the inputs to the LLM, (3) a length-aware speaker ID loss to enhance diarization capability, and (4) an adaptive threshold strategy for ASR loss computation to mitigate hallucinations caused by speech overlaps. These strategies balance training between ASR and diarization tasks. Our system outperforms open-source baseline approaches, achieving relative improvements of 18% on the Al-iMeeting corpus and 24% on the Aishell4 corpus.

ID: 1582
TITLE: Multi-Talker ASR Unaffected by Speaker Change Count
PRESENTER: Naoki Makishima
ABSTRACT:
We develop an autoregressive model for multi-talker automatic speech recognition (ASR) robust to the number of speaker changes. Recent studies on multi-talker ASR handle multiple speakers by recursively estimating a single token sequence formed by concatenating their transcripts with speaker change tokens. However, as this technique heavily relies on context including speaker change tokens, inference performance degrades when the number of speaker changes exceeds those in the training data. To address this limitation, we estimate the token sequence, including speaker change tokens, without relying on how many speaker changes have occurred. This is achieved by introducing a novel speaker change token mask in self-attention, which sets the values of speaker change tokens and randomly selected tokens referenced by other queries to zero, preventing the model from inferring the number of speaker changes from context. Experiments demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed method.

ID: 102
TITLE: WildElder: A Chinese Elderly Speech Dataset from the Wild with Fine-Grained Manual Annotations
PRESENTER: Hui Wang
ABSTRACT:
Elderly speech poses unique challenges for automatic processing due to age-related changes such as slower articulation and vocal tremors. Existing Chinese datasets are mostly recorded in controlled environments, limiting their diversity and real-world applicability. To address this gap, we present WildElder, a Mandarin elderly speech corpus collected from online videos and enriched with fine-grained manual annotations, including transcription, speaker age, gender, and accent strength. Combining the realism of in-the-wild data with expert curation, WildElder enables robust research on automatic speech recognition and speaker profiling. Experimental results reveal both the difficulties of elderly speech recognition and the potential of WildElder as a challenging new benchmark. The dataset and code have been available at https://github.com/NKU-HLT/WildElder.

ID: 907
TITLE: Post-ASR Proper Noun Grounding via Multi-View Phonetic and Semantic Retrieval
PRESENTER: Pranshu Nema
ABSTRACT:
Modern end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems achieve strong overall transcription accuracy but remain vulnerable to proper nouns and long-tail vocabulary. Such errors are often phonetically plausible yet lexically incorrect, degrading downstream systems that rely on accurate entity interpretation. We propose a post-ASR proper noun grounding framework that links noisy mentions to ranked candidates from a predefined lexicon without retraining or modifying the ASR model. The method integrates complementary similarity views: fine-grained phonetic representations via grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P), coarse phonetic encoding using Soundex, and context-aware semantic embeddings, with normalized score fusion for unified ranking. Multi-view fusion increases Recall@1 over vanilla exact match by 36.4 and 23.6 percentage points for Whisper-large-v3 and Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, reaching 74.67% and 59.82% respectively, with up to 87.36% Recall@10 in the strongest configuration.

ID: 338
TITLE: From Text Metrics to Model Internals: A Study of Whisper ASR Hallucination Detection
PRESENTER: Jan Jasiński
ABSTRACT:
Hallucinations of ASR models — fluent transcriptions with no basis in audio — degrade system performance and pose risks in downstream applications. Robust detection of such errors remains a challenge. This paper studies Whisper large v3 hallucination detection on real-speech human-annotated data across three paradigms: text-based, LLM-based, and internal decoder state probing. Text classifiers utilizing metrics for text evaluation achieve high recall but degrade without reference transcripts. LLM-based detection improves precision with domain-specific prompt conditioning, yet remains less competitive than the lightweight text-based methods. Probing Whisper's decoder representations, without a ground-truth reference, yields the strongest performance, revealing that hallucination traits are encoded across intermediate decoding layers. A late-fusion meta-classifier combining text and internal-state outputs achieves the best overall detection performance.

TITLE: Grounding Whisper: An Audio Anchor-Based Approach for Hallucination Mitigation and Throughput-Efficient ASR
PRESENTER: Saurabh Kumar
ABSTRACT:
Whisper exhibits robust ASR performance but suffers from hallucinations on silence and non-speech audio, generating spurious transcriptions that undermine production reliability. We introduce anchor audio, a simple input-level technique that prepends a short audio phrase with near-zero domain occurrence to the input, enabling hallucination detection without modifying or fine-tuning the model. Using this mechanism, we evaluate five inference approaches ranging from single-call augmentation to batch concatenation with automatic fallback, exploring accuracy-latency-throughput trade-offs. Our batch strategy leverages Whisper's fixed 30-second window to concatenate multiple short utterances while maintaining fidelity through anchor-based validation. This reduces overall WER from 32.18% to 13.23%, achieves 0.14% Hallucination Error Rate on non-speech audio, and preserves latency comparable to baseline VAD pipelines, demonstrating an effective, practical solution for hallucination mitigation.
