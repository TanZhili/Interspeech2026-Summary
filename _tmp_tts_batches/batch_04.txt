TITLE: Transcript-Free Flow-Matching Text-to-Speech via Speech Feature Conditioning
ABSTRACT: Recent flow-matching text-to-speech (TTS) models, such as F5-TTS, rely on a reference transcript at inference time, obtained from an external ASR system. This dependency makes zero-shot TTS brittle for accented or dysarthric speakers, precisely the scenarios where it is most needed. Moreover, we find that text-based reference conditioning can propagate atypical acoustic patterns from atypical speech into synthesis, even when ground-truth transcripts are available. To address this, we propose RTFree-F5, which replaces the reference transcript with continuous self-supervised speech representations mapped into F5-TTS's text-conditioning space via a lightweight adapter, while reusing the pretrained checkpoint. On dysarthric speech, RTFree-F5 reduces WER from 24.6% to 10.4%, surpassing even the ground-truth reference transcript baselines, while improving naturalness and remaining competitive on standard benchmarks without requiring any reference transcript.

TITLE: Unified Prosody Restoration Using Diffusion Models for Controllable Text-to-Speech Synthesis
ABSTRACT: Controllable text-to-speech systems that use explicit prosodic features enable fine-grained control, which is particularly useful for emotional speech synthesis. However, specifying frame-level details often imposes a heavy burden on users. To alleviate this burden, we formulate prosody restoration as a unified problem of recovering frame-level prosody from simplified and easy-to-specify prosodic inputs. By expanding two prior tasks with three novel ones, we address a total of five tasks. Together, they support practical user workflows by enabling restoration from prosody that is partial, coarse, or both. We also propose two diffusion-based prosody restorers: a supervised model trained to reverse simulated degradations, and an unsupervised model leveraging samplers for linear inverse problems. Experiments across all five tasks show that our methods restore prosody more accurately than non-diffusion baselines, preserving linguistically valid prosodic structures.

TITLE: Beyond Text-to-Music: Control, Agency, and Evaluation in Generative Audio
ABSTRACT: Generative audio has rapidly expanded from speech synthesis to music, singing, environmental sound, instrument synthesis, and audio production. TITLE: Negation in Audio Generation Models
ABSTRACT: What happens when a text-to-audio (T2A) model is asked to generate silence or exclude a specific sound? Existing models often generate the very events they are instructed to omit. Negation comprehension is critical for faithful audio generation, yet no benchmark has systematically measured it. We introduce Audio Negation Benchmark, comprising one million negated prompts spanning four negation types and three negation scopes, derived from AudioCaps captions. Human annotation of a representative subset confirms that the intended negation is correctly introduced in 99.6% of prompts. To evaluate negation understanding at scale, we propose an Audio Question Answering (AQA) protocol that probes the presence or absence of negated events using multiple-choice questions, scored against the ground-truth absence label. Evaluating three state-of-the-art models, AudioGen, AudioLDM2, and TangoFlux, we find that AQA recall for negated audio falls below 0.05 across all negation types and all models: negated and affirmative prompts produce acoustically near-identical outputs. Re-captioning the generated audio independently confirms that negative prompts consistently default to affirmative soundscapes. These results establish negation handling as a fundamental open problem in multimodal generative AI and motivate negation-aware training objectives for semantically faithful audio generation.

TITLE: DiffRhythm 2: Efficient and High Fidelity Song Generation via Block Flow Matching
ABSTRACT: Generating full-length, high-quality songs requires lyric–music consistency and coherent musical structure. Existing non-autoregressive (NAR) methods are efficient but often struggle with temporal alignment between lyrics and singing vocals. Meanwhile, reinforcement learning from human feedback across multiple preferences often relies on merging separately optimized models, causing performance degradation. We introduce DiffRhythm 2, an end-to-end framework for controllable song generation. It uses semi-autoregressive block flow matching to achieve faithful lyric–vocal alignment without duration labels or explicit constraints, while preserving NAR quality and efficiency. A 5 Hz music variational autoencoder enables long-sequence modeling with high-fidelity reconstruction. For multi-preference optimization, we propose cross-pair preference optimization, which jointly learns preference pairs across reward dimensions in one model and avoids merging-induced degradation. We further introduce stochastic block representation alignment loss to improve musicality and structural coherence. DiffRhythm 2 generates songs up to 210 seconds, outperforms open-source models in subjective and objective evaluations, and remains efficient. Code and weights are availble at: https://github.com/xiaomi-research/diffrhythm2.

TITLE: Scaling Properties of Continuous Diffusion Spoken Language Models
ABSTRACT: Speech-only spoken language models (SLMs) lag behind text and text-speech models in performance, with recent discrete autoregressive (AR) SLMs indicating significant computational and data demands to match text models. Since discretizing continuous speech for AR creates bottlenecks, we explore whether continuous diffusion (CD) SLM is more viable. To quantify the SLMs linguistic quality, we introduce the phoneme Jensen-Shannon divergence (pJSD) metric. Our analysis reveals CD SLMs, mirroring AR behavior, exhibit scaling laws for validation loss and pJSD, and show optimal token-to-parameter ratios decreasing as compute scales. However, for the latter, loss becomes insensitive to choice of data and model sizes, showing potential for fast inference. Scaling CD SLMs to 16B parameters with tens of millions of hours of conversational data enables generation of emotive, prosodic, multi-speaker, multilingual speech, though achieving long-form coherence remains a significant challenge.

TITLE: Word-level Emotional Intensity Control in TTS via Emotion Residual Vectors
ABSTRACT: Modern emotional text-to-speech (TTS) models typically rely on utterance-level conditioning. However, natural emotional speech requires fine-grained variations across words to achieve nuanced, human-like expressiveness. To address this, we introduce emotion residual vectors (ERVs), which capture neutral-to-emotional deviations in word-aligned self-supervised speech embeddings as an annotation-free cue for local prosody. To stabilize intensity control, we compress ERVs into a low-dimensional bottleneck space and train a RoBERTa-based predictor to infer the projected vectors from text and emotion. During inference, the predicted vectors are converted into additive hidden-state offsets and injected into a neutral-conditioned TTS model, enabling continuous word-level intensity control. Experimental results show that our method improves local emotional controllability while preserving naturalness compared to controllable emotional TTS baselines.
