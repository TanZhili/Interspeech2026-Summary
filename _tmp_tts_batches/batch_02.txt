TITLE: SRF-SVB: Style-Consistent Singing Voice Beautifying via Rectified Flow
ABSTRACT: Singing voice beautifying (SVB) aims to correct pitch and rhythm of amateur singing while enhancing vocal quality, preserving lyrics and the singer's timbre. Existing methods, however, suffer from limited generation quality and efficiency, and tend to neglect the preservation of the singer's style. We propose SRF-SVB, a style-consistent model for SVB via rectified flow, which achieves high-fidelity and efficient beautification covering pitch and rhythm correction. Furthermore, we design a context-guided masked mel-spectrogram inpainting mechanism that effectively preserves the amateur singer's style, including unique timbre and expressive patterns. Experiments on both English and Chinese test sets show that SRF-SVB outperforms baseline models in most objective and subjective metrics.

TITLE: Towards Unified Song Generation and Singing Voice Conversion with Accompaniment Co-Generation
ABSTRACT: While song generation and singing voice conversion (SVC) have evolved significantly, they have long been developed isolated: the former lacks zero-shot speaker cloning, while the latter overlooks vocal-accompaniment synergy. To bridge this gap, we propose UniSinger, the first end-to-end framework unifying speaker cloning song generation and accompaniment co-generation SVC. Building on the multimodal diffusion transformer, we construct a unified speaker embedding space transferring speaker representation from SVC to song generation, endowing fine-grained cross-task timbre control. To mitigate multi-task optimization conflicts, we design a curriculum learning strategy using task-specific modality masking to guide the model to gradually master the generative mechanisms among semantic content, vocal timbre, and accompaniment. Experiments show state-of-the-art performance on both tasks and realizes complementary benefits, offering new possibilities for intelligent music production.

ABSTRACT: This paper establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for Singing Voice Synthesis (SVS) of Chinese Yue Opera in the Wu dialect, addressing a critical gap in this unexplored field. TITLE: Back to Ear: Perceptually Driven High Fidelity Music Reconstruction
ABSTRACT: Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are essential for large-scale audio tasks like diffusion-based generation. However, existing open-source models often neglect auditory perception, resulting in poor phase accuracy and stereo representation. We propose ear-VAE, an open-source music reconstruction model that optimizes the VAE training paradigm. Our contributions are threefold: (i) A K-weighting perceptual filter applied prior to loss calculation to align the objective with auditory perception. (ii) A phase-aware correlation loss improving stereo coherence and spatial consistency. (iii) A spectral supervision scheme where magnitude loss is applied to all MSLR components, while phase is supervised only on LR components. Experiments show ear-VAE substantially outperforms leading open-source models across diverse metrics, showing particular strength in reconstructing high-frequency harmonics and spatial characteristics.

TITLE: Low-Resource Speech Synthesis: What Have We Solved, and What Remains?
ABSTRACT: Speech synthesis has traditionally required substantial amounts of transcribed speech, limiting its availability to only a fraction of the world’s languages. Multilingual and cross-lingual transfer, learning from unpaired data, unsupervised speech-unit discovery, and more recently foundation models have dramatically reduced these requirements. Today, models can synthesize hundreds or even thousands of languages, sometimes with little or no transcribed speech in the target language. This talk surveys the key advances behind this progress and highlights what has been successfully addressed in low-resource speech synthesis. It then examines what remains challenging, particularly for genuinely under-resourced languages, including linguistic diversity, synthesis quality, and reliable evaluation. Finally, drawing on experiences from Indigenous and endangered language technology, I discuss how future progress can better connect advances in speech synthesis with the needs and priorities of language communities.

TITLE: Lightweight Cross-Lingual Speaker Adaptation for Indic TTS
ABSTRACT: Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have improved significantly, but most work focuses on high-resource languages, leaving Indian languages less explored. Voice-cloning models trained on Indic data often suffer from word-dropping errors and slow inference, making deployment difficult. We propose a lightweight speaker adaptation pipeline that uses only a 10-second reference sample. Synthetic speech generated from a voice-cloning model is filtered through a four-stage quality-control pipeline and used to fine-tune a FastSpeech2 model with dual-site speaker conditioning and a cosine consistency loss. Phoneme unification through the Common Label Set (CLS) enables the adapted voice to synthesize speech across Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, and Telugu without additional recordings. The resulting system produces deterministic outputs, runs 53x faster than the baseline, and improves intelligibility and naturalness while preserving speaker characteristics in cross-lingual synthesis.

ABSTRACT: Crowdsourced pairwise evaluation has emerged as a scalable approach for assessing foundation models. TITLE: Application context in speech synthesis evaluation: A problem and a solution
ABSTRACT: Advancements in research of synthetic speech can only be properly quantified and driven by well-proven evaluation procedures, the most prevalent of which being the subjective listening test. In this paper, the ecological validity of such tests is evaluated empirically in an experiment comparing rating outcomes of four synthetic speech systems across four different application contexts. It is shown that, depending on the scenario in which they are obtained, the ratings for a respective synthesis system change significantly. Additionally, it is found that the impact of the application also depends on the system in question, highlighting a potential confound in evaluations comparing systems without a specified application context. Further, it is discovered that ratings collected in a digital twin provide statistically equivalent ratings to the ones obtained in a physical setting, suggesting that simulation testing might prove a beneficial path to achieving ecologically valid evaluations.

TITLE: Is Natural Always Appropriate? Investigating Naturalness and Appropriateness Across Different Domains for TTS Evaluation
ABSTRACT: Text-to-speech (TTS) evaluation is an open challenge. While the primary target was &quot;naturalness,&quot; recent fidelity gains shifted focus toward &quot;appropriateness&quot; and whether speech is correct for its context. In this work, we examine how perception changes when the expected downstream use varies. We measure the appropriateness and human-likeness of five SOTA TTS systems across five domains: AI assistant, reader, actor, animated character, and spontaneous speaker. Results show appropriateness varies across domains independently of naturalness. While systems shine at reading, expressive domains remain challenging, and optimizing for one can degrade others. Furthermore, naturalness scores tend to penalize stylized speech while rewarding spontaneity. Finally, our study also highlights blind spots in one-size-fits-all evaluation metrics across more expressive domains. We demonstrate that TTS performance is not &quot;solved&quot; but depends on the target domain, requiring context-aware evaluation.

TITLE: NV-Bench: Benchmark of Nonverbal Vocalization Synthesis for Expressive Text-to-Speech Generation
ABSTRACT: While recent text-to-speech (TTS) systems increasingly integrate nonverbal vocalizations (NVs), their evaluations lack standardized metrics and reliable ground-truth references. To bridge this gap, we propose NV-Bench, the first benchmark grounded in a functional taxonomy that treats NVs as communicative acts rather than acoustic artifacts. NV-Bench comprises 1,651 multi-lingual, in-the-wild utterances with paired human reference audio, balanced across 14 NV categories. We introduce a dual-dimensional evaluation protocol: (1) Instruction Alignment, utilizing the proposed paralinguistic character error rate (PCER) to assess controllability, (2) Acoustic Fidelity, measuring the distributional gap to real recordings to assess acoustic realism. Experimental results demonstrate a strong correlation between our objective metrics and human perception, establishing NV-Bench as a standardized evaluation framework.

TITLE: Phonetically Grounded Vowel Space Metrics for Evaluating Synthetic Speech During TTS Model Training
ABSTRACT: Synthetic speech is generally evaluated using subjective listening tests, which are costly and impractical to perform during text-to-speech (TTS) model training. Traditional loss curves in model training lack linguistic interpretability and fail to reflect perceptual changes. We propose using phonetically grounded objective metrics, Vowel Space Overlap and Procrustes Normalised Disparity, to evaluate synthetic speech during training by quantifying the similarity between synthesised and ground-truth vowel space shapes. We fine-tuned a TTS model on two accents, computed the metrics at training steps, and conducted perception tests to assess the relationship between the metrics and perceived accent similarity. Our results show significant correlations between metrics and accent perception across both accents. This suggests that the proposed metrics provide reliable, perceptually meaningful, and interpretable complements to loss curves for evaluating synthetic speech during training.

TITLE: SALT: Selective Allophone-Level Tokenization for Korean Text-to-Speech Synthesis
ABSTRACT: Recent Korean TTS studies often use direct grapheme input due to the phonetic transparency of the Korean alphabet, Hangul. However, Korean orthography is also morphophonemic, and grapheme sequences do not always correspond to the actual pronunciation. Since the mapping between graphemes, phonemes, and allophones is almost deterministic in the Standard Korean Pronunciation, we propose Selective Allophone-Level Tokenization (SALT), which incorporates a selected set of allophonic phenomena into the input rather than relying on implicit learning by the TTS model. In experiments fine-tuning a pre-trained model, SALT-N, which improves token distributional balance, outperformed baselines. In a 12.75-hour dataset, SALT-N obtained a CER of 3.30% and achieved the highest NMOS, whereas grapheme input resulted in a CER of 4.74%. In an extremely low-resource 1-hour setting, SALT-N was the only method to achieve a CER below 10% (8.19%), whereas all baseline models suffered from severe degradation.

TITLE: G2PO: A Lightweight Lexicon-enhanced Framework for Open-Vocabulary Mandarin Polyphone Disambiguation
ABSTRACT: Polyphone disambiguation is a crucial component for Chinese Text-to-Speech systems. While recent arts achieve high accuracy using large pre-trained models like BERT, their massive parameter sizes hinder on-device deployment. Furthermore, these closed-set classifiers fail to predict pronunciations unseen during training. To address this, we propose g2pO, a lightweight open-vocabulary framework. It adopts a tiny BERT encoder and fuses external dictionary knowledge into hidden states via a lexicon adapter. Instead of using heavy pre-trained word embeddings to encode dictionary information, our method dynamically constructs word representations from hidden states via mix-pooling. These designs minimize the parameter size and enable generalization to unseen pronunciations. Experiments show with only 3.99M parameters (27x smaller than BERT-based methods), g2pO outperforms previous methods, achieving 99.15% accuracy on the CPP dataset and 70.79% zero-shot accuracy on unseen pronunciations.
