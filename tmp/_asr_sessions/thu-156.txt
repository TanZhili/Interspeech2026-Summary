ID: 1195
TITLE: Reducing the Offline-Streaming Gap for Unified ASR Transducer with Consistency Regularization
PRESENTER: Andrei Andrusenko
ABSTRACT:
Unification of automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems reduces development and maintenance costs, but training a single model to perform well in both offline and low-latency streaming settings remains challenging. We present a Unified ASR framework for Transducer (RNNT) training that supports both offline and streaming decoding within a single model, using chunk-limited attention with right context and dynamic chunked convolutions. To further close the gap between offline and streaming performance, we introduce an efficient Triton implementation of mode-consistency regularization for RNNT (MCR-RNNT), which encourages agreement across training modes. Experiments show that the proposed approach improves streaming accuracy at low latency while preserving offline performance and scaling to larger model sizes and training datasets. The proposed Unified ASR framework and the English model checkpoint are open-sourced.

ID: 1455
TITLE: BACON: Boundary-Aware Convolution for Streaming Conformer Models
PRESENTER: Hainan Xu
ABSTRACT:
We propose Boundary-Aware CONvolution (BACON), a drop-in replacement for causal convolution in streaming sequence tasks that leverage chunk-based Conformers. Streaming Conformers typically pair chunked attention with causal convolution to prevent future information leakage. We show that causal convolution is unnecessarily restrictive: within a chunk, all frames are already available, so the convolution kernel can safely look ahead up to the chunk boundary without violating the streaming constraint. BACON exploits this by splitting the depthwise convolution channels into a causal group and a boundary-aware bidirectional group, yielding a wider effective receptive field than the causal baseline while preserving parameter count. Across single-speaker Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), multi-speaker ASR, and speech-to-text translation tasks with two model architectures, BACON improves model accuracy while maintaining comparable latency.

ID: 2132
TITLE: Progressive Alignment Objectives for Aligner-Encoder based ASR
PRESENTER: Jaeyoung Lee
ABSTRACT:
Aligner-Encoders are recently proposed seq2seq end-to-end ASR models that replace decoder attention by predicting the u-th token directly from the u-th encoder position, so the encoder must learn the alignment internally without cross-attention or a transducer lattice. In practice, this alignment often forms abruptly in the upper layers, making training sensitive and brittle on long utterances. We propose InterAligner, which adds an intermediate Aligner objective so alignment can form progressively across depth, together with an intermediate CTC loss (InterCTC) to stabilize optimization. On LibriSpeech with a 17-layer Conformer, a final-only Aligner reaches 5.0/7.8 WER (test-clean/other). InterCTC improves to 3.4/6.0, and InterAligner further reduces WER to 3.1/5.6 with the largest gains on long utterances.

ID: 1738
TITLE: From Bilevel to Trilevel: Joint Training for Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Jen-Tzung Chien
ABSTRACT:
This paper presents the practical optimization strategies to extend the bilevel learning into a trilevel setting for speech recognition. This method jointly trains an acoustic model according to supervised, unsupervised, and distilled learning objectives. To make trilevel training feasible in large-scale ASR models, we employ a sequential penalty-based bilevel gradient descent method that collapses the lower two levels into a bilevel subproblem and then integrates it with the upper level. This formulation allows the model to leverage the general structure from unlabeled data, the task-specific alignment from labeled speech, and the knowledge transfer from a teacher network within a single optimization loop. The experiments on LibriSpeech with a FastConformer backbone demonstrate that these strategies yield the consistent improvements over the conventional two-stage pre-training and fine-tuning pipeline, as well as over the prior bilevel optimization methods.

ID: 2149
TITLE: LLM-as-Joiner: Decoupling Alignment from Language Modeling in Label-synchronous ASR
PRESENTER: Jaeyoung Lee
ABSTRACT:
We present LLM-as-Joiner, an LLM-based ASR architecture that decouples speech-text alignment from language modeling. A speech encoder first converts an input of T acoustic frames into U token-level speech states, one per output token, following the Aligner-Encoder framework. A pretrained LLM then predicts the transcript from these U states, with speech injected at a chosen LLM layer; only upper LLM layers are adapted via LoRA. Compared to conventional decoder-only speech-as-prefix decoding, the LLM operates on a shorter context (U rather than T + U), reducing memory and compute. On LibriSpeech and multilingual Common Voice, the proposed LLM-as-Joiner outperforms size-matched baselines, and joint training with a lightweight recognizer yields additional gains in the lightweight head, suggesting implicit transfer of linguistic knowledge from the LLM to the speech encoder.

TITLE: Accurate Source-Free Speech Classification via Meta-Learned Target-Centric Model Merging
PRESENTER: Ka Hyun Park
ABSTRACT:
How can we build a speech classifier for an unseen domain when access to source-domain data is unavailable? Speech classification is widely used (e.g., voice commands, emotion recognition, etc.), but it is highly sensitive to domain shifts (e.g., languages), often causing substantial performance degradation. This issue becomes critical when source data cannot be accessed and only pretrained source models with limited target data are available. Under such constraints, typical adaptation approaches are inapplicable, and existing model merging methods easily overfit the limited target data. We propose Mᴏᴄʜᴇᴇ, an accurate target-centric model merging framework that combines multiple pretrained source models using limited target data. To improve generalization under scarce target labels, Mᴏᴄʜᴇᴇ exploits meta-learning to resolve representation misalignment and emphasize target-relevant sources. Extensive experiments show that Mᴏᴄʜᴇᴇ outperforms the baselines by up to 14.5 points in Macro-F1.
