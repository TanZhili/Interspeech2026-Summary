TITLE: Cross-Lingual Compositional Learning for Code-Switched Lip Reading
PRESENTER: Jeonghyeon Joo
ABSTRACT:
Code-switching, the alternation of multiple languages in an utterance, is pervasive in multilingual communication but underexplored in visual speech recognition (VSR). Especially, the burden of collecting large-scale and realistic videos has limited the advances in code-switched VSR. We propose CoCoVSR, a cross-lingual compositional learning framework that exploits monolingual corpora. Specifically, without additional data collection or generative synthesis, CoCoVSR adapts a pretrained multilingual VSR model to code-switched scenarios, while preserving shared articulatory features. Experimental results show that CoCoVSR achieves SOTA on Chinese-English CSLR. Importantly, CoCoVSR achieves competitive performance on both seen (MultiVSR Chinese) and unseen (LRS2 English) multilingual datasets, highlighting its capability to preserve multilingual performance with negligible degradation. The code is available at https://github.com/ewha-mmai/CoCoVSR.

ID: 977
TITLE: Refining Pseudo-Audio Prompts with Speech-Text Alignment for Text-Only Domain Adaptation in LLM-Based ASR
PRESENTER: Ryo Magoshi
ABSTRACT:
LLM-based automatic speech recognition models demonstrate strong performance by connecting audio encoders and LLMs. However, data scarcity of paired speech and transcription often hinders the adaptation to new domains, making text-only domain adaptation crucial. Existing methods typically rely on either fine-tuning the LLM alone or employing pseudo-audio prompts. The former neglects essential acoustic context, while the latter either suffers from limited scalability in data-scarce conditions, or yields inexpressive prompts by leveraging only textual features, ignoring audio modality. To address this, we propose a framework that explicitly models speech-text alignment. Our method efficiently generates highly expressive pseudo-audio prompts that bridge the modality gap, enabling effective target-domain adaptation. Experiments demonstrate that our approach outperforms existing text-only methods, improving both overall error rates and out-of-vocabulary coverage.

TITLE: Content-Aware Dynamic Compression for Efffcient Speech Recognition based on Large Language Model
PRESENTER: Bingqian Wang
ABSTRACT:
Current LLM-based speech recognition systems use fixed-rate downsampling for acoustic representation, which overlooks speech content dynamics and causes information loss or redundancy. We propose a content-aware dynamic acoustic map-ping method via the Continuous Integrate-and-Fire (CIF) mechanism, adaptively aligning speech embeddings with transcription token counts. This enables content-guided downsampling during training and inference, cutting LLM input length while preserving or boosting accuracy. On AISHELL-1 and Lib-riSpeech, we achieve 12-26% relative error reduction at comparable average speech embedding length (ASEL). While main-taining comparable performance, we achieve over 45% ASEL reduction with 7-19% lower time to first token (TTFT) across utterance lengths. GigaSpeech results confirm scalability. Our work establishes an efficient paradigm improving the accuracy-efficiency trade-off in LLM-based speech recognition.

ID: 1630
TITLE: Upcycling Pretrained Transformers into Mixture-of-Experts for Multilingual Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Kentaro Shinayama
ABSTRACT:
Fine-tuning large pretrained models is a powerful way to obtain high-accuracy systems for downstream ASR tasks. However, when fine-tuning on highly diverse data, such as multilingual corpora, performance can degrade compared with training separate models per language, largely due to limited model capacity. In this paper, we propose a simple capacity expansion method for multilingual fine-tuning by upcycling the feed-forward network (FFN) components of a pretrained model into a post-hoc Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture. This conversion substantially increases the model's representational capacity while preserving inference efficiency: at test time, we activate only a single upcycled FFN expert, keeping the number of active parameters identical to the original dense model. We investigate two expert selection strategies: (1) hard routing based on language identity, and (2) soft routing using a learned router. Experiments on 10-language speech recognition with CommonVoice and additional ASR evaluations on four Asian languages demonstrate that the proposed upcycling-to-MoE approach consistently improves multilingual fine-tuning effectiveness.

ID: 2455
TITLE: Token-Independent Language Representations for Low-Latency Configurable Multilingual Speech Recognition
PRESENTER: Hongxu Zhu
ABSTRACT:
Configurable multilingual speech recognition enables a single model to be tailored to any user-selected language combination via language-specific modules (LSMs). While providing state-of-the-art accuracy, it incurs high latency as decoder LSMs are executed at every step, leading to extra computational costs that scale linearly with the output token length. To eliminate this per-token overhead, we propose token-independent language representations that decouple language identity from the autoregressive decoding loop. We replace the neural LSMs in the decoder with language representations enriched by one-time, utterance-level encoder cues. This reduces the LSM’s per-token complexity from quadratic to linear relative to the hidden dimension, while preserving utterance-level adaptability. Experiments show that our method achieves accuracy comparable to the original design while maintaining a near-constant LSM’s overhead, reducing peak inference latency by over 90% for long utterances.

ID: 822
TITLE: GC-LoRA: Gated Convolutional LoRA for Parameter-Efficient Acoustic Adaptation
PRESENTER: Abeer Alwan
ABSTRACT:
Transformer-based Speech Foundation Models excel in most Automatic Speech Recognition tasks but often suffer performance degradation when applied to domains with mismatched acoustic characteristics. While Parameter Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), adjust global attention, they lack the local context modeling crucial for capturing domain-specific variations. We propose GC-LoRA, a novel adapter architecture that injects Conformer-style local convolutional processing into pretrained Transformer encoders. By integrating a lightweight adapter to encoder attention output projections, our method efficiently captures local acoustic dependencies without disrupting pretrained global representations. Experiments across diverse datasets (acoustically-degraded, bandlimited, dialectal, child) demonstrate the efficacy of our approach, achieving Word Error Rate (WER) reductions of up to 10.9% compared to baselines while adding minimal trainable parameters.

ID: 1522
TITLE: MambAdapter: Lightweight Mamba-Based Adapters for Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning in Speech and Audio
PRESENTER: Umberto Cappellazzo
ABSTRACT:
Fine-tuning Transformer-based foundation models has become the dominant strategy for domain adaptation in audio and speech processing. To reduce the computational and memory costs of this process, parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods have been widely explored. Meanwhile, Mamba, a recent state-space model, has emerged as a promising alternative to Transformers for sequence modeling. In this work, we present MambAdapter, a parameter-efficient transfer learning approach that integrates Mamba into low-rank bottleneck adapters. Our design combines parameter sharing across adapters with the injection of a lightweight Mamba module, enabling more effective modeling of audio features. We demonstrate that MambAdapter matches or outperforms strong PETL baselines on four audio classification tasks and five speech recognition languages, even when operating under reduced parameter budgets.

ID: 1787
TITLE: PhonePrune: One-shot Phoneme-Aware Pruning for Large-scale ASR Models via Phoneme Set Generation and Calibration
PRESENTER: Minsik Lee
ABSTRACT:
Large-scale Automatic Speech Recognition Models face deployment challenges due to massive parameter counts. While one-shot pruning compresses them, conventional methods indiscriminately discard low-magnitude parameters, degrading fine-grained phonetic distinctions. We conceptualize the sub-networks of these delicate weights as the Phoneme Ticket Hypothesis, arguing they must be preserved. We propose PhonePrune to preserve these weights via Phoneme Set Generation and Phoneme-aware Calibration. Evaluated on LibriSpeech and Common Voice (English, Korean, Japanese) at 50% sparsity, PhonePrune demonstrates strong results specifically on the Korean and Japanese datasets, yielding 13.41% and 13.83% Word Error Rate reductions over Distil-Whisper, respectively. Our success stems from preserving fragile parameters shared by these languages, such as subtle spectral variations in Korean and precise temporal durations in Japanese.

TITLE: Can Large Language Models Reliably Correct Errors in Low-Resource ASR? A Contamination-Aware Case Study on West Frisian
PRESENTER: Yun Hao
ABSTRACT:
Automatic speech recognition (ASR) has improved substantially in recent years, yet performance remains limited for low-resource languages. Large language models (LLMs) have shown promise for improving ASR through generative error correction (GER), but their effectiveness in low-resource settings remains underexplored. In addition, it remains unclear to what extent data contamination influences the reported improvements in LLM-based GER. This study investigates LLM-based GER for low-resource Frisian. In addition to a public corpus, we construct and use a Frisian offline dataset with non-public texts for evaluation to control for potential data contamination. Results show that GER improves ASR performance in most settings, with the best GPT-5.1 results surpassing oracle WERs. Comparable gains on the offline dataset indicate that improvements reflect true correction ability. We further provide a detailed error analysis revealing model correction patterns.
