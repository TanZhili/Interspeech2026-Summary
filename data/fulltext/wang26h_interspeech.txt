Abstract
Existing unsupervised voice-face association methods typically
rely on clustering to mitigate false-negative conflicts but often
struggle with unreliable pseudo-labels due to rigid prototype
assumptions. To overcome this, we propose GMOD, a novel
graph-based framework. First, we introduce a global similar-
ity graph with a curriculum-guided mining strategy to capture
fine-grained identity distributions. This enables the discovery
of latent positive samples for multi-positive contrastive learn-
ing, robustly mitigating early-stage noise. Second, we design
an orthogonal disentanglement mechanism to effectively extract
cross-modal shared identity features, thereby enhancing identity
consistency. Extensive experiments on the V oxCeleb dataset
demonstrate that GMOD significantly outperforms state-of-the-
art baselines. Our code and pre-trained models are available
online https://github.com/BKB00001/GMOD.
Index Terms: voice-face association, multi-positive contrastive
learning, orthogonal disentanglement, unsupervised learning
1. Introduction
Research in human perception and neurology has shown that
there exists an intrinsic association between voices and faces
and humans are able to a certain extent, to infer facial appear-
ance from voices alone or associate voices with corresponding
facial characteristics [1, 2]. Inspired by these findings, Nagrani
et al. introduced the voice-face association learning problem
into the deep learning paradigm by formulating it as a classi-
fication task in SVHF [3]. However, classification-based for-
mulations are inherently constrained by the number of classes
defined by the classification head, making them less suitable for
open-set matching and retrieval scenarios. Moreover, due to the
scarcity of large-scale identity annotations, approaches that di-
rectly exploit videos under unsupervised settings are of greater
practical value.
Under unsupervised settings, existing voice-face associa-
tion learning methods mainly suffer from two challenges. The
first is the false-negative conflict: speech segments from the
same speaker often appear in different videos, causing instance-
level contrastive learning to mistakenly treat cross-video same-
identity samples as negatives and push them mistakenly apart
during optimization. To mitigate this issue, PINs [4] employs
curriculum learning to reduce the influence of false negatives

approximations of class distributions, struggle to capture com-

The second challenge lies in the entanglement of modality-
specific attributes. Due to the heterogeneity between voice
and face modalities, identity representations often contain both
cross-modal shared information and modality-private identity
cues. Without explicit constraints, these modality-private com-
ponents may be projected into the shared embedding space, in-
terfering with cross-modal alignment and weakening identity-
level discriminative consistency.
To address these challenges, we propose GMOD, an unsu-
pervised framework for voice-face association learning that in-
tegrates graph mining and orthogonal disentanglement. GMOD
constructs a global cross-modal similarity graph to mine po-
tential cross-video positives and employs orthogonal disentan-
glement to explicitly separate shared identity features from
modality-private noise, enabling more stable and robust cross-
modal alignment in an unsupervised setting.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as
follows: (1) We analyze false-negative conflicts and entangle-
ment of modality-specific attributes in unsupervised voice-face
association learning; (2) We propose GMOD, which leverages
global graph mining and multi-positive contrastive learning to
alleviate class-structure fragmentation caused by false nega-
tives; (3) We design an orthogonal disentanglement mechanism
to enhance the robustness of identity representations; (4) Exper-
iments on V oxCeleb1[7] demonstrate the superiority of the pro-
posed method in verification, cross-modal retrieval and match-
ing tasks.

3. Method

shared-private feature disentanglement, and (2) graph-guided
positive mining. The former explicitly separates cross-modal
shared identity information from modality-private characteris-
tics, while the latter exploits global similarity structure to mine
latent positive samples, alleviating false-negative issues in con-
trastive learning.

4. Experiments
Implementation Details.GMOD employed ECAPA-TDNN
[19] and FaceNet [20] to extract face and voice embeddings.
Training utilizes the Adam optimizer [21] (lr= 10 −4, batch
size=256) for 200 epochs, with early stopping triggered if vali-
dation AUC stagnates for 10 consecutive epochs. Loss weights
are set toλ cl =λ rec = 1.0andλ o = 0.1, with temperature
τ= 0.07. For graph mining, the neighbor sizeklinearly de-
cays from 20 to 8 over the first 20 epochs to balance mining
breadth and precision.
Dataset.Experiments are conducted on the V oxCeleb1
dataset [7] for voice-face association learning. Sourced from
YouTube, the dataset comprises 1,251 speakers with 153,516
speech utterances and 1,217,558 face images. The data is par-
titioned into a training set of 901 identities, a validation set
of 100 identities, and a test set of 250 identities. Notably, al-
though identity labels are provided, they are not utilized during
the training phase to strictly adhere to the unsupervised setting.
Evaluation Metrics.Following established protocols [5,
6], we employ the Area Under the Curve (AUC), matching ac-
curacy (ACC), and mean Average Precision (mAP) to evalu-
ate performance on cross-modal verification, matching, and re-
trieval tasks, respectively.
4.1. V oice-Face Association Results
We compare the proposed method with several state-of-the-art
(SOTA) voice-face association methods, including Pins [4], FV-
CME [9], LAFV [8], DIMNet [22], VFMR [10], FOP [17],
SL [5], Wen et al. [23], CMPC [6], SBNet [24], and PA-
EFF [18].
As shown in Table 1, GMOD outperforms SOTA meth-
ods across all tasks. In verification, it achieves a leading
AUC of 87.73%, validating the efficacy of global graph min-
ing in recovering latent identity associations. For 1:2 matching,
GMOD attains 87.04% accuracy in both directions, demonstrat-
ing that our framework effectively ensures cross-modal consis-
tency. Furthermore, significant gains in retrieval (mAP 7.14%
and 7.59%) confirm the learned representations are highly dis-
criminative and robust. To further support these quantitative
findings, qualitative retrieval examples and t-SNE visualiza-
tions are provided in the supplementary file Supplementary.pdf.
These intuitively illustrate GMOD’s robustness to intra-class
variations in cross-modal retrieval and its ability to effectively
mitigate false negative conflicts under unsupervised conditions.

4.2. Ablation Study
Impact of Graph Mining Strategies.The superior perfor-
mance of graph-based models over the ”w/o Graph” base-
line confirms that global graph mining effectively mitigates
false negative conflicts inherent in unsupervised learning.
Among construction strategies, GMOD consistently outper-
forms GMOD f2v and dual variants. This is attributed to asym-
metric feature stability: voice embeddings exhibit higher cross-
video consistency, acting as robust anchors (V2F), whereas
merging pose-variant face-retrieved edges (F2V) introduces
false positives that degrade the graph’s structural consistency.
Beyond construction direction, ablating the dynamic neighbor-
hood validates our curriculum learning strategy. A fixedk= 8
fails to capture sufficient latent positives during the unaligned
cold-start phase, causing representation collapse. Conversely, a
fixedk= 20introduces sustained noise that restricts late-stage
fine-grained alignment. Ultimately, decayingkseamlessly bal-
ances early-stage robust exploration with late-stage precise ex-
ploitation, ensuring reliable cross-modal identity alignment. A
more detailed ablation study on the curriculum learning strategy
is provided in the supplementary file Supplementary.pdf.
Impact of Disentanglement.The introduction of the dis-
entanglement yields stable performance gains, particularly in
verification (AUC) and retrieval (mAP). Without this explicit
separation, modality-specific noise tends to leak into the shared
embedding space, interfering with identity consistency. By en-
forcing orthogonality, the model is compelled to focus exclu-
sively on cross-modally sharable identity features, thereby en-
hancing discrimination. Although the framework remains ef-
fective without this constraint, the observed performance drop
confirms that orthogonal disentanglement serves as a critical
stabilizer for optimizing the upper bound of representation qual-
ity. To further validate that the N-branch strictly captures
modality-private noise rather than identity, we conducted an
anti-matching test using solely the modality-private features
(denoted as GMOD n). As expected, the verification AUC
plummeted to near-random levels (e.g., 49.28%), and retrieval
mAP dropped to 0.84%, strongly confirming that our mecha-
nism successfully isolates modality-specific variations without
leaking identity cues.
5. Conclusion
In this work, we presented GMOD, a novel framework for
robust unsupervised voice-face association. By constructing
a global similarity graph with a curriculum-learning mining
strategy, GMOD effectively mitigates false-negative conflicts.
Furthermore, the proposed orthogonal disentanglement mecha-
nism effectively extracts cross-modal shared identity features,
thereby enhancing cross-modal identity consistency. Extensive
experiments on the V oxCeleb1 dataset demonstrate that GMOD
significantly outperforms state-of-the-art unsupervised methods
in verification, matching, and retrieval tasks. These results con-
firm the efficacy of our graph-based multi-positive contrastive
learning approach in establishing reliable and consistent cross-
modal identity alignment without reliance on ground-truth la-
bels. In future work, we plan to explore the adaptability of the
GMOD framework in complex multilingual environments.
