Abstract
Cross-subject generalization remains a major challenge in EEG-
based Auditory Attention Decoding (AAD), limiting the devel-
opment of neuro-steered hearing devices. This challenge arises
from large inter-subject variability in EEG signals and the dif-
ficulty of learning discriminative, subject-invariant representa-
tions. We propose DisenEEG-Net, an end-to-end feature dis-
entanglement framework based on information theory and ad-
versarial learning. It employs a parallel Transformer encoder
to extract latent EEG representations, which are then decom-
posed into orthogonal task- and subject-specific subspaces. A
sufficient information bottleneck retains essential subject infor-
mation while suppressing task leakage, and adversarial train-
ing enforces subject invariance in task features. A decoder pre-
serves feature fidelity during disentanglement. Results show
that our proposed DisenEEG-Net achieves state-of-the-art per-
formance under cross-subject settings on the KUL, DTU, and
A VED datasets.

1. Introduction
Humans can focus on a single sound source amidst compet-
ing noise, a phenomenon known as thecocktail party effect
[1]. This ability is often impaired in individuals with hearing
loss [2], and over 500 million people are projected to expe-
rience disabling hearing loss by 2050 [3]. Traditional hear-
ing aids amplify all sounds indiscriminately, often enhancing
background noise alongside target speech [4]. Neuro-steered
hearing aids, which selectively amplify a listener’s attended
sound in real time, offer a promising solution [5]. This has
motivated extensive research in Auditory Attention Decoding
(AAD), which aims to identify the attended auditory stream
from neural recordings such as EEG [6, 7].
Despite recent advances, AAD still faces two major chal-
lenges that limit cross-subject performance and generaliza-
tion [8]. The first challenge is extracting discriminative features
from auditory-evoked EEG signals. Early approaches designed
specialized architectures to capture spatial and temporal pat-
terns [9]. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were widely
used to learn localized spatial features [10], while recurrent
models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks
captured temporal dependencies [11, 12]. More recently, Graph

Neural Networks (GNNs) have been introduced to enhance spa-
tial modeling by incorporating the neurophysiological topology
of EEG electrodes [6, 13]. However, these architectures are lim-
ited by inherent inductive biases and weak long-range depen-
dency modeling. To overcome these limitations, Transformer-
based attention mechanisms have been widely adopted, en-
abling adaptive feature fusion and significantly improving de-
coding accuracy [14, 15, 16].
The second challenge is inter-subject variability. EEG sig-
nals are highly idiosyncratic due to anatomical and cognitive
differences across individuals [17]. As a result, models trained
on one group of subjects often degrade in performance when
applied to unseen individuals, a problem commonly referred to
as domain shift. To address this, domain adaptation (DA) and
domain generalization (DG) techniques have been explored to
build robust cross-subject models [18, 19]. In particular, disen-
tangled representation learning has emerged as a promising DG
strategy, decoupling latent features into interpretable domain-
invariant (task-relevant) and domain-specific (subject-related)
components [20].
Building on these insights, we introduceDisenEEG-Net,
an end-to-end domain generalization framework grounded in in-
formation theory [19] and adversarial learning [21]. The model
comprises a parallel Transformer encoder that captures latent
spatiotemporal EEG features, followed by a feature disentan-
glement module trained with four complementary objectives:
(i) an orthogonality constraint to enforce non-overlapping task
and subject subspaces; (ii) a sufficient information bottleneck
to confine subject identity to its dedicated subspace while chan-
neling attention-relevant cues into the task space; (iii) an ad-
versarial loss with a gradient reversal layer to suppress subject
leakage into task features; and (iv) a reconstruction loss to pre-
serve feature fidelity throughout decomposition. To the best of
our knowledge, this is the first work to jointly learn discrimi-
native spatiotemporal, subject-invariant features through prin-
cipled disentanglement for EEG-based AAD. Extensive evalu-
ation on three public benchmarks demonstrates state-of-the-art
cross-subject performance.

3. Experiment
We evaluated the effectiveness of DisenEEG-Net on three pub-
licly available EEG datasets: KUL (Dutch) [23], DTU (Danish)
[24], and A VED (Chinese) [25]. The KUL dataset contains 64-
channel EEG recordings from 16 subjects, where participants
attended to one of two competing speech streams presented
from azimuth angles±90 ◦ in a dichotic listening paradigm.
The DTU dataset comprises 64-channel EEG from 18 normal-
hearing subjects, recorded in an echo-free chamber, with speech
stimuli delivered at azimuth angles±60 ◦ under background
noise conditions. The A VED dataset includes 32-channel EEG
recordings from 10 normal-hearing subjects, exposed to both
audio-only and audio-visual conditions. For all three datasets,
EEG signals were segmented into short windows of 1 or 2 sec-
onds for analysis.
To assess cross-subject generalization, we adopted a Leave-
One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation scheme. In each
fold, the EEG data of one subject was held out for testing, while
data from the remainingN−1subjects were used for train-
ing. Reported performance corresponds to the mean accuracy
across all folds. Since the adversarial loss is designed to re-
move subject-specific biases, each training subject is treated as
a separate domain, indexed sequentially from0toN−2. All
models were trained with a batch size of 64 for a maximum
of 100 epochs. Optimization was performed using AdamW
with an initial learning rate of1×10 −4 and a weight decay
of1×10 −3. A ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler dynamically
adjusted the learning rate based on validation performance. Re-
garding the model architecture, we set the number of EEG chan-
nels toC= 64, the number of temporal-spatial patches to
P= 16, and the embedding dimension toD= 128. The
two parallel spatio-temporal Transformer encoders each con-
sist of 2 layers with 8 attention heads. The entire model was
implemented in PyTorch, ensuring reproducibility and flexi-
bility for further experimentation. Code will be available at:
https://github.com/hello1233-maker/DisenEEG-Net.
4. Results
4.1. Comparison with state-of-the-art
We compared DisenEEG-Net against several state-of-the-art
EEG-based auditory attention decoding models, including SSF-
CNN [9], MBSSFCC [11], DGSD [13], DBPNet [14], Listennet
[16], and DARNet [15]. As shown in Table 1, DisenEEG-Net
consistently outperforms all baselines across the KUL, DTU,
and A VED datasets under challenging cross-subject conditions.
On the KUL dataset, our model achieves 75.9% accuracy with
1-second EEG windows, exceeding DARNet by over 5%. Sim-
ilar improvements are observed on DTU and A VED. Further-
more, our experiments reveal that longer temporal windows
(e.g., 2-second segments) lead to improved accuracy, empha-
sizing the importance of extended EEG context for robust and
reliable attention decoding.

5. Conclusion
This study introduces DisenEEG-Net, a novel EEG-based au-
ditory attention detection framework that explicitly disentan-
gles task-relevant and subject-specific features. The proposed
approach achieves robust cross-subject generalization, consis-
tently outperforming state-of-the-art methods across multiple
EEG datasets. Visualization of the learned feature spaces con-
firms that task-specific features form distinct attention-related
clusters while subject-specific features capture individual vari-
ability, providing interpretable evidence of effective disentan-
glement. These results demonstrate that combining disentan-
gled representations with spatio-temporal encoding not only im-
proves performance but also enhances interpretability, paving
the way for more generalizable and practical neuro-steered
hearing applications.
