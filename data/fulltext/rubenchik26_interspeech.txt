Latent Flow Matching Based Speech Separation Using Speaker Diarization

Abstract
This paper presents a novel generative approach to single-
channel speech separation that integrates flow matching with
end-to-end speaker diarization. Discriminative Deep Neural
Networks (DNN)-based separation methods have demonstrated
excellent performance in recent years. More recently, genera-
tive models have emerged as strong competitors for producing
perceptually high-quality speech.
We propose a framework that combines the representational
strength of speaker diarization models with the high-fidelity
generative capabilities of latent space Flow Matching. The
method achieves high-quality separation while requiring train-
ing only of the latent generative U-Net component. In addi-
tion, we introduce a novel Adversarial Speaker Guidance (ASG)
mechanism to mitigate speaker confusion. By conditioning on
mixture-derived speaker attractors rather than traditional enroll-
ment signals, the proposed approach improves intelligibility and
reduces the occurrence of speaker confusion.
Index Terms: Speech-Separation, Generative Modeling, Flow
Matching, Latent-Space
1. Introduction
Speaker separation aims to decompose a given mixture into
separate output channels, each corresponding to an individual
speaker. Numerous successful multi-microphone approaches
have been developed, including methods based on Independent
Component Analysis (ICA) and beamforming [1]. With the ad-
vent of deep learning, single-microphone solutions have also
become viable, significantly broadening the applicability of
speaker separation techniques.
Since the seminal work of [2], numerous separation meth-
ods [3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8] have demonstrated substantial performance
improvements. Several approaches [5, 9, 10] further leverage
speaker identity information available during training to en-
hance inference performance and improve speaker discrimina-
tion.
Speaker diarization [11] addresses the task of estimating
“who speaks when” by assigning speaker identities to their cor-
responding activity segments. Traditionally, diarization systems
follow a modular pipeline that typically comprises Speech Ac-
tivity Detection (SAD), feature extraction, and clustering. The
resulting diarization estimates are widely used to support down-
stream tasks such as Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and
speaker separation [12, 13].
Two key limitations of traditional diarization systems,
namely, handling overlapped speech and directly minimizing
diarization error, were addressed by End-to-End Neural Di-
arization (EEND) [14, 15, 16]. In [14], the problem was for-
mulated as a multi-label classification task, which allows as-
signing multiple speakers to the same time segment and en-
ables direct optimization of the diarization error. Subsequent
works [15, 16] extended this framework by adopting a flexible
Encoder-Decoder-Based Attractor Calculation (EDA) mecha-
nism to handle an unknown number of speakers. The model
first extracts per-frame audio embeddings, which are fed into
the encoder–decoder structure to generate speaker attractors.
The similarity (e.g., inner product) between each speaker at-
tractor and the corresponding audio embedding is then used to
estimate the probability of speaker activity at each frame.
The use of pre-trained speaker representations has also been
explored using diarization outputs [17] to guide the estimation
of separation masks. More recently, [18] proposed a multi-
channel EEND-based approach for speech separation. The
method employs a diarization model to identify segments in
which only a single speaker is active and extracts audio em-
beddings from these regions to serve as speaker-conditioning
signals for the separation network. This strategy, however, re-
lies strongly on accurate diarization, since segmentation errors
directly contaminate the shared embeddings used to represent
the speakers.
In recent years, generative modeling has gained increasing
attention in audio and audio-visual processing. Traditionally,
deep-learning-based speech processing has been dominated by
discriminative models that regress directly to a clean signal or a
time-frequency mask. Such approaches minimize the expected
distance between the estimate and the ground truth, which can
result in over-smoothing and perceptual artifacts due to statisti-
cal averaging under ambiguous conditions.
In contrast, generative methods shift the paradigm from
pointwise regression to distribution matching. By learning the
prior distribution of natural speech, these models generate high-
fidelity samples conditioned on degraded inputs, encouraging
outputs that lie on the clean-speech manifold.
A recent development in iterative generative modeling is
the direct estimation of the vector field between the source and
target distributions, as proposed in Flow Matching and Recti-
fied Flows [19, 20]. By explicitly learning this transport field,
these frameworks enable simpler, more direct training and in-
ference procedures, while significantly reducing the number of
steps required to obtain high-quality samples.
In audio generation, [21] introduced a latent-space text-to-
audio model. Building on this line of work, language-queried
audio source separation was proposed in [22], leveraging a pre-
trained Variational Autoencoder (V AE) [23] and training only a
vocoder and a flow model conditioned on embeddings derived
from textual descriptions of the target signal. Generative ap-
proaches have also been applied to speech enhancement tasks,
including speaker separation [24, 25], denoising [26, 27], and
extraction [28, 29]. In particular, [28] applied flow matching in

A common challenge in audio extraction and separation
models is the failure to correctly estimate the desired source due
to weak conditioning or the presence of dominant interferers,
a phenomenon often referred to as target or speaker confusion
[30]. In generative frameworks, this manifests as a tendency
to extract the same speaker from the mixture regardless of the
conditioning signal, indicating insufficient control over condi-
tioning. To mitigate this issue, SoloSpeech [29] introduced a
dedicated conditional transformer that delivers speaker-specific
features through a cross-attention mechanism, strengthening the
coupling between the conditioning signal and the generative
process. In video-to-audio generation, [31] has applied nega-
tive audio guidance to discourage sound duplication between
steps. They use a representation of the audio in past steps to
negatively condition the flow model in the present step, avoid-
ing the re-generation of the audio.
In this paper, we propose a novel latent-space generative
speech separation framework that leverages EEND-EDA attrac-
tors and speaker-activity probabilities to directly condition the
model during both overlapping and non-overlapping segments.
This conditioning mechanism provides explicit speaker guid-
ance derived from the diarization process. To further miti-
gate speaker confusion, we introduce a novel ASG strategy that
significantly enhances separation performance and strengthens
conditioning robustness. We train a Flow Matching model in the
latent space (building upon [22]) conditioned on the speaker-
related representations derived from the EEND model [32]. Im-
portantly, only the latent-space Flow Matching U-Net is trained,
while all other components (adapted from [21, 22, 32]) remain
fixed and are used as pretrained modules.

2.3. End-To-End Neural Diarization with Encoder-
Decoder-based Attractors
EEND [15] was proposed to address the diarization problem in
an end-to-end manner. It provides a framework in which the di-
arization objective, Diarization Error Rate (DER), can be opti-
mized directly while naturally handling the overlapping speech
among multiple speakers. The method employs a context aggre-
gator over audio input features to produce a frame-level repre-
sentation. Each frame representation is passed through a linear
layer followed by a sigmoid activation to estimate speaker ac-
tivity scores. The model is trained using Permutation Invariant
Training (PIT) [2, 36].
EEND-EDA extends this framework to support an unknown
number of speakers by introducing two complementary rep-
resentations: frame-level embeddings and speaker attractors.
The embeddings serve as frame-level speech representations,
whereas each attractor corresponds to a specific speaker. The at-
tractors may be time-invariant, denoted byas, or time-varying,
denoted byas,m, wheres∈{ 0, 1,...,S } indexes the speaker
andm∈{ 0, 1,...,M − 1} denotes the frame index. Here, S
typically represents the maximum number of speakers consid-
ered, often augmented with one or two additional control chan-
nels.

3. Method
Our approach combines EEND-based speaker activity estimates
and attractors with latent-space Flow Matching to achieve high-
perceptual-quality speaker separation. We use the speaker at-
tractors of the EEND model to condition a latent flow matching-
based separator. The V AE encoder generates a latent represen-
tation of the input mixture, which is concatenated to Gaussian
noise of the same dimensions. The latent U-Net Flow Matching
model estimates the direction of the vector field from the latent
representation. During inference, after iterative generations, the
V AE decoder synthesizes the latent representation into a mel-
spectrogram, which is then synthesized into a waveform.
3.1. End-to-End Neural Diarization (EEND)
We apply the diarization model [32] to the input mixture’s mel-
spectrogram. Speaker attractors are selected based on their esti-
mated per-frame activity and are averaged-pooled across the ut-
terance to obtain a compact, low-dimensional representation for
each speaker. These speaker-specific representations are then
used to condition the Flow Matching U-Net. The generative
model is provided with the concatenation of the two speaker
representations, yielding a single one-dimensional condition-
ing vector. The EEND-derived conditioning vector is the only
speaker condition used for the generative speaker separation,
using no prior enrollment.
3.2. Latent Speaker Flow Matching Separator
For the generative architecture, we adopt the framework of
[22], employing a pretrained vocoder [37], a mel-spectrogram
V AE, both of which remain frozen during training, and a flow-
matching model. The concatenated speaker representation is
injected into the Flow Matching U-Net via Feature-wise Linear
Modulation (FiLM) conditioning [38].
The initial latent representation of the separated signal is
sampled from Gaussian noise, while the target corresponds to
the clean speech latent associated with the selected speaker rep-
resentation. The intermediate state xt is constructed according
to (2).
The input to the Flow Matching model consists of the con-
catenation of the original mixture latent representation and the
intermediate noisy speech latent, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
For each speaker in the mixture, the separation is performed
by exchanging the speaker order in the conditioning vector,
where the first part of the vector (with the same length as the
attractor) represents the desired speaker and the rest of the vec-
tor represents the interfering speaker.
During inference, inputs longer than the model’s input
dimension are processed using chunking with an overlap of
50%. The overlapping segments are combined using cross-
faded overlap-and-add in the mel-spectrogram domain before
being passed to the vocoder.
3.3. Permutation Aware Training
Blind Source Separation (BSS), unlike speaker extraction, must
address the permutation ambiguity between the estimated out-
puts and the reference sources. The representations produced by
EEND are not designed for general speaker verification tasks.
In particular, attractors corresponding to a given speaker are not
guaranteed to be closer to attractors of the same speaker in other
recordings. Similarly, the attractor associated with a speaker in
a mixture is not necessarily closer to the attractor of the corre-
sponding clean source than to that of an interfering speaker.
To address this issue, we propose employing PIT during
training of the Flow Matching model. At each training step,
the intermediate representation is evaluated under all possible
permutations of the target speakers. The permutation that yields
the lowest combined Flow Matching loss is selected, and the
gradients are backpropagated accordingly.
3.4. Adversarial Speaker Guidance (ASG)

ASG to further reduce speaker confusion. Similarly to CFG,

ogous role. Specifically, we exploit the model’s ability to esti-
mate the vector field associated with the interfering speaker and
use it to steer the generative process away from the undesired
source, thereby increasing consistency with the target speaker

4. Experimental Study
This section presents the experimental setup, dataset, results,
and comparison to competing methods.
4.1. Data and Setup
We have trained our model using the LibriSpeech [40] cor-
pus, generating each input mixture by dynamically mixing [9]
source speech with an SIR range of [-5,5] dB. We have evalu-
ated our model on the LibriMix 16kHz min configuration test
set [41]. Since the EEND model operates at 8 kHz, we down-
sample the input mixture to ensure a suitable input. All architec-
ture parameters, except those of the Flow Matching U-Net, are
frozen during training for both the proposed speech separation
model and the ECAPA-based speaker extraction variant. In both
cases, the model is trained using input mixtures of length 10.24
seconds. Model inference, in cases where guidance was used,
employed a weight of 0.5 for null guidance (standard CFG) and
1 for ASG, corresponding to (7). Values were empirically se-
lected. The full hyperparameter search is omitted due to space
constraints.
4.2. Competing Methods
The SoloSpeech results were obtained using the publicly avail-
able checkpoint trained on noisy mixtures. In addition, we
present the results of the DiffSep+SV and DDTSE extraction
models, as reported in [42].
4.3. Evaluation Measures
Our main metrics for evaluating separation quality are non-
intrusive speech quality measures and Word Error Rate (WER).
For perceptual quality assessment, we employ the DNSMOS
and OVRL metrics [43]. For intelligibility evaluation, we per-
form ASR using Whisper-small [44] and compute the resulting
WER. We use a ResNet-34 speaker verification model [45] to
compute the Speaker Similarity Metric (SIM).
In addition, we derive an auxiliary metric based on the ASR
outputs, termed Transcription Similarity (TSIM). This metric
identifies cases in which the WER between the transcriptions of
two different separated signals is below30%, an empirically se-
lected threshold. Such high transcriptional similarity suggests
that both estimates largely share the same linguistic content, in-
dicating a likely separation failure in which at least one source
was not properly isolated.

A common issue with generative separation and extraction
models is their tendency to ignore the conditioning informa-
tion and instead focus on a dominant signal in the mixture.
This phenomenon is revealed by the Transcription Similarity
(TSIM) indicator for both a recent generative extraction method
and our own architecture variant using an ECAPA-based ex-
tractor (ECAPAv). Despite not relying on an enrollment sig-
nal, the proposed EEND- based model reduces the speaker
confusion rate, measured by TSIM, by approximately two or-
ders of magnitude compared with the ECAPA-based variant
and by an order of magnitude compared with the competing
method. One possible explanation for the superior performance
of EEND attractors is that they are trained specifically to dis-
tinguish among speakers in a mixture, whereas speaker embed-
dings such as ECAPA are optimized primarily for speaker ver-
ification and therefore provide a more general representation.
We further note that performance depends on attractor discrim-
inability rather than diarization accuracy; robustness analysis
is left to future work. Finally, we observe that incorporating
ASG significantly reduces speaker confusion in both methods
and yields a slight improvement in the speech quality score.
5. Conclusions
We propose a novel flow-matching-based speech separation
model that leverages End-to-End Neural Diarization (EEND)
attractors. Our approach enables the use of large and difficult-
to-train components—such as V AEs, vocoders, and diarization
models—while requiring only training of a relatively simple
latent-space flow-matching model. Despite operating in a com-
pact latent representation space, the proposed pipeline achieves
speech quality comparable to that of larger competing methods
and outperforms them in intelligibility, as measured by WER.
Using the proposed TSIM metric, we show that mixture-
derived speaker attractors significantly outperform enrollment-
based representations for speaker conditioning, reducing
speaker confusion by approximately two orders of magnitude
relative to ECAPA-based enrollment and by one order of mag-
nitude relative to a generative extraction model.
In addition, we demonstrate that the proposed Adversarial
Speaker Guidance (ASG) training-free mechanism yields no-
table improvements for both enrollment-based methods and the
proposed latent-space separation model.
