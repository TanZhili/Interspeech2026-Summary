DsNA(Digital sigNature for Audios): A Unique Method to Fingerprint Audio
Files Generated by Text to Speech
Vishal Gourav, Phanindra Mankale
Oracle Corporation, India
vishal.gourav@oracle.com*, phanindra.mankale@oracle.com
Abstract
The rapid advancement of text-to-speech (TTS) systems has
enabled the large-scale generation of highly natural synthetic
speech, raising critical concerns around audio provenance, au-
thenticity, and misuse. In this work, we present DsNA (Digi-
tal sigNature for Audios), a lightweight post-generation finger-
printing framework designed to embed verifiable signatures di-
rectly into TTS-generated audio. Unlike traditional audio fin-
gerprinting methods that rely on external matching or feature
extraction, DsNA introduces a structured embedding mecha-
nism in which a cryptographic signature derived from the audio
and metadata is partitioned into shards and interleaved with seg-
mented audio chunks. Preliminary evaluation demonstrates that
the proposed method maintains audio fidelity, with no observ-
able degradation in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) between origi-
nal and fingerprinted audio. This paper discusses this idea, that
enables intrinsic identification of generated speech while pre-
serving signal quality.
Index Terms: Digital Signature, Deepfake, Text To Speech,
Authentication
1. Introduction
In general a TTS system consists of 5 stages as shown in Figure
1. These are as follows:
Figure 1: Flow diagram of a TTS System
• Input Text: The input from the user in string format.
• Text Preprocessing : The input text is passed through a
Text Normalizer to convert inputs like currency symbols,
acronyms, etc. into their spoken forms. This spoken form
is then passed through the G2P which converts the text into
phonemes and then the phonemes to their mapped integers
which is the form in which the model can understand.
• Acoustic Model : The Acoustic Model takes the phoneme
integer numpy array and produces Mel Spectograms which is
a tensor containing elements of float datatype.
• Neural V ocoder: The vocoder accepts the output of the
acoustic model and produces the waveform tensor contain-
ing float type elements.
• Speech Waveform: The output from the vocoder is con-
verted to a numpy array which is then converted to .wav file.
Recent advances in neural text-to-speech (TTS) systems
such as WaveNet and Tacotron-based architectures have sig-
nificantly improved the realism of synthetic speech [1, 2], en-
abling widespread adoption across virtual assistants, accessibil-
ity tools, and content generation platforms. However, the grow-
ing realism of generated speech has introduced challenges re-
lated to audio provenance, authenticity, and misuse.
Audio fingerprinting techniques have traditionally been
used for multimedia identification and copyright protection [3,
4]. However, existing methods primarily focus on content iden-
tification rather than embedding verifiable provenance informa-
tion into generated speech.
In this work, we introduce DsNA (Digital sigNature for Au-
dios), a lightweight framework for embedding structured digi-
tal signatures into TTS-generated audio. The proposed method
partitions generated audio into chunks and interleaves them
with shards of a cryptographic fingerprint derived from the au-
dio and associated metadata. This creates a self-contained and
verifiable audio artifact without modifying the underlying TTS
architecture.
Preliminary evaluation demonstrates that the proposed fin-
gerprinting mechanism preserves signal fidelity without observ-
able degradation in audio quality. We further present a show-
and-tell demonstration of the complete DsNA pipeline, includ-
ing fingerprint generation, injection, and verification on TTS
outputs.
2. Experiment
The experimental workflow follows the system shown in Fig.2:
a user prompt is first processed by the TTS service to generate
an audio waveform, which is then paired with associated meta-
data. This audio stream is passed through the proposed finger-
printing pipeline, where it is divided into two equal chunks by
the audio chunker. In parallel, the fingerprint generator pro-
duces a compact digital signature from the audio and metadata.
The stage uses cryptographic hashing and digital signature prin-
ciples inspired by RSA-based authentication mechanisms [5]
and the shard generator splits this signature into three shards.
These shards are then interleaved with the audio chunks to form
the final fingerprinted output.
To assess the impact of fingerprint insertion on audio fi-
delity, we compare the original TTS output with the finger-
printed audio using signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). In the current
prototype, the measured SNR remains unchanged after finger-
printing, with both the original and fingerprinted audio report-
ing an SNR of 45.2. This indicates that the proposed embed-
ding strategy does not introduce measurable degradation under
the tested conditions.

3. Evaluation
The results presented in the previous section are analyzed to
assess the effectiveness of the proposed DsNA framework in
preserving audio quality while enabling reliable fingerprint em-
bedding. The signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the fingerprinted
audio remains identical to that of the original TTS output (45.2
dB), indicating that the fingerprint injection process does not
introduce measurable distortion under the evaluated conditions.
In addition to signal fidelity, perceptual and linguistic quality
were evaluated using Mean Opinion Score (MOS), Character
Error Rate (CER), and Word Error Rate (WER). The minimal
variation between the original and the fingerprinted audio sug-
gests that the proposed interleaving-based embedding mecha-
nism preserves both perceptual quality and transcription con-
sistency while maintaining the structural recoverability of the
embedded fingerprint. The evaluation was carried out on 250
samples generated from the TTS model that were then passed
through the pipeline to generate ”signed” audios. Then both sets
of audios were passed through the evaluation pipeline to get the
metrics as shown in Table 1.

While the current study serves as an initial proof of con-

as strengthening the security guarantees of the fingerprinting
mechanism.
Overall, DsNA represents a promising step toward enabling
traceable and verifiable synthetic speech, addressing emerging
challenges in audio authenticity and provenance in the era of
highly realistic TTS systems.
