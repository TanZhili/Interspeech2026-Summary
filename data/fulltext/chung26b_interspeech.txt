Robust Audio-Visual Emotion Recognition via Conditional Transformer
U-Nets with Frequency-Injected Visual Stream
Hanwook Chung ID 1
1 Faurecia IRYStec Inc., Montreal, QC, Canada

Abstract
We present a convolutional transformer (CTr)–based dual U-
Net for robust audio-visual emotion recognition (A VER) in
noisy and reverberation environments. Each CTr U-Net en-
coder learns compact, modality-specific bottleneck representa-
tions that capture contextual and temporal–spatial cues from au-
dio and visual inputs, while a fusion-based emotion decoder
predicts emotional states from the joint bottleneck features.
Emotion-conditioned auxiliary decoders reconstruct intermedi-
ate features to enhance supervision and consistency. The au-
dio stream incorporates an inverse filtering-based front-end,
while the visual stream employs frequency-injected spatial-
spectral modeling to better capture facial dynamics. Exper-
iments demonstrate improved robustness and accuracy com-
pared to existing unimodal and multimodal benchmarks.
Index Terms: emotion recognition, multimodal learning, trans-
former, classification
1. Introduction
Emotion recognition (ER) aims to infer human emotional states
from observable cues such as speech and facial expressions
[1]. As an important component of human–machine inter-
action, ER enables systems to respond more naturally and
adaptively, with applications in healthcare, customer analyt-
ics, and safety-critical environments. Most ER systems rely
on speech and/or visual signals, where feature extraction learns
modality-specific representations followed by a classifier pre-
dicting emotional states. With advances in deep learning, par-
ticularly transformer-based architectures [2], ER performance
has improved significantly by modeling complex contextual and
spatio-temporal dependencies in speech emotion recognition
(SER) [3, 4], and visual emotion recognition (VER) [5, 6].
Recently, multimodal audio-visual emotion recognition
(A VER) has gained increasing attention, as integrating speech
and facial expressions provides complementary affective cues.
Various fusion strategies and transformer-based frameworks
have been explored to enhance cross-modal representation
learning, e.g., [7, 8, 9]. Beyond audio-visual settings, multi-
modal extensions incorporating text or body cues have also been
studied [10, 11]. In this work, we focus exclusively on A VER
and investigate robustness under adverse acoustic conditions.
In real-world environments, acoustic degradations such as
background noise and reverberation remain major challenges
for SER, often degrading multimodal performance. While data
augmentation and speech enhancement (SE) techniques, e.g.,
[12, 13, 14, 15], can mitigate these effects, their effectiveness
is limited under severe distortions. Moreover, relatively few
works explicitly address acoustic robustness in A VER, where
degraded audio must be integrated with visual information. For
example, [16] showed that visual cues can alleviate acoustic
noise in hybrid fusion networks, although robustness was not
explicitly modeled. Similarly, [17] combined early and late fu-
sion with cross-modal attention to improve performance under
noisy conditions, but mainly relied on increased data variability
rather than explicitly addressing adverse acoustic environments.
In this paper, we propose a convolutional transformer
(CTr)-based dual U-Net architecture for robust A VER. The
framework comprises an audio stream module (ASM) and
visual stream module (VSM), both adopting CTr-based en-
coder–decoder structures to learn compact modality-specific
bottleneck representations. The ASM incorporates a CTr-based
audio front-end (FE) that directly enhances degraded log-Mel
filterbank (LMFB) features via inverse filtering-based recon-
struction, improving robustness under highly reverberant con-
ditions. For the VSM, we introduce a frequency-injected CTr
that jointly models spatial and spectral facial cues to better cap-
ture subtle expression dynamics. To encourage consistent fea-
ture learning, auxiliary U-Net feature decoders for both ASM
and VSM reconstruct intermediate features conditioned on pre-
dicted emotion embeddings. This enables the network to cap-
ture richer feature characteristics during training, as the re-
constructed outputs allow the use of regularized reconstruc-
tion losses, further strengthening both unimodal representation
learning and multimodal fusion. Experiments show that the pro-
posed model improves robustness and accuracy compared to ex-
isting unimodal and multimodal benchmarks.
2. Related Works
Auxiliary Feature Decoder: In SER, predicted emotion em-
beddings have been incorporated into CNN-based U-Net auxil-
iary decoders [3] and later extended to CTr-based U-Net frame-
works with advanced conditioning schemes [18]. In A VER,
auxiliary reconstruction has been used in ASM and VSM to sup-
port multimodal learning [8, 19]. In our framework, both ASM
and VSM decoders are conditioned on predicted emotion em-
beddings, encouraging emotion-consistent representations that
strengthen unimodal learning and multimodal fusion.
Front-end SE: SE front-ends have been widely adopted for
speech recognition. For example, [13] used a progressive mask
estimation on log-power spectra, which incurs high computa-
tional cost due to feature dimensionality. To reduce complexity,
[12] directly enhanced lower-dimensional Mel-frequency cep-
stral coefficients. Following this strategy, we operate in the log-
Mel domain and incorporate an inverse filtering–based recon-
struction method [20] to better handle severe reverberation.

ing has been studied in image restoration, where spectral cues
complement spatial features for tasks such as deblurring, de-
noising [21, 22, 23]. In VER, however, frequency-domain cues

3. Proposed Architecture
Block diagrams of the proposed architecture are illustrated in
Fig. 1. The following subsections describe each ASM and VSM
and fused emotion decoder in detail.
3.1. Audio Stream Module (ASM)
Audio feature extraction: In general, background noise is
modeled as an additive process, whereas reverberation is char-
acterized by convolution with room impulse response (RIR).
Accordingly, the time-domain noisy reverberant speech signal
can be formulated as y = h∗ s + n, where y,h,s and n
denote the noisy reverberant speech, RIR, clean speech and
noise, and ∗ denotes convolution. For a given y, LMFB co-
efficients are extracted as input to the ASM U-Net, denoted as
Y = [Ytf ]∈ RTa×Fa whereTa is the number of time frames
and Fa is the feature dimensions. LMFB provides a percep-
tually motivated spectral-domain representation approximating
human auditory frequency resolution, yielding a compact and
robust encoding of speech energy distribution.
Audio FE: For given Y, the FE directly estimates the clean
speech LMFB coefficients S = [Stf ]∈ RT ×F . The FE mod-
ule comprises multiple CTr blocks followed by a reconstruction
stage, and we consider a frame-wise processing (Kt = 1).
In conventional SE, frequency-domain masking is widely
used for clean speech reconstruction, e.g., [13, 27]. However,
this assumes that the RIR is shorter than the time-frequency
analysis window, which often fails under strong reverberation.
The convolutive transfer function (CTF) model [28] addresses
this by representing the reverberant signal as a temporal con-
volution between clean spectral coefficients and spectral fil-
ters. Based on this principle, [20] proposed an inverse filter-
ing CTF model for dereverberation. We extend this frame-
work to jointly handle reverberation and noise directly in the
LMFB domain. The enhanced LMFB feature ˆStf is computed

The audio and visual bottleneck features, Oa and Ov, are fed
into the fused emotion decoder (Fig. 1-(e)) to obtain the emo-
tional states, OE∈ RCe×Te. The decoder first performs tem-
poral alignment by compressing Oa to match T e
v , followed
by convolutional projection for dimensional alignment. The

specific prompts RCE ×CP ×1×Ff (CP = 256 ), which interact
with fused features via element-wise operations and concatena-
tion to guide attention toward emotion-relevant representations.

4.2. Methodology
The maximum clip duration is set to 4s for CREMA-D and
RA VDESS, and 8s for IEMOCAP, will all audio resampled
to 16 kHz. For the ASM, a pre-emphasis filter is first ap-
plied to the waveform, followed by short-time Fourier trans-
form (STFT) using the 512-sample Hamming window with
50% overlap and a 512-point FFT. We extract 64-D LMFB
features with frame-wise zero-mean normalization. For the
VSM, facial regions are detected using MTCNN [39] and
resized to (H,W,C ) = (224 , 224, 3), normalized using
ImageNet mean [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] and standard deviation
[0.229, 0.224, 0.225]. Video frames are downsampled by a fac-
tor of 4 to reduce temporal redundancy, and random horizontal
flipping is applied to 25% of training samples. Additive noise is
introduced at SNR levels from -10 to 20 dB for both training and
testing. Models are trained using the Adam optimizer for 200
epochs with a batch size of 20 and initial learning rate of 10−4,
decayed by 15% every 20 epochs. Evaluation is conducted us-
ing unweighted and weighted average recalls (UAR and W AR),
and confusion matrices under 5-fold cross validation.

5. Conclusion
We presented a CTr-based dual U-Net framework for robust
A VER. The architecture combines modality-specific encoders
with emotion-conditioned auxiliary decoders to improve feature
consistency and multimodal fusion. An inverse filtering–based
FE enhances robustness to noise and reverberation, while a
frequency-injected CTr enhances visual modeling of subtle spa-
tial–spectral facial cues. Experiments show consistent improve-
ments over existing unimodal and multimodal benchmarks. Fu-
ture work will explore incorporating textual cues for richer mul-
timodal understanding and self-supervised training strategies.
We also plan to address visual conditions (e.g., blur and noise)
for improved real-world robustness.
