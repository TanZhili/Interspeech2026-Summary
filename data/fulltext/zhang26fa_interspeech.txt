Abstract
For open-vocabulary keyword spotting, phoneme-level align-
ment has been introduced to improve the performance on acous-
tically confusable words. However, most existing studies utilize
non-streaming methods, which are unsuitable for streaming sce-
narios. Recent methods achieve streaming phoneme alignment
via connectionist temporal classification (CTC) algorithms, but
they are limited to text-only enrollment. To address these issues,
we propose a novel streaming multimodal open-vocabulary key-
word spotting method based on phoneme-level alignment. This
method achieves fine-grained modeling with training-inference
consistency through the W-CTC forced alignment algorithm
and multimodal phoneme-level contrastive learning. Addition-
ally, we propose a data augmentation method based on CTC
beam-search to dynamically mine hard negative samples. Ex-
periments on the LibriPhrase dataset demonstrate that the pro-
posed method achieves the best results.
Index Terms: open-vocabulary keyword spotting, contrastive
learning, W-CTC, multi-modal
1. Introduction
Open-vocabulary keyword spotting [1, 2, 3] enhances human-
computer interaction by detecting user-defined keywords with-
out retraining. Compared to audio enrollment-based approaches
[4, 5, 6], text enrollment-based methods [1, 2, 3] offer greater
stability and convenience by mapping audio and text into a uni-
fied latent space. However, discriminating acoustically similar
confusing words remains a critical challenge.
Recent studies indicate that keyword spotting methods em-
ploying phoneme-level alignment [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14]
achieve superior results on confusing words compared to meth-
ods learning solely at the utterance level. This improvement is
attributed to the retention of more fine-grained discriminative
speech information. Techniques including self-attention mech-
anisms [1, 7], cross-attention mechanisms [9], DSP algorithms
[10, 11], and the Montreal forced aligner [12] have been applied
to obtain phoneme-level alignments. However, these alignment
methods depend on full-sequence information and generally ex-
hibit high time complexity, making them difficult to apply in
streaming scenarios. Several recent approaches [13, 14] intro-
duce streaming phoneme alignment strategies based on CTC.
These strategies support frame-by-frame computation with low
time complexity and are suitable for streaming execution. Nev-
ertheless, these methods are limited to text-only enrollment and
cannot further enhance performance through modality fusion.
Building upon phoneme-level alignment, phoneme-level
contrastive learning is utilized to further close the modality gap

between audio and text. For instance, the InfoNCE loss is ap-
plied to phoneme-level contrastive learning to align phonemes
across modalities and enhance audio consistency [12, 15].
However, when performing contrastive learning between au-
dio and text, the text representation is deterministic, whereas
the audio representation varies due to changing acoustic en-
vironments. The InfoNCE loss fails to fully exploit this in-
herent asymmetry. Furthermore, these methods employ differ-
ent phoneme alignment strategies during contrastive learning
and actual inference. This discrepancy can lead to a training-
inference mismatch and ultimately degrade the final perfor-
mance.
To address these issues, we utilize the W-CTC forced align-
ment [16] to uniformly process the support audio and the query
audio. The alignment results are used to aggregate phoneme-
level acoustic embeddings. We conduct contrastive learning
across both audio pairs and audio-text pairs to narrow the
modality gap. Considering the intrinsic differences between au-
dio and text, we apply an asymmetric proxy loss for the cross-
modal matching. To improve model performance on confusing
words, we additionally propose a hard negative sample mining
method. Moreover, a cross-attention bias module is incorpo-
rated to inject keyword priors into the acoustic encoder without
additional latency. Our main contributions can be summarized
as follows:
• We propose MPA-KWS, a streaming open-vocabulary key-
word spotting framework that supports both text-only and
text-audio enrollment.
• We introduce a CTC beam-search-based data augmentation
method that leverages the acoustic encoder’s recognition bias
to dynamically generate hard negative samples.
• We design a phoneme-level contrastive learning framework
that incorporates asymmetric proxy loss for cross-modal
alignment and symmetric InfoNCE loss for intra-modal
alignment.

2.3. Phoneme-Level Contrastive Learning
As illustrated in Figure 1, we design a phoneme-level con-
trastive learning module to finely align audio and text features
within a shared latent space. Notably, the contrastive loss is
computed exclusively on positive sample pairs to prevent in-
valid forced alignments.
For the audio-text contrastive learning, we consider that
while the acoustic embeddings of a given keyword vary across
different speakers and acoustic environments, its text embed-
ding remains unique and serves as a noise-free invariant refer-
ence. Given this inherent asymmetry, we employ Asymmetric
Proxy (AsyP) loss [20], which utilizes the text representation
as a proxy and applies asymmetric similarity functions condi-
tioned on the modality of the anchor.

2.4. Training
2.4.1. Multi-Task Learning
The verifier receives the aligned embeddings from the con-
trastive learning module and explicitly computes the match-
ing features for audio-audio and audio-text pairs. For any two
given sequencesE 1 andE 2, we construct an interaction fea-
tureF= [E 1,E 2,E 1−E 2,E 1⊙E 2][23], where⊙denotes
element-wise multiplication. These matching features are fed
into two separate bidirectional gated recurrent unit networks.
The final hidden states of these networks are concatenated and
passed through a fully connected classifier to produce the fi-
nal logits, which are subsequently used to calculate the binary
cross-entropy lossL U AT.
During the training phase, we initially pre-train the acous-
tic encoder independently using the CTC loss. The entire model
then undergoes end-to-end training utilizing a multi-task learn-
ing approach. The total loss function is defined as:

whereλ P AT ,λ P AA, andλ U AT act as hyperparameters regu-

To accommodate text-only enrollment scenarios where sup-
port audio is absent, we simulate this condition during training
by masking the support audio with a 50% probability. Under
this condition, the audio-audio contrastive learning is bypassed,
and the output of the gated recurrent unit network responsible
for processing the audio-audio matching features is explicitly
set to a zero tensor.

2.5. Streaming Inference
Prior to inference, support text embeddings are computed and
cached. Similarly, for text-audio enrollment, W-CTC forced
alignment and feature aggregation are pre-computed on the sup-
port audio.
While the forced alignment is computed offline over com-
plete speech segments during training, we adopt a sliding win-
dow decoding strategy during inference to maintain streaming
capabilities, similar to [13]. Specifically, for each input win-
dow of sizeW, the end index of the alignment is determined
by the position yielding the highest forward score for the fi-
nal phoneme of the keyword. A backtracking operation from
this end index produces the alignment matrix for the current
window. The time complexity of this alignment process is
O(W×L p), which is acceptable for streaming inference since
bothWandL p are small.
Once the local alignment results are extracted, the query
audio embeddings are computed by Eq. (2). These aggregated
embeddings, alongside the cached support audio and text em-
beddings, are fed into the verifier to determine the final proba-
bility of the keyword presence.
Since text and support audio embeddings are cached prior
to inference, the complexity is dominated by acoustic feature
extraction, forced alignment, and verifier matching. Frame-by-
frame cross-attention avoids global dependencies, the sliding
window constrains alignment complexity, and the verifier incurs
minimal overhead as inputs are compressed toLp. This synergy
makes real-time processing feasible on resource-constrained de-
vices.

3.2. Implementation Details
The model is implemented using the PyTorch framework. The
Conv1dNet acoustic encoder comprises 18 causal convolutional
layers with 300 channels and employs depthwise separable con-
volutions alongside DoubleSwish activation functions. The text
encoder features a single-layer BiLSTM with a hidden dimen-
sion of 128. The bias module is configured with 4 attention
heads. The acoustic and text embedding dimensionsdare
uniformly set to 256. The verifier employs two single-layer
BiGRU, preceded by a 256-dimensional bottleneck projection
layer. The total number of trainable parameters in the model is
4.03 M.
SpecAugment [24] is applied with frequency and time
masking on the log-Mel filterbanks, while CTC beam search-
based data augmentation is employed on the text inputs. Using
the AdamW optimizer (initial learning rate5×10−4, weight de-
cay 0.05, batch size 500), we set the contrastive learning hyper-
parameters asτ= 0.04,λ= 0.85,α= 20.0, andβ= 10.0,
with loss weightsλ P AT = 0.1,λ P AA = 0.1, andλ U AT = 1.
3.3. Performance of MPA
Table 1 presents the performance of various models on the
LPH andLP E datasets, utilizing the area under the curve
(AUC) and equal error rate (EER) as evaluation metrics. For
text-only enrollment mode, our proposed model outperforms
the streaming baseline [13], which similarly employs W-CTC
forced alignment. On the most challenging LibriPhrase-Hard
dataset, our text-only model achieves an AUC of 96.04% and
an EER of 9.53%, surpassing the majority of existing sys-
tems. For text-audio enrollment mode, the performance further
improves, exceeding the current state-of-the-art PLCL method
[12] and achieving optimal results. Notably, the Whisper-Tiny
encoder employed in [12] possesses a significantly larger pa-
rameter scale compared to our acoustic encoder.

4. Conclusions
In this work, we proposed MPA-KWS, a streaming multi-
modal open-vocabulary keyword spotting framework based on
phoneme-level alignment. By employing W-CTC forced align-
ment, our method unifies the processing of text and audio en-
rollments while maintaining training-inference consistency. We
effectively close the audio-text modality gap through a novel
phoneme-level contrastive learning framework utilizing asym-
metric and symmetric proxy losses. Additionally, a dynamic
CTC beam-search data augmentation strategy is introduced to
tackle the challenge of acoustically confusable words. Exper-
iments on LibriPhrase demonstrated that MPA-KWS achieves
state-of-the-art performance with only 4.0M parameters, and
ablation studies validated the contribution of each proposed
component.
