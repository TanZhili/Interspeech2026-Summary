Prosody-Aware Speech Representations for Emotion Recognition under
Pragmatic Ambiguity

Abstract
Speech emotion recognition (SER) systems often rely on se-
mantic or spectral representations learned by speech encoders,
while prosodic cues conveying emotional intent are not explic-
itly modeled. In real-world conversations, emotional meaning
is conveyed through prosody when linguistic cues alone are
insufficient, especially in pragmatically ambiguous utterances.
We propose a prosody-aware speech representation for emotion
recognition and downstream applications by injecting pitch and
energy into shared hidden representations of a pretrained Whis-
per encoder. We evaluate the approach using a 59-class emo-
tion classification task and a Pragmatic Ambiguity Resolution
(PAR) evaluation task. Experiments show improvements up to
13.93%p in emotion classification and 11.6%p in PAR com-
pared to the same Whisper model without prosody. The pro-
posed model also outperforms text-based LLM inference (GPT-
4o mini) on PAR, highlighting the importance of prosody-aware
representations for emotion systems.
Index Terms: speech emotion recognition, speech sense dis-
ambiguation, automatic speech recognition, subtitle generation
1. Introduction
Speech emotion recognition (SER) has made significant
progress with the adoption of large-scale pretrained speech en-
coders [1, 2, 3]. These models achieve strong performance on
standard benchmarks by learning rich spectral and semantic rep-
resentations [2]. However, such improvements often obscure
a critical limitation: prosodic cues that convey emotional in-
tent are not explicitly preserved in the learned representation
[4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. This limitation becomes particularly criti-
cal in real-world speech understanding scenarios. In applica-
tions such as emotion-aware subtitle generation or spoken di-
alogue systems, emotional meaning is often conveyed through
prosody rather than lexical content alone [10, 11]. Our focus
on Korean is motivated by the development of a Korean down-
stream system and informed by recent linguistic studies show-
ing that intonation in Korean contributes to fine-grained seman-
tic distinctions [12, 13]. For example, a short utterance such
as “Geurae” (meaning “okay”) in Korean can convey agree-
ment, sarcasm, hesitation, or frustration, depending on the in-
tonation and intensity patterns. We refer to this phenomenon as
pragmatic ambiguity, where lexical information alone is insuffi-
cient to determine the intended emotion. Despite its importance,
pragmatic ambiguity is not adequately captured by conventional
SER evaluation protocols [14, 15]. In our findings, metrics such
as Hamming accuracy measure label-wise agreement and tend
to compress differences between models, making it difficult to

assess whether a system truly captures emotion-relevant infor-
mation or merely performs well under conventional evaluation
[1]. Therefore, current evaluations may overestimate the robust-
ness of speech representations in real-world settings. We argue
that this limitation is fundamentally a representation-level prob-
lem. Modern speech encoders, particularly those optimized for
speech-to-text (STT) applications, are designed to abstract away
surface variations to enhance semantic consistency [16, 17, 9].
While this property is beneficial for transcription, it may sup-
press fine-grained prosodic variation that is essential for emo-
tion and pragmatic interpretation. As a result, representations
that perform well on conventional benchmarks may still fail un-
der pragmatically ambiguous conditions.
In this work, we take a diagnostic perspective and investi-
gate when prosody becomes decisive for emotion recognition.
We introduce aPragmatic Ambiguity Resolution(PAR) evalua-
tion setting that explicitly measures a model’s ability to recover
utterance-level affective meaning under prosody-dependent am-
biguity in the absence of context information. Rather than
proposing a novel architecture, our goal is to diagnose whether
commonly used speech representations preserve prosodically
relevant information and to assess the extent to which their
representation-level limitations can be mitigated. To this end,
we analyze a prosody-aware speech representation that injects
pitch and energy into the shared hidden representations of a
pretrained Whisper encoder. Whisper is not chosen merely
as a high-capacity backbone, but as a representative STT en-
coder widely used in downstream speech-understanding sys-
tems [16, 18, 19, 20]. This allows us to directly examine
whether STT-oriented representations retain the information re-
quired for fine-grained emotion and pragmatic interpretation.
Through controlled comparisons, we make the following
contributions: (1) we define a PAR evaluation setting that ex-
poses failure modes not captured by conventional metrics, (2)
we show that large speech encoders such as Whisper are not
inherently robust to pragmatic ambiguity, (3) we demonstrate
that prosody injection yields substantially larger improvements
than context conditioning within the same backbone, and (4)
we show that prosody-aware speech representations can out-
perform strong text-based LLM inference on PAR. These find-
ings suggest that the central challenge in SER is not solely
model scale, but whether emotion-relevant prosodic informa-
tion is preserved in the representation, particularly under prag-
matically ambiguous conditions.

only and prosody-injected representations using the same pre-
trained encoder backbone. All input audio was converted to

Given an input waveform, we extract frame-level hidden repre-
sentations from Whisper. In parallel, we compute two prosodic
features, F0 and energy [4, 21], and align them to the same
temporal resolution as the encoder outputs. These features are
concatenated with the encoder representations and projected
into a shared space before being processed by a Transformer-
based classification head. Because the prosodic features are in-
jected after the frozen encoder, the temporal interpolation used
for alignment affects only the downstream classification head
and leaves Whisper’s internal attention unchanged. Unlike late-
fusion approaches that combine decisions at the output level,
our method integrates prosodic information directly into the
representation. This design enables joint modeling of Whisper-
derived speech representations and explicit prosodic cues.
2.1. Whisper-based shared representation
We usewhisper-large-v3-turbo[16] as the backbone encoder.
Our motivation is not merely to adopt a large pretrained model
but to analyze a representation that is structurally relevant to
downstream speech-understanding systems. In many practi-
cal pipelines, STT encoders serve as shared representations
from which transcription, emotion recognition, and intent un-
derstanding tasks are extended. However, STT encoders are
optimized for semantic abstraction and robustness to varia-
tion, which may underrepresent prosodic information [16, 20].
Therefore, we keep the Whisper encoder fixed and investigate
whether explicit prosodic augmentation can recover informa-
tion that is otherwise weakly preserved. The Whisper encoder
was kept frozen during training to isolate the effect of prosodic
feature injection at the representation level.
2.2. Prosodic feature extraction
Based on preliminary prosodic feature screening, we retained
threshold-linearized F0 and energy as the inputs. F0 contour
was extracted using Parselmouth and Librosa within a valid
range of 50–500 Hz [22], with unvoiced or silent frames as-
signed zero values. Minor F0 fluctuations within a fixed thresh-
old were linearized prior to temporal alignment, following our
previous preprocessing setup. Energy was computed as the
root-sum-square of Librosa STFT magnitudes over the valid
frequency range. Both F0 and energy features were temporally
interpolated to match the frame-level resolution of the Whisper
encoder outputs before concatenation [23, 24].
2.3. Emotion classification head
The classification head consists of two Transformer encoder
layers followed by three linear layers for multi-label emotion
prediction.
3. Experiments
Our focus is on diagnosing how different representations be-
have in conventional and stricter evaluation settings, including
pragmatic ambiguity, to test a real-world task.
Evaluation metrics.We report Hamming accuracy, Subset ac-
curacy, and a PAR score. In the fine-grained 59-class emo-
tion classification setting, Hamming accuracy reflects coarse,
label-wise agreement and tends to compress differences be-
tween models. In contrast, Subset accuracy requires exact la-
bel matching and provides a stricter evaluation. The PAR score
is reported as Precision atk(P@k) on the PAR benchmark
and measures whether a model can resolve emotional intent in
pragmatically ambiguous utterances, where lexical information
alone is insufficient.
PAR benchmark.We construct an unseen Pragmatic Ambigu-
ity Resolution (PAR) benchmark from 1,000 Korean utterances
where multimodal and text-based labels disagree. This setting
explicitly tests whether a model preserves prosodically relevant
information required for disambiguation.
Compared models.We compare text-based, audio-based, and
fusion-based models, as well as Whisper-based variants with
and without prosodic injection. We also include GPT-4o mini
as a strong transcript-based reference for downstream inference,
with access to sentence context but not to acoustic or prosodic
cues.
Controlled comparison.Rather than comparing model scale,
we focus on within-backbone comparisons to isolate the effect
of prosody. Specifically, we compare Whisper without prosody,
with prosody, and with prosody plus context. In the context-
conditioned setting, we use±5utterances around the target ut-
terance as context to predict its emotion.
3.1. Training and validation dataset
We use a Korean emotional speech style dataset from AI-Hub
(The Open AI Dataset Project, South Korea) [25], which pro-
vides fine-grained emotion annotations together with speaker
metadata such as age and sex. The dataset contains 59 emotion
labels and approximately 457 hours of Korean utterances. We
use this dataset for training and validation of the emotion clas-
sification models. The data is split into training and validation
sets with an 8:2 ratio while preserving the original label distri-
bution through stratified sampling. Table 1 summarizes the age
and sex distribution of reciters in the dataset.

3.2. Unseen test dataset: Pragmatic Ambiguous Sentences
The goal of PAR is to test whether a model can recover emo-
tional intent when text and emotion are not deterministically
aligned, as often occurs in real-world conversation.
The benchmark is derived from 5,600 Korean conversa-
tional video clips covering approximately 100 hours, sourced
from AI-Hub [26]. Human annotators assigned multimodal
emotion labels by jointly reviewing audio, text, and visual in-
formation, while unimodal labels based on audio, text, and vi-
sual modalities were annotated separately. We define utterances
as follows:
• Pragmatic Ambiguity: The multimodal label agrees with the
audio unimodal label but differs from the text unimodal label,
indicating ambiguity.
• Non-ambiguity: The multimodal, audio unimodal, and text
unimodal labels all agree.
For evaluation, audio is extracted from video clips and
aligned to utterance boundaries. From this collection, we con-

struct an unseen test set of 1,000 pragmatically ambiguous utter-
ances. To prevent contextual leakage and ensure situational di-
versity, target utterances were randomly sampled such that their
source dialogue clips did not overlap.
3.3. Main comparison across model families
We compare multiple model families on fine-grained emotion
recognition and PAR to examine how representation type affects
performance under conventional and stricter evaluation settings.
The compared models include text-based encoders, audio-based
SSL encoders [17, 9], a text-audio fusion model, and Whisper-
based models. Additionally, GPT-4o mini is used to test the
LLM’s ability to classify emotions based on the transcribed text.
For Whisper, we evaluate three controlled variants: (1) the
original Whisper representation without explicit prosodic fea-
ture concatenation, (2) Whisper with F0 and energy injected
into the shared hidden representation, and (3) Whisper with F0,
energy, and contextual information. This comparison allows us
to isolate the contribution of prosody within the same backbone.
All models are trained using a Binary Cross-Entropy Loss,
applied per label as is standard for multi-label classification,
together with the AdamW optimizer, with an initial learning
rate of 5e-3 and weight decay of 0.01. The training environ-
ment utilizes a single A100 GPU, and mixed precision train-
ing is applied to optimize training speed. After training, the
models are evaluated on the validation dataset and an unseen
Pragmatic Ambiguous Sentences dataset to assess real-world
generalization. The evaluation uses three metrics: Hamming
Accuracy, which measures correct predictions at the individual
label level; Subset Accuracy, which checks if the entire pre-
dicted set matches the ground truth; and Precision atk(P@k),
which evaluates the relevance of the topkpredictions. Emotion
predictions were obtained using sigmoid activation, reflecting
the multi-label nature of the task. These metrics together pro-
vide insight into the model’s generalization performance. Ham-
ming Accuracy and Subset Accuracy are used for the validation
dataset, and P@k is used for the unseen test dataset, PAR.
4. Results
Table 2 presents the performance of different model families
under both conventional and stricter evaluation settings. The
key finding is that prosody becomes decisive specifically under
pragmatically ambiguous conditions, and this effect is not visi-
ble under coarse evaluation metrics.
4.1. Conventional metrics compress meaningful differences
Under Hamming accuracy, most models achieve similar high
performance, with differences between model families appear-
ing marginal. Concretely, Hamming accuracy spans only about
two points across all models (roughly 96–98), whereas Sub-
set accuracy and PAR each span more than twenty-five points;
the coarse metric therefore compresses real variation by nearly
an order of magnitude. Models that appear comparable un-
der Hamming accuracy exhibit significantly different behavior,
and even different rankings, when evaluated under stricter met-
rics, indicating that conventional evaluation may overestimate
robustness in fine-grained emotion recognition.
4.2. Model scale alone does not ensure robustness
We next examine whether larger models inherently pro-
vide better performance under pragmatic ambiguity. Al-
though Whisper-large-v3-turbo has substantially more parame-
ters (663M) than HuBERT-base (95M) and the text-audio fusion
model (222M), without prosodic injection it does not exceed
these baselines under the stricter metrics. On Subset accuracy
it reaches only 12.46, below both the fusion model (20.51) and
HuBERT-base (15.30); on PAR (21.00) it falls below the fu-
sion model (24.40) and only marginally exceeds HuBERT-base
(20.80). This indicates that neither model capacity nor an STT
training objective alone guarantees improved robustness when
emotional intent depends on prosodic cues.
4.3. Prosody injection substantially improves representa-
tion under ambiguity
Within the same Whisper backbone, prosodic augmentation
yields large and consistent improvements under stricter eval-
uation. Injecting F0 and energy into the shared representa-
tion improves Subset accuracy from 12.46 to 26.39 and PAR
from 21.00 to 32.60, corresponding to gains of +13.93%p and
+11.6%p, respectively. These improvements are substantially
larger than those observed under Hamming accuracy, confirm-
ing that prosody becomes particularly important when evalua-
tion focuses on fine-grained and ambiguous conditions.
4.4. Prosody provides stronger gains than contextual aug-
mentation
We further compare prosodic augmentation with contextual
modeling within the same Whisper backbone. While adding
contextual information yields additional improvements, the
gains are notably smaller than those obtained from prosody
injection. Specifically, context provides +4.83%p improve-
ment in Subset accuracy and +1.6%p in PAR, whereas prosody
yields +13.93%p and +11.6%p, respectively. This indicates that
prosodic information plays a more direct and decisive role than
contextual cues in resolving pragmatic ambiguity.
4.5. Prosody-aware speech representations match text-
based LLM inference
Finally, we compare the prosody-aware model with a text-based
LLM baseline on PAR. The prosody-aware Whisper model
achieves a PAR score of 32.60, matching GPT-4o mini, which
achieves 31.10. This result suggests that pragmatic ambigu-
ity cannot be reliably resolved from textual context alone, even
with a strong language model, and that explicit modeling of par-
alinguistic cues provides critical information for disambigua-
tion.
4.6. Effect of prosody depends on representation character-
istics
The effect of prosody injection is less pronounced in smaller
Whisper models, where improvements on PAR and Subset ac-
curacy are comparatively limited. As shown in Table 3, the
impact of prosody injection varies across model sizes, suggest-
ing that prosodic suppression depends on representation charac-
teristics, though Whisper-base is near the floor on the 59-class
task. Larger STT-oriented models, which are more strongly op-
timized for semantic abstraction, may suppress prosodic varia-
tion more aggressively, making explicit prosody injection more
beneficial. This observation supports our central claim that the
limitation is representation-dependent rather than purely a func-
tion of model scale.

5. Discussion
Our findings reveal a fundamental limitation in current SER re-
search: evaluation protocols based on coarse, label-wise met-
rics can obscure meaningful differences in how models rep-
resent and utilize emotion-relevant information. While Ham-
ming accuracy suggests comparable performance across mod-
els, stricter metrics such as Subset accuracy and PAR expose
substantial variation, indicating that conventional evaluation
may overestimate robustness, particularly when emotional in-
tent is not explicitly encoded in lexical content.
The proposed PAR benchmark makes this limitation ex-
plicit by isolating pragmatically ambiguous utterances where
prosodic information becomes decisive. Under this setting, per-
formance differences are significantly amplified, revealing the
limitations of text-centered and semantically oriented represen-
tations. This highlights that evaluation design critically deter-
mines which aspects of representation are being measured.
Our analysis further suggests that this limitation is funda-
mentally a representation-level issue rather than a function of
model scale. Although large pretrained encoders such as Whis-
per achieve strong performance on conventional benchmarks,
they remain limited under pragmatic ambiguity. Without ex-
plicit modeling of prosody, such representations may fail to
preserve fine-grained paralinguistic cues essential for emotion
and intent recognition. The observations on smaller Whisper
models reinforce our view that pragmatic ambiguity exposes
a representation-level limitation rather than a simple effect of
model scale.
Within this context, prosodic augmentation should be un-
derstood as a diagnostic intervention rather than a mere feature
addition. By injecting F0 and energy into the shared representa-
tion, we observe substantial improvements under stricter evalu-
ation settings, particularly in Subset accuracy and PAR. These
gains are significantly larger than those obtained from contex-
tual augmentation, indicating that prosody provides more direct
evidence for resolving pragmatic ambiguity than textual or con-
textual information [4].
This interpretation is further supported by comparison with
a text-based LLM. Despite strong contextual reasoning capabil-
ities, the LLM baseline remains limited when emotional intent
depends on paralinguistic realization rather than lexical content.
This suggests that pragmatic disambiguation is not solely a rea-
soning problem, but also a representation problem requiring ac-
cess to acoustic cues unavailable in text-only inputs.
Taken together, these findings suggest a shift in SER re-
search: rather than focusing on model scale or marginal gains
on coarse metrics, future work should prioritize representation-
level diagnostics and evaluation settings that reflect real-world
ambiguity. In particular, modeling and preserving prosodic in-
formation should be treated as a core requirement for emotion-
aware speech understanding systems.
6. Conclusion
In this work, we presented a diagnostic study of speech emo-
tion recognition under pragmatically ambiguous conditions. We
showed that conventional metrics such as Hamming accuracy
can obscure differences that stricter metrics, including Subset
accuracy and PAR, reveal.
Our analysis demonstrates that the limitation of current
SER systems is fundamentally a representation-level issue.
STT-oriented encoders such as Whisper, while effective for se-
mantic abstraction, do not inherently preserve prosodic infor-
mation required for fine-grained emotion and pragmatic inter-
pretation. As a result, increasing the scale of the model alone
does not guarantee robustness under ambiguity.
By injecting F0 and energy into the shared representation,
we showed that prosodic augmentation can substantially miti-
gate this limitation, yielding large improvements on Subset ac-
curacy and PAR within the same backbone. These gains are
significantly larger than those obtained through contextual aug-
mentation and may not be fully captured by text-based LLM
inference, indicating that pragmatic disambiguation requires ex-
plicit access to paralinguistic cues.
