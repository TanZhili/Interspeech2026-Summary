Abstract
Understanding how the brain represents speech in adverse
environments is vital for neuroscience and speech technology.
Decoding Clean versus Noisy speech from fMRI is challenging
due to high-dimensional multivoxel patterns and cross-subject
variability. To address this, we developed NeuroPAS-Net, a
three-phase fMRI decoding framework. We evaluated 25 par-
ticipants listening to sentences under four conditions: Clean,
Noisy, DNN-based enhancement (DNN-SE), and classical en-
hancement (Classic-SE). Across 12 speech-related regions of
interest (ROIs), the proposed framework consistently outper-
formed Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Convolutional
Neural Network (CNN) baselines, achieving a peak accuracy
of 79% in the Right Precentral Gyrus (R PreCG). We further
introduced the novel Neuro-Perceptual Assessment Score (Neu-
roPAS), an fMRI decoder–derived metric of enhancement shifts
toward the Clean neural pattern. NeuroPAS correlates with in-
telligibility and ranks DNN-SE closer to Clean than Classic-SE.
Index Terms: fMRI, speech intelligibility, speech enhance-
ment, transfer learning, incremental learning
1. Introduction
Speech is frequently perceived in noisy environments, which
reduces intelligibility and increases listening effort [1, 2]. A
central question in computational neuroscience is how the brain
differentiates Clean speech from Noisy speech during compre-
hension, and whether speech enhancement (SE) can shift neural
responses toward patterns elicited by Clean speech [3–6]. Neu-
ral decoding provides a direct means to assess how separable
these conditions are in brain activity and to quantify the neural
impact of enhancement.
However, achieving high-accuracy Clean-versus-Noisy de-
coding from fMRI remains challenging because fMRI sig-
nals are high-dimensional and show substantial between-subject
variability in both functional organization and speech compre-
hension. While most prior studies have relied on multivoxel
pattern analysis (MVPA) with linear classifiers [7, 8], it re-
mains unclear whether deep learning approaches deliver con-
sistent gains across canonical speech-related regions of inter-
est (ROIs) [9–12]. Moreover, decoding performance is rarely
converted into a practical neural metric that supports direct
comparison across enhancement systems. Here, we present

deep learning approach (DNN-SE) [16] and a classical ap-

• Decoding performance: We propose NeuroPAS-Net, which
outperforms conventional linear SVM models and standard
CNN architectures in speech-condition decoding.
• Key ROI identification: We identify R PreCG as the highest-

• Neural evaluation of SE: We introduce NeuroPAS, a continu-

against subjective intelligibility ratings. NeuroPAS differen-

intelligibility, and shows that DNN-SE elicits more Clean-
like neural responses.

using a Siemens Magnetom Skyra 3T scanner. High-resolution

3. Proposed Method
3.1. Model Overview
We propose NeuroPAS-Net, a transfer-learning-based three-
phase fMRI decoding framework for speech-condition decod-
ing and neural SE evaluation. To address the key challenge
of inter-subject variability in fMRI decoding, NeuroPAS-Net
integrates self-supervised learning (SSL), incremental learn-
ing (IL), and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the same CNN-
based encoder architecture [20]. It uses a transfer-learning strat-
egy [21, 22] in which encoder weights from Phase 1 initial-
ize Phase 2, and encoder plus classifier weights from Phase 2
initialize Phase 3. This enables faster adaptation to a new tar-
get subject by leveraging representations learned from multi-
subject data. The input fMRI signals are represented asX∈
RP , wherePdenotes the number of voxel-wise activation val-
ues within each ROI (up to 11,669 voxels in the largest ROI),
forming a high-dimensional input representation. The overall
structure is shown in Fig. 2.
3.1.1. Phase 1: Self-Supervised Learning
In the first phase, NeuroPAS-Net employs an SSL [23] strat-
egy to learn generalizable representations from a large set of
unlabeled data using a masking-based reconstruction objective.

3.2. Implementation Details
The MVPA-SVM decoding analyses were performed using The
Decoding Toolbox (TDT) [31] within MATLAB R2022a. We
employed leave-one-run-out cross-validation (4 folds), training
on three runs and testing on the remaining run, and then av-
eraged accuracy across folds. An L2-regularized SVM was
used as a baseline classifier, trained to discriminate Clean vs.
Noisy speech conditions within ROIs for each subject. Imple-
mentation details are available at:https://github.com/
JohnSung0501/fMRI-Decoding
4. Experiments
We evaluated the NeuroPAS-Net framework using an fMRI
dataset collected from 25 healthy native Mandarin speak-
ers with normal hearing (12 male, 13 female; aged 18–25
years).The overall workflow is summarized in Fig. 1. Dur-
ing scanning, participants lay supine in the MRI scanner and
were instructed to remain still while listening to speech through
MRI-compatible headphones; responses were recorded using
an MRI-compatible button box. Each participant completed
96 sentence trials (10-word sentences) [3] presented under four
acoustic conditions (24 trials per condition): Clean, Noisy,
DNN-SE, and Classic-SE. Noisy stimuli were generated by
adding stationary speech-shaped noise at an SNR of -3 dB [32].
The two enhancement conditions were produced by applying
SEMamba for DNN-SE [16] and the classical MMSE enhance-
ment algorithm for Classic-SE [17] to the sentence materials.
The 96 trials were split into four runs (24 trials per run). Each
run contained all four conditions, with six sentences per con-
dition, ensuring balanced sampling across runs. Trial order
within each run was pseudo-randomized, and condition order
was counterbalanced using a Latin square design to reduce sys-
tematic order effects across runs and participants [33].
As illustrated in Fig. 1, each trial followed a fixed sequence:
a 1-second fixation cue, a 3-second sentence presentation, a
brief 1-second gap, and a 3-second rating period. After each
sentence, participants reported perceived intelligibility on a 5-
point scale (1 = least intelligible; 5 = most intelligible). These
condition labels and ratings were used for ROI-based decoding
and for relating neural measures to subjective speech compre-
hension. All decoding models were evaluated under identical
outer LORO-CV folds.
5. Results and Discussion
5.1. Decoding of Speech Conditions Across Cortical ROIs
The fMRI analyses revealed condition-dependent multivoxel
activation patterns that reliably differentiated Clean from Noisy
speech across a distributed set of cortical regions. Across all 12
bilateral auditory and language-related ROIs, decoding accura-
cies were consistently above the chance level of 50%, indicating
that information about acoustic clarity is robustly represented
within the human speech processing network rather than being
localized to a single area (Fig. 3). We next compared conven-
tional baselines with our proposed model to quantify the added
value of the architecture. Both SVM and CNN achieved signif-
icant decoding performance across ROIs and exhibited broadly
similar region-wise profiles, suggesting that the observed ROI
differences reflect stable properties of the underlying neural rep-
resentations rather than classifier-specific effects. Importantly,
as shown in Fig. 3, NeuroPAS-Net consistently outperformed
both baselines in every examined region, indicating that it more
effectively captures task-relevant structure in fMRI responses
than standard linear or convolutional approaches.
Among all ROIs, R PreCG showed the highest discrim-
inability, reaching 79.3% accuracy. Additional reliable contri-
butions were observed in bilateral STG and MTG, supporting
a model in which separability is distributed across the network
but is strongest in specific speech-relevant regions.

5.2. Ablation Study
We evaluated NeuroPAS-Net on five high-performing ROIs that
demonstrated reliable effects in our full analysis (Table 1). We
compared three distinct training configurations: Phase 3 only
(direct SFT), Phase 2 + Phase 3 (adding IL), and the full Phase
1 + Phase 2 + Phase 3 pipeline (integrating SSL).
Accuracy improved consistently and monotonically as ad-
ditional phases were incorporated. While the absolute gains
were modest, their uniformity across anatomical regions sug-
gests that earlier phases provide complementary features rather
than overfitting to specific sites. The clearest improvements
were observed in precentral regions; for instance, L PreCG per-
formance rose from 66.46% to 69.25%, while R PreCG reached
a peak of 79.30%. Overall, these results demonstrate that both
self-supervised pre-training and IL contribute meaningfully to
decoding performance, validating the rationale of our proposed
multi-phase strategy.
5.3. NeuroPAS: A Neural Metric for SE Evaluation
To provide a physiologically grounded metric for evaluating SE,
NeuroPAS serves as a continuous decoder-derived neural index
of speech intelligibility. NeuroPAS is derived from a classi-
fier trained on ROI-level neural responses from R PreCG, the
region with the highest discriminability in our decoding analy-
sis. For each stimulus, the corresponding multivoxel response
pattern is input to the trained Clean–Noisy decoder, and the sig-
moid output is used as NeuroPAS. Enhanced-condition trials are
used only for NeuroPAS inference. Higher scores indicate neu-
ral patterns that are more Clean-like. We applied NeuroPAS to
compare two representative enhancement approaches, DNN-SE
and Classic-SE, using R PreCG responses under enhanced con-
ditions. As shown in Fig. 4, NeuroPAS was normalized within
each subject, with the Clean reference set to 1 and the Noisy ref-
erence set to 0. After normalization and averaging across sub-
jects, DNN-SE yielded a higher mean NeuroPAS (0.60) than
Classic-SE (0.41), indicating that DNN-SE shifted neural re-
sponses closer to the Clean-speech target.
Finally, to evaluate metric validity, we quantified the asso-
ciation between NeuroPAS and subjective intelligibility ratings
using subject-level, condition-wise averages (n = 50; 25 sub-
jects×2 enhancement conditions) and reported Spearman’s
rank correlation coefficient to assess the monotonic relation-
ship. As shown in Fig. 5, NeuroPAS was correlated with per-
ceived intelligibility (Spearmanρ= 0.43, correctedp≈0.17),

6. Conclusion
Using NeuroPAS-Net, we decoded speech conditions from
fMRI responses during Clean, Noisy, and speech-enhanced lis-
tening conditions. Across language-related ROIs, the model re-
liably distinguished Clean from Noisy speech, indicating robust
neural sensitivity to acoustic degradation. We also introduced
NeuroPAS, a decoder-derived continuous neural index that re-
lates to subjective intelligibility and quantifies enhancement-
driven shifts toward clean-like neural responses. These find-
ings provide a proof of concept for neural evaluation of SE
in normal-hearing adults and suggest that decoder-derived neu-
ral metrics can directly complement behavioral testing in chal-
lenging acoustics. Future work should validate this framework
in hearing-loss and aging populations and integrate EEG/MEG
with fMRI for brain-guided adaptive SE applications [34–39].
