Abstract 
Misophonia causes strong emotional and physiological 
reactions to specific everyday sounds, yet audiology lacks a 
standardised, scalable assessment pathway. Sound Reactor 
Mission (SRM) is a browser -based, gamified speech -in-noise 
assessment requiring only headphones. In a six -minute space 
mission, participants complete adaptive Speech Reception 
Threshold estimation, select a personally annoying masker, 
compare speech intelligibility across three masker conditions, 
and complete an Acceptable Noise Level measure. Pilot data (N 
= 14; 100% completion; mean time = 6.5 min) showed lower 
intelligibility for the selected annoying sound (59.2%) than for 
synthetic (71.7%) and standard masking noise (86.3%). The 
live Interspeech demonstration lets attendees complete th e 
mission and compare their results with other delegates. 
Index Terms: misophonia, sound sensitivity, speech 
perception in noise, adaptive testing, gamification, hearing 
engagement. 
1. Introduction  
Misophonia is a condition in which specific, everyday sounds, 
such as chewing, slurping, or tapping, trigger strong emotional 
and physiological reactions including irritation, anger, and an 
urge to escape [1]. Affecting an estimated 5–20% of the general 
population, with approximately 6% experiencing clinically 
significant impairment [1,2], misophonia sits at an under -
recognised intersection of audiology, cognitive load, and 
emotional regulation. Onset typically occurs in childhood or 
adolescence; over 50% o f affected individuals first experience 
symptoms between ages 6 and 25 [2], yet the condition is absent 
from most diagnostic frameworks and frequently conflated with 
hyperacusis or anxiety disorders. Critically, approximately 68% 
of affected individuals report functional impairment in at least 
one life domain [2], and misophonia directly affects listening 
behaviour and speech understanding in everyday environments, 
placing it squarely within the scope of audiological practice. 
Despite this scale of impact, there is no standardised clinical 
pathway for misophonia. Assessments rely almost exclusively 
on self -report questionnaires; objective, behavioural, or 
psychoacoustic measures are virtually absent, and current tools 
rarely qua ntify the impact of misophonia on speech 
understanding, a critical gap for clinical decision -making and 
treatment monitoring [1,2]. Standard audiological paradigms, 
including speech-in-noise tests and the Acceptable Noise Level 
(ANL) measurement [3], requi re calibrated transducers, 
specialist rooms, and trained examiners, making them 
unscalable for population -level screening. Web -based and 
mobile digital health tools offer a scalable alternative, and there 
is growing evidence that gamification substantially  improves 
engagement and completion rates [4]. No established platform, 
however, combines rigorous adaptive psychoacoustic 
assessment with a gamified, misophonia -sensitive design 
accessible to the populations most in need. 
Sound Reactor Mission (SRM) fills this gap. By framing a 
validated speech -in-noise battery as an immersive space 
mission, "Discover Your Inner Misophonia," SRM provides the 
first scalable, browser-based tool to quantify how personally  
salient sounds affect speech understanding, producing objective 
psychoacoustic data directly relevant to audiological 
assessment and hearing care. 
2. Platform description  
SRM runs in any modern browser. The full session takes 
approximately 6 minutes (Figure 1) and proceeds through five 
stages 
 
Figure 1: SRM welcome screen on participant device 
2.1. Onboarding and audio check 
Participants report age, grouped into 10 -year bands, and self -
rated sound sensitivity, then complete a guided audio check 
(channel verification, volume calibration) to ensure cross -
device comparability without laboratory equipment. 
2.2. Calibration: SRT estimation 
An adaptive 1-up/1-down staircase [5] estimates the individual 
Speech Reception Threshold (SRT) in amplitude -modulated 
noise using VCV syllable tokens. Starting at +10 dB SNR, step 
size decreases from 5 dB to 2 dB after the first reversal; the 
mean of six reversal points determines the SRT. This anchors 
all subsequent masker conditions to each participant's 
individual noise floor, making the task simultaneously a 
calibration tool and an ecologically informative measure of 
hearing-in-noise ability.

2.3. Fuel discovery: personalised masker selection 
Participants rate a library of everyday sounds (slurping, 
chewing, Styrofoam squeak, snoring, cutlery clinking, clock 
ticking, distant alarm) on an annoyance scale drawn from the 
misophonia literature [1]. The most annoying sound is 
nominated as their "Pre mium Fuel," the personalised masker. 
This stage acknowledges the diversity of individual auditory 
salience and grounds the assessment in personally meaningful 
noise, going beyond the generic maskers used in clinical 
protocols.  
2.4. Comparison: three-masker speech intelligibility 
Participants complete blocked speech identification under three 
masker types at their individual SRT: Premium Fuel (self -
selected annoying sound); Synthetic Fuel (envelope-modulated 
noise sharing the temporal modulation but not spectral 
character of Premiu m Fuel); and Standard Fuel (amplitude -
modulated noise). Per -condition accuracy, reaction time, and 
consonant confusion matrices are recorded, enabling direct 
within-subject comparison of personalised versus generic 
masking. 
3. Pilot data  
Feasibility. Fourteen participants spanning six age bands (18 –
24 to 65+) completed the full mission, yielding a 100% session 
completion rate and mean session duration of 6.5 min (range 
3.1–10.7 min). The majority reported rarely or never being 
sound-sensitive (79%), confirming recruitment beyond the 
clinically motivated sample that typically seeks audiological 
assessment. 
SRT estimation. Adaptive thresholds converged reliably 
(M = −9.4 dB SNR, SD = 2.2 dB), with wide individual 
variation consistent with normative SIN distributions [5]. 
Several participants had very low thresholds (‒34 to ‒38 dB), 
suggesting possible ceiling effects. Future  versions should use 
more effective maskers to better challenge high -performing 
listeners. 
Masker-type effects. Among the six participants with full 
comparison data, intelligibility followed a clear gradient: 
Premium Fuel = 59.2%, Synthetic Fuel = 71.7%, Standard Fuel 
= 86.3%. Crucially, Synthetic Fuel, which preserves the 
temporal envelope of the self -selected annoying sound but not 
its spectral identity, produced intermediate performance, 
suggesting the intelligibility decrement of personally annoying 
maskers is partly spectro-temporal but additionally attentional 
or affective in nature. This distinction has direct implications 
for hearing aid noise-reduction algorithm design. 
ANL. Noise tolerance was lowest for Premium Fuel (M = 
69.4 AU), intermediate for Synthetic Fuel (71.8 AU), and 
highest for Standard Fuel (75.2 AU), directionally consistent 
with the intelligibility gradient and consistent with the view that 
personally salient sounds impose an additional attentional cost 
beyond their acoustic properties [1]. 
Sound preferences. Slurping was the most frequently 
selected annoying masker (29%), followed by Styrofoam 
Squeak (21%) and Cutlery Clinking (14%), with eight distinct 
sounds nominated across 14 participants, demonstrating the 
substantial individual heterogeneity this paradig m is designed 
to capture. 
4. Show and tell demostration  
The demonstration uses the live SRM application, which 
participants can access on their own devices or on tablets 
available at the display. The interactive experience includes 
four components: 
(1) Live SRT estimation : attendees experience the 
adaptive staircase and receive personalised feedback on their 
speech-in-noise threshold, a measure most have never 
encountered. 
(2) Personalised masker selection : attendees browse and 
rate the sound library and nominate their own Premium Fuel, 
experiencing the misophonia -inspired personalisation first -
hand. 
(3) Masker comparison task : attendees complete the 
three-condition intelligibility battery and observe how their 
own performance shifts across masker types. 
(4) Real -time group dashboard : a live aggregated 
leaderboard shows the emerging SRT distributions in real time, 
including ANL, masker preferences, and sound-sensitivity self-
ratings across all session participants, enabling in -situ 
discussion of individual differences, risk patterns, and hearing 
healthcare implications.  
Presenters will discuss the platform architecture, the 
adaptive algorithms, the unmet -needs context for young adult 
hearing engagement, and directions for scale-up research. 
5. Conclusion  
Misophonia is common, early -onset, and functionally 
impactful, yet it remains under -recognised, under -measured, 
and under -served by current audiological practice. Sound 
Reactor Mission reframes misophonia assessment as a 
personalised, gamified experience d esigned to bridge this 
clinical gap, achieving 100% pilot completion and revealing 
systematic masker -type effects that implicate attentional, not 
merely acoustic —mechanisms. We invite Interspeech 2026 
attendees to experience the mission first -hand and to j oin the 
conversation on how speech science can close this unmet 
clinical need.
