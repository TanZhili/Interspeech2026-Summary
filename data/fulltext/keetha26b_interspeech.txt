Abstract
Continuous verification of customer identity during telephone
interactions is essential to prevent unauthorized access to per-
sonal information in medical insurance applications and bank-
ing scenarios. We propose a real-time speaker verification sys-
tem that continuously monitors customer’s speech and com-
pares it with an enrolled voice-print to detect speaker changes
during an active call. Upon mismatch, the system generates
alerts on the agent interface to prevent disclosure of sensitive
information.The proposed method uses a two stage architec-
ture with a ReDimNet-B1 backbone and a projection network
trained with triplet sampling for robust speaker embedding ex-
traction. Cross-lingual, noise, and reverberation-based data
augmentation techniques are used to improve robustness. The
system operates in real time with RTF 0.05 and a low computa-
tional footprint of 318 M MACs. The model achieves an Equal
Error Rate (EER) of 2.08% on TidyV oice evaluation dataset,
demonstrating strong robustness.

nition, continuous authentication, agent alert systems
1. Introduction
Speaker verification systems are widely deployed in voice-
driven security and authentication applications such as bank-
ing, smart home devices, and contact center platforms. Robust
speaker representations are also essential for related tasks in-
cluding speaker diarization, where recordings are segmented by
speaker identity, and target speaker extraction, where a desired
speaker is isolated from interfering sources in noisy environ-
ments. These applications require discriminative and noise-
robust embeddings that generalize across diverse acoustic and
linguistic conditions.
In call center and banking scenarios, speaker verification
plays a critical role in ensuring secure customer interactions.
However, conventional systems rely on one-time authentication
at the start of a call and assume speaker consistency through-
out the session. This assumption often fails in practice due to
call handovers, impersonation attempts, or unauthorized usage
of registered devices. Continuous speaker verification (CSV)
is therefore necessary to monitor speaker consistency in real
time and prevent the disclosure of sensitive information. CSV
is particularly useful for long-duration voice interactions requir-
ing persistent identity monitoring and secure access control, in-
cluding banking, medical insurance, customer support services,
smart home IoT systems, automotive voice control, and govern-
ment or enterprise access systems.
Designing such systems introduces several practical chal-
lenges. Real-time deployment requires low-latency processing
and computationally efficient models. Telephony speech is of-
ten degraded by noise, compression artifacts, and reverberation,
necessitating robust feature representations. Furthermore, mul-
tilingual environments and frequent code-switching introduce
additional variability, making it difficult to maintain consistent
speaker embeddings across languages. To address these chal-
lenges, we propose a two-stage continuous speaker verification
framework for learning robust and language-invariant speaker
representations, with details provided in Section 3.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec-
tion 2 presents the demonstration setup, including the customer
and agent interfaces and the real-time alerting mechanism. Sec-
tion 3 describes the proposed methodology and details of the
training configuration, including the two-stage architecture with
the ReDimNet backbone and the projection network. Conclu-
sions are presented in Section 4 of the paper.
2. Demonstration Setup
2.1. System Overview
As illustrated in Fig. 1, we demonstrate a real-time continu-
ous speaker verification system using a two-interface setup with
customer and agent views. The customer initiates a call and
provides a username to retrieve the enrolled voiceprint; if un-
available, the system allows on-the-fly enrollment using a short
speech segment. Audio speaker verification is conducted at the
beginning of the call, and if verification fails three consecutive
times, authentication is reverted to a conventional password-
based process. Once the speaker is authenticated, call is trans-
ferred to the agent. The agent interface displays live verification
status and continuously monitors speaker consistency, generat-
ing alerts on mismatch to prevent sharing sensitive information.
Upon detecting a mismatch for a longer duration ( 3 s), the agent
is provided with options to terminate the call or continue the
interaction. Although demonstrated for banking scenarios, the
proposed system can be extended to other telephone-based ap-
plications.
2.2. Streaming and Decision Pipeline
Audio is processed in a streaming manner using a 1sec win-
dow with a 0.5sec hop, providing sufficient speech context for
stable verification decisions while avoiding frame-level fluctua-
tions. Speech segments are passed through a voice activity de-
tection module, followed by speaker embedding extraction and
cosine similarity scoring against the enrolled voiceprint. The
system performs continuous verification with 500 ms decision
updates. The system requires approximately 50 ms to process
1 s of audio on an Intel Core i7-8650U CPU, corresponding to

3. Proposed Methodology
3.1. Framework Overview
We propose a two-stage framework for real-time continuous
speaker verification under multilingual and noisy conditions.
The system uses a ReDimNet-B1 backbone [2] to extract com-
pact and discriminative speaker embeddings from log-mel filter-
bank features through temporal and spectro-temporal process-
ing. In Stage 1, the backbone is trained as a multilingual speaker
classification model using a margin-based loss with augmenta-
tion including noise, reverberation, and speed perturbation. In
Stage 2, a lightweight projection network is trained with triplet
sampling on frozen backbone embeddings to improve speaker
separation and cross-lingual consistency. Triplets contain both
same-language and cross-language speaker pairs, enabling ro-
bust language-invariant speaker representations. The overall
pipeline is shown in Fig. 2.
3.2. Model and Training Details
The proposed model consists of a ReDimNet-B1 backbone (2.2
M parameters, 290 M MAC/s) and a lightweight projection
network (256.13 K parameters, 27.36 M MAC/s). Training is
performed on the TidyV oice dataset (457 hours, multilingual
speakers) with augmentation including additive noise (5–20 dB
SNR), reverberation (RT60: 0.2–0.8 s), and speed perturbation
(0.9, 1.1). Stage 1 training follows the WeSpeaker toolkit setup
using SGD (lr:10 −3, momentum: 0.9, weight decay:2×10 −5).
In Stage 2, the projection network is optimized using Adam (lr:
10−3) while keeping the backbone frozen.
4. Conclusion
We present a real-time continuous speaker verification system
for detecting speaker changes and alerting agents to unautho-
rized access.The proposed two-stage model learns robust and
language-invariant speaker representations and achieves 2.08%
EER on the TidyV oice development set. The model has a low
footprint with 2.5M parameters and 318 MMAC/s, and operates
in real time with RTF of 0.05 and gives decision updates every
500 ms.
