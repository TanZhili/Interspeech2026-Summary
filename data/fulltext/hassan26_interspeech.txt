Abstract
Aligning non-invasive neural activity with speech stimuli is
a foundational challenge in neural speech decoding. The
difficulty lies in mapping noisy, high-dimensional electroen-
cephalography (EEG) signals to the temporal dynamics of
speech features. We propose SCANS, a supervised contrastive
learning framework for neural-speech temporal alignment. We
define this as a classification task where, given an EEG seg-
ment and multiple non-overlapping candidate segments from
the same speech signal, the model must identify the single
matching stimulus temporally aligned with the EEG. SCANS
utilizes a dilated convolutional frontend and cross-modal atten-
tion to extract and fuse features across modalities. To bridge
the modality gap, we employ a multi-task objective combining
cross-entropy classification with a contrastive loss. Evaluated
on the SParrKULee dataset, SCANS demonstrates significant
improvement in neural-speech alignment accuracy.
Index Terms: neural speech decoding, electroencephalography
(EEG), supervised contrastive learning, cross-modal attention
1. Introduction
The human auditory system’s ability to decipher complex
speech is increasingly studied using electroencephalogra-
phy (EEG) [1–4]. EEG offers non-invasive recording and
millisecond-level temporal resolution, making it well suited for
capturing the rapid dynamics of cortical speech processing. Be-
yond basic research, EEG-based measures provide objective di-
agnostic tools for assessing auditory function in populations
where subjective responses are unreliable, such as infants or
individuals with cognitive impairments [5, 6]. A central line
of work investigates neural tracking of speech, linking specific
neural responses to attended acoustic inputs. Recently, the tem-
poral alignment of neural responses and speech stimuli has been
formalized as a “match–mismatch” classification task [7]. The
goal is to determine which speech segment among several can-
didates corresponds to a given neural response. This task is es-
sential for establishing ground-truth mappings used in speech-
reconstruction brain–computer interfaces, validating physiolog-
ical speech perception in intelligent hearing aids, and advancing
robust cross-modal models for noisy, high-dimensional data.
Recently, deep learning approaches have been introduced
to address the temporal alignment problem [8–17]. Accouet al.
[11] proposed a dilated convolutional neural network (DCNN)
that jointly extracts EEG and speech features and classifies
matched speech segments using cosine similarity. To enhance
this model, Thorntonet al.[12] applied an ensemble of 50

task-relevant EEG components and a gated recurrent unit [19]
to model temporal dependencies in speech.
Despite these advancements, most architectures rely on a
“late fusion” approach, comparing EEG and speech only at the
final layer, which often fails to bridge the modality gap between
EEG and speech. To address this, Cuiet al.[14] introduced
the attention-based correlation module, inserted after every hid-
den layer, to progressively align both modalities during feature
extraction. Similarly, Qiuet al.[17] proposed a bidirectional
mapping framework that calculates similarities at various net-
work depths. By comparing early layers of one network with
the late layers of another, these methods ensure the final clas-
sification incorporates both low-level and high-level features
across multiple stages of abstraction. However, the effective-
ness of these models is often capped by the inherent noise and
cross-subject variability of EEG data, which obscure the shared
underlying patterns needed for generalization. To overcome
this, Wanget al.[17] introduced a contrastive learning objec-
tive using the InfoNCE (Information Noise Contrastive Esti-
mation) loss [20]. By explicitly maximizing the similarity be-
tween matching EEG-speech pairs while minimizing it for mis-
matched “impostors,” this approach forces the networks to learn
a more discriminative, shared latent space.
However, despite these efforts, the approach by Wanget al.
[17] faces two critical limitations. First, while self-supervised
contrastive learning is powerful, it typically requires massive
datasets to be effective; in the EEG-speech match-mismatch
task, the strict one-to-one mapping between signals signifi-
cantly restricts the availability of diverse positive and nega-
tive pairs, leading to poor generalization. Second, their inde-
pendent encoder architecture prevents the modalities from ex-
changing information until the final comparison, missing vi-
tal cross-modal dependencies during feature extraction. To
overcome these hurdles, we propose SCANS (Supervised Con-
trastive temporal Alignment of Neural responses and Speech
stimuli). SCANS uses a dilated convolutional frontend and
cross-modal attention [21]. This enables the model to capture
long-range temporal context while facilitating direct interaction
and fusion between the EEG and speech streams throughout
the entire feature extraction process. Finally, it is trained with
a multi-task objective that combines a supervised contrastive
alignment loss with a standard cross-entropy classification loss.
This strategy more effectively uses label information to stabilize
the shared latent space and improve generalization despite lim-
ited data samples. SCANS demonstrates significant improve-

2. Proposed Approach
LetXrepresent an EEG segment andSbe a set ofNcandi-
date speech segments. Within this set, only one segment (s +)
is the ground-truth “match” played during the recording, while
the remainingN−1segments are “impostors” taken from dif-
ferent time offsets. The goal is to learn a function that correctly
identifies the matching stimuluss + from the setS. SCANS is
proposed for neural-speech temporal alignment. The model’s
architectural components, the conceptual framework, and train-
ing objectives are described in this section.
2.1. Model Architecture
The architecture of SCANS is shown in Fig. 1. It comprises two
modality-specific encoders-one for EEG and one for speech-
that share the same structure. Each encoder contains two main
components: a dilated convolutional frontend for local feature
extraction and a cross-modal attention transformer for global
inter-modal alignment.
2.1.1. Dilated Convolutional Frontend
The Dilated Convolutional Frontend (DCF) inspired by Accou
et al.[11, 22], serves as the initial feature extractor for both
modalities. As shown in Fig. 1, the EEG pathway first uses
a spatial convolutional layer to project 64-channel data into a
128-dimensional latent space, acting as a learnable spatial filter.
Simultaneously, the speech pathway projects the mono-channel
acoustic envelope into the same 128-dimensional space to en-
sure alignment. To capture long-range temporal context, both
signals pass through three dilated convolutional layers with ex-
ponentially increasing dilation rates (2 0,2 1,2 2) and a kernel
size of 3. This configuration expands the receptive field while
preserving resolution. Within this frontend, each layer is fol-
lowed by a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation and sup-
ported by residual connections to maintain signal integrity and
stabilize training. The processed sequences are then normalized
with layer normalization.
2.1.2. Cross-Modal Attention (CMA) Transformer
Following the DCF, features enter the Cross-Modal Attention
(CMA) transformer blocks, following Caiet al.[21]. Each
CMA block applies layer normalization to ensure stable gradi-
ent flow. Unlike isolated processing, SCANS utilizes two sym-
metric attention blocks: the EEG features act as the query for
the speech key and value in one pathway, while the roles are re-
versed in the other. Fig. 1 presents the CMA block for the EEG
encoder. This dual-pathway interaction allows each encoder to
selectively attend to and refine representations based on the op-
posing modality’s context. Following layer normalization, a po-
sitional embedding is added to the sequences to preserve tem-
poral order, which is critical for alignment. Each block con-
tains four attention heads and a 512-dimensional feed-forward
network, stabilized by dropout. Finally, global average pooling
compresses the sequences into fixed-length embeddings (ze and
zs), which areL 2 normalized to enable similarity measurement
via cosine distance in a shared latent space.
2.1.3. Classifier Head
The final classifier head translates the embeddings into a match-
mismatch decision. For each of theNcandidate speech seg-
ments, the EEG and audio embeddings are concatenated and fed
into a shallow multi-layer perceptron that has a 64-unit ReLU
hidden layer, where dropout (0.3) is also performed. The model
outputs a scalar logit for each candidate, selecting the index
with the highest value as the predicted temporal match.

3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Dataset and Pre-processing
SCANS is trained and evaluated using theSParrKULeedataset
[23], used in the ICASSP 2023 and 2024 Auditory-EEG Decod-
ing Challenges [24, 25]. It contains 168 hours of 64-channel
EEG from 105 participants. Subjects listened to continuous
Dutch stories via headphones in a sound-insulated booth, with
stimuli presented monaurally or dichotically to capture neural
tracking in both quiet and competing-speaker conditions. We
follow the predefined training and testing data splits. The test
set is divided into two subsets-Within Subjects:no unseen
subjects, but the stimuli are unseen andHeld-out Subjects:un-
seen subjects, but stimuli may be seen, see Table 1.
EEG is pre-processed via a 0.5 Hz high-pass filter, multi-
channel Wiener filtering for artifact suppression, and average
referencing, where it is then downsampled to 64 Hz. Speech
envelopes are extracted using a 28-subband gammatone filter-
bank with power-law compression (x 0.6) and downsampled to
64 Hz. Finally, both modalities undergo Z-score normalization
to account for inter-subject amplitude variability and ensure a
consistent statistical distribution for cross-modal attention.
3.2. Experimental Setup
The synchronized EEG and auditory streams are segmented into
windows of lengtht∈{3,5}seconds. During training, we uti-
lize an overlapping sliding window with a 64-sample (1 s) hop
length to maximize training density. Conversely, evaluation sets
are non-overlapping and include a 1s buffer between consecu-
tive segments to prevent temporal leakage and ensure statistical
independence. For each EEG segment, the model must iden-
tify the synchronized audio fromn∈{2,5}candidates. The
n−1impostors are sampled from different stimuli or distant
time offsets to serve as hard negatives.
SCANS is implemented using the PyTorch framework, op-
timizing the model via AdamW with an initial learning rate of
0.001. To ensure stable training, we use a a learning rate sched-
uler (reduce learning rate on plateau) and early stopping.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced SCANS, a framework that effec-
tively addresses the challenges of neuro-speech temporal align-
ment through a dilated convolutional frontend and cross-modal
attention by integrating a supervised contrastive alignment loss
within a multi-task objective. Our experimental results demon-
strate that SCANS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in neural-
speech alignment and exhibits better generalization to unseen
subjects.
