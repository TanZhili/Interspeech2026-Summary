Emotional TTS

Abstract
Diffusion-based TTS has demonstrated strong capability in
emotional speech generation. However, existing emotion align-
ment methods primarily reinforce target emotional expressions
while overlooking modeling of competing emotional modes,
limiting fine-grained controllability. This issue becomes more
pronounced under classifier-free guidance (CFG), which relies
on contrasting conditional trajectories to shape generation. We
propose Emotion Bidirectional Preference Optimization (Emo-
BPO), a simple effective framework that jointly learns emotion-
aligned and emotion-contrastive score functions from reordered
same-text emotional pairs. Our method requires neither auxil-
iary reward models nor additional annotations, and can be seam-
lessly integrated into existing diffusion-based TTS systems to
improve emotional controllability and expressiveness.
Index Terms: Speech Synthesis, Diffusion Model, Emotional
Text-To-Speech (Emo-TTS)
1. Introduction
Emotional text-to-speech synthesis (TTS) seeks to generate
speech that is both intelligible and affectively expressive. Fine-
grained emotional control is critical for applications such as
conversational agents [1, 2], expressive narration [3, 4], and hu-
man machine interaction [5, 6]. Achieving this requires mod-
eling stress, intonation, rhythm, and their interactions within an
emotional space, rendering emotional TTS a multi-dimensional
generation problem.
Early neural TTS architectures established the foundations
of high-quality synthesis. Tacotron [7] introduced end-to-end
sequence-to-sequence acoustic modeling, FastSpeech [8] im-
proved efficiency via non-autoregressive duration prediction,
and VITS [9] unified acoustic and waveform modeling in a
variational framework. Emotional control in these systems
is achieved through categorical embeddings or conditioning
vectors, which limits fine-grained expressiveness and struc-
tured emotion manipulation. Recent advances adopt large lan-
guage model (LLM)-based architectures and diffusion model-
ing to improve controllability and generalization. LLM-based
TTS [10, 11, 12, 13, 14] demonstrates strong zero-shot ca-
pability and synthesis quality. Diffusion approaches, such as
EmoMix [15] and EmoDiff [16], incorporate emotion condi-
tioning into the denoising process for controllable generation.
However, these methods rely on predefined labels or heuristic
conditioning and do not optimize human perceptual preference.
Preference-based optimization reframes emotional align-
ment as a learning problem defined by human judgments. Emo-
DPO [17] performs direct preference optimization on paired
samples without reward modeling. EMORL-TTS [18] formu-
lates emotional alignment as reinforcement learning with com-
posite reward functions. EASPO [19] incorporates stepwise
preference signals to guide the diffusion denoising process. De-
spite these advances, a structural limitation persists in diffusion-
based preference alignment. Diffusion models commonly rely
on classifier-free guidance (CFG) [20], which combines con-
ditional and unconditional predictions at each denoising step.
While existing methods optimize the conditional trajectory to-
ward preferred outputs, they do not explicitly model diver-
gence from non-preferred emotional modes. This asymmetric
optimization underutilizes the contrastive mechanism inherent
in CFG and may weaken inter-emotion discrimination. This
raises a central question:Can diffusion-based emotional TTS
benefit from explicitly modeling both preference-aligned and
preference-contrastive trajectories?
We propose Emotion Bidirectional Preference Optimiza-
tion (Emo-BPO), a structured framework for diffusion-based
emotional TTS. Emo-BPO constructs reordered same-text emo-
tional pairs to jointly learn emotion-aligned and emotion-
contrastive score functions. During inference, these branches
are integrated through a contrastive guidance formulation com-
patible with CFG, strengthening target emotional trajectories
while suppressing competing modes. Emo-BPO does not re-
quire auxiliary reward models or additional annotations and
can be seamlessly integrated into existing diffusion-based TTS
systems.Our contributions are as follows:(1) We iden-
tify a structural asymmetry in existing diffusion-based prefer-
ence alignment methods, namely the absence of explicit mod-
eling of emotion-contrastive trajectories under classifier-free
guidance. (2) We propose Emo-BPO, a bidirectional diffusion
framework that jointly learns emotion-aligned and emotion-
contrastive score functions from reordered same-text pairs, en-
abling improved emotional controllability without auxiliary re-
ward models or additional datasets.

3.2. Emotion Bidirectional Preference Optimization
Existing preference-based emotional TTS methods typically
optimize only the emotion-aligned direction, increasing like-
lihood under the target emotion without explicitly separating
competing emotional modes, which limits controllability. Mo-
tivated by the log-likelihood ratio view of classifier-free guid-
ance, we propose Emo-BPO, a contrastive diffusion framework
that models directional preference between emotional condi-
tions. Emo-BPO jointly learns (i) an emotion-aligned objective
that increases likelihood under the desired emotion and (ii) an
emotion-contrastive objective that suppresses alternative emo-
tions. Intuitively, the aligned branch forms an attractive energy
basin, while the contrastive branch induces repulsive gradients,
enlarging inter-emotion margins. The bidirectional formulation
is realized through preference-based optimization during train-
ing and contrastive guidance at inference.
3.2.1. Train with Emo-BPO
An important observation of Emo-BPO is that bidirectional
emotional supervision can be realized without introducing ad-
ditional annotations or auxiliary objectives. Given standard
emotion-labeled corpora, complementary training signals nat-
urally arise by reordering audio samples that share identical
linguistic content but differ in emotion. Specifically, for a
text conditionc, we construct pairs of speech samples(a 1,a 2)
with identical text but different emotion labels. Without loss
of generality, leta 1 express the target emotion anda 2 ex-
press an alternative emotion. The ordered tuple(c,a 1,a 2)de-
fines the emotion-aligned direction, while reversing the order to
(c,a 2,a 1)produces the complementary contrastive direction.
This reordering provides supervision for both emotional attrac-
tion and emotional separation under matched linguistic condi-
tions. To capture these two opposing directions, we optimize
two dedicated diffusion branches. The emotion-aligned branch
models the conditional score field associated with the target
emotion. For a clean audio samplea 1, we follow the standard

4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets and Experimental Setup
We train our model on two English emotional speech cor-
pora: the Emotional Speech Database (ESD) [23] and
EmoV oiceDB [22]. EmoV oiceDB contains approximately
22,100 emotion-labeled utterances (about 40 hours of speech)
from multiple speakers, covering diverse expressive patterns
and prosodic variations. We adopt the official train/test split
provided by the dataset. The English subset of ESD com-
prises 10 speakers expressing five emotions (Angry, Happy,
Sad, Surprise, and Neutral), with 350 utterances per speaker per
emotion, totaling 17,500 utterances (approximately 12 hours of
speech). We follow the official 8:1:1 train/validation/test split
protocol per speaker–emotion [24, 25]. We construct prefer-
ence pairs using utterances with identical text content, follow-
ing the text-matched pairing strategy of Emo-DPO [17]. For
each text prompt, audio samples expressing the target emotion
are treated as preferred, while samples with other emotion la-
bels are treated as dis-preferred.
Our backbone TTS system is Grad-TTS [26] operating on
80-dimensional mel-spectrograms. We freeze the text encoder
and duration predictor and update only the diffusion decoder
(score network). The model is initialized from the Grad-TTS
pre-training configuration and optimized using Adam with a
learning rate of1×10 −4 and a batch size of 16. Training
is performed on randomly cropped 2-second mel-spectrogram
segments [27]. Waveforms are synthesized using a pretrained
HiFi-GAN vocoder [28]. During inference, we employ a late-
step guidance coefficient defined asω(t) = 1− t
T , whereTde-
notes the total number of diffusion steps. This schedule applies
weaker guidance at early denoising stages to preserve coarse
acoustic structure and gradually increases the guidance strength
at later stages to refine fine-grained emotional prosody.

Subjective Evaluation.Subjective experiments involve 30 par-

MOS, Emotional consistency, AB preference testing, and an
Emotion Accuracy Test (EAT-EMO). In MOS, listeners rate
overall speech naturalness on a 5-point scale (1: poor, 5: ex-
cellent). Emotion MOS measures the perceived similarity be-
tween generated and target emotions on the same scale. MOS
EC measures consistency between the generated audio and the
instruction (emotion + text). In AB tests, listeners select the pre-
ferred sample between two systems based on quality and emo-
tional expressiveness, with balanced emotion categories across
comparisons. In Emotion Accuracy Test participants identify
the perceived emotion of each sample, and accuracy is com-
puted against the target labels. All subjective evaluations use
emotion-balanced samples.
Results and Discussion.Tabs. 1 and 2 report objective and
subjective results. Emo-BPO achieves the best performance
in emotion similarity, prosody similarity, UTMOS, and mean
emotion recognition accuracy, indicating stronger emotional
alignment and perceptual quality. Fig. 2 further shows clear lis-
tener preference over EmoSpeech and CosyV oice2. These gains
are consistent with the bidirectional score shaping of Emo-
BPO, which enlarges inter-emotion margins through comple-
mentary attractive and repulsive gradients while preserving nat-
uralness. Although CosyV oice2 attains the lowest WER due to
its autoregressive LLM-based design prioritizing linguistic fi-
delity, Emo-BPO maintains competitive intelligibility and sub-
stantially improves emotional expressiveness. Overall, combin-
ing emotional attraction and separation with progressive guid-
ance scheduling enables more controllable emotional TTS. Au-

5. Conclusion
We introduced Emo-BPO, a diffusion-based framework for
emotional TTS that addresses a key limitation of exist-
ing preference-driven approaches: they reinforce target emo-
tions but do not explicitly model separation from compet-
ing emotional tendencies under classifier-free guidance. Emo-
BPO learns complementary emotion-aligned and emotion-
contrastive score functions from reordered same-text pairs and
combines them with progressive guidance scheduling during in-
ference. Experiments demonstrate improvements in emotional
alignment, prosodic control, and perceptual quality while main-
taining competitive intelligibility. These results indicate that
jointly modeling emotional attraction and separation leads to
controllable and expressive emotional speech generation.
