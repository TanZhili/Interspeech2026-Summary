Abstract
Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) achieve strong
performance on multiple-choice Audio Question Answering
(AQA) but often exhibit modality bias, over-relying on textual
priors in questions and candidate options rather than grounded
acoustic evidence. We present CoRE, a training-free, plug-and-
play test-time option re-scoring method. CoRE constructs coun-
terfactual audio via chunk permutation and random segment
reversal to disrupt long-range temporal structure while largely
preserving short-time acoustics. It estimates option-level evi-
dence gain by contrasting scores from original and counterfac-
tual audio, and applies an adaptive evidence-aware gate for fi-
nal prediction. Under a unified option-scoring protocol, experi-
ments on DCASE 2025 Task 5 and AIR-Bench SoundQA show
consistent gains withQwen2-AudioandKimi-Audio.
Index Terms: Audio Question Answering, Large Audio-
Language Models, Modality Bias, Test-Time Rescoring
1. Introduction
Audio Question Answering (AQA) is a multimodal question-
answering task that requires joint analysis of input audio signals
and natural-language content to produce the corresponding an-
swer [1, 2]. Recent Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs)
combine large-scale pre-trained acoustic encoders with Large
Language Models to capture fine-grained acoustic cues and se-
mantic representations, and they have achieved strong perfor-
mance on AQA tasks [3, 4, 5, 6, 7].
Despite this progress, LALMs often exhibit modality bias
in AQA, which reduces reliance on grounded acoustic evi-
dence during option selection and final prediction [8, 9, 10].
In multiple-choice AQA, decisions can be dominated by lin-
guistic priors and semantic associations between the question
and candidate options, rather than evidence in the audio sig-
nal [8, 11, 12]. For example, given the question“What follows
the sound of thunder?”, a model may predict“heavy rain”be-
cause of strong linguistic co-occurrence, even when the record-
ing contains acoustic evidence that is more consistent with a
“car alarm”, as shown in Figure 1. The resulting prediction is
linguistically plausible but acoustically incorrect [13, 14].
Recent efforts to mitigate modality bias in multimodal
question answering have explored both training-time and
inference-time strategies. Training-time approaches improve
grounding through debiasing objectives [13], auxiliary super-
vision [15], or architectural modifications [16], but they typi-
cally require retraining and substantial computation, which lim-
its practicality for model deployment. By contrast, training-free
contrastive methods at inference time, which improve faithful-
ness by comparing model outputs under an original input and a
perturbed input condition, have attracted growing interest [17].
Figure 1:Modality bias in multiple-choice AQA.
However, this paradigm does not directly transfer to
multiple-choice AQA. First, designing a negative audio con-
dition is more delicate than in visual or text settings because
naive audio removal or masking may introduce strong interven-
tion artifacts and distribution shift, so the resulting score differ-
ence may not faithfully represent relative acoustic evidence for
candidate options [18, 19]. Second, many existing contrastive
methods operate at the token decoding level, whereas multiple-
choice AQA is naturally formulated through option-level scor-
ing and re-scoring under a unified scoring protocol [1]. These
limitations motivate a dedicated training-free test-time method
for multiple-choice AQA that can construct an informative neg-
ative audio condition while preserving local acoustic statistics
to a practical extent, derive option-level evidence gain from
score contrast, and support reliable option re-scoring.
To address these limitations, we propose CoRE, a training-
free and plug-and-play test-time option re-scoring method for
multiple-choice AQA. CoRE constructs a counterfactual au-
dio condition via chunk permutation and random segment re-
versal to disrupt long-range temporal structure while approx-
imately preserving short-time acoustics. It estimates option-
level evidence gain by contrasting option scores under the origi-
nal and counterfactual audio conditions within a unified option-
scoring protocol, and applies an adaptive evidence-aware gate
for final prediction. Experiments on DCASE 2025 Task 5
and AIR-Bench SoundQA show consistent improvements with
Qwen2-AudioandKimi-Audio, and further analyses sup-
port the counterfactual design and adaptive gating strategy.

Prior work on mitigating modality bias and improving ground-
ing in multimodal question answering can be broadly grouped
into training-time and inference-time approaches. Training-

In contrast, training-free contrastive inference has emerged
as a promising direction to improve faithfulness. The core
idea is to estimate a corrective signal by contrasting model be-
havior under an original input and an intervention condition,
and then use it to steer predictions toward better-grounded out-
puts. In vision-language models, Visual Contrastive Decoding
[17] perturbs visual inputs to suppress hallucination and over-
reliance on unimodal priors, and Instruction Contrastive Decod-
ing [21] perturbs instruction conditions to expose and counter-
act hallucination-prone decoding behavior. In audio-language
models, Audio-Aware Decoding (AAD) [12] constructs an
audio-absent reference via silence and applies token-level con-
trastive decoding under audio-present and audio-absent condi-
tions. Recent adaptive contrastive decoding in text-only LLMs
leverages distributional disagreement and confidence cues to
modulate contrast strength [22, 23]. Whether such signals re-
main reliable indicators of cross-modal bias in large audio-
language models is still underexplored. This line of work pro-
vides the closest conceptual paradigm to CoRE, but in multiple-
choice AQA it leaves two components insufficiently specified:
constructing an informative audio-side contrastive reference
and using contrastive signals for stable option-level correction.
Compared with prior contrastive inference methods, CoRE
addresses these limitations in three aspects. First, CoRE con-
structs counterfactual audio instead of audio removal or silence,
disrupting long-range semantic continuity while approximately
preserving short-time acoustic statistics. This provides a more
informative reference for estimating relative acoustic evidence
across candidate options. Second, CoRE formulates contrastive
correction directly at the candidate-option level under a unified
option scoring protocol, avoiding decoding-specific confounds
such as prefix dynamics and lexical uncertainty. Third, CoRE
uses adaptive gating for option re-scoring: the gate weight is
computed from Jensen-Shannon divergence and one-sided en-
tropy reduction, which suppresses over-correction when the
original and counterfactual conditions exhibit low conflict.

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Dataset
We conduct experiments on the DCASE 2025 Challenge Task 5
AQA dataset [1], which comprises three complementary sub-
sets: Bioacoustics QA (BQA) [24], Temporal Soundscapes QA
(TSQA) [25, 26], and Complex QA (CQA, MMAU-derived
[27]). BQA focuses on bioacoustic recognition and reasoning
over recordings with highly variable durations, TSQA empha-
sizes temporal reasoning in soundscape scenarios, and CQA
targets complex acoustic scenes that require higher-level infer-
ence. We follow the official split and report top-1 accuracy
on each subset. To assess cross-benchmark generalization, we
additionally evaluate on AIR-Bench [28] Sound Question An-
swering, using the same option-level scoring protocol.
4.2. Baseline Models
We use Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct [4] 1 and Kimi-Audio-7B-
Instruct [7]2 as baselines. Qwen2-Audio combines a Whisper-
initialized audio encoder with a large language model for audio-
conditioned instruction following. Kimi-Audio adopts hybrid
audio representations and initializes an audio-language model
from a pre-trained text LLM. Both provide token-level logits
during inference, enabling a unified option-scoring interface
without architectural modifications or parameter updates.

We evaluate all methods under a unified option-level scoring
protocol. For each candidate option, we compute a score from

4.4. CoRE Implementation Details
We construct counterfactual audio in the waveform domain after
resampling to each backbone input rate. The waveform is split
into non-overlapping blocks of durationτ= 40ms, followed
by a random block permutation and independent time reversal
with probabilityp rev = 0.5. This short-time window largely
preserves local acoustic statistics while disrupting long-range
semantic continuity [29, 30]. We generate one counterfactual
waveform per example (Ncf = 1) and apply 3 ms linear cross-
fading at block boundaries. To isolate ordering robustness in
theM= 8shuffle evaluation, we fix the counterfactual sam-
pling seed and reuse the same counterfactual audio across all
permutations of the same example.

6. Conclusion
We propose CoRE, a training-free contrastive re-scoring
method for multiple-choice AQA that uses an informative coun-
terfactual audio reference and adaptive evidence-aware gating,
and improves performance on two benchmarks. CoRE incurs
a modest inference overhead, requiring one additional forward
pass per example to score the counterfactual audio. We plan to
extend CoRE to generative AQA in future work.
