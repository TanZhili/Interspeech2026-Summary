An Immersive VR System for Experiencing Spatial Speech-in-Noise 
Challenges in Clinical Audiology 
Nicky Chong-White, Thomas Ho 
National Acoustic Laboratories, Sydney, Australia 
nicky.chong-white@nal.gov.au, thomas.ho@nal.gov.au 
 
Abstract 
Clinical hearing test results can be difficult for patients and 
families to connect with real -world listening difficulties, 
making it harder to explain why everyday communication is 
challenging and how hearing devices and strategies can help. 
This paper presents a VR -based immersive listening 
demonstration system on Apple Vision Pro that allows patients 
to experience common listening challenges directly. The 
system places users in one of three 3D environments (café, train 
station, and living room) with a target speaker, competing 
talkers, and spatially rendered environmental noise. Users listen 
to short dialogues and answer comprehension questions, while 
clinicians configure difficulty through presets that vary signal-
to-noise ratio, source count, and spatial separation. The system 
is designed as a portable clinical demonstration tool, not a 
diagnostic assessment.

clinical audiology 
1. Introduction 
Effective hearing rehabilitation depends on more than accurate 
diagnosis.  It requires patients and families to understand why 
everyday communication is difficult and how hearing devices 
and communication strategies can help. Standard audiological 
assessments provide essential diagnostic information, but 
hearing thresholds and speech perception scores do not always 
convey why following a conversation in a noisy café or 
separating one talker from several, or maintaining attention in a 
noisy environment remains challenging. Without this shared 
understanding, counselling is less effective, expectations may 
be unrealistic, and patients may disengage from rehabilitation. 
Virtual reality (VR) offers a way to present listening 
situations that are closer to everyday communication while 
retaining control over the auditory and visual scene. Serafin et 
al. [1] reviewed the use of VR in hearing research, noting 
growing potential for assessment, rehabilitation, and training, 
but observing that clinical audiology applications remain at an 
early stage. Seol et al. [2] demonstrated the feasibility of VR-
based speech-in-noise testing in listeners with normal hearing 
and hearing loss, including hearing-aid users. More clinically 
oriented work, such as the VR-FIT approach by Hendrikse et 
al. [3], has used virtual everyday scenes to support hearing-
device fine-tuning. Together, these studies suggest that VR can 
help bridge controlled clinical testing and real-world listening 
experience. However, existing systems largely focus on 
assessment, rehabilitation, or device fitting. The present work 
instead focuses on a portable clinical tool for counselling and 
demonstration of everyday listening challenges. 
This work presents an immersive listening demonstration 
system implemented on Apple Vision Pro. The system renders 
three everyday environments with spatially positioned target 
speech, competing talkers, and environmental noise, and 
supports clinician-configurable difficulty progression. The aim 
is not to replace audiological assessment but to provide an 
experiential tool that makes real-world listening challenges 
more visible and discussable during clinical conversations. 
2. System description 
2.1  Overview 
The system is an immersive spatial listening demonstration 
application for Apple Vision Pro, designed for clinician-guided 
use in standard clinical rooms without requiring a sound-treated 
booth, loudspeaker array, or laboratory setup. It combines 
immersive 3D visual scenes, spatially rendered audio with 
multiple competing sources, short speech-based listening tasks, 
and voice-based interaction. An overview of the system 
workflow is shown in Fig. 1.

The application is implemented using SwiftUI, RealityKit,

2.2  Spatial Audio Design 
Audio sources are spatialised using RealityKit's spatial 
audio framework. Target speech, competing talkers, and 
environmental sounds are attached to 3D entities at specified 
positions within each scene, so that each source is perceived 
from a distinct direction relative to the listener. Apple Vision 
Pro's head tracking ensures that sound sources remain anchored 
to their positions in the virtual environment as the user moves, 
preserving spatial relationships between listener and sources. 
Target and competing talkers/sounds are represented as 
visual characters or objects within the scene, reinforcing the 
audiovisual congruence of the listening environment and 
helping users identify the spatial location of competing sources, 
as they would in a real situation. 
This spatial rendering reproduces a core challenge of real-
world listening: attending to a target speaker while competing 
speech and noise arrive from different directions.  
2.3  Speech Materials and Task Design 
Speech materials include both audio captured in real 
environments and pre-recorded voice or sound effect clips, 
spatially positioned within the scene. The target dialogue (20–
30 seconds) is presented from a defined speaker location while 
competing audio sources are layered from other spatial 
positions.  After listening, the user answers one or more 
multiple-choice comprehension questions about the dialogue 
content. The number of dialogues and questions are 
configurable, and the system can be extended to allow for multi-
speaker conversations or more varied task sequences. 
The comprehension questions are designed to focus 
listening attention and to provide a structured basis for post-task 
discussion about what was heard or missed, rather than to serve 
as a calibrated speech perception measure. 
2.4  Difficulty Configuration 
Before each task, the operator selects the environment and a 
difficulty preset (Easy, Medium, or Hard). Presets vary the 
number, position, and distance of competing sources relative to 
the listener. Because the spatial audio rendering attenuates 
sound with distance, placing competing sources nearer to the 
listener increases masking and effectively reduces the signal-
to-noise ratio. This structure allows the difficulty to progress 
from straightforward listening conditions to more complex 
configurations within a single session.  
2.5  Interaction Design 
The system supports voice-based responses via Apple's Speech 
framework, allowing users to answer comprehension questions 
without learning VR gestures, relying on fine motor control, or 
requiring eye-tracking calibration. This is particularly relevant 
for clinical use with older adults or users unfamiliar with VR. 
Gaze-based selection and pinch gestures are available as 
alternatives. 
3. Clinical use case 
Many people with hearing difficulties describe listening 
problems that are hard to convey in a clinical setting, such as 
following a conversation when other distracting voices are 
present. These experiences can also be difficult for family 
members to appreciate, particularly when audiometric results 
suggest relatively mild hearing loss. 
The immersive demonstration provides a shared 
experiential reference point for clinician-patient discussion. By 
stepping through increasing difficulty levels within a session, 
the clinician can illustrate how background noise, competing 
speech, and spatial complexity interact to create listening 
difficulty, connecting clinical concepts to a concrete, felt 
experience. The system is designed for use without hearing 
devices, typically before discussions about device options and 
communication strategies. The ap proach is intended to 
complement standard audiological practice by  supporting 
conversations about which everyday communication situations 
are difficult, and what strategies and device features can help. 
4. Future directions 
The prototype has been demonstrated to a small group (N = 
8) of clinicians and industry professionals. Participants 
completed the full workflow across the environments. Users 
noticed increased listening difficulty as preset levels became 
more demanding. Initial feedback was positive regarding 
usability and engagement, though formal evaluation of 
perceived clinical value has not yet been conducted. 
The system has not yet been evaluated with hearing -
impaired users, and the perceptual difference between difficulty 
presets has not been formally evaluated . Future directions 
include assessment of clinician-rated usefulness, relevance 
across a range of hearing profiles, and adaptation to other VR 
platforms such as Meta Quest. Longer-term, the platform could 
be extended toward assessment by incorporating standardised 
speech materials and calibrated scoring.   
5. Conclusion 
This paper presented a VR -based immersive listening 
demonstration system for Apple Vision Pro that represents 
everyday listening challenges using spatially rendered speech, 
competing sources, and realistic 3D environments. The system 
supports configurable difficulty progression and voice-based 
interaction in a portable headset format, without requiring 
specialist laboratory infrastructure. 
Initial demonstrations with clinicians and industry 
professionals support the feasibility of the approach. Future 
work will evaluate perceived realism, clinical utility, and 
usability with hearing-impaired users, and explore adaptation to 
other VR platforms and potential extension to standardised 
assessment tasks.
