Abstract
Skin-attachable accelerometers (ACCs) capture noise-robust
speech vibrations and complement acoustic microphones
(AMs). However, multimodal speech enhancement models
combine ACC and AM signals with parallel encoders or at-
tention blocks, which increases edge-deployment cost. We
present a lightweight ACC-assisted U-Net v2 (LAU-NetV2)
based on efficient cue-guided feature-wise linear modulation
(FiLM). The ACC stream is compressed into temporal voice ac-
tivity to suppress non-speech regions and spectral harmonics to
preserve voiced structures. These speech-informative represen-
tations generate FiLM parameters that modulate intermediate
feature maps in the time-frequency domain. The LAU-NetV2
with FiLM has 46k parameters, 68× fewer than prior multi-
modal models. ACC-guided modulation improves PESQ from
1.78 to 2.78 on TAPS+DNS and outperforms baselines. Finally,
the LAU-NetV2 runs in 48.66 ms on a wearable microcontroller
prototype, enabling real-time voice interfaces.

voice activity, harmonic, FiLM, lightweight, on-device
1. Introduction
Speech serves as a fundamental interface in human-machine
interaction (HMI) applications, such as wearable earbuds and
smart glasses [1, 2]. Acoustic microphones (AMs) are widely
used to capture airborne speech; however, their signal quality
degrades severely in noisy environments. Numerous speech en-
hancement (SE) models have been proposed to suppress such
background noise [3, 4]. However, these methods typically re-
quire substantial computational and memory resources, which
hinders their deployment on low-resource edge devices. To
address these hardware constraints, recent studies have devel-
oped lightweight end-to-end architectures [5, 6]. For instance,
full-band and sub-band path extension network (FSPEN) em-
ploys dual-path gated recurrent units (GRUs) to capture spec-
tral patterns using 79k parameters [7]. Furthermore, a state-
of-the-art (SOTA) lightweight SE network (LiSenNet) achieves
a highly compact design using sub-band convolutions, operat-
ing with only 37k parameters and 56M multiply-accumulate
operations per second (MACs/s) [8]. Despite these advance-
ments in efficiency, highly compact architectures often reduce
network depth and recurrent state size, constraining their ability
to model complex speech structure [7]. This limitation becomes
pronounced at signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) below 0 dB, where
networks relying solely on noisy AM inputs degrade in perfor-
mance because limited representational capacity hinders the re-

covery of voice characteristics and harmonic structures [5, 9].
Bone-conduction microphones (BCMs)—including
skin-attachable accelerometers (ACCs) and in-ear micro-
phones—offer a noise-robust alternative to assist the AM in
low-SNR conditions [10, 11, 12]. Unlike AMs, BCMs capture
structural resonances and vocal vibrations at the neck skin or
ear canal [13]. However, as these vibrations propagate through
soft tissues, high-frequency phonetic details are severely
attenuated, resulting in muffled and less intelligible signals
[14]. To overcome these physical limitations, multimodal SE
models have been developed to combine the noise-robust BCM
signals with wideband AM signals to recover full-band clarity
and intelligibility [15, 16]. For example, a low-complexity
own-voice reconstruction system integrates multimodal inputs
using a time-frequency long short-term memory (LSTM)
architecture, improving perceptual evaluation of speech quality
(PESQ) by 1.11 over BCM-only processing [17]. To further
enhance SE quality, recent advanced architectures extract
complementary features using complex integration strategies
[18, 19]. Attention-based densely connected convolutional
recurrent network (DCCRN) outperforms simple feature
concatenation by applying dynamic weights to BCM and
AM features during fusion [20]. Additionally, VibV oice
processes both signals through separate parallel encoders
to learn modality-specific features as auxiliary inputs for
AM enhancement [21]. However, these complex integration
strategies substantially increase computational overhead and
memory footprints, often exceeding 10 MB in model size
[20, 21]. These footprints are difficult to accommodate on
wearable SE systems built on microcontroller units (MCUs),
which offer limited on-chip memory (hundreds of kB RAM
and MB-scale Flash) [22]. Therefore, further advances in
multimodal SE are needed to enable real-time operation with
minimal overhead.
In this study, we propose a lightweight ACC-assisted
U-Net v2 (LAU-NetV2) to achieve high-performance multi-
modal SE under edge-device resource constraints. Unlike prior
multimodal SE models that incur substantial overhead from
attention-based fusion or parallel encoders, our LAU-NetV2
performs multimodal integration through efficient cue-guided
feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM) [23]. The ACC stream is
compressed into two speech-informative representations: tem-
poral voice activity for suppressing non-speech regions and
harmonic structure for reinforcing voiced harmonic patterns.
These representations are projected to FiLM parameters (γ,
β) and used to modulate the U-Net [24] bottleneck equipped
with time- and frequency-axis GRUs, providing targeted time-
frequency guidance without costly multimodal fusion. The pro-
posed FiLM-based approach improves PESQ from 1.78 (noisy-

2. Methods
2.1. LAU-NetV2 architecture and cue-guided modulation
The system takes noisy AM (xN oisy) and skin-attachable ACC
(xACC ) signals sampled at 8 kHz. We compute the short-time
Fourier transform (STFT) using a 512-sample Hanning window
with a 128-sample hop size. The network operates on the log-
magnitude spectrograms of these input streams.
Unlike previous multimodal SE models, the LAU-NetV2
integrates ACC through cue-guided FiLM. We extract compact,
noise-robust representations from the ACC and use them as
modulation parameters for the enhancement backbone. Specif-
ically, the ACC provides low-frequency body-conducted vibra-
tions that remain stable under ambient noise. From the ACC
magnitude spectrum, we derive two cues. (i) V oice activity de-
tection (V AD): we compute a frame-level power (P ACC ) and
mark a frame as speech-containing ifP ACC > Pth, where the
power threshold (Pth) is set to five times the meanP ACC com-
puted over non-speech frames in the training set [14]. (ii) Har-
monic: for frames with detected speech activity, we estimate
the fundamental frequency and construct a soft harmonic mask
based on its integer multiples as in [26], assigning linearly de-
caying weights (min = 0.5) around each harmonic.

three downsampling convolution (DS-Conv) blocks, a FiLM-
guided bottleneck, and three upsampling convolution (US-
Conv) blocks, where convolutions operate along the frequency
axis. The first DS-Conv utilizes standard convolutions, while
the subsequent two blocks employ a sequence of pointwise and
depthwise convolutions to reduce parameters. In the bottleneck,
the FiLM module dynamically modulates intermediate feature
maps (xc,f,t) as follows:

2.2. Deployment for wearable prototype
The LAU-NetV2 was implemented in 32-bit floating-point pre-
cision and embedded in a custom multimodal wearable proto-
type for real-time operation. As shown in Fig. 2(a), the proto-
type integrates the AM and skin-attachable ACC with the MCU.
The MCU acquires synchronized dual-sensor data, performs
on-device enhancement, and streams the processed audio to a
host PC for recording and evaluation.

cludes buffering, dual-channel STFT, cue extraction, model in-
ference, and iSTFT synthesis. For interactive speech applica-
tions, end-to-end latency is typically required to be below 200

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset and training details
We trained and evaluated the proposed model using the TAPS
dataset [14], which consists of AM and ACC recordings from 60
Korean speakers. The dataset was divided into speaker-disjoint
subsets: 40 for training, 10 for validation, and 10 for testing.
Both signals were downsampled to 8 kHz to enable real-time
operation on the resource-constrained MCU, while higher sam-
pling rates require more samples to be processed per unit time
and impose a greater computational load. We corrupted the AM
signals to simulate noisy environments by mixing 6,000 noise
clips randomly selected from the DNS Challenge dataset [25].
The SNR was uniformly distributed between -20 dB and 20 dB.
To process the input time-domain signals, we applied the
short-time Fourier transform (STFT) using a Hanning window
with a frame length of 64 ms (512 samples) and a hop size of 16
ms (128 samples). The model context length was constrained
toT= 4frames (112 ms) during training and inference.
The training objective was to minimize the mean squared er-
ror (MSE) between the estimated and clean AM log-magnitude
spectrograms. We trained the LAU-NetV2 for 100 epochs using
the AdamW optimizer [28] with a learning rate of4×10 −4 and
a batch size of 64. The final model weights were selected based
on the lowest validation loss. All training procedures were con-
ducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel i7-6800K CPU,
128 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3080 Ti GPU.
3.2. Evaluation metrics and baselines
We assessed the SE performance using narrowband PESQ [29],
short-time objective intelligibility (STOI) [30], and DNSMOS
P.835 [31]. Furthermore, computational complexity was mea-
sured using the number of parameters and MACs/s. Real-
time processing feasibility was evaluated on the target MCU
by measuring on-device inference time and end-to-end latency.
We compared the LAU-NetV2 against two categories of base-
line models: (i) Noisy-AM-only SOTA lightweight networks
(FSPEN [7] and LiSenNet [8]) and (ii) multimodal architec-
tures utilizing paired noisy AM and ACC inputs (FT-JNF S [17],
VibV oice [21], and LAU-NetV1 [26]). To ensure a fair com-
parison, all baseline models were retrained from scratch using
their respective public repositories 2,3,4,5 under the exact same
TAPS+DNS mixing protocol.

We conducted an ablation study to isolate the contribution

of the ACC signal to the U-Net input improved performance,
confirming that bone-conducted information is beneficial under
severe noise. Building on this, introducing cue-guided FiLM

whereas the harmonic pattern helped preserve voiced structures.
The full LAU-NetV2, combining both V AD- and harmonic-
guided FiLM pathways, achieved the best overall performance.
4.2. Analysis of cue-guided modulation
We analyzed the distributions of the FiLM parameters (γ,β)
over the test set to characterize how cue-guided modulation
operates (Fig. 3). As defined in Eq. (1),γcontrols the fea-
ture amplitude:γ≈0leaves the activation nearly unchanged,
γ >0increases it, andγ <0suppresses it. In contrast,βacts
as an additive bias that shifts the feature baseline. The V AD-
guidedγshows a more positively shifted distribution than the
harmonic-guidedγ, whereasβremains tightly centered near
zero in both cases. This suggests that the ACC-derived repre-
sentations mainly affect feature amplitudes, with voice-activity
information providing stronger gain than harmonic information.
We further assessed the functional importance of the four
FiLM parameter sets (harmonic-guidedγ/βand V AD-guided
γ/β) by setting each parameter to zero individually during in-
ference (Table 3). Setting the scaling termγto zero caused sub-
stantially larger PESQ/STOI degradation than setting the shift
termβto zero. These results indicate that multiplicative scaling
plays a dominant role compared to additive shifts in gating bot-
tleneck features. Overall, these analyses show that dynamically
generated FiLM parameters are critical to the enhancement be-
havior and provide mechanistic evidence for FiLM-based mod-
ulation.

4.3. On-device real-time demonstration
The real-time feasibility of LAU-NetV2 was verified by de-
ploying the model on the target MCU. The inference stage was
constrained to a 58.25 ms budget, as detailed in Section 2.2.
As summarized in Table 4, the uncompressed model required
87.12 ms per inference on the MCU, whereas 40% structured
channel pruning reduced the inference time to 48.66 ms, satis-
fying the real-time constraint. This latency reduction incurred
only a 5.8% PESQ drop (from 2.78 to 2.62). Pruning reduced
the on-device footprint from 224.38/232.32 KiB (Flash/RAM)
to 153.73/151.22 KiB, where the Flash and RAM correspond
to model storage and runtime buffers, respectively. We fur-
ther evaluated the wearable prototype running the pruned LAU-
NetV2 in a real-world setting, as shown in Fig. 4(a). A speaker
recited the Rainbow Passage [32] under server room noise at
a level of 92.3 dBA. Fig. 4(b) compares the noisy AM record-
ing and the real-time enhanced output produced on-device. The
spectrogram shows that our system suppresses severe broad-
band noise and preserves voiced harmonic structures.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced the LAU-NetV2, a compact ACC-
assisted architecture for multimodal SE on wearable edge de-
vices. The proposed network extracts noise-robust voice-
activity and harmonic information from bone-conducted ACC
signals. This information dynamically guides the U-Net bottle-
neck features through FiLM, adding an overhead of only 30k
parameters. This low-overhead modulation improves PESQ
from 1.78 to 2.78 over the noisy-AM-only U-Net baseline. Fur-
thermore, we applied structured channel pruning to meet the
inference-time budget of the MCU. Consequently, the LAU-
NetV2 demonstrates consistent robustness in extremely noisy
environments, establishing an efficient framework for real-time
voice communication on resource-limited embedded systems.
