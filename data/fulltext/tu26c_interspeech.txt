A V-SNINet: A multi-channel audio-visual speech-noise interaction network for
Target Speaker Extraction with cross-beam attention

Abstract
This paper proposes a multi-channel audio-visual speech-noise
interaction network (A V-SNINet), which combines mask-based
beamforming with a deep learning (DL) cross-beam atten-
tion mechanism. Unlike existing methods that mainly rely
on lip-speech synchronization cues, the proposed cross-beam
attention explicitly models frame-level interactions between
target-beam and interference-beam features, enabling mutual-
exclusion style suppression in the beam domain. In the first
stage, an A V-DPCRN-guided GEVD beamformer uses spatial
information to obtain target and interference beams. In the sec-
ond stage, A V-SNINet jointly estimates speech and noise masks
through a dual-branch interactive architecture. On our real-
world recording dataset, A V-SNINet achieves a relative WER
reduction of 28.23%, from 28.40% to 20.38% (A VG), over the
first-stage baseline. On far-field LRS2 simulation, A V-SNINet
achieves the best average WER of 17.20%.
Index Terms: audio-visual speech separation, beamforming,
multi-speaker scenarios, cross-beam attention
1. Introduction
The speech signals recorded by distant microphones are of-
ten corrupted by reverberation, background noise and multiple
speakers, leading to considerable degradation in ASR perfor-
mance, particularly at low signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs), which
is termed the ”Cocktail Party Problem” [1, 2, 3]. The multi-
channel speech enhancement uses spatial information in ad-
dition to the temporal and spectral information. Representa-
tive algorithms in this category include multi-channel Wiener
filtering [4], blind source separation [5, 6], and beamform-
ing [7, 8, 9, 10]. For multi-channel speech enhancement and
separation, the target speaker location can effectively improve
algorithm performance. In [11], a location-based angle feature
computing the cosine distance between the steering vector and
inter-channel phase difference (IPD) for each speaker in mix-
ture is proposed. Beamforming techniques also can utilize the
direction of arrival (DOA) information to enhance the speaker
from the desired direction [12]. For the mask-based beamform-
ing [13, 14, 15], approaches utilizing regression model esti-
mated time-frequency (T-F) masks to estimate target speaker
and noise specific power spectral density (PSD) matrices to ob-
tain the beamforming filter parameters, while alleviating the
need of explicit direction of arrival (DOA) estimation.
Recently, visual information has been incorporated into
speech applications to improve performance, such as audio-
visual voice activity detection (A VV AD) [16], audio-visual
speech separation and enhancement (A VSS and A VSE), [17,

18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24], and diarization [25, 26, 27]. Com-
pared with acoustic signals, visual streams are less affected by
background noise and interfering speakers. A common A VSS
strategy is to encode lip regions into visual embeddings and fuse
them with acoustic features for target speech extraction. While
recent research explores richer visual cues (e.g., cue decou-
pling, pose invariance, and co-occurring face attention), most
approaches still focus on target enhancement rather than explic-
itly modeling target-noise mutual exclusion.
In this paper, we propose a cross-beam attention mecha-
nism to suppress non-target information through explicit target-
noise interactions. The mechanism performs frame-level cross-
attention between target-beam and interference-beam features.
Highly correlated components are penalized to prevent leak-
age from interference into the extracted target speech. Since
interference beams contain both background noise and com-
peting speakers that are typically asynchronous with the target
speaker’s lip movements, we further introduce a parallel dual-
branch interaction block to jointly estimate target speech and
interference masks. The contributions in this paper are summa-
rized as follows:
• A two-stage framework is built, where A V-guided beamform-
ing first generates spatially separated target and interference
beams to provide high-quality acoustic priors for the second-
stage network.
• A dual-branch cross-beam attention module is proposed to
perform explicit frame-level mutual suppression between tar-
get and interference features, enabling more direct non-target
cancellation than conventional inter-modal fusion.
• To improve reproducibility and benchmarking fairness, we
conduct additional far-field LRS2 simulation experiments
with matched array/noise settings and report clear gains over
strong A V baselines.
2. The Proposed Method
The overall framework of A V-SNINet is illustrated in 1(a). In
the first stage, an A V-guided beamforming module estimates
target and interference beams. In the second stage, both beams
are fed into A V-SNINet, which performs explicit frame-level in-
teractive suppression between target and interference features.
Details of each module are presented below.

3. Experiments
3.1. Data corpora
3.1.1. In-house real-recording set
The commercial system is designed for smart interactive de-
vices (e.g., ATM-like kiosks) that capture both audio and video
from near-frontal users. The device camera is placed approxi-
mately at face height with a resolution of1080×768(height×
width), and is paired with a circular microphone array of radius
4.25 cm.
The clean speech set contains 50 hours from 50 speakers
recorded in an anechoic chamber. The noise corpus contains
200 recordings (2 minutes each) from 50 noise types (airport,
cafe, kitchen, bar, etc.). We use 200 room impulse responses
(RIRs) withT 60 = 80ms and source-array distances from 0.5
to 2 m. SNR is sampled from[−10,−5,...,+10]dB, and the
average SDR is around 0 dB. All signals are sampled at 16 kHz
with 16-bit precision. The total mixed training duration is 300
hours. We treat the backend ASR as a black box and use word
error rate (WER) as the final evaluation metric.
3.1.2. LRS2 far-field simulation set
To address benchmarking fairness and reproducibility concerns,
we additionally build a far-field simulation protocol on the pub-
lic LRS2 corpus [29]. The simulation pipeline follows the same
acoustic assumptions as our in-house set: (1) circular array ge-
ometry matched to the real device, (2) source-array distance
range of 0.5–2 m, (3) reverberation with comparableT 60 set-
tings, and (4) SNR sampling from[−10,−5,...,+10]dB.
Background noise categories are aligned with the in-house
setup.
3.2. Implementation details
We use a 1024-point STFT with a 1024-sample Hann window
and a 256-sample hop size, resulting inF= 513frequency
bins. In Stage 1, the input features are the first-channel LPS,
the selected IPD feature, and the interpolated-and-projected lip
feature map. In Stage 2, the speech and noise branches both
use a DPCRN backbone consisting of 2 convolution blocks, 3
DPRNN blocks, and 2 deconvolution blocks, while the pro-
posed cross-beam attention modules are inserted into the bot-
tleneck layers. During training, the initial learning rate is10 −3
and is decayed by 3% every three epochs. The maximum epoch
number is 150, and validation is performed every three epochs.
The visual frame rate and audio sampling rate are 25 fps and
16 kHz, respectively. We crop the center lip region to60×60
for efficiency while preserving key articulatory cues. Following
prior A VSS work [20], the visual encoder is initialized from Lip
Reading in the Wild pretraining and kept frozen during training.
The batch size is set to 25. Unless otherwise specified, the loss
weights are set toλ s =λ n = 1.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed A V-SNINet for multi-channel audio-
visual target speaker extraction. The first stage uses A V-guided
mask-based beamforming to produce target/interference beams,
and the second stage uses dual-branch cross-beam attention
to perform explicit speech-noise mutual suppression. On the
in-house real-recording dataset (Table 1), A V-SNINet reduces
average WER from 28.40% to 20.38% (relative 28.23%) and
achieves strong improvements in low-SNR settings. On the
LRS2 far-field simulation set, A V-SNINet also achieves the best
average WER (17.20%), outperforming USEV + GEVD and
RTFS-Net + GEVD. In future work, we will extend this inter-
action mechanism to stronger backbones (e.g., Conformer and
Mamba) and evaluate on broader public benchmarks such as
LRS3.
