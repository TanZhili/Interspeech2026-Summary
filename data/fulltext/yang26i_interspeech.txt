Ambiguity in Lightweight Dual-Channel Speech Enhancement

Abstract
Multi-channel speech enhancement based on deep learning has
achieved significant progress. While keeping low complexity,
lightweight dual-channel models exploit spatial cues to out-
perform single-channel models on edge devices. However,
their performance often degrades for closely-spaced sources
due to unconstrained spatial reliance. To mitigate this, we
propose a Dynamic Spatial-aware Knowledge Distillation (D-
SKD) framework that enhances spatial discrimination. Specifi-
cally, D-SKD introduces a dynamic arbitration mechanism into
the distillation process with a spatially invariant single-channel
teacher, enabling the dual-channel model to adaptively regulate
spatial reliance without explicit angular supervision. Experi-
ments on dual-channel GTCRN and other models show that
D-SKD consistently improves performance for closely-spaced
sources while preserving quality elsewhere. Requiring no ad-
ditional inference overhead or auxiliary inputs, it is well suited
for lightweight deployment.
Index Terms: Speech enhancement, dual-channel, spatial am-
biguity, knowledge distillation
1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) aims to recover clean target speech
in noisy environments. While single-channel SE relies on tem-
poral or spectral characteristics to suppress interference [1, 2],
multi-channel SE further exploits spatial information captured
by microphone arrays, introducing an additional dimension to
separate target speech from interferers [3]. Due to this advan-
tage, multi-channel SE has become an important front-end mod-
ule for applications such as teleconferencing, smart glasses, and
automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems [4, 5, 6].
Recent advances in deep neural networks (DNNs) have sig-
nificantly improved multi-channel SE by enabling data-driven
modeling of complex nonlinear relationships in adverse acous-
tic environments [7, 8]. In particular, end-to-end multi-channel
architectures can jointly model spatial and spectro-temporal
information without relying on hand-crafted signal process-
ing modules, and they consistently outperform single-channel
counterparts in most conditions [9, 10, 11]. Among these ap-
proaches, lightweight dual-channel models are especially at-
tractive for low-footprint edge devices, as they offer a favorable
balance between enhancement performance and computational
cost [12].
However, the performance gains of multi-channel models
are not consistent across all scenarios. In closely-spaced source
conditions, where the target speaker and the interferer are lo-
cated in similar directions, spatial cues become less discrimina-
tive. In such cases, unconstrained reliance on spatial informa-
tion can introducespatial ambiguity, leading to severe perfor-
mance degradation and even making multi-channel models un-
derperform their single-channel counterparts [13, 14, 15]. This
issue is particularly critical for lightweight models, as the lim-
ited capacity of these architectures makes them more vulnerable
to unreliable spatial cues.
Existing studies have attempted to address this problem by
explicitly or implicitly exploiting the source distance to regu-
late the use of spatial features. A representative approach is to
jointly estimate the target speech and the source distance in a
multi-task framework, and then switch between multi-channel
and single-channel processing modes accordingly [13]. How-
ever, such model integration schemes may introduce additional
memory and deployment overhead. Another line of work uses
direction-of-arrival (DOA) information to guide attention mech-
anisms over spatial features across varying source distances
[16], but this requires prior localization information, which may
not always be available in practical applications. To the best
of our knowledge, existing methods still rely on auxiliary geo-
metric information or additional model components to mitigate
spatial ambiguity.
To address this limitation, we propose a Dynamic
Spatial-aware Knowledge Distillation (D-SKD) framework for
lightweight dual-channel SE. The key idea is to use the spa-
tially invariant single-channel model as the teacher to provide
a stable reference when spatial cues are unreliable, while al-
lowing the dual-channel student model to retain the advantage
of spatial information when it is informative. Specifically, D-
SKD introduces a dynamic arbitration mechanism into the dis-
tillation process, enabling the student to adaptively regulate its
reliance on spatial cues during training without explicit source-
angle supervision. During inference, no additional parameters
or auxiliary information are required. We apply the framework
to the dual-channel variant of GTCRN [17] (DC-GTCRN), an
effective end-to-end lightweight SE model. Experiments show
that D-SKD consistently improves DC-GTCRN performance
in closely-spaced source scenarios (0 ◦–15◦) while largely pre-
serving performance in other conditions. Additional experi-
ments on other state-of-the-art (SOTA) lightweight models fur-
ther demonstrate the generalizability of D-SKD.

3. Proposed method
As illustrated in Figure 1, the proposed D-SKD framework
builds upon the GTCRN baseline to improve dual-channel per-
formance in closely-spaced source scenarios without compro-
mising overall effectiveness. Section 3.1 details the GTCRN
architecture and its input feature representations adopted in dif-
ferent conditions. Section 3.2 introduces D-SKD in detail, in-
cluding the Dynamic Arbitrator Module (DAM), loss function,
and training strategy. Specifically, DAM evaluates sample dif-
ficulty on an instance-wise basis during training to generate the
corresponding distillation weights. In addition to the primary
task loss, a dynamic distillation loss is introduced to guide the
network to adaptively regulate its reliance on spatial informa-
tion. Notably, D-SKD operates exclusively during training and
requires no additional computational overhead or model param-
eters during inference.
3.1. Model overview
Based on the U-Net architecture, GTCRN processes time-
frequency signals through a sequence of modules: Band Merg-
ing (BM), Subband Feature Extraction (SFE), encoder, G-
DPRNN bottleneck, decoder, and Band Splitting (BS). To re-
duce redundancy, BM compresses high-frequency subbands
into equivalent rectangular bandwidth (ERB) subbands, which
are reconstructed in BS. SFE integrates adjacent subbands into
the channel dimension for efficient convolutional processing.
The encoder maps these features to high-dimensional embed-
dings, which are passed to the G-DPRNN for time-frequency
modeling, and finally decoded to estimate the complex ratio
mask (CRM) [19] for waveform reconstruction via iSTFT.
For the single-channel version (SC-GTCRN), the magni-
tude spectrum of the first microphone is selected as the input,
containing the principal spectral patterns. For the dual-channel
model (DC-GTCRN), in addition to the magnitude spectrum of
the first microphone, the sine and cosine of inter-channel phase
difference (sin/cos IPD) are incorporated. These two types of
features are concatenated along the channel dimension to serve
as the final input. Such a sin/cos IPD structure provides robust
spatial cues for separating the target speech from interference,
while avoiding the numerical discontinuity problem caused by
high-frequency phase wrapping [20, 21].
3.2. D-SKD training framework
Spatial ambiguity is a persistent challenge in multi-channel
speech separation and enhancement [22, 23]. As the target
speaker and the interferer spatially approach each other, the
spatial diversity relied upon by multi-channel models progres-
sively diminishes, leading to continuous performance degrada-
tion. Since spatial cues typically offer higher discriminability
than spectral features in most scenarios, DNNs are prone to de-
veloping an over-reliance on spatial information, resulting in in-
sufficient emphasis on spectral features. As a consequence, the
model attempts to rely on ambiguous or invalid spatial informa-
tion for separation, ultimately failing in closely-spaced source
scenarios. Under these extreme conditions, the performance
of the single-channel model—which relies solely on spectral
information—can be regarded as a reliable reference. Inspired
by knowledge distillation [24], we introduce a distillation loss
to guide the dual-channel student in learning spatially invariant
patterns from the single-channel teacher, thereby encouraging
stronger reliance on spectral features in closely-spaced source
scenarios.

3.2.3. Training and inference
We employ a pre-trained SC-GTCRN as the teacher and a DC-
GTCRN with the same backbone configuration as the student.
During training, independent forward propagations yield their
respective losses (Eq. 3, Eq. 4). The DAM evaluates their rel-
ative performance to generate the dynamic distillation weight
λ(i)
dyn. Subsequently, the total loss (Eq. 10) updates only the stu-
dent, keeping the teacher frozen. During inference, the teacher
and the DAM are discarded, leaving only the dual-channel stu-
dent model.
4. Experiments and results
4.1. Dataset
We simulate a 16 kHz dual-channel speech enhancement dataset
using speech and noise from the DNS-3 corpus [25]. The train-
ing set comprises 50,000 noisy-clean pairs, each with a duration
of 10 s. RIRs are simulated using the image method [26] for a
two-microphone array with a spacing of 4 cm. Room dimen-
sions (L,W∈[3,10]m,H∈[2.5,3]m) and RT60 (0.1–0.4 s)
are randomly sampled. Sources are placed coplanar with the ar-
ray at randomly selected distances of 0.5, 1, 2, or 3 m, maintain-
ing≥0.5 m wall clearance and a0 ◦–180◦ angular separation.
Training SNR ranges from−5 to 5 dB. A500-sample validation
set follows identical settings. Three test sets are constructed at
SNR = 5 dB for the subsequent experiments:
•Segmented-angle:Five DOA difference intervals (0 ◦–15◦,
15◦–30◦,30 ◦–60◦,60 ◦–90◦,90 ◦–180◦) with500samples
uniformly distributed within each interval at a 1 m distance.
•Average-angle:1000samples with uniformly distributed
DOA differences (0◦–180◦) at a 1 m distance.
•Angular-scan:In a fixed acoustic environment selected from
the average-angle test set, noise is fixed at (0◦, 1 m) while the
target speaker is moved from5 ◦ to175 ◦ in10 ◦ increments,
yielding 200 samples per position.
4.2. Implementation details
Dual-channel mixtures are processed via STFT (32 ms Hann
window, 16 ms shift) to extract the magnitude spectrum from
the first channel, alongside the sin/cos IPD, as input fea-
tures. The default hidden size (h.size) for convolutional and
G-DPRNN layers is 16, with 32 and 64 available for larger vari-
ants.
Both the teacher and student models are trained using the
Adam optimizer [27]. The learning rate is initialized to0.001,
featuring a linear warmup over the first20epochs followed by
a cosine annealing decay schedule. Training proceeds for200
epochs with a batch size of8, randomly sampling 10,000 items
per epoch. The teacher is optimized via the hybrid loss (Eq. 9),
while the student employs the D-SKD loss (Eq. 10), whereµ=
1andγis adjusted according to the scale of the network.

To assess the enhancement performance, we use two objective

learns more comprehensive spectral and spatial patterns through
D-SKD and effectively balances the reliance on them, thereby
further validating the immense potential of the proposed frame-
work in mitigating the spatial ambiguity problem.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose the D-SKD framework to address
the challenge of spatial ambiguity in lightweight dual-channel
speech enhancement. By introducing a dynamic arbitration
mechanism into the distillation process with a spatially invari-
ant single-channel teacher, D-SKD enables the dual-channel
model to adaptively regulate reliance on spatial cues without
explicit angular supervision. Experiments show that D-SKD ef-
fectively enhances the performance of DC-GTCRN and other
SOTA lightweight models in closely-spaced source scenarios
while largely preserving performance in other conditions. No-
tably, our framework requires no additional inference overhead
or auxiliary inputs, making it suitable for lightweight deploy-
ment on edge devices.
