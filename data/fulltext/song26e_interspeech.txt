Abstract
Multimodal Dialogue Emotion Recognition (MDER) plays a
role in human–computer interaction, yet existing approaches of-
ten rely on global context aggregation that violates speaker pri-
vacy and introduces temporal information leakage. We propose
Speaker-Filtered Heterogeneous Graph Network (SF-HGN), a
privacy-aware framework for multimodal emotion recognition
in conversations. SF-HGN restricts graph propagation to single-
speaker induced subgraphs to maintain speaker-level privacy,
while preserving contextual information through a Context-
Aware Graph Injection (CAGI) module. CAGI extracts cross-
speaker emotional cues using tri-modal attention within a strict
causal window, preventing future information leakage and re-
ducing noisy inter-modal interactions. By combining causal
masking, speaker filtering, and heterogeneous graph modeling,
SF-HGN enables robust emotion reasoning without exposing
non-target speaker features. Experiments on the IEMOCAP and
MELD datasets show its effectiveness.

Recognition in Conversation, Heterogeneous Graph Neural
Networks, Speaker Filtering, Causal Graph Modeling
1. Introduction
With the rapid growth of intelligent customer service, human-
computer interaction, and social media, Multimodal Dialogue
Emotion Recognition (MDER) has emerged as an important
task in Natural Language Processing. In conversations, emo-
tions are often expressed through text, voice, and facial expres-
sions. However, this task still faces three main challenges.
First, privacy risk. Real conversations may contain private
information in audio, video, and text, and global graph prop-
agation may mix non-target speaker representations. Second,
complex relations across modalities. Because modalities may
be noisy or not aligned, simple fusion cannot capture deep re-
lations. Third, conversational context. Emotions often spread
in conversations, and a speaker’s emotion can be affected by
context and other speakers.
To solve these problems, we propose the Speaker-Filtered
Heterogeneous Graph Network (SF-HGN). This model com-
bines single-speaker node filtering with a causal graph structure.
It keeps strong emotion recognition ability while also building a
structural privacy boundary for multimodal data, which reduces
direct propagation of features from non-target speakers.

2. Related Work
2.1. Emotion Modeling in Conversation
Early MDER relied on sequence-based models. DS-LSTM [1]
used Dual-Sequence LSTM for spatio-temporal features, while
DialogueRNN [2] tracked speaker/context states via recurrent
structures. However, these lack topological representation for
complex, non-linear dependencies.
Consequently, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have be-
come widely adopted in MDER [3]. DialogueGCN [4] first
modeled dialogues as directed graphs, followed by RGAT [5]
and DAG-ERC [6], which introduced relational attention and
directed acyclic structures, respectively. Despite their accuracy,
most rely on static graphs and global aggregation, causing com-
putational redundancy and ignoring temporal causal constraints
in real-time scenarios.
2.2. Multimodal Fusion Strategies
Fusion remains central to MDER [7]. Beyond traditional early
or late fusion [8, 9], graph-based interaction has emerged.
DMINet [10] utilized cross-modal graphs and attention-
integrated GCNs for deep modality interaction. Nevertheless,
MM-DFN [11] and MGraphBN [12] highlight that indiscrimi-
nate propagation often triggers negative transfer and semantic
dominance in high-noise environments.

Privacy protection in MDER remains under-explored. Dia-

In contrast, our SF-HGN proposes a Single-Speaker Node
Filtering mechanism with a Causal Mask. By physically isolat-
ing non-target nodes after semantic injection, it helps preserve
privacy boundaries and ensures causal consistency, preventing
future information leakage.
3. Methods
We propose SF-HGN, a two-stage framework for privacy-aware
MDER. CAGI summarizes cross-speaker cues within a causal
window and writes the context only into the target speaker’s
features. Graph propagation is then performed exclusively on
single-speaker induced heterogeneous subgraphs, thereby es-
tablishing a structural privacy boundary (Figure 1).

4. Experiments
4.1. Benchmark Datasets
To validate the effectiveness of the proposed SF-HGN, we con-
duct extensive experiments on two widely adopted benchmarks
for MDER shown in Table 1.IEMOCAP [24]:A dyadic
dataset with 151 dialogues and six emotion categories:Happy,
Sad, Neutral, Angry, Excited,andFrustrated.MELD [25]:A
multi-party dataset fromFriends, containing 1,433 dialogues,
304 speakers, and seven emotion categories:Neutral, Surprise,
Fear, Sadness, Joy, Disgust,andAnger.
4.2. Baseline Models
To comprehensively evaluate SF-HGN, we compare it against
10 representative state-of-the-art methods.Sequence-based:
bc-LSTM[26],DialogueRNN[2],CMN[27].Graph-based:
DialogueGCN[4],RGAT[5],MMGCN[28],LR-GCN[29],
andDialogueCRN[30].Context-Augmented:CoMPM[31]
andCOGMEN[32].
4.3. Implementation Details
Four evaluation metrics are employed: Accuracy, F1-score,
Weighted Accuracy (W A), and Weighted F1-score (WF1).
Given the significant class imbalance in both datasets, we prior-
itize W A and WF1 as primary indicators. The hidden dimension
is 128,W= 12, andK= 2. We employ the Adam optimizer
with a learning rate of10−4 and a dropout rate of 0.1.

5.3. Ablation Study
We conduct an ablation study to evaluate each component of
SF-HGN. As shown in Table 4, removing any module degrades
performance. Removing the residual connection causes the
largest drop, while removing Event Injection also leads to sub-
stantial degradation, indicating that both stabilize propagation
and contribute cross-speaker cues. Speaker Filtering is vital to
prevent feature contamination between speakers. Additionally,
residual connections help stabilize training and alleviate over-
smoothing in deep graph structures.
5.4. Computational Efficiency Analysis
SF-HGN reduces complexity by using intra-speaker subgraphs
for graph convolutions. Table 5 shows that our model achieves
63.7–94.3% lower inference time than strong baselines while
maintaining high accuracy. The GPU memory usage is also
lower. These results shows SF-HGN incurs lower computa-
tional cost than the compared baselines under identical settings.

6. Conclusion
We propose the Speaker-Filtered Heterogeneous Graph Net-
work (SF-HGN) for privacy-constrained multimodal emotion
recognition in conversations. SF-HGN restricts graph propaga-
tion to single-speaker induced subgraphs, thereby structurally
isolating sensitive representations of non-target speakers. To
preserve contextual awareness, we introduce Context-Aware
Graph Injection (CAGI), which selectively injects salient cross-
speaker affective triggers within a strict causal window, prevent-
ing future-context leakage and suppressing noisy interactions.
We further perform relation-aware propagation on the single-
speaker heterogeneous graph to jointly capture intra-modal and
inter-modal dependencies, and leverage Sentiment Dynamics to
strengthen the modeling of abrupt affective shifts. Experiments
on the IEMOCAP and MELD benchmarks demonstrate that SF-
HGN achieves improved performance while maintaining lower
computational overhead under the same evaluation setting.
