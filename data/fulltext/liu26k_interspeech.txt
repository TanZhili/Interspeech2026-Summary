HWB-plus: A Lightweight Speech Bandwidth Extension Method with
Separate Modeling for Consonants and Vowels

Abstract
Speech Bandwidth Extension (BWE) aims to reconstruct the
missing high-frequency components of narrowband signals for
enhanced perceptual quality. However, existing methods often
face a trade-off between performance and resource efficiency—
a critical constraint for edge device deployment. This paper pro-
poses an improved lightweight BWE approach based on HWB-
Net, which achieves performance improvement with almost no
change in model parameters and computational load. The key
innovations are: (1) Dual-WGMM, grounded in STRAIGHT’s
periodic/aperiodic decomposition, including ConsWGMM (for
high-frequency consonants) and V owelWGMM (for mid-to-
low frequency vowels); (2) mel-scale initialization for V owel-
WGMM. Evaluated on the VCTK dataset, the proposed method
outperforms SOTA lightweight models (HWB-Net, BAE-Lite)
by significant margins across DNSMOS P.808, PESQ, and
NISQA. This work provides a practical BWE solution for real-
time communication on edge devices.

1. Introduction
In modern communication and voice interaction, demand for
high-quality speech under low-bandwidth constraints is grow-
ing. Speech Bandwidth Extension (BWE)—reconstructing
missing high-frequency (HF) components from narrowband
signals to boost perceptual quality—is critical for edge de-
vices (telecommunications, smart speakers), wherehigh-
performance + lightweightsolutions address narrowband
speech’s poor clarity [1].
Recent lightweight neural networks advance efficient BWE,
with HWB-Net standing out. Built on BAE-Net [2], HWB-
Net uses Half-Wave Rectification (HWR) [3] to enrich input
HF cues and replaces complex decoders with a Weighted Gaus-
sian Mixture Model (WGMM), achieving 194K parameters and
12M MACs [4]. Despite its merits, HF reconstruction in HWB-
Net still faces two practical limitations.
(1) Structural mismatch in consonant HF modeling.
Consonants are critical for human auditory perception and
recognition [5, 6, 7, 8, 9] and can also affect ASR accuracy [10].
In BWE, narrowband speech (typically limited to 2–3 kHz)
loses precisely the HF components where fricative consonants
carry substantial energy (often≥2 kHz). STRAIGHT’s per-
ceptually isomorphic decomposition [11, 12] identifies the root
cause: speech decomposes into aperiodic componentP(t,f)
(F0 harmonics, dominating vowels) and anaperiodic compo-
nentA(t,f)(spectrally flat noise, dominating consonants).
**indicates the corresponding author.
SinceP(t,f)≈0in consonant frames, HWR—a harmonic-
generation operator—produces no coherent HF output and may
introduce intermodulation artifacts, revealing astructural mis-
matchbetween HWB-Net’s input enrichment and consonant re-
construction.
(2) Perceptually grounded parameterization of spectral
modeling.HWB-Net’s WGMM parameter initialization is
largely empirical and not explicitly tied to perceptual frequency
resolution. Human auditory sensitivity is well modeled by the
Mel scale [13], and perceptually meaningful initialization can
improve training efficiency and focus modeling capacity on rel-
evant HF regions.
Grounded in STRAIGHT’s periodic/aperiodic orthogonal-
ity [11, 12], we proposeHWB-pluswith two key refine-
ments. First, we introduceDualWGMM:VowelWGMMtargets
P(t,f)via HWR-enriched features, whileConsWGMMtar-
getsA(t,f)—whose spectrally flat nature is directly matched
by a white-noise base signal—bypassing HWR entirely. This
grounding in STRAIGHT’s orthogonal decomposition yields
implicit specialization: in consonant-dominant frames where
P(t,f)≈0, the HWR-enriched base signal carries neg-
ligible harmonic energy, causing V owelWGMM to passively
contribute near-zero output without explicit gating; in vowel-
dominant frames, a learned fusion weight actively suppresses
ConsWGMM’s noise-like contribution. This asymmetric, self-
organizing specialization emerges directly from the energy ex-
clusivity of STRAIGHT’s periodic and aperiodic components,
requiring no explicit supervision. Second, we adopt mel-scale-
guided initialization for V owelWGMM, setting Gaussian means
and bandwidths to align with mel-filter center frequencies, with
predicted variance offsets to dynamically approximate triangu-
lar mel-filter shapes.
Our work explicitly targetsdeployable lightweightmodels
for edge deployment, and thus comparisons focus on meth-
ods with similar computational budgets. With essentially un-
changed complexity (12.39M MACs/s), HWB-plus improves
perceptual quality over lightweight baselines through Dual-
WGMM for STRAIGHT-grounded periodic/aperiodic decou-
pling and mel-scale-guided V owelWGMM initialization.

3. Dataset & Implementation
3.1. Dataset Configuration
To evaluate high-frequency consonant reconstruction, we ex-
tend HWB-Net’s setup with key modifications using the VCTK
corpus [17], which contains 48 kHz clean speech from 110
speakers. We adopt a speaker-independent partitioning strategy:
the training set includes 90 speakers to ensure data diversity for
optimization, the validation (dev) set uses 5 male and 5 female
speakers, and the test set consists of another 5 male and 5 fe-
male speakers (non-overlapping with training/validation) for fi-
nal evaluation. Low-resolution (LR) signals are generated via a
Butterworth low-pass filter with a cutoff frequency of 2 to 3 kHz
and an order of 5 to 10, consistent with HWB-Net. A key mod-
ification is adjusting the high-resolution (HR) sampling rate to
22050 Hz (compared to 16 kHz in HWB-Net) to better match
the frequency range of consonants.
3.2. Signal Preprocessing
The Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) parameters are op-
timized for 22050 Hz sampling rate and 513-dimensional fea-
tures: we use a Hann window to reduce spectral leakage, with a
frame length of 1024 samples, a hop length of 256 samples, and
an NFFT of 1024.
3.3. Model Architecture Parameters
The model framework follows HWB-Net with a key modifica-
tion: replacing a single WGMM with DualWGMM modules.
To maintain the same parameter count and computational com-
plexity as the original, each WGMM contains N=32 Gaussian
components (vs. N=64 in HWB-Net). The White noise input to
the ConsWGMM module has a mean of−1. For comparabil-
ity, the encoder structure matches HWB-Net: 4 Conv1D layers
(channel configurations: 128→128, 128→64, 64→64; kernel
size=3) and 2 Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) layers (input and
hidden size=64) in Grouped GRUs for temporal dependency
modeling.
3.4. Training Details
The training setup uses the Adam optimizer [18] with an initial
learning rate of 5e-4, a batch size of 32, and a total of 40 train-
ing epochs. Select the model with the lowestL 1 loss on the
validation set for testing.
3.5. Evaluation Metrics
Performance is assessed across three core metric categories:
spectral fidelity (Log-Spectral Distance, LSD), perceptual qual-
ity (DNSMOS P.808[19], PESQ[20], VISQOL[21], NISQA[22,
23]), and deployment efficiency (parameter count, Para.;
multiply-accumulate operations per second, MACs).

4. Results & Analysis
4.1. Performance Comparison with Deployable
Lightweight Models
This section evaluates the proposed method (denoted as
HWB-plus) against deployable lightweight bandwidth extension
(BWE) models. Candidate models include BAE-Lite, the base-
line HWB-Net (our primary reference, sharing a lightweight ar-
chitecture), andsinc linear(a sinc-based upsampling method
included for contextual comparison but not core BWE model
benchmarking). All selected BWE models (BAE-Lite, HWB-
Net,HWB-plus) target edge deployment with small parameter
counts and low computational complexity.
To ensure a fair comparison, all baseline models were re-
trained from scratch using the same dataset partition and ex-
perimental configurations (e.g., sampling rate) as the proposed
method.
Detailed results are summarized in Table 1. We additionally
report the full BAE model as a high-capacity reference; how-
ever, due to its substantially larger computational cost (2827K
parameters, 246M MACs/s), it isnotincluded in the fair com-
parison set for edge-deployable lightweight BWE models.
Across key perceptual quality metrics,HWB-plusoutper-
forms lightweight BWE models. It achieves the highest score
in DNSMOS P.808 (an ITU-T P.808-compliant metric for hu-
man auditory perception) and maintains superiority in PESQ,
VISQOL, and NISQA—surpassing both BAE-Lite and the
baseline HWB-Net. These comprehensive auditory-quality im-
provements directly validate the effectiveness of the proposed
design.
Notably, whileHWB-plusexhibits a slightly higher LSD
than BAE-Lite (which has the lowest LSD), this trade-off
stems from its reduced model complexity:HWB-plusmatches
HWB-Net’s lightweight footprint, whereas BAE-Lite requires
far more parameters and computations. Crucially, perceptual
metrics—directly tied to human auditory experience—confirm
HWB-plusdelivers superior listening quality despite the minor
LSD difference, aligning with BWE’s practical goal of edge-
device compatibility (low parameters, low latency) and user-
centric clarity.

4.2. Ablation Study of Key Design Components
To validate the contribution of each proposed modification, an
ablation study takes HWB-plus as the baseline and sequentially
removes three of its key components (DualWGMM, V owel-
WGMM mel-scale initialization, log-domain processing), with
results presented in Table 2.
All components enhance performance, with core elements
DualWGMM and mel-scale initialization being especially crit-
ical: Removing DualWGMM maintains LSD but sharply re-
duces perceptual quality (DNSMOS P.808:3.55→3.36;
PESQ:3.83→3.64), while removing mel-scale initializa-
tion worsens LSD (0.94→1.07) and lowers DNSMOS P.808
(3.55→3.49) and PESQ (3.83→3.62). The critical log-
domain processing also aids training stability and HF detail
preservation (Fig. 3), supporting the framework’s rationale.
5. Conclusion
This paper proposesHWB-plus, an edge-oriented lightweight
speech bandwidth extension (BWE) method upgraded from
HWB-Net—retaining its low overhead (194K parameters,
12.39M MACs/s) with key innovations: DualWGMM (decou-
pling consonant/vowel modeling) and V owelWGMM mel-scale
initialization, and log-magnitude spectral processing for numer-
ical stability. Experiments showHWB-plusoutperforms HWB-
Net and BAE-Lite in perceptual metrics: DNSMOS P.808 (3.55
vs. 3.31/3.25) and PESQ (3.83 vs. 3.35/3.37). Its lightweight
footprint confirms suitability for resource-constrained edge sce-
narios.
