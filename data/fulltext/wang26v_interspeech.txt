Inference Strategy for Speech Synthesis with Continuous Block Flow Matching

Abstract
Block Flow Matching combines block-level autoregression with
intra-block parallelism for efficient and high-quality Text-to-
Speech (TTS). However, using a fixed block size during in-
ference ignores the non-uniform modeling difficulty of speech,
which can cause unstable velocity estimation in challeng-
ing regions. This motivates the need for a reliability metric
that can guide dynamic adjustment of block size. We pro-
pose Trajectory Confidence (TC), a geometric measure de-
rived from trajectory straightness during ODE integration, and
introduce TC-Guided Dynamic Block Inference (TC-DBI), a
plug-and-play strategy that truncates and regenerates unreli-
able regions to adapt block size without architectural changes.
Experiments show that TC correlates strongly with gener-
ation errors, and TC-DBI improves robustness and percep-
tual quality while maintaining comparable efficiency. Audio
samples are available athttps://thuhcsi.github.io/
interspeech2026-TC-DBI/.

1. Introduction
Modern Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems primarily adopt two
paradigms: Autoregressive (AR) and Non-Autoregressive
(NAR) models. AR models [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7] generate speech
sequentially, enabling strong temporal and prosodic depen-
dency modeling but incurring high latency and exposure bias
[8, 9, 10]. In contrast, NAR models [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17]
relax strict sequential conditioning to achieve parallel gener-
ation and improved efficiency. However, they often strug-
gle to capture fine-grained temporal dynamics and expressive
prosody, partly because parallel generation lacks the instanta-
neous, incremental context available in AR decoding.
To balance expressiveness and efficiency, block-wise gener-
ative modeling [18, 19] has recently emerged as an intermediate
paradigm. By partitioning sequences into blocks, these methods
maintain long-range contextual coherence across blocks while
preserving parallel generation within each block. Among block-
wise generative frameworks, continuous Block Flow Matching
has recently shown strong performance in speech synthesis due
to its continuous latent modeling and stable probability flow
formulation. Representative systems such as ARDiT [20] and
DragonFM [21] adopt Block Flow Matching for high-fidelity
generation. Similar designs have also been explored in music
generation (e.g., Diffrhythm2 [22]).
Despite the effectiveness of block-wise modeling, applying
it to speech synthesis is inherently constrained by the use of a

fixed block size, which poorly matches the intrinsic characteris-
tics of speech. Speech exhibits highly non-uniform information
density. Rapid phonetic transitions and prosodically complex
regions demand strong contextual conditioning, whereas steady
segments can be generated reliably with limited conditioning.
As a result, a fixed block size creates an unfavorable trade-off.
Small block size improves conditioning but greatly reduces par-
allelism and efficiency. While large block size boosts efficiency,
it may provide insufficient context for locally difficult regions,
which can degrade quality and cause error accumulation. To ad-
dress this issue, we need a computationally lightweight metric
that can quickly assess the reliability of the generated segments
and dynamically adjust the block size accordingly.
Prior work [23] leverages token-level probabilities in dis-
crete generative models (e.g., cross-entropy–based confidence)
to dynamically control block-wise inference. However, such
strategies are difficult to transfer to continuous block-wise
models, where ODE-based sampling does not expose an ex-
plicit probabilistic confidence signal. Meanwhile, recent works
[24, 25] on continuous diffusion and flow-based models ex-
plore leveraging sampling-trajectory geometry in probability
flow ODEs to improve scheduling strategies. This prompts a
natural question: can such trajectory characteristics—readily
available during inference—also serve as a training-free signal
of local generation reliability?
In this work, we propose Trajectory Confidence (TC), a
training-free and computationally lightweight reliability sig-
nal. TC directly derives from the sampling trajectory of the
probability-flow ODE during inference. It measures the local
straightness of the trajectory, and serves as an on-the-fly indica-
tor of generation stability. Building on TC, we introduce Trajec-
tory Confidence–Guided Dynamic Block Inference (TC-DBI),
a plug-and-play decoding strategy for continuous Block Flow
Matching (and potentially other continuous block-wise ODE
samplers). TC-DBI detects unreliable suffix regions within a
block and selectively refines them by truncation and regener-
ation with refreshed contextual conditioning, enabling adap-
tive block granularity without any model retraining or architec-
tural changes. We apply TC-DBI to our reproduced continuous
Block Flow Matching TTS system and demonstrate consistent
improvements in robustness and speech quality with minimal
inference overhead.
(1) We propose TC, a training-free and low-cost reliability met-
ric and provide quantitative evidence that TC correlates with
generation errors.
(2) We introduce TC-DBI, a plug-and-play dynamic block in-
ference strategy that adaptively identifies unreliable suffixes
and refines them via truncation and regeneration to obtain
stronger contextual conditioning in challenging regions.

2. Methodology
2.1. Model Architecture
Fig. 1(a) shows our reproduced Block Flow Matching architec-
ture, which consists of two components:
Speech variational autoencoder (V AE): We employ a pre-
trained V AE to compress high-dimensional waveforms into
continuous latent variablesZ. It operates as a frozen acous-
tic codec, ensuring high-fidelity reconstruction while reducing
the sequence length for efficient generation.
Diffusion Transformer (DiT): The core generator is a DiT
designed for block-wise autoregressive modeling. Conditioned
on historical blocks, textT, and reference latentL ref , the DiT
predicts the flow trajectories for the current block, which are
subsequently decoded by the V AE.

2.3. Trajectory Confidence: Definition & Geometric Intu-
ition
To overcome the limitations of fixed block size, we need a
metric that assesses generation reliability to dynamically adjust
the block size. Under ideal optimal transport interpolation, the
probability flow follows a straight path from noise to data; de-
viations from this straightness can indicate local modeling dif-
ficulty and instability in the estimated velocity field. This in-
stability can reflect the model’s predictive confidence. As il-
lustrated in Fig. 2, when the model is certain about the target
output, the trajectory remains approximately straight across in-
tegration steps. If the model is uncertain about the target, its ve-
locity predictions may become inconsistent across integration
steps and lead to curved trajectories and unreliable results.
Motivated by this observation, we quantify Trajectory
Confidence using the straightness of the integration path.

2.4. Trajectory Confidence-Guided Dynamic Block Infer-
ence (TC-DBI)
Building upon the proposed Trajectory Confidence metric, we
propose TC-DBI that adaptively adjusts the effective generation
length according to local modeling difficulty.

This Trajectory Confidence guided mechanism dynami-
cally modulates the effective block size during inference. In re-
gions where the flow trajectory remains approximately straight,
large parallel steps are preserved for efficiency. In contrast,
when trajectory deviations indicate local uncertainty, the block
size is automatically reduced, preventing unreliable predictions
from propagating across subsequent blocks.
3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
Our reproduced Block Flow Matching (BFM) is built upon a
Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with 22 layers, hidden dimension
1024, and 16 attention heads. Block-wise autoregressive gener-
ation is performed with a fixed block size of 32 during training.
For acoustic representation, we adopt the open-source V oxCPM
[26] V AE, which compresses 44.1 kHz waveforms into 25 Hz
continuous latent sequences, serving as a frozen neural codec.
Our model is trained on approximately 100k hours of speech
data from the Chinese and English subsets of Emilia. Training is
conducted on 16 NVIDIA A100 (80G) GPUs for approximately
two weeks. During inference, we perform ODE integration with
32 steps. For TC-DBI, the trajectory confidence threshold is
fixed toτ= 0.75to determine the truncation boundary.
3.2. Experiment Results
3.2.1. Main Results
Table 1 reports the main results on the Seed-eval benchmark.
Since no publicly available BFM model matches our training
scale, we reproduced a Block Flow Matching model (BFM) as
a baseline. For a comprehensive comparison, we also include
CosyV oice2, MaskGCT, and F5-TTS, covering AR and NAR
paradigms.
We evaluate performance using three metrics. Word Error
Rate (WER,↓) measures intelligibility via Whisper-large-v3 for
English and Paraformer-zh for Mandarin and reflects genera-
tion robustness. Speaker Similarity (SIM,↑) calculates the co-
sine similarity of speaker embeddings extracted by a fine-tuned
WavLM-large model, which quantifies speaker consistency be-
tween synthesized and reference audio. Naturalness MOS (N-
MOS,↑) evaluates perceptual quality, rated by 15 experienced
listeners on 10 utterances per language.
As shown in Table 1, BFM with TC-DBI demonstrates the
most balanced performance across both test sets. It attains
the lowest WER on seed-test-en (1.798%) and the second-best
WER on seed-test-zh (1.568%), close to CosyV oice2 (1.504%),
indicating high content fidelity while maintaining strong per-
ceptual quality. Notably, our method delivers the highest
N-MOS on seed-test-zh/en (3.809/3.973), surpassing the best
baselines by consistent margin, demonstrating improved real-
ism and prosody that generalizes across languages. Speaker
similarity is comparable to the strongest systems, suggesting
that the improvements are not driven by similarity optimization
but by enhanced perceptual quality. Finally, removing TC-DBI
degrades WER (zh: 1.568%→1.628%; en: 1.798%→1.921%)
and N-MOS (zh: 3.809→3.673; en: 3.973→3.727) with min-
imal SIM change, confirming the effectiveness of TC-DBI in
improving robustness and perceptual quality.

3.2.3. Ablation Study: Impact of Confidence Thresholdτ
To further examine how trajectory confidence translates into in-
ference gains, we evaluate TC-DBI under different thresholdsτ
(Table 2). Across all tested thresholds, TC-DBI consistently im-
proves WER over the Fixed-Block baseline of our reproduced
BFM. Atτ= 0.65, only very low-confidence segments are
truncated, yielding limited WER reduction due to their low fre-
quency. Increasingτto 0.75 allows TC-DBI to cover a broader
set of moderately low-confidence segments, resulting in the best
WER on both Seed-ZH (1.568%) and Seed-EN (1.798%) while
incurring only a modest 1.08×relative RTF. Further increas-
ingτto 0.80 triggers more aggressive truncation, which raises
RTF to 1.63×with diminishing returns. These results demon-
strate that trajectory confidence effectively identifies high-risk
regions, and that balancing error density and occurrence fre-
quency is crucial for optimal performance.

4. Conclusion
We proposed Trajectory Confidence (TC) as a training-free and
computationally lightweight reliability metric for continuous
Block Flow Matching in text-to-speech synthesis. Building on
TC, we introduce TC-DBI, a plug-and-play decoding strategy
that adaptively adjusts block-wise generation by truncating low-
confidence regions and regenerating uncertain segments, re-
quiring no architectural changes or retraining. Experiments on
bilingual Seed-eval benchmarks demonstrate that TC-DBI ef-
fectively identifies high-risk regions, improves robustness, and
enhances perceptual quality while maintaining efficiency and
speaker similarity.
