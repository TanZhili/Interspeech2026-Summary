Abstract
Automatic speaker verification (ASV) systems are vulnerable to
synthetic speech attacks. However, existing feature-level fusion
for spoofing-robust ASV (SASV) generally relies on unidirec-
tional CM-to-ASV interactions, ignoring speaker context. We
propose BiSASV , a bidirectional modulation spoofing-robust
automatic speaker verification system that establishes a recip-
rocal information flow between the ASV and countermeasure
(CM) subsystems. A bidirectional modulation network is intro-
duced to inject global speaker context into the CM, while the en-
hanced CM features inversely gate the ASV embeddings. Fur-
thermore, a dual-granularity fusion strategy is explored to cap-
ture both fine-grained and global discriminative cues. Experi-
mental results show that the proposed system yields an SASV
equal error rate (SASV-EER) of 0.73% and a minimum agnostic
detection cost function (min a-DCF) of 0.0153 on the ASVspoof
2019 LA set, significantly outperforming unidirectional base-
line methods.
Index Terms: speaker verification, anti-spoofing, spoofing-
robust speaker verification
1. Introduction
Automatic Speaker Verification (ASV) systems play a crucial
role in biometric authentication. However, existing ASV sys-
tems exhibit significant vulnerability to synthetic speech at-
tacks, such as text-to-speech (TTS) and voice conversion (VC).
To construct robust systems capable of effectively distinguish-
ing target speakers from zero-effort impostors while defend-
ing against diverse synthetic attacks, Spoofing-Robust ASV
(SASV) has emerged as a core focus in the current voice se-
curity domain.
Current SASV research primarily focuses on three archi-
tectural paradigms: end-to-end networks, score fusion, and em-
bedding fusion. End-to-end networks attempt to jointly opti-
mize both tasks [1–3], but achieving an optimal balance is dif-
ficult due to the inherent conflict in feature requirements be-
tween ASV (which suppresses environmental artifacts) and CM
(which captures them). Furthermore, highly coupled networks
compromise decision interpretability. Although score fusion
is widely adopted in past challenges [4, 5] and related stud-
ies [6–10], it only performs shallow interaction at the decision
level, severing the potential complementarity between ASV and
CM in the deep feature space. In contrast, feature-level fusion
[10–13] integrates high-dimensional representations from inde-
pendent pre-trained expert models (e.g., ECAPA-TDNN [14]
and AASIST [15]). This paradigm inherits the discriminative

power of single-task models and achieves deep cross-task fine-
grained interaction, while its modular architecture offers plug-
and-play flexibility.
However, existing feature-level interaction mechanisms are
largely restricted to unidirectional information flow, relying on
CM scores as attention weights to unidirectionally gate ASV
features. This asymmetrical design ignores the fact that spoof-
ing artifacts are often coupled with specific speaker acoustic
characteristics. Existing studies indicate that the global identity
context extracted by the ASV branch can assist the anti-spoofing
model in better calibrating its decision boundary [16].
Motivated by this, this paper proposes a novel Bidirectional
Modulation Spoofing-Robust ASV (BiSASV) system based on
pre-trained ASV and CM models, shifting from existing unidi-
rectional architectures. The primary contributions of this work
are as follows: (1) We propose a bidirectional feature modula-
tion mechanism that injects ASV global features into the CM
branch via FiLM for conditioned enhancement, and then lever-
ages the enhanced CM features to perform channel-wise gating
on ASV representations, breaking the traditional unidirectional
limitation; (2) We design a dual-granularity fusion strategy in-
corporating a coarse-grained fusion module with global cosine
similarity to mitigate the overfitting risk of fine-grained mod-
ulation and enhance defensive stability; (3) On the ASVspoof
2019 LA dataset, the proposed system achieves 0.73% SASV-
EER and 0.0153 min a-DCF, comprehensively outperforming
existing fusion baselines.
2. Proposed System
In this section, we introduce the architecture of the proposed
BiSASV system for solving the SASV task. The system es-
tablishes a closed-loop interaction between ASV and CM via
a bidirectional feature modulation mechanism, and designs a
dual-granularity fusion strategy conditioned on global features
to enhance decision robustness.

2.2. Bidirectional Feature Modulation
Similar to the baseline system, BiSASV employs a parallel
network architecture to process ASV and CM embeddings.
However, to overcome the limitations of unidirectional gating,
this paper further introduces bidirectional feature modulation
and dual-granularity fusion logic at the foundational architec-
ture level, achieving decision robustness under a joint training
paradigm without the need for alternating training.
In traditional SASV architectures, the feature representa-
tion of the CM module lacks awareness of the target speaker’s
identity. However, existing studies indicate that spoofing arti-
facts in synthetic speech are influenced to some extent by target
speaker attributes [16]. Inspired by this, the proposed struc-
ture allows information to be passed bidirectionally between
the ASV and CM modules. The interaction process consists of
two steps: the global statistical attributes extracted by the ASV
module first regulate the feature distribution computation of the
CM module. After adjusting the feature representation, the CM
features are then used to execute dimension-wise gating on the
ASV representations.
2.2.1. ASV-Conditioned CM Feature Modulation
The Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) mechanism [17]
is adopted to explicitly inject ASV global statistics into the CM
branch for speaker-conditioning, enabling CM features to adap-
tively capture the acoustic distribution of the current speaker.
An ASV condition vectorz stat is constructed by concatenating
the meanµand standard deviationσof the enrollment and test
ASV embeddings along with their cosine similarityscos, explic-
itly characterizing the statistical properties and identity match-
ing degree of the current input:
zstat = [µ enr,σ enr,µ tst,σ tst,s cos]∈R 5 (2)
The statisticsz stat is mapped by a two-layer MLP into a scaling
factorγand a shifting parameterβ, performing a channel-wise
affine transformation on the CM featureh cm:

The CM featuref cm, derived from ˜hcm via subsequent network

sigmoid function anddis the hidden dimension. The gating
vectorgthen acts on the processed ASV representations via

each channel for fine-grained feature recalibration.

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Dataset and Evaluation Metrics
This study is based on the ASVspoof 2019 Logical Access (LA)
dataset [23] to ensure a fair comparison with the baseline [13]
and prior works. The primary evaluation metric is SASV-EER
[4], complemented by SV-EER and SPF-EER to diagnose sub-
task performance respectively. Additionally, min a-DCF [24]
is introduced to address the limitation of EER in reflecting the
cost differences of various error types.
3.2. Model Architecture and Implementation Details
The front-end feature extraction is consistent with ATMM-
SAGA [13]: pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN [14] and AASIST [15]
are utilized to extract 192-dimensional ASV features and 160-
dimensional CM features, respectively.
The back-end fusion network is implemented using the Py-
Torch framework. The model is trained for 20 epochs with a
batch size of 1024. The Adam optimizer is employed with an
initial learning rate of3×10 −4 and a weight decay of10 −3,
coupled with a scheduling strategy of linear warmup for 10
epochs followed by inverse time decay. To mitigate the data
imbalance problem between bona fide and spoof speech sam-
ples in the ASVspoof 2019 LA dataset, a weighted binary cross-
entropy loss is adopted during training (the bona fide and spoof
weights are set to 0.9 and 0.1, respectively). The final model is
selected based on the best SASV-EER on the development set.
4. Results and Discussion
In this section, we detail the performance evaluation of the pro-
posed BiSASV system. First, we compare it with various base-
line methods to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method.
Subsequently, ablation studies are conducted to explore the con-
tributions of the bidirectional modulation and dual-granularity
fusion mechanisms. Finally, we evaluate the impact of introduc-
ing global speaker information on the system’s cross-domain
generalization capability. Performance assessment relies pri-
marily on the SASV-EER and min a-DCF metrics. Confidence
intervals are computed via bootstrapping [25] using 1,000 iter-
ations and a 95% confidence level.
4.1. Comparison of Different SASV Fusion Strategies and
Baseline Systems
Table 1 presents a performance comparison between the pro-
posed system and mainstream SASV methods on the ASVspoof
2019 LA evaluation set, covering end-to-end models, various
score fusion strategies, and recent embedding fusion baselines.
Notably, except for end-to-end methods, all other systems use
pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN and AASIST as front-ends.
Results show that BiSASV achieves the best performance
across all metrics, surpassing recent score fusion methods and
demonstrating the effectiveness of deep cross-task feature inter-
action. Compared to Baseline2, which simply concatenates em-
beddings into an MLP, the proposed system reduces the SASV-
EER from 6.37% to 0.73%, indicating that naive concatena-
tion fails to exploit cross-subsystem complementarity and that a
well-designed interaction mechanism is indispensable. Further-
more, relative to ATMM-SAGA, the proposed method achieves
an approximately 66.5% relative SASV-EER reduction (from
2.18% to 0.73%), with the min a-DCF decreasing from 0.0480
to 0.0153.

4.2. Ablation Studies and Component Analysis
To further analyze the effect of FiLM modulation and speaker
conditioning, Table 2 reports per-attack SPF-EER variations af-
ter introducing FiLM, while Table 3 studies whether ASV→CM
modulation relies on correct speaker context.
To verify this, Table 3 evaluates three perturbation settings
under a fixed architecture: randomly shufflingz stat within a
batch, replacing it with a batch-level constant, and removing
conditioning. The proposed method significantly outperforms
the unconditioned baseline. Shufflingz stat degrades perfor-
mance, indicating that incorrect speaker context corrupts CM
feature distributions, while replacing it with a constant yields
results comparable to the unconditioned baseline. This shows
that performance gains depend on correct speaker context. Ad-
ditionally, removing the coarse-grained fusion module (w/o
Coarse) further raises the SASV-EER to 1.14%.
Table 2 further shows per-attack SPF-EER with and without
the coarse-grained fusion module: without it, FiLM degrades
several attacks, whereas incorporating it improves most attacks
and mitigates prior regressions, preventing overfitting to seen
attacks.
4.3. Domain Shift Analysis
Figure 2 plots the SPF-EER DET curves of AASIST and the
proposed system on the Dev and Eval sets. Although the
proposed system yields a slightly higher SPF-EER on Dev, it
achieves a markedly lower SPF-EER on Eval, indicating that
the standalone CM model overfits the seen domain. The in-
jection of global speaker context via FiLM yields more stable
decision boundaries and greater robustness to domain shift on
unseen attacks.
