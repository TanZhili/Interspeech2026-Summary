Tense modal voice, not falsetto: AnF 0-specific physiological byproduct of the
extreme high-pitch tone in Kaihui Xiang
Yi Zhang ID 1, Aini Li ID 1
1 City University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong SAR
yzhang7498-c@my.cityu.edu.hk, ainili@cityu.edu.hk
Abstract
Understanding the interplay between lexical tone and voice
quality is crucial for disentangling physiological pitch mechan-
ics from active phonological features, yet the phonatory charac-
teristics of extreme high-pitch tones remain underexplored. In
Xiang Chinese, the extreme high-pitch tone (T4) has tradition-
ally been described as falsetto, though recent limited acoustic
evidence suggests tense modal voice. To resolve this debate,
this study conducts a multidimensional acoustic analysis across
the whole tonal inventory of Kaihui Xiang. Results reveal that
T4 is produced with tense modal voice, characterized by a flat-
tened lower-frequency spectral tilt, rather than the steep tilt and
turbulent noise typical of falsetto. Furthermore, results indi-
cate this tense quality is anF0-specific physiological byproduct,
since T4 and T6 exhibit indistinguishable voice qualities where
theirF 0 levels converge.

1. Introduction
The interplay between lexical tone and voice quality remains
a central focus of phonetic research [1]. While low-pitch tones
are frequently associated with vocal fry, creaky voice or breathy
voice [2, 3], high-pitch tones are typically accompanied by ei-
ther tense modal voice or a transition into falsetto [3]. To date,
research has predominantly focused on voice qualities associ-
ated with low-pitch targets [4, 5]. Consequently, systematic
acoustic descriptions of high-pitch tones in tonal languages re-
main relatively limited. Although [6] demonstrated that high-
pitch tones in Black Miao are tenser than their lower-pitched
counterparts, systematic acoustic data on extreme high-pitch
tones are virtually non-existent. To address this gap, we in-
vestigate the exceptionally high tone in Xiang Chinese – a ma-
jor yet under-documented Sinitic branch characterized by tonal
systems with extreme high-pitch targets [7].
The relationship between lexical tone and voice quality can
be categorized as eitherF0-specific (physiologically predictable
byproducts of pitch mechanics) or tone-specific (intrinsic fea-
tures of a tonal category) [1, 6, 8]. For instance, creaky voice
in Mandarin isF 0-specific [8], as it may emerge whenever a
tone reaches a sufficiently low target. Conversely, some voice
qualities are tone-specific and independent ofF 0. For exam-
ple, [2] found that the mid-level tone in Black Miao is breathier
than other tones; because this occurs in a mid-range rather than
at pitch extremes, the quality cannot be attributed solely toF 0
effects.
In most Xiang varieties, the extreme high-pitch Tone 4 (T4)
is perceptually characterized as falsetto, suggesting a distinct
high register [9, 10, 11]. For instance, [9] identifies the high
tone in Yueyang Xiang as having falsetto phonation based on
auditory impression. However, systematic acoustic descriptions
of this phenomenon remain scarce.
Falsetto is a laryngeal register where vibration is restricted
to the thinned edges of the vocal folds [12, 13], typically result-
ing in steeper spectral tilts and higher noise levels. In contrast,
tense voice–a modal register modification–is characterized by
increased medial compression and longitudinal tension [14],
yielding flatter spectral tilts and lower noise levels. While [15]
found that T4 in Changsha Xiang exhibited significantly flatter
spectral tilts than the mid-level Tone 1 (T1), suggesting a tense
modal voice, these findings diverge from previous auditory de-
scriptions of falsetto. Given that these preliminary results were
limited to a single speaker, a larger-scale study is essential to
resolve this discrepancy.
Distinguishing these phonation types requires multidimen-
sional acoustic parameters. Formant-corrected spectral tilt (e.g.,
H1*–H2*) isolates the glottal source by accounting for vowel
resonance [16, 17], while periodicity metrics like Harmonics-
to-Noise Ratio (HNR) and Cepstral Peak Prominence (CPP)
evaluate vibration regularity. Tense voice, marked by abrupt
glottal closure, generates enhanced high-frequency energy (flat-
ter tilt) and strong periodicity (high HNR/CPP) [18]. Con-
versely, falsetto involves reduced glottal closure, producing
steeper spectral tilts and increased turbulent noise (lower
HNR/CPP) [13].
We investigate the extreme high-pitch tone (T4) in Kai-
hui Xiang, an unstudied subvariety of Changsha Xiang with
a six-tone system [19, 20]. Using multidimensional analysis,
we address two research questions: 1) RQ1: What kind of
voice quality characterizes T4? Using formant-corrected spec-
tral tilt (H1*–H2*) alongside periodicity and noise metrics (CPP,
HNR), we test whether T4 exhibits the steep spectral tilt and
turbulent noise associated with falsetto, or the flatter spectral
tilt and strong periodicity characteristic of tense modal voice.
2) RQ2: Are these phonatory adjustmentsF 0-specific or tone-
specific? We assess whether these characteristics are physiolog-
ical byproducts correlating strictly withF 0, or active, linguisti-
cally controlled features independent of pitch mechanics.
By addressing these questions, this study provides, to the
best of our knowledge, the first systematic acoustic evidence to
resolve whether the extreme high tone in Xiang Chinese is re-
alized as falsetto or tense voice. By distinguishing betweenF 0-
specific and tone-specific adjustments, the findings refine our
theoretical understanding of the laryngeal mechanisms under-
lying high-register tones in Chinese languages.

2. Methods
This study employs a controlled production experiment to in-
vestigate the voice quality of T4 in Kaihui Xiang.
2.1. Participants
Sixteen native Kaihui Xiang speakers (9 female, 7 male; age
range: 42–80,M= 60.19,SD= 12.86) were recruited to
participate in the experiment. Notably, the recruited speakers
were all over the age of 40 at the time of participation. This
older demographics reflects the local sociolinguistic landscape,
where younger generations increasingly favor Mandarin, result-
ing in the dialect being spoken primarily by older adults. All
participants were also fluent in Mandarin, reported no speech or
hearing disorders, provided informed consent, and were com-
pensated for their time.
2.2. Materials
To develop a systematic understanding of the extreme high-
pitch tone, we compiled a word list designed to capture T4 –
alongside the other five tones for comparison – across a maxi-
mally diverse set of segmental contexts. A total of 104 mono-
syllabic words in Kaihui Xiang were selected, covering a wide
range of vowel qualities (e.g., /i/, /e/, /a/, /o/, /u/) and consonant
types, including aspirated and unaspirated stops and affricates,
as well as unaspirated fricatives. All target syllables conformed
to CV or V syllable structure and were distributed across all six
tones of the language.
2.3. Procedure
Recordings were captured in a quiet environment using a Zoom
H4n Pro recorder (44.1 kHz, 24-bit). Participants were in-
structed to produce each target word twice within the carrier
sentence “NO fei kaNTARGETts@ k@ tsi” (I will say the word
TARGET), yielding a total of 3,328 tokens. After excluding
536 tokens due to noise, artifacts, or production errors, the fi-
nal dataset was analyzed. Many of the excluded tokens were
productions influenced by Mandarin rather than reliable Kaihui
Xiang forms. This exclusion rate likely reflects Mandarin in-
terference during formal elicitation and the relative infrequency
of certain target syllables – a pattern consistent with previous
fieldwork observations [21].
2.4. Acoustic and statistical analysis
Acoustic measures were extracted using V oiceSauce [22] fol-
lowing manual annotation in Praat [23]. A dual-tier annotation
approach was employed: the first tier marked the effective tonal
portion of the vowel, while the second captured the entire rime
(including any syllable-final creak). This distinction was nec-
essary as syllable-final creak frequently disruptsF 0 tracking al-
gorithms.
For each token,F 0, spectral tilt, HNR, and CPP were calcu-
lated at nine equidistant time points. To capture spectral tilts, we
selected four parameters (H1*-H2*, H2*-H4*, H4*-H2k*, H2k*-
H5k*) in accordance with [24]. Spectral slope measures were
derived from formant-corrected harmonic amplitudes to mini-
mize vocal tract resonance interference and better approximate
glottal source characteristics. To ensure cross-speaker compa-
rability,F 0 values were log-transformed, and all acoustic pa-
rameters (includingF 0) were subsequently z-scored within each
speaker.

3. Results
This section presents the modeling results forF 0, spectral tilts,
HNR, and CPP. Given limited space, results are selectively re-
ported in tables or text. Regarding the dynamic analysis, dif-
ference smooths are presented in figures only for contrasts that
exhibited a statistically significant smooth difference.
3.1.F 0
The GAMM for z-score-normalized log-F0 reveal that all tones
differ significantly from T4 in overallF 0 level. Relative to T4,
T6 (β=−0.42,p <0.001), T1 (β=−1.44,p <0.001), T3
(β=−1.75,p<0.001), T2 (β=−2.06,p<0.001) , and T5
(β=−2.25,p <0.001) all had significantly lower meanF 0.
These results indicate that T4 occupies the highest tonal register
among the six tones in Kaihui Xiang.
Analysis of the smooth terms shows a significant nonlin-
ear effect of time for T4 (p <0.001), confirming dynamicF 0
movement across the syllable. Crucially, the difference smooths
reveal that several tones exhibit contour shapes significantly dif-
ferent from T4. T6, T2, and T3 showed clear nonlinear devia-
tions from the T4 trajectory (allp <0.001), indicating struc-
turally distinct tonal movements. In contrast, T5 displays only
a marginal contour divergence (p= 0.08), and T1 did not differ
significantly from T4 in overall contour shape (p= 0.58).

3.3. Harmonic-to-noise ratio (HNR)
Our GAMM results show that the tonal system of Kaihui Xiang
exhibits clear differentiation in HNR. T1 and T6 consistently
show significantly higher HNR values than T4 (p <0.05),
while T2, T3, and T5 remain significantly lower (p <0.001).
Although T6 differs significantly from T4, the magnitude of
this contrast is the smallest among all comparisons. Moreover,
while the overall nonlinear temporal effect is significant, the dif-
ference smooths indicated that T4 does not exhibit a structurally
divergent trajectory relative to the other tones.
As illustrated in Figure 4, T4 occupies an intermediate po-
sition within the HNR space. Rather than defining the extremes,
T4 remains centrally distributed, with the most pronounced sep-
arations occurring between the high-HNR T1 and the low-HNR
T2 and T3.

4. Discussion and conclusion
This study investigated whether the extreme high-pitch tone
(T4) in Kaihui Xiang is produced with falsetto or tense voice,
and whether these adjustments areF 0-specific. Our results
identify T4 as associated with tense modal voice, characterized
by a flatter spectral tilt compared to other tones. This profile
aligns with increased medial compression and longitudinal ten-
sion [18]. Crucially, this tension isF 0-specific. At the sylla-
ble onset whereF 0 levels converge, T4 and T6 exhibit indis-
tinguishable voice quality. This lack of contrast identifies the
observed tense voice as a physiological byproduct of extreme
pitch – a laryngeal consequence rather than an independent,
tone-specific phonological feature.
These results align with the findings of [15], extending their
work through a systematic comparison of T4 against the entire
tonal inventory in this language variety. This comprehensive
comparison reinforces the view that high-pitch tones in Xiang
are not produced with falsetto. Typologically, falsetto is rare in
daily speech and typically serves stylistic or expressive func-
tions rather than lexical ones [27]. Consequently, a categorical
shift to falsetto for a specific lexical tone would be unexpected.
The absence of canonical falsetto correlates in T4 is thus consis-
tent with both prior acoustic data and general phonetic typology.
Previous descriptions of T4 as falsetto were largely
perception-based and lacked acoustic verification [9]. This
misidentification likely stems from the perceptual overlap be-
tween the extreme longitudinal tension of tense voice and the
“thin” quality characteristic of falsetto. Beyond acoustics, the
classification of T4 as a “high register” tone reflects a phono-
logical necessity to distinguish its extreme high-pitch tone from
other high tones (e.g., Mandarin high-level T1) [28]. While tra-
ditional pitch-based frameworks, such as Chao’s [29], might ob-
scure this difference, T4’s identity is defined by both its abso-
lute pitch height and its salient timbre. This distinctiveness is
further evidenced by its retention as a regional marker in Xiang-
accented Mandarin [30] and its role as an important perceptual
cue for listeners [15]. Although the tense phonation in T4 isF0-
specific, it consistently co-occurs with elevated pitch to form a
structured “high domain” contrast that goes beyond a purely
pitch-based distinction.
Apart from the general pattern of a flattened lower-
frequency spectral tilt, T4 exhibits a significantly steeper H2k*-
H5k* slope compared to all tones except T6. This indicates
that the relatively high-pitched T4 and T6 present a contrast-
ing trend in the high-frequency spectrum relative to their lower-
frequency characteristics. Moreover, the temporal dynamics
of H4*-H2k* reveal further complexities: at the syllable onset,
where T4 and T6 share a similarly highF 0, T4 exhibits signifi-
cantly lower H4*-H2k* values than T6. Conversely, toward the
syllable offset – where T4 reaches its extreme high pitch and T6
falls – their values converge. This trajectory contrasts sharply
with the lower-frequency spectral differences, suggesting that
higher-frequency spectral tilt may play a distinct and dynamic
role in differentiating phonation types. Furthermore, no signif-
icant effect of noise level (HNR and CPP) was observed across
these specific contrasts. This suggests that spectral tilt plays
a more prominent role than noise metrics in characterizing the
voice quality associated with extreme high-pitch tones.
In conclusion, T4 in Kaihui Xiang is characterized by tense
modal voice rather than falsetto. This laryngeal tension is a
physiological byproduct of extremeF 0 targets rather than an
independent phonological feature. Several limitations should
be noted: the study relies mainly on acoustic evidence, and the
older speaker sample means that possible age-related effects on
voice quality cannot be ruled out. Finally, the analysis was
limited to target syllables in sentence-medial carrier phrases.
Because higher-level prosody and utterance position can sig-
nificantly alterF 0 ranges and laryngeal mechanics, future re-
search should investigate how different sentence positions and
prosodic environments influence the voice quality of this ex-
treme high-pitch tone in continuous speech.
