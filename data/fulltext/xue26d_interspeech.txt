Abstract
Effective video reasoning requires the joint understanding of vi-
sual and acoustic cues. However, existing Multimodal Large
Language Models (MLLMs) either discard audio entirely or
rely solely on ASR transcripts, forfeiting rich acoustic features
such as prosody, environmental sounds, and speaker character-
istics. Naively forwarding all audio tokens to an MLLM in-
curs prohibitive computational overhead. We proposeFlash-
V AReason, a framework that integrates efficient audio token
compression into video reasoning via a three-stage pipeline
grounded in information uniqueness theory: Audio Time Fu-
sion, Budget Control, and Spatial Dynamic Compression. By
quantifying the acoustic distinctiveness of each token, our
method reduces the audio token count by an order of magnitude
while retaining the most informative cues, enabling joint visual-
audio reasoning without sacrificing inference speed. More in-
formation is available on the Project page.

As shown in Figure 1, video reasoning takes an input video

Unlike conventional video understanding, where a single visual

and detecting a change in vocal tone, while “What triggered the

indicator with its accompanying siren. Any reasoning step that
depends on auditory information, including speaker emotion,
environmental context, and off-screen events, is inaccessible to
visual-only models.
A growing number of studies have recognised the value of
audio and begun incorporating it into video pipelines. A com-
mon strategy is to use automatic speech recognition [20] to con-
vert audio into text before feeding it to the language model.
ASR captures lexical content but discards paralinguistic and

Existing token compression approaches [5, 14, 25, 26] have
been designed exclusively for visual tokens. Audio tokens,
however, form one-dimensional temporal sequences that ex-
hibit strong short-time stationarity and global redundancy pat-
terns, which differ from the spatial redundancy found in video
frames. Directly applying visual compression strategies to au-
dio is therefore not straightforward, leaving a clear efficiency
gap for audio-inclusive video reasoning. To address the gap, we
proposeFlash-V AReason, a framework centred on efficient au-
dio token compression for video reasoning. Our contributions
are threefold:
• We propose Flash-V AReason, the framework to compress au-
dio tokens for MLLM-based video reasoning, reducing token
counts by an order of magnitude while preserving acousti-
cally distinctive information.
• We unify audio and visual compression under the same
information-uniqueness principle, requiring no architectural
modification or additional training, and enabling the MLLM
to jointly reason over both modalities.
• Experiments on multiple video reasoning benchmarks vali-
date that acoustic cue preservation and efficient compression
are mutually reinforcing.

3.1. Implementation Details
We evaluate our method on two video reasoning datasets: Cha-
radesEgo [3] and Ego4D [29]. CharadesEgo contains 619 test
questions with video durations ranging from 4.33 s to 29.92 s.
Ego4D includes 634 test questions, with videos spanning from
10.17 s to 29.97 s. For audio representation, we use the Whisper
encoder to extract raw audio features before applying our com-
pression pipeline. We report BERTScore [30] Precision (P),
Recall (R), and F1 to measure the lexical overlap between gen-
erated answers and references. We further report QwenJudge-
based scores using Qwen3-VL-8B [31] as the evaluator, follow-
ing a protocol similar to [32]. To stabilize the results, we re-
peat the evaluation three times for each sample and average the
scores. The full evaluation prompts are provided in the Supple-
mentary Material. Qwen-Sem measures semantic accuracy (1–
5), Qwen-Comp measures completeness (1–5), Qwen-NoHall
measures non-hallucination (1–5), and Qwen-Overall is the av-
erage of the three (1–5). Average inference time is reported in
seconds (s), converted from milliseconds byt s =t ms/1000
and rounded to two decimals. All experiments are conducted
on 24 NVIDIA L40 (48GB) GPUs.

3.2. Main Results (RQ1)
As shown in Table 1, Flash-V AReason achieves the best Re-
call (0.7285), F1 (0.5840), Sem (2.3795), Comp (2.3062), No-
Hall (2.5776), and Overall (2.3789) on CharadesEgo, and it also
ranks first on every metric on Ego4D, reaching P of 0.6875, R of
0.6485, F1 of 0.6585, and Overall of 1.6480. Grounded-Video-
LLM [28] obtains the second-highest F1 (0.5832) on Charade-
sEgo, but its inference time of 111.74 s per sample makes it
impractical for deployment, as shown in Table 2. mPLUG-
Owl3 [4] is the fastest method at 5.32 s, but it produces the
lowest quality scores across all evaluation dimensions. Uni-
Comp [5], a visual-only baseline, achieves the second-best
Overall (2.2818) on CharadesEgo and the second-best Over-
all (1.6476) on Ego4D; Flash-V AReason surpasses it on every
quality metric on both datasets while maintaining comparable
inference speed, confirming that acoustic cues provide com-
plementary information beyond visual frames alone. Figure 3
presents an example from CharadesEgo. Visual frames lack
sufficient details to identify the activity, but the audio track pro-
vides critical context. While baselines fail, Flash-V AReason
correctly answers by leveraging compressed audio features, ef-
fectively resolving ambiguities that vision alone can not handle.
3.3. Efficiency Analysis (RQ2)
The audio compression pipeline in Flash-V AReason adds neg-
ligible computational overhead. On CharadesEgo, Flash-
V AReason runs at 6.10 s per sample with a speedup of
18.33×relative to the slowest method (Grounded-Video-LLM
at 111.74 s), while achieving the highest Overall score of
2.3789. Compared with UniComp (6.49 s), Flash-V AReason is
slightly faster and delivers a higher Overall (2.3789 vs. 2.2818).

3.4. Ablation Study (RQ3)
Table 3 compares the full compression pipeline with several
ablated variants on CharadesEgo. Using all extracted audio
tokens without compression (Full token) achieves the high-
est Qwen-Overall of 2.3838, but at an average inference time
of 10664.71 ms, roughly 1.75×slower than Flash-V AReason
(6096.97 ms). Replacing the compression pipeline with Uni-
form Sampling reduces inference time to 6144.51 ms, but the
Qwen-Overall drops sharply to 2.0121, indicating that naive
downsampling discards semantically important audio content.
Removing ATF (w/o ATF) raises inference time to 7221.89 ms
and lowers Qwen-Overall to 2.3011, confirming that tempo-
ral redundancy removal is essential for both speed and qual-
ity. Removing SDC (w/o SDC) keeps a high Qwen-Overall of
2.3807, close to the full pipeline, but increases inference time
to 7249.35 ms because the spatial deduplication stage is respon-
sible for the final token reduction. Flash-V AReason combines
both stages to achieve a Qwen-Overall of 2.3789 with the fastest
inference time of 6096.97 ms, demonstrating that the two-stage
compression pipeline preserves nearly all quality of the uncom-
pressed representation while reducing latency by 42.8%.
4. Conclusion and Discussions
We presented Flash-V AReason, a framework for joint visual-
audio video reasoning that incorporates an efficient audio to-
ken compression mechanism. We identify findings worth not-
ing: 1 Acoustic cues can improve video reasoning quality,
yet the current pipeline does not incorporate fine-grained au-
dio event information due to speed constraints, leaving room
for richer audio-semantic integration. 2 The temporal com-
pression mechanism works well in general scenarios, but when
information uniqueness varies greatly across time, compres-
sion efficiency degrades. Better compression strategies, partic-
ularly ones that can be jointly optimized with visual represen-
tations, remain an open problem. 3 The current pipeline is
designed for offline processing. Adapting it to streaming sce-
narios, where audio and visual tokens must be compressed on-
the-fly, is a meaningful direction for future work.
