Abstract
Ultrasound-to-Speech aims to synthesize intelligible speech
from tongue ultrasound video. Prior approaches rely mainly
on purely convolutional or recurrent architectures. We present
Tongue2Speech, a lightweight framework combining explicit
3D spatio-temporal encoding with a multi-layer Transformer
to model long-range articulatory dynamics. Spectral distortion
metrics (such as MSE) commonly used in prior work are weak
indicators of lexical accuracy when used in isolation. There-
fore, we evaluate performance using word error rate (WER). On
the English TaL corpus,Tongue2Speechachieves15.93%WER
in single-speaker and31.64%in multi-speaker settings, out-
performing six strong baselines. Speaker-specific fine-tuning
further improves intelligibility for unseen users. These results
highlight tongue-only ultrasound as a promising pathway to-
ward practical silent speech synthesis. Audio samples are avail-
able at:https://tongue2speech.github.io/

1. Introduction
Human speech arises from coordinated physiological processes
in which airflow from the lungs excites the vocal folds and the
vocal tract shapes the resulting sound through movements of the
tongue, lips, jaw, and velum. As the most dynamic and acousti-
cally influential articulator,the tonguegoverns vowel formants
and many consonantal contrasts through its deformable sur-
face [1, 2, 3], making its motion highly informative of the in-
tended speech signal. The task of Ultrasound-to-Speech (U2S)
aims to reconstruct intelligible speech from real-time tongue
imaging, enabling Silent Speech Interfaces (SSI) without an
acoustic signal [3]. Such systems are vital for individuals
with preserved tongue movements but impaired phonation, such
as laryngectomy patients [4] or those with neurological disor-
ders [5, 6] that compromise laryngeal or respiratory control.
Existing SSI techniques present several challenges: elec-
tromagnetic articulography requires intraoral sensors, real-time
MRI is costly and temporally constrained, and surface elec-
tromyography measures indirect muscle activity rather than ar-
ticulator geometry [7, 8, 9]. In contrast, ultrasound directly cap-
tures tongue surface geometry, providing access to lingual ges-
tures that are otherwise invisible [10, 3]. The development of
cost-effective ultrasound hardware [11] and the current avail-
ability of portable probes 1 further enhances its viability for
practical deployment. Consequently, among sensing modalities,

Ultrasound Tongue Imaging (UTI) offers a favorable trade-off
between physiological fidelity and practical deployability.
Although U2S research has made steady progress, several
important limitations remain. First, modeling long-range coar-
ticulation is critical for accurate speech reconstruction from
tongue-only ultrasound videos. To address this, most existing
methods rely on convolutional encoders combined with recur-
rent LSTM-based temporal modeling [12, 13]. However, more
recent advances in self-attention architectures remain largely
underexplored. Second, evaluation is often conducted on rela-
tively small speaker sets and relies on spectral distortion metrics
such as mean squared error (MSE) or mel-cepstral distortion
(MCD) [14], which provide limited insight into perceptual intel-
ligibility. Large-scale multi-speaker WER-based assessment re-
mains scarce, particularly for tongue-only systems [15, 16, 17].
Moreover, UTI is inherently speaker-dependent: anatomical
differences, probe placement variation, and individual articula-
tory strategies produce substantial geometric variability across
speakers. Hence, zero-shot cross-speaker generalization re-
mains extremely challenging.
To this end, we introduceTongue2Speech, a lightweight
architecture that maps ultrasound tongue imaging to mel-
spectrograms. We replace traditional LSTM or purely convo-
lutional backbones with a 3D spatio-temporal front-end and a
multi-layer Transformer encoder to capture long-range dynam-
ics. Moving beyond standard spectral distortion metrics, we
prioritize ASR-based word error rate (WER) as the principal
measure of intelligibility. Our core contributions are:
• We proposeTongue2Speech, a U2S architecture that com-
bines 3D convolutional encoding with a self-attention Trans-
former to model spatio-temporal dynamics and long-range
coarticulation. The lightweight, non-autoregressive de-
sign uses only19.17M parameters (6.25M excluding the
vocoder), enabling real-time applications.
• We present an extensive investigation into U2S, providing
comprehensive single- and multi-speaker performance across
six baselines. We report both MSE and WER, with WER
serving as the primary measure of usability
• Our method achieves high-fidelity reconstruction with WERs
of16%in single-speaker and31%in multi-speaker eval-
uations, demonstrating thatnatural soundingandintelligi-
bleU2S is achievable with tongue-only input. We show
that zero-shot multi-speaker performance remains challeng-
ing, but speaker-specific fine tuning significantly restores in-
telligibility for unseen users.

2. Related Work
Ultrasound imaging of tongue motion has been used in pho-
netic research since the 1960s, with systematic acquisition pro-
tocols established by Stone and colleagues in the 1980s and
1990s [7]. The U2S problem was first demonstrated by Denby
and Stone [18], who mapped reconstructed tongue contours to
vocal tract filter parameters using a multilayer perceptron and
synthesized intelligible speech. A subsequent study [19] fur-
ther evaluated spectral parameter prediction from ultrasound.
The broader SSI framework was later formalized in [3], defin-
ing sensing modalities and application scenarios.
Modern U2S systems have evolved substantially in archi-
tectural design. Early approaches employed fully connected
DNNs for articulatory-to-acoustic mapping [12]. These were
followed by convolutional encoders combined with recurrent
temporal modeling (typically LSTMs), predicting mel spectro-
grams rendered with neural vocoders [13, 20]. Subsequent work
incorporated transfer learning from Tacotron2-style TTS sys-
tems [17], multi-task learning [21], speaker embeddings [22],
ConvLSTM hybrids, data augmentation [23], and geometric
normalization using Spatial Transformer Networks [24]. More
recently, conformer-based architectures introduced attention
mechanisms into U2S modeling [14]. The release of the Tongue
and Lips (TaL) corpus [25] enabled larger-scale English U2S
research. Multi-modal systems such as TaLNet combine ultra-
sound and lip video streams [15], and co-learning frameworks
incorporate auxiliary audio supervision [16].
Several studies have explored ultrasound-to-text silent
speech recognition, demonstrating that tongue motion provides
complementary phonetic information. Landmark-based ap-
proaches using DeepLabCut improved multi-speaker recogni-
tion performance [26, 27]. UTI-derived articulatory features
have also enhanced ASR for atypical speech [28], and ul-
trasound has been used in multimodal speech enhancement
through knowledge distillation [10].
Although prior work acknowledges session and speaker-
level variability due to anatomical differences and probe place-
ment in ultrasound imaging, systematic evaluation of large-
scale multi-speaker generalization using intelligibility-based
metrics remains limited, and robust zero-shot generalization re-
mains challenging. Moreover, speaker personalization has re-
ceived relatively little attention in U2S synthesis. In this work,
we therefore investigate fine-tuning a multi-speaker model
trained on a diverse speaker pool for a target speaker, showing
that efficient personalization can restore intelligible speech syn-
thesis while highlighting the need for stronger zero-shot U2S
models.
3. Dataset and Preprocessing
We conduct experiments on the TaL corpus [25], a synchronized
multi-speaker dataset of ultrasound tongue imaging, audio, and
lip videos. The corpus comprises two subsets:TaL1, con-
taining six sessions from a single native English male speaker,
andTaL80, consisting of single-session recordings from81na-
tive English speakers, totaling approximately24hours of syn-
chronized ultrasound, audio, and video data. Ultrasound was
recorded at∼80fps with a 92 ◦ field of view, producing raw
frames of size64×842(scanlines×radial samples). Audio
was captured at48kHz (16-bit). We extract80-dimensional
Mel-spectrograms (window/FFT size2048, hop512) as acous-
tic targets.

4.T ongue2Speech
Tongue2Speechcomprises three stages: a spatio-temporal con-
volutional encoder, a self-attention Transformer backbone, and
a neural vocoder. Ultrasound sequences in wedge-transformed
Cartesian geometry are processed by four stacked 3D convo-
lutional blocks (Conv3D + BatchNorm + ReLU), operating
jointly across spatial and short-term temporal dimensions to
capture local articulatory motion. Spatial dimensions are ag-
gregated via adaptive average pooling, producing a per-frame
latent embedding of dimension256while preserving temporal
resolution. The resulting latent sequence is processed by an
encoder-only Transformer with six layers, eight attention heads
per layer, and a feed-forward hidden dimension of1024. Sinu-
soidal positional encodings preserve temporal order; each layer
consists of multi-head self-attention followed by a position-
wise feed-forward network with GELU activation and dropout
(p=0.1). The final hidden states are projected to the acoustic
domain using a two-layer MLP, yielding80-dimensional mel-

5. Baselines
We evaluateTongue2Speechagainst six baselines: Conformer-
U2S [14], STN-CNN [24], 3D-CNN, 3D-CNN+BiLSTM, 3D-
CNN+BiLSTM-Skip, and 2D-CNN+BiLSTM. The baselines
span the major design axes of U2S mapping: input dimensional-
ity (2D vs. 3D), temporal modeling (no explicit temporal aggre-
gation vs. BiLSTM), and multi-scale feature aggregation. Since
the source code for Conformer-U2S and STN-CNN is not pub-
licly available, we re-implement both methods. The remain-
ing four baselines are purpose-built architectural variants de-
signed to isolate specific modeling factors. Unless stated oth-
erwise, all baselines share the same mel extraction pipeline,
vocoder, optimizer, and data splits. Conformer-U2S and STN-
CNN are trained under their original MSE objectives, whereas
Tongue2Speechand all proposed variants useL 1 loss on mel
spectrograms. For MSE evaluation, we compute log-mel spec-
trograms by clamping values to a minimum of10 −7 before
applying the natural logarithm. For the re-implemented base-
lines [14, 24], the generated samples qualitatively match the
originals (both the spectrograms and speech); however, MSE
values may differ due to variations in normalization procedures
(see webpage for samples).
Conformer-U2S[14] processes resized scanline frames
(64×128) through a single Conformer block (encoder256
dimensions,32attention heads, convolution kernel size31)
followed by two Bi-LSTM layers, combining multi-head self-
attention, depthwise separable convolutions, and recurrent tem-
poral modeling.STN-CNN[24] prepends a Spatial Trans-
former Network module to a CNN regression backbone, which
learns an affine transformation of input frames to compensate
for probe misalignment and inter-speaker geometric variability.
3D-CNNcaptures joint spatio-temporal features through
four 3D convolutional blocks (Conv3D + BatchNorm + ReLU
+ Dropout,p=0.1) with3×3×3kernels and spatial-
only downsampling, followed by a linear mel projection.
3D-CNN+BiLSTMaugments three 3D convolutional blocks
(Conv3D + BatchNorm + MaxPool3D) with a two-layer bidi-
rectional LSTM (hidden256dimensions) before mel projec-
tion.3D-CNN+BiLSTM-Skipuses a three-stage 3D convo-
lutional encoder (3×3×3kernels, BatchNorm, ReLU) with
1×2×2MaxPool3d layers that downsample spatial resolution
by a factor of 8 while preserving temporal length. A multi-scale
skip-connection mechanism projects intermediate feature maps
via1×1×1convolutions, trilinearly interpolates them to a uni-
fied8×8resolution, and concatenates them through a fusion
layer. The features are passed to a two-layer,256-unit BiLSTM
followed by an MLP.2D-CNN+BiLSTMprocesses each frame
independently through four 2D convolutional blocks (Conv2D
+ BatchNorm + ReLU + MaxPool2D), omitting max-pooling
in the final block. Spatial features are collapsed to1×1via
adaptive average pooling and passed to a three-layer BiLSTM
(hidden256dimensions), with a linear output projection.
6. Results
We evaluateTongue2Speechand six baselines across single-
speaker, multi-speaker, and personalized settings. Synthesized
speech is evaluated using OpenAI Whisper Medium ASR [30].

6.2. Multi-Speaker Experiments
Multi-speaker experiments are conducted on the TaL80 cor-
pus [25], comprising articulatory recordings from81speakers
with anatomical and probe-placement variability. We evaluate
the two strongest single-speaker baselines (3D-CNN+BiLSTM
and 3D-CNN+BiLSTM w/ Skip) alongsideTongue2Speech.
A. Multi-Speaker Training.All 81 speakers are pooled
and partitioned using a95/5train–test split at the utterance
level. This setting evaluates the ability to learn speaker-robust
articulatory representations under mixed-speaker supervision.
Results are shown in Table 2 (Section A). Compared to the
single-speaker setting, WER increases substantially across all
systems, highlighting the difficulty of modeling cross-speaker
articulatory variability.Tongue2Speechachieves the lowest
WER (31.64%), outperforming 3D-CNN+BiLSTM (42.46%)
and the skip variant (33.23%). Despite broadly compara-
ble MSE values, intelligibility differences persist, indicating
that attention-based aggregation over 3D motion embeddings
improves robustness to speaker heterogeneity, though cross-
speaker variability remains a limiting factor.
B. Personalization via Fine-Tuning.To assess adapta-
tion to unseen speakers,Tongue2Speechis first trained on77
speakers (1500epochs), with four speakers (IDs78–81) held
out. For each held-out speaker, the pre-trained model is fine-
tuned on95%of that speaker’s data for20epochs and evalu-
ated on the remaining5%. Results are reported in Table 2 (Sec-
tion B). After fine-tuning, WER ranges between27.62–46.07%
across speakers, with the best result observed for Speaker80
(27.62%). These results indicate that while cross-speaker gen-
eralization remains challenging, limited speaker-specific adap-
tation substantially improves intelligibility without architectural
modification. Together, these findings support personalization
as a practical deployment strategy for tongue-only U2S.

7. Conclusion
We presentedTongue2Speech, a lightweight tongue-only
ultrasound-to-speech architecture that combines 3D spatio-
temporal convolution with a Transformer encoder for
articulatory-to-acoustic mapping. Across six baselines, ASR-
based evaluation shows that explicit modeling of articulatory
motion and long-range temporal dependencies substantially
improves intelligibility over purely convolutional or weakly ag-
gregated systems. Our results further demonstrate that spectral
MSE does not reliably reflect lexical correctness, reinforcing
the need for intelligibility-driven evaluation in U2S research.
Overall, our results demonstrate that intelligible speech can be
reconstructed from tongue ultrasound across single-speaker,
multi-speaker, and personalized settings. Nevertheless, robust
cross-speaker generalization and efficient personalization
remain important open challenges and key directions for future
research in ultrasound-to-speech synthesis.
