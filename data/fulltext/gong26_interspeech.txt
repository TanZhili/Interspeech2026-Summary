Abstract
In personal sound zone (PSZ) systems, linear control meth-
ods suffer from significant performance degradation on devices
with miniature loudspeakers due to severe nonlinear distortion
(NLD). To overcome this limitation, we propose a nonlinear
control network for PSZ (NCPSZ), a data-driven framework
tailored for sound leakage prevention. We introduce an asym-
metric dual-network architecture: a high-fidelity modeling net-
work (ModelNet) characterizes the loudspeaker’s nonlinear be-
havior offline, which supervises a lightweight, causal control
network (CtrlNet) for real-time operation. Crucially, we em-
ploy an ”anchor speaker” strategy, where the primary earpiece
speaker maintains the target sound pressure in the bright zone
(BZ), while the CtrlNet optimizes assisting speakers to actively
cancel nonlinear leakage in the dark zone (DZ). Validated in a
realistic smartphone leakage-prevention task, NCPSZ achieves
a consistent acoustic contrast (AC) improvement of 3.78 dB
over the linear V AST baseline and 1.80 dB over a parameter-
matched causal CNN, demonstrating effective privacy protec-
tion under nonlinear regimes.

1. Introduction
Personal sound zone (PSZ) systems are conventionally formu-
lated as multichannel linear filter optimization problems. and
two foundational control algorithms are acoustic contrast con-
trol (ACC) [1, 2] and pressure matching (PM) [3, 4]. ACC max-
imizes the acoustic energy ratio between the bright zone (BZ)
and dark zone (DZ), while PM minimizes the weighted sound
reproduction error in both regions. After that, several improved
approaches have been proposed, including hybrid ACC–PM
methods [5, 6, 7] and robust formulations to handle model un-
certainties [8, 9, 10]. A more general framework, termed vari-
able span trade-off (V AST) [11, 12, 13], balances acoustic con-
trast and reproduction precision.
However, the efficacy of these linear methods can be sig-
nificantly limited by nonlinear distortion (NLD) in miniature
loudspeakers widely used in consumer devices such as smart-
phones [14]. In privacy-sensitive scenarios, such as sound leak-
age prevention, assisting loudspeakers are often driven at high
playback levels to generate anti-phase cancellation signals. Un-
der these conditions, nonlinear artifacts (e.g., harmonic and
inter-modulation distortion) degrade audio quality and reduce
achievable acoustic contrast [15]. Since linear filters are in-
herently limited in reproducing nonlinear harmonic components

Prior work addressing loudspeaker nonlinearity can be
broadly categorized into classical model-based methods and
modern data-driven approaches. Classical techniques based on
V olterra modeling and nonlinear pre-distortion have been ex-
plored, including early attempts in PSZ contexts [16, 17, 18].
More recent studies employ deep neural networks (DNNs) for
general audio distortion compensation [19, 20, 21]. How-
ever, these existing approaches are not explicitly designed for
phase-sensitive multichannel control or real-time deployment
on resource-constrained devices. Unlike prior audio enhance-
ment networks that treat the loudspeaker as a black-box proces-
sor, our framework explicitly integrates a differentiable electro-
acoustic model into the sound-zone optimization loop.
In this work, we propose a nonlinear control network for
PSZ (NCPSZ), a data-driven framework tailored for nonlinear
sound leakage prevention. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the pro-
posed method adopts an asymmetric dual-network architecture:
a high-fidelity modeling network (ModelNet) that character-
izes loudspeaker nonlinearity offline, and a lightweight causal
control network (CtrlNet) that generates pre-compensated sig-
nals for real-time operation. Crucially, we introduce anAnchor
Speakerstrategy, in which the primary earpiece speaker main-
tains the target sound pressure in the BZ, while CtrlNet opti-
mizes assisting speakers to suppress nonlinear leakage in the
DZ. This design reflects practical smartphone playback con-
straints, where the main speaker delivers the intended content
and auxiliary speakers are primarily used for leakage control.
The proposed approach is validated in a realistic smart-
phone leakage-prevention task, and the experimental results
show that the proposed method consistently suppresses low-
frequency nonlinear distortion. Compared with linear baselines,
NCPSZ improves leakage suppression while preserving high-
fidelity reproduction in the BZ.
The main contributions of this work are threefold: (i) a
nonlinear dual-network control framework tailored for minia-
ture loudspeaker scenarios, (ii) an anchor-speaker strategy en-
abling practical deployment in handheld devices, and (iii) a
lightweight causal implementation suitable for real-time edge
processing.

2.2. Shared Network Architecture Design
Both ModelNet and CtrlNet share a structurally identical
“Encoder–Bottleneck–Decoder” backbone [22], optimized for
efficient, causal processing required for on-device deployment.
The overall structure is depicted in Figure 2. The key compo-
nents of this architecture, Encoder, Bottleneck, Decoder, and
the 3D Complex Mask Head, are detailed below.
Encoder:The encoder stacks multiple depthwise-separable
2D convolutions with residual connections. It extracts joint
time-frequency features by progressively downsampling the fre-
quency resolution while preserving the time resolution to sup-
port low-latency control. Causality is maintained by using one-
sided (past-only) padding along the temporal axis.
Bottleneck:To aggregate temporal context and model loud-
speaker nonlinearity, a causal gated recurrent unit (GRU) [23] is
employed. The feature maps from the encoder are first reshaped
and passed through a fully-connected (FC) layer before being
fed into the GRU. This recurrent summary expands the tempo-
ral receptive field without violating causality. ModelNet uses a
larger hidden size for fidelity, while CtrlNet uses a smaller size
for efficiency.
Decoder:The decoder mirrors the encoder, restoring frequency
resolution via sub-pixel upsampling to avoid checkerboard arti-
facts. The skip connections from the encoder are incorporated
to preserve fine spectral detail.
3D Complex Mask Head:The decoder output is fed into a 3D
complex mask head, which generates a complex-valued mask.
This mask is applied multiplicatively to the decoder’s complex

2.3. Training Schedule
The NCPSZ framework employs a sequential, two-stage train-
ing strategy:Stage I (ModelNet training):ModelNet is
trained on real-world data (loudspeaker input vs. microphone
recordings), minimizingL model to accurately capture the loud-
speaker’s nonlinear dynamics.Stage II (CtrlNet training):
With ModelNet’s parameters frozen, CtrlNet is trained by min-
imizingL ctrl, optimizing the input signal to achieve the desired
acoustic properties (BZ fidelity and DZ suppression) through
the simulated nonlinear system.
To benchmark the performance advantage of explicitly
compensating for NLD, we compare two distinct baselines.
First, we use V AST as a linear baseline controller. As a purely-
linear technique, V AST cannot account for the severe nonlinear
behavior of miniature loudspeakers. For this baseline, the al-
gorithm was configured with a subspace dimension ofV= 4
and a regularization parameter ofµ= 10 3, following com-
monly used settings in prior work and empirically verified to
provide stable near-optimal performance under our experimen-
tal conditions. Second, to demonstrate the superiority of our
proposed method against other nonlinear solutions, we imple-
ment a causal convolutional network (CNN) [24] as a nonlinear
baseline.
3. Experimental Setup
We collect real data to verify the performance of the proposed
network in the smartphone sound leakage prevention scenario,
and the data collection setup is depicted in Figure 3. The details
are provided as follows.
Hardware and Environment:The measurement system com-
prised a RME UCX sound interface, a eight-channel analog
power amplifier, four 1115 miniature loudspeakers, and seven
B&K measurement microphones. All recordings were con-
ducted in a semi-anechoic chamber. The hardware was arranged
as depicted in Figure 3, simulating a user holding a phone in a
typical listening posture. The microphones at the artificial ears
are defined as the BZ, while the surrounding microphones con-
stitute the DZ.
Loudspeaker Characterization:A key premise of this work
is that NLD in miniature loudspeakers limits the performance
of linear controllers. To quantify this, we measured the total
harmonic distortion (THD) using a 250 Hz pure tone sinusoid
as input, with the loudspeaker driven to a level representative of

maximum user volume. Under these conditions, we measured a
THD of 3.2%. This level of harmonic distortion, being funda-
mentally unpredictable by a linear model.
Dataset and Task:The experiment was conducted on a speech-
to-speech basis. Our dataset contains over 1300 recordings,
created by passing speech signals from the LibriSpeech [25]
through various equalization filters for data augmentation. All
signals were reproduced at the high voltage level character-
ized above to ensure that both training and evaluation were per-
formed under prominent nonlinear distortion conditions.
The experimental setup consists of seven microphones.
Two in-ear microphones located at the ears of the dummy head
are defined as the BZ, representing the intended listening po-
sition of the user. The remaining six microphones positioned
around the head are defined as the DZ, modeling sound leakage
to the surrounding environment.
As the primary objective of this work is sound leakage pre-
vention, the control task is formulated to minimize the total
acoustic energy within the DZ. Accordingly, the weighting pa-
rameters in Eq. 5 are set toα= 0.8andβ= 0.2, respec-
tively. The time–frequency trade-off parameter in Eq. 4 is set to
γ= 0.4to balance waveform reconstruction and spectral con-
sistency. The sound pressure in the BZ is physically maintained
by the anchor-speaker strategy, in which the primary earpiece
speaker is constrained to reproduce the target signal, while the
remaining assisting speakers are optimized to generate cancel-
lation signals for nonlinear leakage suppression.

Network Implementation and Parameters:For the STFT,
we used a window size of 2048 and a hop size of 1024. The
key difference between the two networks lies in their capacity,
which is directly reflected in their parameter counts. All models
were trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of
1×10 −3. The details of each network are as follows.
ModelNet:A 4-layer architecture (4 encoder, 4 decoder blocks)
with no frequency downsampling (stride=1). We used 128
channels and a GRU bottleneck with 80 hidden units, resulting
in a total of 32.71M parameters to ensure high-fidelity model-
ing.
CtrlNet:A 2-layer architecture (2 encoder, 2 decoder block)
with a downsampling/upsampling factor of 2. We used 16 chan-
nels and a GRU bottleneck with 8 hidden units. This lightweight
design results in a network with only 0.18M parameters.

4. Results and Analysis
Before evaluating the control performance, we first assess the
modeling accuracy of ModelNet. Figure 4 shows a representa-
tive time- and frequency-domain comparison between the pre-
dicted and measured microphone signals. The predicted wave-
form closely follows the measured response, demonstrating that
ModelNet effectively captures the nonlinear loudspeaker behav-
ior across the speech band.
In the frequency domain, strong agreement is observed over
most frequencies. A localized increase in error appears around
2.5 kHz, mainly due to the low signal energy in this region,

where small absolute deviations lead to relatively larger spec-
tral differences. This discrepancy contributes little to the over-

We then evaluate the overall sound-zone performance us-
ing AC as the primary metric, defined as the energy ratio be-
tween the BZ and DZ. Figure 5 summarizes the average results
across the test set. Compared with the unprocessed playback,
the linear V AST baseline, and the causal CNN baseline, the
proposed NCPSZ consistently achieves higher AC across the
speech band. Averaged over 200–2000 Hz, NCPSZ provides a
consistent AC improvement of 3.78 dB over V AST and 1.8 dB
over the CNN baseline. Notably, V AST was tuned close to its
linear performance ceiling, suggesting that the observed gain
primarily originates from nonlinear compensation.
From a sound-zone perspective, NCPSZ yields lower
broadband SPL, indicating more effective leakage suppression.
Meanwhile, the SPL in the BZ remains close to the desired
reference level, demonstrating that the improvement in AC is
primarily achieved through enhanced cancellation rather than
degradation of the target listening region.
The strong relative improvement over the linear V AST con-
troller highlights the importance of explicitly modeling non-
linear harmonic distortion in miniature loudspeaker scenarios.
Furthermore, the additional gain over the parameter-matched
causal CNN suggests that incorporating temporal dynamics
through a recurrent bottleneck is beneficial for compensating
the state dependent nature of loudspeaker nonlinearities.
5. Conclusion
In this work, we introduced NCPSZ, a data-driven framework
designed to address performance limitations caused by nonlin-
ear distortion (NLD) in miniature loudspeakers for PSZ applica-
tions. In a sound leakage prevention task, the proposed method
achieved a consistent AC improvement of 3.78 dB over the
linear V AST baseline and 1.80 dB over a parameter-matched
causal CNN baseline. These results highlight the importance of
accounting for NLD in practical PSZ scenarios and demonstrate
the effectiveness of the proposed framework.
