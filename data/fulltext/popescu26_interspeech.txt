Abstract
Romanian Sign Language (LSR) was officially recognized in
2020, yet it remains severely under-resourced and insufficiently
supported in educational contexts, including special educa-
tion. Accessible and interactive learning tools are limited. We
present,lisero, a freely accessible mobile app designed to sup-
port learning LSR through structured lessons and interactive
exercises. Originally developed to support infant-caregiver in-
teractions and support bimodal bilingualism, the app has been
extended to cover general LSR learning scenarios.liserofea-
tures lesson-based content, practice exercises, and a growing
sign dictionary. Gamification elements, as well as options to
save and share signs, aim to enhance user engagement and com-
munity participation. The system is designed for both hearing
users and deaf and hard-of hearing learners.
Index Terms: language learning mobile app, Romanian Sign
Language, under-resourced language, L2 language learning
1. Introduction
Romanian Sign Language (LSR) was officially recognized as a
national language in 2020, representing an important milestone
for the deaf community in Romania. However, this recognition
has not yet translated into general access to education or learn-
ing resources. LSR is not systematically integrated into school
curricula, and opportunities for formal instruction remain lim-
ited. The scarcity of resources also impacts the development
of professional ecosystems around LSR. There is a shortage of
qualified instructors and interpreters, with only 77 certified in-
terpreters currently available. Individuals seeking to train these
roles often lack accessible and structured materials to support
their learning. As a result, both deaf and hearing individuals
face significant barriers in acquiring LSR skills, limiting com-
munication access and broader social inclusion.
In this work, we introducelisero, a mobile application de-
signed to provide structured and interactive support for learn-
ing LSR across diverse user groups, including parents of new-
born deaf children as well as individuals seeking to learn LSR
more broadly. The app offers lesson-based content grounded in
real-life interaction scenarios, supporting practical and context-
driven language acquisition.

3. Design and Core Features
The design of the app draws on prior language learning tools
[3, 4] , and established principles from digital L2 language
learning [5]. Development was carried out by an interdisci-
plinary team, including a programmer, a linguist, deaf native
signers, and certified LSR instructors.

3.1. Videos
All LSR content in the app is recorded by two deaf native sign-
ers of LSR. Videos, are presented in a continuous loop and ran-
domly alternate between signers, exposing learners to natural
variation.

3.2. Dictionary
The app includes a searchable dictionary containing approxi-
mately 1000 signs. In cases of Romanian homophony, mean-
ings are disambiguated in parentheses. No other Romanian or
LSR definitions are provided. User search activity is tracked to
guide vocabulary expansion.
3.3. Lessons
Users select either a parent-focused or a general curriculum.
Lessons are organized into chapters reflecting real-life commu-
nication situations (e.g., family, bath-time, travel) and introduce
signs in isolation before embedding them in short contextual-
ized sentences with Romanian transcriptions.
Learning is reinforced through three exercise types (illus-
trated in Fig. 2): (1) Multiple-Choice selection with seman-
tic and phonological competitors, (2) Video-to-Word matching,
and (3) Sentence construction from video input. Difficulty is
adjusted by introducing competing options based on phonolog-
ical features. Immediate feedback is provided, and incorrectly
answered items are repeated at the end of each lesson. Com-
pleted lessons remain accessible, enabling users to review and
revisit previously learned content.
3.4. Features
To support learning, the app includes a half-speed video play-
back feature that helps users observe signing details such as
hand-shape and facial expressions, as well as a mirror func-
tion that uses the front-facing camera to enable self-comparison.
Additional features include saving and sharing signs, along with
gamification elements such as points, accuracy scores, streaks,
lesson time tracking, and progress monitoring (see Fig. 1).
4. Conclusion
The present paper introduceslisero, a freely accessible interac-
tive mobile app designed to support Romanian Sign Language
acquisition through structured lessons and practice-based exer-
cises. By addressing the lack of accessible and engaging LSR
learning resources, the app provides a scalable tool for diverse
user groups, including parents and prospective learners, and is
currently available on both iOS and Android platforms. Future
work will focus on expanding content and further enhancing in-
teractive features, and in the longer term, supporting additional
sign languages.
