Abstract
Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) suffer from high la-
tency due to the autoregressive decoding of long audio se-
quences. We propose Audio-NSP (Next Sequence Prediction),
a framework that activates semi-autoregressive parallel gener-
ation in pre-trained LALMs solely through data-centric Super-
vised Fine-Tuning. By restructuring training sequences with
blockwise attention, Audio-NSP enables simultaneous multi-
token prediction without architectural modifications. Crucially,
applying this to audio reveals a decoding dilemma caused by
the entropy gap between deterministic text and highly vari-
able audio tokens. To resolve this, we introduce a modality-
aware dynamic truncation strategy. It applies strict thresholds
for text and lenient thresholds for audio, preventing degenera-
tion into single-step decoding while preserving fidelity. Experi-
ments show Audio-NSP achieves speedups of up to 3.42x while
significantly outperforming Multi-Token Prediction baselines in
quality preservation.
Index Terms: Large Audio Language Models, efficient infer-
ence, semi-autoregressive generation
1. Introduction
The speech processing field is converging on Large Audio-
Language Models (LALMs) [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. While integrating
speech and text into a shared autoregressive backbone enables
seamless interaction, its deployment is bottlenecked by infer-
ence latency. Discrete audio sequences are orders of magnitude
longer than text, making token-by-token decoding prohibitively
slow. Thus, enabling parallel decoding is imperative.
Achieving parallel generation typically involves Non-
Autoregressive (NAR) paradigms [6, 7, 8, 9] or auxiliary au-
toregressive techniques. However, applying these to LALMs
presents severe challenges. Native NAR architectures require
pre-training from scratch, destroying the inherent cross-modal
reasoning abilities of the foundation model. Alternatively,
Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) [10, 11] and Speculative Decod-
ing [12, 13] preserve the backbone but introduce separate draft
models or prediction heads, increasing memory overhead. More
importantly, employing lightweight auxiliary heads for aggres-
sive multi-token prediction struggles to model complex continu-
ous acoustic mappings, often causing severe quality degradation
in audio tasks.
To overcome this, we propose Audio-NSP (Next Sequence
Prediction). Inspired by data-centric text generation like SDLM
[14] and Block Diffusion [15], Audio-NSP extends standard
next-token prediction to generating a dynamic-length sequence

simultaneously. It activates block-wise parallel generation
solely through data-centric Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). By
restructuring sequences, customizing attention masks and ad-

with zero parameter overhead.
However, seamlessly applying parallel generation to unified
models faces a severe decoding dilemma due to modal asym-
metry. Text generation is tightly constrained by semantics and
exhibits low entropy, whereas speech exhibits an inherent one-
to-many mapping [16, 17], resulting in high entropy. Conse-
quently, applying a strict text-calibrated confidence threshold to
audio shrinks the dynamic sequence length to one, degenerat-
ing the process into standard autoregressive decoding with no
speedup. Conversely, employing fixed-step parallel decoding
forces predictions across highly uncertain acoustic boundaries,
severely destroying audio fidelity.
We address this with a modality-aware dynamic truncation
strategy. By differentiating between semantic text and acous-
tic audio tokens, this mechanism adaptively adjusts the gener-
ation step size: accelerating in high-confidence regions while
reverting to fine-grained steps during high-entropy transitions,
thereby balancing inference speed and fidelity. Our main con-
tributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose Audio-NSP, a data-centric semi-autoregressive
framework for LALMs. It enables parallel decoding via data-
centric SFT, requiring zero structural modifications.
• We identify the decoding dilemma caused by modal entropy
gaps and resolve it via modality-aware dynamic truncation.
• Audio-NSP achieves significant speedups of up to 3.42×
with superior quality preservation compared to MTP base-
lines across understanding and synthesis tasks.
2. Related work
LALMs and NAR Paradigms.Recent LALMs, such as GLM-

speech and text via shared autoregressive decoders. They
achieve strong cross-modal reasoning but suffer from high la-
tency due to excessively long audio sequences [18]. While
native NAR models like SoundStorm [6], MaskGCT [7], and
DIFFA [8, 9] offer parallel decoding, they are typically limited
to specific audio tasks. Adapting generalist LALMs to NAR ar-
chitectures incurs prohibitive data and compute costs compared
to accelerating existing autoregressive models.

3. Methods
3.1. Training sequence construction
To enable efficient parallel generation without compromising
the LALM’s pre-trained autoregressive capabilities, we append
multiple prediction blocks to the original sequence.
Formally, letX={x 1,x 2,...,x L}denote the original in-
terleaved sequence of lengthL, wherex i can be a text or audio
token. As illustrated in Fig 1(a), we randomly sample a set ofK
starting indicesI={t 1,t 2,...,t K}from the response part of
X. For each start indext k∈I, we construct an Anchor-Mask
BlockB (k) of lengthW. This block consists of the anchor to-
kenxtk followed byW−1learnable mask tokensM. The final
training input sequenceX train is constructed by concatenating
the original sequence with allKblocks:

where||represents concatenation. To ensure the model pro-
cesses the appended blocks at their correct logical positions,
their positional embeddingsP(·)are explicitly assigned to
match the original sequence positions. Specifically, for the an-
chor tokenx tk and its subsequent mask tokensM j (1≤j <
W) in blockB (k), their positional IDs areP(x tk) =t k and
P(Mj) =t k +j.

The appended-block construction maximizes training effi-

pack an arbitrary number of prediction blocks into a single se-
quence (up to the maximum context window), thereby comput-
ing parallel losses for all blocks in one forward pass.

4. Experiments
4.1. Settings
Model and Training Configurations.We utilize VITA-Audio-
Plus-Vanilla [11] as our backbone model and fine-tune it on a
subset of its open-source training corpus. Following SDLM
[14], we set the block length toW= 4. Rather than fixing
the number of appended blocksK, we adopt a dynamic pack-
ing strategy to maximize training efficiency. Specifically, for
each sequence, we append blocks for all valid token positions if
the total sequence length remains within the model’s maximum
context window (L max). If it exceedsL max, we uniformly
sampleKstarting indices until the length limit is reached. The
model is trained using a learning rate of 5e-6 with a batch size
of 256 for 140k steps.
Benchmarks and Metrics.We evaluate our method
across several benchmarks spanning Automatic Speech Recog-
nition (ASR), Spoken Question Answering (SQA), and Text to
Speech (TTS). We employ Paraformer [27] to transcribe Chi-
nese speech and Whisper-large-v3 [28] to transcribe English
speech. Generation quality is measured by Word Error Rate
(WER) / Character Error Rate (CER) for ASR/TTS and Ac-
curacy (ACC) for SQA. The inference speedup is reported as
Tokens Per Step (TPS). It is calculated as the total number of
generated tokens divided by the number of forward passes, rep-
resenting a hardware-agnostic, theoretical acceleration ratio.
4.2. Main results
High Acceleration and Quality Preservation in Understand-
ing Tasks.The main experimental results are summarized
in Table 1. Our method demonstrates substantial acceleration
across diverse tasks, achieving up to a3.42×speedup. Impor-
tantly, compared to the Multi-Token Prediction baseline (VITA-
MTP), Audio-NSP significantly better preserves the generation
quality of the original autoregressive model. In ASR tasks,
MTP suffers from a severe performance drop (e.g., a+2.78%
WER degradation on AIShell). This indicates that forcing fixed-
length token generation derails the model’s precise acoustic
recognition. Conversely, our adaptive mechanism effectively
prevents this degradation, restricting the WER increase to a
marginal+0.35%while maintaining a3.17×speedup. Similar
advantages in accuracy retention are observed in multi-modal
reasoning scenarios (SQA).

tasks (TTS), Audio-NSP strictly outperforms MTP in gener-

4.3. Ablation and analysis
To investigate the optimal configuration for blockwise infer-
ence, we conducted ablation studies on the TTS task using a
random subset of the Seed-test-en [25] dataset.
The Modality Gap and the Necessity of Decoupling.We
analyzed the confidence and entropy distributions of generated
tokens. As illustrated in Fig 2, a distinct modality gap is ob-
served. Text tokens exhibit sharp distributions with high con-
fidence, whereas audio tokens show significantly flatter distri-
butions. Statistics reveal that for audio tokens, seemingly low
Top-1 scores like 0.4 already represent quite certain prediction.
We hypothesize that this gap stems from the inherentone-
to-many mappingnature of speech generation [16, 17]. Unlike
text, a single semantic unit in speech can be realized through di-
verse acoustic variations, such as pitch, duration, and prosody.
This continuous latent space forces audio tokens to distribute
their probability mass more broadly, resulting in higher en-
tropy. Consequently, imposing a text-calibrated strict threshold
on audio forces single-token acceptance, reverting the process
to naive autoregressive decoding. This justifies our modality-
aware design, which decouples the validation criteria for text
and audio.
Modality-Aware Dynamic Truncation vs. Fixed-Step.
To evaluate the quality-efficiency trade-off, we sweep the audio
thresholdτaudio across a range of 0.05 to 0.4, measuring gener-
ation quality via 1-WER and speed via TPS. For the fixed-step
baseline, we vary the constant acceptance lengthk, where the
empirical TPS is naturally bounded by early sequence termina-
tion upon encountering an EOS token.
As shown in Fig 3, the dynamic strategy consistently out-
performs the fixed-step approach. This superiority manifests
in two aspects. First, it achieves a better overall quality-speed
Pareto frontier by aggressively accepting tokens during stable
acoustic segments and safely falling back to finer steps at com-
plex boundaries. Second, sweeping the continuous threshold
provides flexible and fine-grained acceleration control. In con-
trast, fixed-step decoding is restricted to rigid discrete points,
limiting its adaptability to specific deployment latency budgets.

5. Conclusion
We presented Audio-NSP, a novel framework transforming au-
toregressive LALMs into blockwise generators without archi-
tectural modifications. Through rigorous entropy analysis, we
identified the inherent uncertainty of audio tokens and pro-
posed a modality-aware dynamic decoding mechanism to mit-
igate degradation in speech synthesis. Our method achieves a
superior Pareto frontier between speed and fidelity compared
to fixed-step approaches, delivering1.89×to3.42×speedups
across diverse tasks.
