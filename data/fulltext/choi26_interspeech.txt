Abstract
Whisper and similar encoder–decoder ASR models are in-
creasingly deployed on mobile and edge devices, yet it
remains unclear how quantization format choices affect
their accuracy in practice. We systematically evaluate
post-training quantization (PTQ) for Whisper (tiny.en,
base.en) across 80+ configurations covering INT8/4/3 and
FP8/FP4/NVFP4/MXFP4. Our main finding is that activation
bit-width matters far more than weight format: dropping acti-
vations from 16-bit to 8-bit costs 1–3% absolute WER, while
INT16 and FP16 activations are indistinguishable in accuracy.
Among 4-bit formats, NVFP4 W4A16 comes within 0.07% of
full-precision at6.4×compression, whereas MXFP4 fails un-
der standard PTQ and needs additional fixes. FP activation
paths are also the better hardware choice, since FP multipli-
ers are more area-efficient than INT at the same bit-width. We
close with a Pareto analysis and six practical guidelines for for-
mat selection across memory budgets from 20 to 80 MB.

1. Introduction
Transformer-based ASR models such as Whisper [1] have be-
come dominant for speech recognition, yet deploying them on
mobile phones, hearing aids, and edge devices remains chal-
lenging due to their memory footprint—39M to 1.5B parame-
ters depending on variant. Post-training quantization (PTQ) is
the most practical compression technique, and recent work has
pushed weight precision to 4-bit using GPTQ [2], AWQ [3], and
SmoothQuant [4], with floating-point formats NVFP4 [5] and
MXFP4 [6] extending these gains further. However, all prior
work targets decoder-only LLMs; Whisper’s encoder–decoder
architecture presents distinct challenges including stronger ac-
tivation outliers [7, 8] and mel-spectrogram inputs, making it
unclear whether the W4A16 paradigm transfers to encoder–
decoder ASR.
Two quantization design questions for on-device ASR re-
main open. First,does activation precision matter?ASR-
specific work has explored lower activation precision [9]
without isolating its effect on Whisper. Second,is inte-
ger or floating-point activation preferable?Most mobile
NPUs—including the Arm Ethos-U85 [10] and Qualcomm
Hexagon [11]—rely on INT8 or INT16, yet [12] shows FP mul-
tipliers occupy1.4×–2.4×lesschip area than INT at the same
bit-width (Figure 1), suggesting FP may be preferable.

We answer both questions through the first systematic PTQ
study of Whisper across 80+ configurations, with the following
contributions. First, we jointly sweep weight format, activation
format, and quantization granularity across integer (INT8/4/3)
and floating-point (FP8/4/NVFP4/MXFP4) regimes. Second,
we find thatactivation bit-width is the dominant accuracy fac-
tor: reducing activations from 16-bit to 8-bit costs 1–3% abso-
lute WER, while INT16 and FP16 are interchangeable. Third,
NVFP4 W4A16 achieves near-lossless compression (0.07%
WER gap at6.4×size reduction), and combined with the area
advantage of FP multipliers [12], this supports FP activation
datapaths for efficient on-device ASR. Fourth, fine-group quan-
tization introduces a dual-precision arithmetic requirement that
NPU designers should account for. Finally, we provide a Pareto
analysis and six deployment guidelines spanning memory bud-
gets from 20 to 80 MB.
2. Background and Related Work
2.1. Post-training quantization
PTQ [13, 14] converts pre-trained weights and activations
to lower-bit representations without retraining. Weight-only
(W4A16) and weight-and-activation (W4A8, W8A8) modes
trade off storage, bandwidth, and compute precision. Scaling
granularity—per-tensor, per-channel, or per-group (Gelements
per scale)—balances quantization error against scale overhead.
Large-magnitudeoutlier featuresin transformer activations [15]
dominate per-tensor scales and reduce effective precision.
2.2. Quantization formats
2.2.1. Integer formats (INT8, INT4, INT3)
Symmetric weight quantization uses scales= max(|x|)/q max
withˆx=s·clip(round(x/s), q min, qmax). Asymmetric
activation quantization adds a zero-pointzto handle non-
zero-centered post-GeLU distributions. INT8 is hardware-
accelerated on most NPUs/GPUs; INT4/3 provide higher com-
pression at the cost of reduced dynamic range.
2.2.2. FP8 format (E4M3)
FP8-E4M3 [16] (1 sign, 4 exponent, 3 mantissa bits) encodes
values as(−1) s·2e−bias·(1 +m/2 M ), maximum magnitude
448. Its non-uniform grid concentrates representable values
near zero while covering large-magnitude outliers—a key ad-
vantage over INT8 for heavy-tailed transformer weights.
2.2.3. FP4, NVFP4, and MXFP4
FP4 (E2M1)usesM=±{0,0.5,1,1.5,2,3,4,6}(16 val-
ues), which is insufficient alone; block-wise scaling is required.

2.3. Outlier mitigation schemes
We adopt SmoothQuant as the sole PTQ preprocessing method
because it operates jointly on weights and activations and is
format-agnostic, making it applicable across all integer and
floating-point configurations in our study. Weight-only methods
such as AWQ [3] and GPTQ [2] are orthogonal to our activation
precision sweep and are left for future work.

3. Methodology
3.1. Models and quantization targets
We evaluate Whispertiny.en(39M parameters, 4+4 lay-
ers, 384-dim, 6 heads) andbase.en(74M parameters, 6+6
layers, 512-dim, 8 heads). Quantization targets all linear lay-
ers:q/k/v/out projin encoder self-attention, decoder self-
attention, and decoder cross-attention, plusfc1/fc2in every
FFN block. Q, K, V projection outputs are additionally quan-
tized to simulate low-precision KV-cache. We apply FakeQuant
PTQ [20, 21]—injecting clipping and rounding error into the
forward pass while keeping arithmetic in FP32—for hardware-
agnostic comparison.
3.2. Dataset and evaluation
We use LibriSpeech [22]: 400 calibration samples from
the training splits (to avoid test-set leakage) and all sam-
ples fromtest-cleanandtest-otherfor evaluation.We
report results ontest-cleanand presenttest-other
in a separate section. WER uses standard text normaliza-
tion. All runs use deterministic beam decoding (beam size 3,
do sample=False); WER differences are attributable solely
to quantization. Hardware: NVIDIA RTX 3080/4090, PyTorch
2.4.1.

5. Guidelines for Edge ASR Deployment
Our results yield six practical guidelines for deploying Whisper
on edge devices and informing mobile NPU design.
(1) Choose FP over INT for activation datapaths.INT16
and FP16 activations produce virtually identical WER (Table 4),
yet FP multipliers are1.4×–2.4×smaller than INT at the same
bit-width [12]. FP datapaths therefore deliver more compute per
mm2 at no accuracy cost.
(2) Activation bit-width is your most important knob.
Dropping activations from 16-bit to 8-bit costs 1–3% absolute
WER, while finer weight group sizes add memory with little
accuracy gain. If you can only optimize one thing, keep activa-
tions at 16-bit.
(3) Block scale quality matters as much as bit-width for
4-bit formats.MXFP4’s power-of-two block scales require al-
gorithmic correction on top of standard PTQ [18]. NVFP4’s
finer FP8 block scales avoid this entirely, reaching 4.88% WER
onbase.enwith no extra steps.
(4) Fine group sizes require dual-precision hardware
support.AtG≤16, each matrix multiply requires a per-group
floating-point rescaling after the low-precision accumulation.
NPUs need to support FP scaling computation alongside the
MAC computation.
(5) For Whisper, memory is dominated by weights, not
KV cache.Whisper’s fixed 30-second window keeps the KV
cache below 5% of total memory, so weight quantization is
the primary lever for reducing footprint—unlike autoregressive
LLMs.
(6) When in doubt, quantize the larger model.Above
roughly 36 MB, quantizedbase.en(44.1 MB, 4.88% WER)
consistently outperformstiny.en(27.3 MB, 6.22%). A 40–
50 MB budget suffices for near-lossless accuracy.
6. Conclusion
We presented the first systematic PTQ study of Whisper across
integer and floating-point formats, and our results challenge two
widely held assumptions in on-device ASR design. First, ac-
tivation bit-width—not weight precision—is the dominant ac-
curacy factor: W4A16 outperforms W4A8 by 1–3% absolute
WER, showing that keeping activations at 16-bit matters more
than refining weight quantization. Second, INT16 and FP16
activations yield equivalent accuracy, and since FP multipliers
are1.4×–2.4×more area-efficient than INT [12], FP activation
paths are the better choice for efficient NPU design. Among 4-
bit formats, NVFP4 W4A16 reaches within 0.07% of FP32 at
6.4×compression. Future work covers larger Whisper variants,
QAT for INT3, and real-device latency measurements.
