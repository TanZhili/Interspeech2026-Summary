Abstract
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) typically relies on
utterance-level annotations, obscuring temporal emotional dy-
namics. While recent multi-task approaches achieve fine-
grained alignment via Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR),
they heavily depend on costly human transcripts. To overcome
these limitations, we propose a novel Conflict-Aware Pseudo-
Labeling method that derives frame-level auxiliary supervision
solely from acoustic signals. Using collaborative inference
across diverse data partitions, it adaptively segments speech into
consensus and conflict regions. We assign hard pseudo-labels
to consensus frames to capture local discriminative cues, while
introducing a global back-filling mechanism to rectify conflict
regions using the utterance-level label as a global prior, mitigat-
ing pseudo-label noise. Experiments on three datasets, IEMO-
CAP, EmoDB, and MELD, show that our approach achieves
state-of-the-art performance without auxiliary text or elaborate
structural redesigns.
Index Terms: speech emotion recognition, frame-level
pseudo-labels, multi-task learning, conflict-aware learning,
consistency-driven learning
1. Introduction
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) serves as a pivotal technol-
ogy in human-computer interaction, enabling systems to per-
ceive and respond to human emotional states. With the advent
of deep learning, Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) representa-
tions, such as wav2vec 2.0 [1] and HuBERT [2], have become
the predominant backbone for extracting robust acoustic fea-
tures. These models have significantly advanced the state-of-
the-art in various speech benchmarks [3, 4, 5].
Despite these successes, an inherent limitation persists in
standard SER paradigms: models are typically trained with
utterance-level labels that assign a single global emotion cat-
egory to an entire speech segment. However, human emotion
is inherently dynamic and transient; a single utterance often
contains neutral silences, abrupt emotional shifts, or ambigu-
ous transitions [6]. Relying solely on coarse-grained global su-
pervision forces the model to average out these local nuances,
inevitably leading to suboptimal discriminability [7, 8].
To capture fine-grained emotional cues, recent approaches
have explored diverse paradigms. Some works focus on struc-
tural redesigns to enhance local feature modeling [9, 10, 11].
In contrast, a prevalent paradigm integrates Automatic Speech
Recognition (ASR) as an auxiliary task, aiming to implicitly
leverage fine-grained linguistic information [12, 13, 14, 15].

However, incorporating ASR with explicit transcription super-
vision introduces additional annotation dependency and train-
ing overhead. In parallel, to fully exploit the acoustic modal-
ity, recent studies have explored signal-driven supervision re-
finement. For instance, recent studies leverage pseudo-labeling
strategies [16, 17] to generate dense targets, or explicitly model
label uncertainty [8] to handle subjective ambiguity. Neverthe-
less, without explicit conflict handling, naive pseudo-labels re-
main inherently noisy, as models frequently make conflicting
predictions on ambiguous frames.
To address these challenges, we propose a novel Conflict-
Aware Pseudo-Labeling framework. Our core insight is that,
while individual models may produce unreliable predictions,
consensus among models trained on diverse data views reveals
reliable local discriminative cues. Specifically, we design a col-
laborative inference mechanism to dynamically segment the se-
quence into consensus regions and conflict regions based on
prediction agreement. To prevent noise propagation, we in-
troduce a global back-filling strategy that supervises conflict
regions using the utterance-level label as a global prior. This
allows the model to learn fine-grained details from consensus
frames while maintaining global semantic consistency.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose a multi-granularity framework that constructs
frame-level auxiliary supervision from acoustic signals, elim-
inating the dependency on auxiliary text or elaborate struc-
tural redesigns.
• We introduce a Conflict-aware Label Assignment strategy
with a global back-filling mechanism, effectively mitigating
the temporal noise inherent in pseudo-labels.
• Experimental results in IEMOCAP, EmoDB, and MELD in-
dicate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance
on both acted and conversational datasets.

2.1. Collaborative Frame-level Prediction
To construct the frame-level pseudo-labels, we propose a col-
laborative inference mechanism based on internal data parti-
tioning. As illustrated in Figure 1a, this mechanism leverages
agreement among models trained on various data views to gen-
erate initial candidate predictions for subsequent pseudo-label
construction.

2.3. Multi-granularity Joint Supervision Framework
As illustrated in Figure 1c, we design a multi-granularity frame-
work to enforce discriminative feature learning by jointly opti-
mizing global and local objectives. The architecture employs a
pretrained HuBERT [2] as the Shared Encoder to extract the
frame-level feature sequenceE={e 1,e 2,...,e T}, which
feeds into two complementary branches:
Utterance-level Branch.To capture macro-level seman-
tics, we employ Attention Pooling to adaptively aggregate the
variable-length sequenceEinto a fixed-length utterance vector
based on temporal importance. This vector is mapped to the
emotion space, ensuring global class consistency under ground-
truth supervision.
Frame-level Branch.Operating directly onE, this branch
imposes frame-wise auxiliary constraints using the conflict-
aware pseudo-labels derived in Section 2.2. This fine-grained
supervision acts as a regularizer, preserving local discrimina-
tive cues that might otherwise be smoothed out during pooling.
Notably, since the auxiliary branch is discarded after train-
ing, this joint optimization enhances representation quality
without introducing additional computational overhead during
inference.

3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed framework on
three widely adopted SER datasets:
IEMOCAP[6]. An English interactive emotional dyadic
motion capture database containing approximately 12 hours
of audiovisual data from 10 actors. Consistent with previous
works [9, 10, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19], we merge happiness and ex-
citement into a single class, selecting a total of 5,531 utterances
from 4 categories (angry,happy,neutral,sad). The evalua-
tion is performed using 5-fold Leave-One-Session-Out (LOSO)
cross-validation.
EmoDB[20]. A German database comprising 535 acted
utterances from 10 professional speakers (5 male, 5 female).
It covers 7 emotion categories (anger,boredom,disgust,
fear,happiness,sadness, andneutral). We perform speaker-
independent 10-fold LOSO cross-validation.
MELD[21]. A multi-party conversational dataset collected
from the TV seriesFriends. It contains over 13,000 utterances
annotated with 7 emotions (anger,disgust,fear,joy,neutral,
sadness,surprise). We adhere to the official data splits defined
by the dataset.
3.2. Implementation Details
The experiments are implemented in PyTorch [22] and con-
ducted on four NVIDIA A40 GPUs. Specifically, to reduce
same-backbone confirmation bias, we employ emotion2vec-
base [18] for upstream frame-level pseudo-label generation,
while using HuBERT-base-ls960 [2] as the backbone encoder
for downstream joint training.
The network is optimized using the AdamW optimizer with
a fixed batch size of 64. The multi-granularity balance weightλ
and the conflict suppression coefficientαare set to 10 and 0.35,
respectively. The learning rate is set to2×10−4 for IEMOCAP
and EmoDB, and5×10 −5 for MELD. For upstream pseudo-
label generation, the number of internal partitionsKis set to
4, 9, and 5 for IEMOCAP, EmoDB, and MELD, respectively,
following the available LOSO structure when applicable.
3.3. Evaluation Metrics
To ensure a fair comparison with existing baselines, we adopt
specific metrics based on the data distribution:
• For IEMOCAP and EmoDB, we report Weighted Accuracy
(W A) and Unweighted Accuracy (UA). W A calculates the
overall accuracy based on the total number of samples, while
UA averages the accuracy of each class to account for class
imbalance.
• For MELD, due to its highly imbalanced nature, we report
the Weighted F1 score (WF1) in line with previous studies
[9, 10, 12, 18].
3.4. Main Results
To assess the effectiveness of the proposed framework, we com-
pare it with recent competitive methods. In particular, de-
spite the prevalence of multi-task paradigms assisted by ASR
[12, 14, 15, 16, 19], our results show that the proposed ap-
proach achieves improved performance using only acoustic sig-
nals. Performance comparisons on the IEMOCAP and MELD
datasets are presented in Table 1, while EmoDB results are de-
tailed in Table 2. Overall, our approach consistently achieves
superior performance across all three corpora. Specifically, it
achieves 78.10% W A and 79.07% UA in IEMOCAP, 97.30%
W A and 97.19% UA in EmoDB, and 55.48% WF1 in MELD.
These results highlight its practical advantage without the need
for elaborate structural modifications or auxiliary transcripts.

4. Conclusions
In this paper, we proposed a Conflict-Aware Pseudo-Labeling
framework for SER. Using collaborative inference, we derive
frame-level auxiliary supervision solely from acoustic signals.
The core contribution lies in the Conflict-aware Label Assign-
ment strategy, specifically the global back-filling mechanism,
which effectively mitigates label noise by rectifying conflict re-
gions with utterance-level priors. Results on three datasets show
that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance while
maintaining a simple architecture without auxiliary text. Code
