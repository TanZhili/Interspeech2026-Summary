BACON: Boundary-Aware Convolution for Streaming Conformer Models
Hainan Xu, Kunal Dhawan, Dongji Gao, Jagadeesh Balam
NVIDIA Corporation
hainanx@nvidia.com
Abstract
We proposeBoundary-Aware CONvolution(BACON), a drop-
in replacement for causal convolution in streaming sequence
tasks that leverage chunk-based Conformers. Streaming Con-
formers typically pair chunked attention with causal convo-
lution to prevent future information leakage. We show that
causal convolution is unnecessarily restrictive: within a chunk,
all frames are already available, so the convolution kernel can
safely look ahead up to the chunk boundary without violat-
ing the streaming constraint. BACON exploits this by splitting
the depthwise convolution channels into a causal group and a
boundary-aware bidirectional group, yielding a wider effective
receptive field than the causal baseline while preserving param-
eter count. Across single-speaker Automatic Speech Recogni-
tion (ASR), multi-speaker ASR, and speech-to-text translation
tasks with two model architectures, BACON improves model
accuracy while maintaining comparable latency.
Index Terms: streaming speech recognition, conformer,
boundary-aware convolution, chunk-based processing
1. Introduction
Real-world speech applications (e.g., voice assistants, real-
time captioning, and simultaneous translation) typically require
streamingprocessing: instead of waiting for the complete in-
put audio, the model produces outputs incrementally as audio
arrives, within a bounded latency budget. Among streaming
architectures,chunk-basedmodels [1] accumulate a short win-
dow of frames before processing them as a unit, trading a small
fixed delay for richer within-window acoustic context than fully
frame-to-frame causal approaches.
The Conformer [2, 3] is a prominent architecture in mod-
ern speech models, combining self-attention [4] with depth-
wise separable convolutions [5] which enables the model to
capture both global dependencies and local temporal fea-
tures. For chunk-based streaming, two standard modifications
are typically applied: (1)chunked attention, which restricts
each frame’s attention to prevent access to future chunks, and
(2)causal convolution, which restricts the depthwise kernel to
current and past frames only. Together, these modifications en-
sure that model computation does not depend on future chunks.
We argue that the second modification, i.e. the use of a
causal convolution, is overly conservative. In a chunk-based
system, all frames within a chunk are processed together; there-
fore, a convolution kernel can look aheadwithin the chunkwith-
out violating the streaming constraint, while causal convolution
unnecessarily discards this available context. Some existing
works try to improve on this aspect, e.g. DCConv [6] adapts
convolutional context to the active chunk, while SSCFormer [7]
introduces additional parameters to exploit the extra contexts.
This work introducesBoundary-Aware CONvolution(BA-
CON), which splits the depthwise channels into two groups: a
causalgroup with full backward context, and abidirectional
group whose right context is confined to the current chunk. For
convolution with kernel sizek, the aggregate effective recep-
tive field spans[−(k−1),(k−1)/2], strictly wider than the
causal baseline’s[−(k−1),0], while using the same number of
parameters. The key contributions of this work are:
• We identify that causal convolution is unnecessarily strict
in chunk-based streaming Conformers and that within-chunk
look-ahead is streaming-safe.
• We propose BACON, a split-channel design that widens
the effective receptive field without breaking chunk-based
streaming constraints and without adding parameters.
• We demonstrate improvements across three tasks (single-
speaker Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), multi-
speaker ASR, and speech-to-text translation) across two
model families (CHATandRNN-T).
2. Background
2.1. Chunk-Limited Attention
Typically conformers use standard self-attention, where each
frame compares against all frames in the full input, which is
incompatible with streaming. To adapt conformers for stream-
ing,chunk-limited attention[1, 8] partitions the input into non-
overlapping chunks and restricts each frame’s attention context
to its own chunk and prior chunks. This avoids dependency on
future chunks and enables chunk-wise streaming.
2.2. Causal Convolution
Standard streaming Conformers replace the symmetric depth-
wise kernel with acausal convolutionthat sees only current and
past frames. This guarantees no future chunk information is
accessed and is therefore sufficient for streaming. However, it
is stricter than the chunked streaming constraint requires: in the
chunk-based setting, frames within the same chunk are all avail-
able simultaneously, so right context within the chunk is already
present at processing time. In contrast, BACON focuses on a
parameter-neutral split-channel replacement for causal convo-
lution that preserves full causal history in part of the channels
while adding chunk-confined right context in the remainder.
3. Method
Our goal is to allow right-context convolution up to the chunk
boundary. A straightforward approach is to replace causal con-
volution with a fully symmetric bidirectional kernel (similar to
[6]). However, near the right boundary of each chunk, the right

3.1. Dual-Context Channel Design
We increase convolutional receptive field to the future whenever
possible while preserving past receptive field. This is achieved
with a split-channel design. Theddepthwise channels are di-
vided into two equal groups with different padding strategies:
•Causal group(d/2channels):
Requires left zero-padding of lengthk−1only once at the
beginning of the utterance. In all other places, it accesses full
past context without chunk boundary restrictions.
•Bidirectional group(d/2channels):
Utilizes a mixed padding strategy withp= (k−1)/2. While
its left context crosses chunk boundaries freely similar to the
causal group; for right context,pzeros is padded at the end
of each chunk, to be used for right context of convolutions
without breaking chunk boundaries.
Since depthwise convolution applies an independent kernel
per channel, the two groups have fully separate learned weights
despite residing in the same convolution module. No parameter
sharing occurs between the causal and bidirectional channels.

5.2. Convolution Benefits from Right Context Indepen-
dently of Attention
In a chunk-based Conformer, both attention and convolution
operate on the same frames, and within each chunk the atten-
tion module already has bidirectional access up to the chunk

6. Conclusion
We show that causal convolution in chunk-based streaming
Conformers is unnecessarily restrictive, and we propose BA-
CON, which splits depthwise channels into a causal group and
a boundary-aware bidirectional group. This widens receptive
field without changing model architecture or parameter count.
Across three tasks and two model families, BACON improves
accuracy while maintaining (and in our measurements, slightly
reducing) emission latency. For future work, we plan to apply
BACON to larger-scale datasets and other speech-to-text tasks,
including streaming speech LLM systems.
