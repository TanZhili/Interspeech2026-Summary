Abstract
This paper presents the Fosafer team’s system developed for the
TidyV oice2026 Cross-lingual Speaker Verification Challenge.
To mitigate the over-reliance of current models on language-
specific features, we propose a robust system based on orthog-
onal feature projection and manifold-constrained neural Prob-
abilistic Linear Discriminant Analysis (PLDA). For front-end
feature extraction, orthogonal projection decouples redundant
self-supervised features and dynamically integrates language-
agnostic components into speaker embeddings. For back-end
scoring, we employ a manifold-constrained neural PLDA to
perform discriminative optimization while adhering to proba-
bilistic generative constraints. To optimize PLDA training, we
design a dynamic hard sample mining strategy for multilingual
scenarios. Our final fused system achieved equal error rates
(EERs) of 1.39% and 1.95% on the two test tracks, respectively,
securing 1st place among 42 participating teams.
Index Terms: cross-lingual speaker verification, orthogonal
feature projection, manifold-constrained neural PLDA
1. Introduction
Automatic speaker verification (ASV) has become increasingly
vital as a core technology for ensuring biometric security, ac-
cess control, and personalized services in smart devices [1]. The
rapid advancement of ASV is largely attributed to the continu-
ous momentum from standardized evaluations, such as NIST
SRE [2] and V oxSRC [3], along with the availability of large-
scale open-source training datasets like the V oxCeleb [4, 5] and
V oxBlink [6, 7] series. However, mainstream ASV training
often ignores language imbalance. This causes models to en-
tangle linguistic cues with speaker identities, severely limiting
cross-lingual generalization. To address these challenges, the
TidyV oice 2026 Challenge has been introduced [8]. Utilizing a
large-scale multilingual corpus derived from Mozilla Common
V oice, this challenge aims to encourage the speech community
to develop robust, language-agnostic ASV systems.
Recent research has extensively explored front-end feature
extraction and back-end scoring to address diverse ASV chal-
lenges. For front-end extraction, self-supervised models such
as WavLM [9, 10] and w2v-BERT 2.0 [11] have yielded signif-
icant performance breakthroughs. Driven by pretext tasks such
as masked prediction and contrastive learning, these models ex-
tract rich acoustic and linguistic content from massive unlabeled

data. These highly generalizable representations significantly
bolster ASV performance across diverse environments. For
back-end scoring, cosine similarity remains the mainstream ap-
proach in ASV . To surpass its performance, recent studies have
integrated PLDA into deep learning frameworks. Neural PLDA
innovatively transforms all traditional statistical parameters, in-
cluding the core scoring matricesPandQ, into mutually inde-
pendent learnable weights within a neural network [12]. While
enabling end-to-end optimization, this approach severs proba-
bilistic dependencies between matrices and sacrifices the rigor-
ous mathematical constraints of the generative paradigm. Alter-
natively, the recent PLDA-diag achieves performance surpass-
ing that of cosine scoring on modern features by constraining
the within-class covariance [13]. However, focusing primarily
on channel compensation limits the cross-lingual performance
of these PLDA paradigms.
In this paper, we present our winning system for the
TidyV oice 2026 Challenge. For front-end extraction, we pro-
pose a geometric decoupling strategy using orthogonal pro-
jection. By explicitly decomposing the WavLM representa-
tion into acoustically redundant and complementary orthogo-
nal components based on speaker embeddings, we leverage its
representational power while discarding linguistic interference.
For back-end modeling, we introduce a manifold-constrained
Neural PLDA. Instead of unconstrained parameter learning, this
model defines learnable eigenvalue vectors that uniquely drive
the generation of scoring matricesPandQ. This constrains
the score to remain interpretable as a PLDA LLR in the trans-
formed diagonal space during discriminative optimization. Fur-
thermore, a dynamic hard sample mining strategy filters cross-
lingual target and mono-lingual non-target trials, explicitly forc-
ing the separation of linguistic content from identity informa-
tion. Integrated with Adaptive Score Normalization (AS-Norm)
and score fusion, our system achieved an EER of 1.39% and a
minDCF of 0.10 in Task 1, and an EER of 1.95% and a minDCF
of 0.06 in Task 2, securing 1st place among 42 teams.

2.3. Manifold-Constrained Neural PLDA
As a classic generative model in ASV , PLDA aims to sepa-
rate speaker identity information from channel variations within
speaker embeddings. The model assumes that an observed
feature vector is generated by a linear combination of la-
tent speaker variables and Gaussian-distributed channel noise.
Building upon this probabilistic framework, PLDA formulates
the verification task as a hypothesis test. It computes the log-
likelihood ratio (LLR) to evaluate whether the enrollment and
test utterances originate from the same speaker. Because the
internal latent variables follow Gaussian distributions, the LLR
calculation transforms the exponential terms into the following
quadratic scoring function:

Traditional generative PLDA relies on the Expectation
Maximization (EM) algorithm to estimate fixed parameters,
complicating direct optimization for the verification metric.
To enable discriminative training, existing neural PLDA ap-
proaches treatPandQas independent weight matrices updated
via backpropagation. However, this practice completely ignores
the inherent algebraic constraints established betweenPandQ
through the covariance matrices. Consequently, the model de-
viates from the valid probabilistic generative manifold during
parameter updates, rendering it highly susceptible to overfitting
when trained on limited data.
To address this manifold deviation, we propose the
manifold-constrained neural PLDA method illustrated in Fig-
ure 2. This scheme strictly reconstructs the PLDA diagonaliza-
tion process within the neural network, enforcing probabilistic
generative constraints on the model parameters. Specifically,
instead of directly learning the parameter matricesPandQ,
we define a learnable parameter setΘ ={µ,A,ψ}. Here,

Our manifold-constrained neural PLDA method reduces the

Furthermore, we propose a dynamic hard sample mining
strategy. Instead of conventional random sampling, we explic-
itly leverage language labels to construct adversarial pairs, forc-
ing the network to learn language-invariant identity representa-
tions. This strategy consists of two core dimensions:
a. Hard Cross-lingual Target Mining: Cross-lingual utter-
ance pairs from the same speaker that yield the lowest current
scores are selected, forcing the network to ignore prosodic and
phonetic variations.
b. Hard Mono-lingual Non-target Mining: Mono-lingual
utterance pairs from different speakers that yield the highest
current scores are selected, strictly penalizing the model for
false acceptances caused by shared linguistic traits.
These strategies force the model to suppress linguistic inter-
ference while preserving speaker identity, ensuring robustness
in complex multilingual scenarios.
2.4. Score Fusion
To further enhance system robustness and leverage the com-
plementary strengths of various methods, we apply logistic re-
gression for linear score-level fusion. Specifically, the optimal
weights and bias term are fitted on the development set to map
the individual system scores into a calibrated final LLR.
3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
The training data is categorized into three distinct subsets:
a. Base Dataset: The base subset is designed to establish a
robust initial feature space, comprising approximately 200,000
speakers. It primarily consists of mainstream open-source cor-
pora (e.g., V oxCeleb [4, 5], V oxBlink [6, 7], and CN-Celeb
[21, 22]) augmented with internal data.
b. Extended Dataset: The extended dataset expands the
Base Dataset to 600,000 speakers by incorporating proprietary
speakers sourced via compliant commercial licenses and pub-
lic platforms. All data is strictly cleaned and deduplicated to
ensure diverse coverage across devices, channels.
c. Multilingual Dataset: The multilingual corpus combines
the official TidyV oice training set with a custom-collected pro-
prietary set of 2,000 speakers. Additionally, applying language
and dialect identification models to the extended dataset yielded
a final subset of 10,000 multilingual speakers.
3.2. Implementation Details
In the first stage of front-end training, the model is pre-trained
using data from 200,000 speakers. The input features are 80-
dimensional FBank features extracted with a window length of
25 ms and a frame shift of 10 ms. During training, data aug-
mentation is applied by simulating reverberation with the RIR
dataset [23] and adding noise with the MUSAN dataset [24].
We employ the SGD optimizer with a momentum of 0.9 and
a weight decay of1×10 −4. The input frame length for each
sample is fixed at 200 frames. The learning rate incorporates
2 warm-up epochs and follows an exponential decay strategy,
decaying from 0.01 to5×10 −5. The margin parameter is grad-
ually increased from 0 to 0.1. This stage is trained for a total of
60 epochs. The training data is sourced from the Base Dataset
described in Section 3.1.
The second front-end training stage replaces traditional
large-margin fine-tuning with a curriculum learning paradigm,
which significantly outperforms standard fine-tuning on large-
scale datasets. Therefore, we include all 600,000 speakers for
fine-tuning and re-initialize the weights of the projection layer.

The input frame length is increased from 200 to 600, and the
margin parameter is elevated from 0.1 to 0.2. The learning
rate is initialized at1×10 −4 and decays to a final value of
2.5×10 −5. This training stage lasts for 5 epochs. The fi-
nal model is obtained by averaging several intermediate check-
points that yield the best performance on massive open-source
and private test sets. The training data is sourced from the Ex-
tended Dataset described in Section 3.1.
The third front-end training stage trains the orthogonal pro-
jection fusion architecture. During this process, the parameters
of both the WavLM and ResNet branches are frozen. The model
is trained using the SGD optimizer with a momentum of 0.9 and
a weight decay of1×10 −4, coupled with an exponential decay
learning rate scheduler for 10 epochs. The initial learning rate
is set to1×10 −4, decaying to2.5×10 −5, while the margin
parameter is consistently maintained at 0.2. The training data is
sourced from the Multilingual Dataset described in Section 3.1.
In the first stage of back-end training, speaker embeddings
extracted by the fully trained front-end model are used to train a
generative PLDA model. The parameter estimation of the initial
PLDA model is iteratively optimized using the EM algorithm.
We set the number of EM iterations to 5. During the M-step
of each iteration, a covariance diagonalization constraint is en-
forced, where the off-diagonal elements of the updated within-
class covariance matrix are set to 0, retaining only the diago-
nal elements. The training data is sourced from the Extended
Dataset described in Section 3.1.
The second back-end training stage aims to migrate the
pre-trained generative model to a manifold-constrained neural
PLDA architecture for further training. We apply simultaneous
diagonalization to the within-class and between-class covari-
ance matrices obtained in the first stage, solving for the trans-
formation matrixAand its corresponding eigenvalue vector
ψ. We initialize the learnable parameters with the global mean
µ, transformation matrixA, and log-parameterized eigenvalues
logψ, followed by end-to-end discriminative fine-tuning using
the Adam optimizer and a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss
function. Training proceeds for 10 epochs with a learning rate
of10 −4. Each epoch utilizes a dynamic hard negative mining
strategy to generate 500,000 balanced sample pairs. To ensure
sufficient sample difficulty, we generate three times the required
number of candidate pairs and strictly select the hardest subset
for training. The training data is sourced from the Multilingual
Dataset described in Section 3.1.
In the back-end scoring phase, we implement AS-Norm
method for PLDA scores using the TidyV oice development set
[25]. We calibrate the scores by selecting the top 200 imposter
scores and finally apply score fusion techniques to further en-
hance system performance.

development EER to 0.56% and yielding 1.39% and 1.95% on

4. Conclusion
This paper presents the winning system developed by the Fos-
afer team for the TidyV oice 2026 Cross-lingual Speaker Veri-
fication Challenge. To mitigate language cue interference, our
front-end employs a geometric orthogonal projection strategy.
This extracts components complementary to acoustic features,
capturing cross-lingually invariant identity clues. For the back-
end, our manifold-constrained neural PLDA architecture main-
tains valid probabilistic generative properties during end-to-end
discriminative optimization. Integrated with a dynamic hard
sample mining strategy tailored for multilingual scenarios, the
system significantly suppresses linguistic interference. Our fi-
nal submission achieved EERs of 1.39% and 1.95% on the two
test sets, securing first place among 42 participating teams.
