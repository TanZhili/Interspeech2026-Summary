Abstract
This paper proposes DroFiT (Drone Frequency lightweight
Transformer), a single-microphone speech enhancement net-
work for severe drone ego-noise. A Full/Sub-band encoder-
decoder compresses the noisy input at different ratios, where
the sub-band path focuses on low-frequency detail and the full-
band path retains global spectral context. A Pre-TCN(Temporal
Convolutional Network) captures the harmonic and stationary
structure of drone noise and a frequency-wise Transformer at-
tends over the concatenated Full/Sub band tokens, allowing the
Full/Sub-band features to complement each other in a shared
attention space. The model is evaluated on the V oiceBank-
DEMAND dataset mixed with recorded drone noise. Compared
to drone-oriented baselines such as DCU-net and SMoLnet-
T, DroFiT achieves enhancement performance while reducing
computation by 15–26× and 9–15×, respectively. With 168k
parameters, it supports incremental(frame-wise streaming) in-
ference and is suitable for resource-constrained UA Vs.
Index Terms: Speech enhancement, drone noise, frequency-
wise Transformer, lightweight architecture
1. Introduction
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UA Vs) are widely employed for ap-
plications including parcel delivery, mountain rescue, and dis-
aster monitoring [1]. Although UA Vs primarily rely on vision-
based sensing, interest is growing in using acoustic information
to enhance situational awareness and interaction [2, 3]. How-
ever, wideband, periodic propeller- and motor-induced ego-
noise causes speech to be captured in a severely degraded form,
necessitating effective noise reduction for UA V audition. Re-
searchers have explored various mitigation methods, ranging
from conventional multichannel techniques like DoA estima-
tion and beamforming [4, 5] to single-channel deep-learning ap-
proaches that does not require an additional hardware [6, 7, 8].
Single-channel models generally fall into time-domain
[6, 9] and time-frequency (T-F) domain approaches. While T-F
methods commonly rely on magnitude-only masking [10, 11],
recent attention has shifted to complex-domain modeling that
considers both magnitude and phase information[12, 13]. By
jointly estimating real and imaginary components, complex-
domain methods better capture the periodic and harmonic char-
acteristics of UA V noise [14] and improve waveform recon-
struction quality in speech enhancement(SE) systems [13].
Despite these advances, deploying SE models on low-
power UA V platforms remains challenging due to limited com-

Existing architectures struggle to satisfy these constraints

ment but imposes heavy computational and memory demands.
SMoLnet-T [7] achieves a low parameter count but relies on
chunk-based processing, which requires storing large feature
maps—often overflowing limited on-chip memory and trigger-
ing expensive external memory access. While incremental (i.e.,
frame-wise streaming) models like DCCRN [12] or DTLN [20]
aim to minimize latency and activation storage, their parameter
counts still exceed realistic on-chip memory capacity, leading
to external weight access.
To overcome these limitations, we propose DroFiT, which
decouples temporal and spectral modeling to enable memory-
efficient UA V speech enhancement. In the temporal domain,
DroFiT utilizes a TCN for incremental inference with mem-
ory reuse; despite the TCN’s limited receptive field, a moderate
temporal context is sufficient for effective speech enhancement
[21, 22]. In the frequency domain, DroFiT employs sub-band
processing to improve computational efficiency while preserv-
ing speech-dominant cues [23, 24], and uses a Transformer to
model frequency correlations critical for UA V noise suppres-
sion [25]. Finally, DroFiT jointly estimates magnitude and
phase for reconstruction. As illustrated in Fig. 1, DroFiT sup-
presses dominant UA V ego-noise and recovers clean speech,
while reducing activation and weight memory footprints to limit
external memory access for energy-efficient UA V deployment.

2. DroFiT: Proposed Framework
In this section, we describe the proposed DroFiT architecture
in detail. First, the model takes the input STFT (spectrogram)
and applies frequency-axis compression to obtain a compact
spectral representation. Second, the compressed features are
refined by anL-layer Pre-TCN, and processed by anN-layer
frequency-wise Transformer with parallel Full-/Sub-band paths,
unlike serial or cross-connected designs [26] (Fig. 2-(a)). Third,
the features are decoded, and anM-layer Post-TCN provides
temporal refinement. Finally, a Mask Generation Block predicts
a complex mask that is applied to the input STFT to reconstruct
enhanced speech.
2.1. Full-Band Encoder & Decoder
As illustrated in Fig. 2, the encoder processes the input
through a full-band path. The full-band encoder is composed
of Conv1D-based C(T)NA(Conv(T)-Norm-Activation) blocks,
which operate independently along the time axis while com-
pressing features along the frequency axis. A global convolu-
tion layer (GConv) is appended to capture long-range spectral
dependencies. The decoder reconstructs the enhanced signal by
integrating both encoder representations and the latent module
through learnable skip connections, as shown in Fig. 2. Un-
like fixed skip connections, these projections are parameterized
and optimized during training, enabling the network to adap-
tively balance local sub-band details and global full-band in-
formation. This design not only improves the reconstruction
quality but also provides greater flexibility in fusing multi-scale
spectral features.
2.2. Sub-Band Encoder & Decoder
As shown in Fig. 2-(a), the sub-band encoder divides the in-
put spectrogram into five groups along the frequency axis and
transforms them into magnitude representations [24]. Specif-
ically, the 513 frequency bins are partitioned into groups of
32–32–64–128–257 bins following a Mel-like allocation. Each
group is processed through the path illustrated in Fig. 2-(c),
where a lightweight 1D convolutional layer is applied for group-
wise compression, in contrast to the more complex operations
of the full-band encoder. The sub-band decoder follows a struc-
ture similar to the full-band decoder, with each group connected
via learnable skip connections. Restoration is performed inde-
pendently for each group using a fully connected (FC) layer to
recover the original feature size.
2.3. Pre-TCN
Unlike generic environmental noise, UA V propulsion noise ex-
hibits a quasi-stationary harmonic structure induced by rotat-
ing blades. Although the fundamental frequency varies with
RPM, its temporal evolution is significantly slower than speech
articulatory dynamics. Motivated by this property, we intro-
duce the Pre-TCN module to model slowly drifting harmonic
interference prior to spectral attention. By stabilizing tempo-
rally correlated drone components, the subsequent frequency-
wise Transformer can focus more effectively on non-stationary
speech cues.
In the Full-band path, a TCN refines temporal features and
captures the quasi-stationary characteristics of propulsion noise.
As drone noise structures are mainly handled in Full-band
path, the Sub-band branch—focusing on speech-dominant low-
frequency regions—applies a lightweight C-Linear (channel-
wise projection) to maintain efficiency without sacrificing criti-
cal speech information. Finally, an F-Linear (linear transforma-
tion) layer performs inter-band integration.
2.4. Frequency-wise Transformer
The core component of DroFiT is the Frequency-wise Trans-
former, which applies Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) ex-
clusively along the frequency axis to maintain linear time com-
plexity with respect to the input duration. We incorporate a
learnable frequency positional encoding and perform a single
self-attention over concatenated full-band and sub-band tokens
derived from the same input using different compression ra-
tios. This design captures both intra-branch spectral dependen-
cies and cross-branch interactions, enabling effective fusion of
global contextual information from the full-band path with fine-
grained low-frequency details obtained through non-uniform
compression in the sub-band path.

To mitigate inter-band interference, the full-band and sub-
band paths are structurally separated. For computational ef-
ficiency and reduced complexity, the conventional FFN(Feed-
Forward Network) is replaced with a Conv-FFN composed
of pointwise(PW) and depthwise(DW) convolutions, operating
independently within each path. Furthermore, to accommo-
date extreme resource constraints, DroFiT-Lite adopts Linear
Attention[27], further reducing computational cost while mini-
mizing performance degradation. Detailed analysis is provided
in Section 4.
2.5. Post-TCN
Following frequency-domain modeling, DroFiT employs a
TCN [22] to capture temporal dependencies. Since the pre-
ceding modules sufficiently refine the structural characteristics
of drone noise—effectively acting in a V AD-like manner—this
stage focuses primarily on temporal scale modeling rather than
additional noise characterization. To reduce computational cost
and parameter count, the Full-band energy is compressed via
average pooling before being fed into the TCN. This design
leverages the fact that frequency-axis modeling has already
been handled by the preceding Transformer, allowing the TCN
to operate more efficiently on temporal dynamics.
2.6. Mask Generation Block
Finally, DroFiT generates a complex-valued mask through the
Mask Generation Block (MGB), as illustrated in Fig. 2. In-
stead of directly summing the Full-band and Sub-band outputs,
the two representations are concatenated and processed via a
Conv2D layer, reducing the channel dimension to two corre-
sponding to the real and imaginary components. A Tanh ac-
tivation is then applied, and the result is multiplied with the
Post-TCN output to produce the final complex mask.
The generated complex maskM(f,t)is applied to the
noisy input STFTX(f,t)through complex multiplication, i.e.,
ˆS(f,t) =M(f,t)⊙X(f,t), followed by inverse STFT to re-
construct the enhanced speech signal.

3. Evaluation Settings
3.1. Dataset Preparation
We record drone noise from a DJI Flip UA V during hover-
ing and segment it with fixed strides. We create simulated
mixtures in pyroomacoustics by mixing clean utterances from
V oiceBank-DEMAND [29] with the recorded drone noise. We
fix all utterances to 5 seconds and sample at 16 kHz, using a
1024-point FFT and a 512-sample hop. The training, valida-
tion, and test sets contain 7200, 800, and 810 simulated mix-
tures. Training/validation use SNRs from -5 to -25 dB, with -30
dB only for testing. We also collect real-world recordings using
an external loudspeaker placed away from the hovering UA V
with microphone. We record approximately 650 s of speech
per speaker (male and female) and segment it with a 1 seconds
stride, yielding 1300 real test samples.
3.2. Baseline Models & Experimental Settings
DCU-net is jointly evaluated for drone noise suppression [6],
where it is reported to be particularly well suited to drone noise
conditions. SMoLnet-T is a lightweight speech enhancement
model specifically designed to leverage drone noise character-
istics, and is included for comparison with drone-aware archi-
tectures [7]. Since DroFiT is designed as an incremental, we ad-
ditionally include DTLN[20] and DCCRN[12] as representative
incremental speech enhancement models for structural compar-
ison.

3.3. Evaluation Metrics
Model performance is evaluated in terms of speech enhance-
ment quality and computational efficiency. On simulated
datasets, objective performance is assessed using SI-SDR [30]
for waveform reconstruction accuracy, ESTOI [31] for speech
intelligibility, and NB-/WB-PESQ [32] for perceptual qual-
ity. For real-recorded test data, perceptual quality is assessed
using DNSMOS (P.835)[33], which provides SIG, BAK, and
OVRL scores. Computational efficiency is measured in terms
of parameter count, peak runtime memory per processing unit
(chunk/frame), and MACs.
4. Experimental Results
4.1. Structural Effect of Pre-TCN on Drone Noise
To examine the effect of Pre-TCN, we visualize the full-band
representation before and after the module (Fig. 4). Fig. 4-
(a) shows the noisy input spectrogram, and (b) presents the
representation fed into Pre-TCN. After passing through Pre-
TCN, as shown in Fig. 4-(c), the UA V ego-noise components
become more distinct, forming clearer harmonic bands across
frequency. Compared to the input representation, the drone-
induced interference appears more pronounced and organized,
making its spectral pattern easier to identify. This enhanced
visibility of drone noise provides a more informative represen-
tation for the subsequent frequency-wise attention module.

4.2. Ablation Study
To quantify the effect of each module, we ablate the Sub-band
path, conv-FFN, and Post-TCN by removing one component at
a time from DroFiT-base while keeping the rest of the architec-
ture and training protocol unchanged. We also include two w/o
Pre-TCN variants with different receptive fields (see Table 2) to
rule out receptive field effects (Table 2). As shown in Table 1,
removing the Sub-band path and Pre-TCN causes the largest
and the second largest performance drops, respectively, indicat-
ing that the dual-path design and drone-aware temporal align-
ment prior are key contributors. In addition, conv-FFN provides
a modest but consistent improvement while reducing parameter
count and MACs, demonstrating a favorable quality–efficiency
trade-off. Post-TCN further improves the objective scores, sup-
porting its role as a lightweight temporal refinement stage.
4.3. Objective Evaluation
As shown in Table 1, DroFiT delivers strong performance
across all SNRs, particularly under extreme low-SNR con-
ditions (below−25dB), while using far fewer parameters
and MACs than DCU-net and fewer MACs than SMoLNet-
T. Fig. 3 corroborates these trends; at−20dB, DroFiT better
preserves speech-dominant time–frequency structures than the
competitive baselines (DTLN and SMoLNet-T). Table 3 fur-
ther shows that DCU-net achieves stronger noise suppression
(BAK), whereas DroFiT yields higher perceived speech and
overall quality (SIG/OVL) under drone-dominant noise with re-
duced model size and computation.
Although DroFiT utilizes more MACs than DTLN, its
smaller parameter count enables on-chip storage, which is criti-
cal given that DRAM access energy is200×higher than a MAC
[19]. To further reduce computation, we adopt Linear Attention
in DroFiT-Lite, reducing MACs relative to DroFiT-base with
minimal performance degradation. DroFiT-Quant, the weight-
only INT8 quantized version of DroFiT-Lite that keeps biases in
floating point, reduces storage with negligible performance loss
(Table 3), demonstrating suitability for embedded deployment.
5. Conclusion
We presented DroFiT, a lightweight incremental speech en-
hancement model for UA V ego-noise suppression. With a
drone-aware Pre-TCN and frequency-wise attention with dual-
path fusion plus a lightweight TCN back-end, DroFiT achieves
competitive quality and intelligibility under tight compute
and memory budgets. Experiments under extremely low-
SNR drone-noise conditions demonstrate a favorable perfor-
mance–efficiency trade-off over heavier baselines. Future work
will extend DroFiT to broader UA V scenarios and integrate
downstream tasks such as ASR and KWS.
