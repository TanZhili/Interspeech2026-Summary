Improving Adversarial Robustness in Spoken Language Identification through
Self-Defensive Distillation
Spandan Dey
Samsung R&D Institute India-Bangalore
Bengaluru, Karnataka, India
spandan.dey@samsung.com
Abstract
Robustness against adversarial attacks is majorly explored
for security-critical speech applications, such as speaker veri-
fication and speech recognition. Concerning their on-growing
multilingual expansion, we present one of the first exten-
sive studies on developing adversarial robustness for spoken
language identification (LID). We show the vulnerability of
state-of-the-art LID models under widely-explored white-box
gradient-based adversarial attacks. We then propose a novel
adversarial robust LID framework, Self-Defensive Adversarial
Re-Training (SDART). It expands the conventional adversarial
re-training with adversarial sample mining, teacher-free defen-
sive distillation using dynamic online label smoothing, and lin-
guistic consistency regularization. Across multiple databases
and architectures, SDART outperforms several conventional ad-
versarial robustness methods, improving performance on both
genuine and adversarially attacked samples.
Index Terms: Spoken language identification, Adversarial at-
tack, Adversarial re-training, Defensive distillation.
1. Introduction
Contrary to the commonly known replay attacks [1] or text-
to-speech (TTS), voice conversion (VC) based synthetic at-
tacks [2, 3],adversarial attackssystematically alter input sam-
ples with small, human-imperceptible systematic perturbations
that can lead to inevitable misclassifications [4]. From the initial
conceptualization by Szedey et al. [5] for computer vision appli-
cations, over the past decades, recognizing the threats posed by
doctored misclassifications from these applications, researchers
across fields have developed a wide variety of adversarial at-
tacks and their mitigation approaches.
Naturally, in speech domain, the majority of adversarial
robustness studies are centered around security-critical appli-
cations, such as automatic speaker verification (ASV) [6] for
voice biometrics or automatic speech recognition (ASR) for
voice commands [7]. Kan et al. [8] proposed segment-wise
score distribution matching approach to detect targeted black-
box adversarial attacks in ASV . In [9], diffusion-based adver-
sarial purification (DAP) was proposed to remove the perturba-
tion from the ASV inputs. A learnable mask detector (LMD)
approach was proposed in [8] to detect attacked ASV inputs us-
ing score distribution mismatch. Wang et al. [10, 11] introduced
the AdvSV dataset to improve ASV robustness against over-the-
air attacks using a contrastive domain-aligned one-class classi-
fier. On the ASR side, in [12], low-frequency energy of audio
input is used to detect the adversarial inputs for the wav2vec
2.0 [13] and Whisper architecture [14]. Gao et al. [15] proposed
speech-based gradient optimization for developing ASR robust-
ness against transferable white-box attacks. In [16], a short uni-
versal acoustic adversarial segment was appended to Whisper
inputs to compel it to conduct translation even though its prompt
was set for speech transcription. A cross-lingual adversarial at-
tack was proposed in [17] to force a multilingual ASR model to
generate a distorted transcript in the target language.
With the ongoing trend of extending multilingual support
for different speech applications, we believe a new threat is
emerging, in which the front-end spoken language identifica-
tion (LID) module can be subjected to adversarial attacks. Ad-
versaries can simply alter the language prediction of the input
speech by attacking the LID module, leading to catastrophic
performances for the diverse multilingual backends. Although
LID development has shown impressive progress over the past
few years [18, 19, 20], surprisingly, in the literature, we could
not find major LID-related studies dedicated to adversarial at-
tacks. To our knowledge, this work presents one of the first
studies to analyze the sensitivity of standalone state-of-the-art
LID models to a range of widely explored adversarial attacks.
Thereafter, we focus on developing effective adversarial attack
robustness for LID frameworks. Across diverse LID architec-
tures and databases, our proposed adversarial robustness ap-
proach has consistently outperformed several widely popular
robustness approaches. In this line, the major contribution of
our work is summarized as follows:
• We launch multiple well-known adversarial attacks against
state-of-the-art standalone LID architectures, ECAPA-
TDNN [21] and Conformer [22]. We report an in-depth
analysis of their sensitivity to performance degradations by
varying attack step sizes and numbers, controlling the amount
of input perturbation and attack launch time.
• We propose a novel adversarial robustness approach for LID,
Self-Defensive Adversarial Re-Training(SDART), that enables
(i) progressive adversarial domain exposure withadversar-
ial sample mining, (ii) a teacher-free version of defensive-
distillation [23] building upononline label smoothing[24], and
(iii) linguistic relation guided consistency regularization.
• Considering the top-10 most widely spoken languages in the
world, we have shown that across different architectures and
widely explored LID databases (V oxLingua107 [25] and Com-
mon V oice [26]), the proposed SDART consistently outper-
forms other widely popular robustness approaches.
• Under black-box scenarios, we also demonstrate the effective-
ness of SDART for providing robustness against transferable
adversarial attacks.

3. Methodology
Adversarial robustness approaches can be either reactive (detect
attacked inputs before they are fed tof θ) or proactive (mod-
ify the parameters off θ so that attacked inputs yield correct
classifications). Reactive approaches fall into a high-risk-high-
reward regime, and the model’s compromise becomes inevitable
if the detector fails. Hence, proactive adversarial robustness ap-
proaches are more common,adversarial re-training(ART) [27]
being the most widely explored among them.

successful approach for adversarial robustness is defensive dis-
tillation (DD) [23]. It uses a teacher network (often same net-
work trained prior with genuine samples) to guide a student net-
work (trained with adversarial samples) with soft-labels. DD
helps in reducing the sensitivity of perturbation size by reduc-
ing the gradient magnitude of the input.
3.2. Self-defensive adversarial re-training (SDART)
Motivation & hypotheses:Upon analyzing these main-
stream approaches, we hypothesize three key aspects that can
help in enabling adversarial attack robustness, (i) controlled
perturbation-domain exposures, (ii) consistency regularization
between genuine and their respective attacked samples, (iii)
gradient sensitivity reduction. Encapsulating all these, we
proposeself-defensive adversarial re-training(SDART) that
blends ART with teacher-free knowledge distillation [31] and
language-aware consistency regularization using dynamicon-
line label smoothing[24]. Conventional regularization ap-
proaches aim to minimize representation or logit distance be-
tween a clean sample and its attacked counterpart. It does
not consider whether the clean sample is correctly predicted
by the classifier. We propose to perform logit-pairing only for
correctly predicted clean samples. For linguistic consistency,
we adopt epoch-wise and language-specific soft labels for logit
pairing instead of sample-wise labels. This approach stabilizes
training convergence while inducing inter-language dynamics
as crucial information for the LID task. For clarity, we discuss
SDART in terms of its different incremental sub-methods.

4. Experiment details
Databases & features:We primarily use the widely-explored
V oxLingua107 [25] subset (V oxLingua-10), considering all its
utterances from the top ten most widely spoken languages in
the world: English, Mandarin, Hindi, Spanish, French, Arabic,
Bengali, Portuguese, Russian, and Urdu. We split the data into
session-disjoint training, validation, and evaluation splits using
80:10:10 ratio. Additionally, we verify our proposed framework
on the Common V oice dataset [26] for the same languages and a
similar data split. During training, we split the utterances from
the training and validation sets into segments of 3s. The evalua-
tion is conducted at utterance-level. The non-speech and silence
segments are discarded by using V AD. For each segment, we
extract 80-dimensional log Mel-spectrograms and apply chan-
nel mean normalization.
Classifier training procedure:Following the recent LID
literature [32, 33, 31], we primarily use the ECAPA-TDNN ar-
chitecture [21] for developing LID frameworks. We train the
models for 50 epochs with early-stopping, with a batch size of
64, Adam optimizer, and learning rate of 0.0001. Cross-entropy
loss is used for training the baseline and ART, while OLS loss
is used for training SDART. To perform the adversarial attacks,
we follow the torchattacks library [34]. We report the results
using equal error rate (EER) (in %) andC avg (∗100).

5.2. LID performance analysis of SDART
In Table 2, we present the performance analyses of the pro-
posed SDART approach for the ECAPA-TDNN model trained
with V oxLingua-10. We report performances on the original
evaluation set with genuine samples, along with the evalua-
tion sets generated by FGSM and PGD attacks (perturbation
size0.03). To develop a deeper understanding of SDART,
we also observe performances of the major sub-modules in-
crementally. For comparison with the literature, along with
ART, we further implement several popular adversarial robust-
ness approaches (their hyperparameters are optimized based
on V oxLingua-10 validation set), such as TRADES [27],
Defensive-distillation [23, 35], MART [30]. The proposed
SDART approach consistently outperforms ART and other
comparable robustness approaches. Notably, SDART achieves
the best performance for both genuine and attacked evaluation
sets. For this, we believe the controlled attack exposure using
adversarial sample mining, the effective handling of the bor-
derline high-entropy genuine samples, and the incorporation of
language-specific consistency regularization play a pivotal role.

5.4. Ablation experiments: A look into the aspect of trans-
ferable adversarial attacks for black-box use cases
Owing to the ongoing trend of open-source development, we
explore white-box adversarial attacks in which the adversary
has access to the LID model. Here, we also examine SDART
into black-box attack scenario, assuming the adversary does not
have access to the ECAPA-TDNN model. In such cases, at-
tackers usually generate adversarial examples using other re-
lated architectures to attack the unseen framework. To simulate
such a scenario, we apply transferable black-box adversarial at-
tacks generated from the Conformer model to attack ECAPA-
TDNN-based frameworks. In Figure 2, we report the EERs of
the baseline and SDART systems, showing the effectiveness of
the proposed method against black-box adversarial attacks.
6. Conclusions
We present one of the first studies analyzing and improving the
robustness of LID systems against adversarial attacks. Build-
ing on conventional adversarial re-training, we propose SDART,
a novel adversarial robustness method for LID that integrates
controlled adversarial domain exposures via adversarial sample
mining, teacher-free defensive distillation with dynamic online
label smoothing, and language-conditioned consistency regu-
larization. Compared with other popular robustness methods,
SDART shows superior performance across LID frameworks
and databases for both genuine and adversarial LID evaluations.
In the future, we plan to apply SDART for diverse evolving ad-
versarial attacks and to other speech processing applications.
