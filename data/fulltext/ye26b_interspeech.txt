Abstract
Achieving fine-grained emphasis control in speech synthesis re-
mains challenging due to data scarcity and the inherent com-
plexity of prosody. To address this issue, we extend F5-TTS
with an additional Emphasis Encoder and propose a three-stage
optimization framework that progressively enhances emphasis
controllability. The framework begins with Supervised Fine-
Tuning on manually annotated data, followed by Direct Pref-
erence Optimization, where preference pairs are constructed
by ranking SFT-generated samples using the Wavelet Prosody
Toolkit (WPT). In the final stage, we adapt Flow-CPS to per-
form online reinforcement learning (RL) for flow-matching
models. Using WPT as a reward model, this stage refines
the flow trajectories through the estimation of the relative ad-
vantage of the group. Experimental results demonstrate that
the proposed pipeline substantially improves emphasis inten-
sity and controllability while preserving natural prosody. Audio

1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of neural speech synthesis has enabled
highly intelligible and natural-sounding speech [1], yet fine-
grained emphasis control remains challenging for truly expres-
sive human-computer interaction. As a key prosodic element,
emphasis conveys semantic focus and maintains discourse flow.
Without it, generated speech often suffers from prosodic flat-
ness, increasing cognitive load and risking semantic ambiguity.
Enhancing emphasis control is therefore crucial for bridging the
gap between machine-generated audio and human-like expres-
siveness, ensuring synthesized speech delivers intended promi-
nence in real-world applications.
The lack of high-quality, accurately annotated data re-
mains a fundamental barrier to the development of robust em-
phasis control in speech synthesis. To address this scarcity,
SpeechCraft [2] leverages FastSpeech2 [3] to generate a cor-
pus of annotated speech by manipulating acoustic features
such as pitch and energy. Similarly, the WhiStress [4] frame-
work utilizes the Google Text-to-Speech API to create the
TINYSTRESS-15K dataset, where emphasis is placed through
adjustments of pitch, energy and duration. However, both ap-
proaches struggle to match the nuance of natural human expres-
sion. These parametric adjustment methods often produce an
“unnatural” or “robotic” auditory experience because they rely

Other works [5, 6, 7] show that leveraging language priors, such
as phrase and word boundaries, intonation units, and syntactic
features can improve sentence stress prediction. While such ap-
proaches offer a relatively objective framework for word-level
assessment, they often fail to encapsulate the intricate prosodic
nuances perceived by human listeners. Consequently, these
automated tools lack the precision required to generate high-
quality labeled data for training robust models. EmphAssess
[8] introduced EmphaClass, an emphasis detection model pri-
marily fine-tuned on English data; however, its performance is
constrained by a limited number of speakers and a relatively
small training corpus.
CosyV oice [9, 10, 11] employs an instruction fine-tuning
approach for emphasis control, where specific tags (e.g.,
<strong>) are embedded within the input text to guide the
language model. By training on instruction-following data,
the model learns to generate speech tokens with emphasized
prosody corresponding to these textual cues without explicit
acoustic feature manipulation. However, the training of LLM-
based TTS models is inherently data-hungry [12]. Empha-
sis control in these systems is often suboptimal, primarily due
to the scarcity of accurately annotated data within fine-tuning
sets. Acquiring high-quality emphasis labels is both labor-
intensive and time-consuming, making controllable synthesis
under low-resource conditions a critical challenge. Current Au-
dio LLMs [13, 14, 15] also exhibit limited proficiency in ex-
tracting precise, fine-grained emphasis annotations. Address-
ing data scarcity calls for more data-efficient architectures and
training strategies. Non-autoregressive (NAR) models have
demonstrated strong performance while requiring substantially
less fine-tuning data [16, 17]. Motivated by these insights,
we leverage F5-TTS [18], a state-of-the-art non-autoregressive
model, to achieve precise emphasis control with limited re-
sources. Our approach establishes a comprehensive multi-stage
training pipeline—progressing from Supervised Fine-Tuning
(SFT) to Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) [19] and Group
Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) [20], applied within a
flow-matching framework [21, 22, 23, 24]—while addition-
ally incorporating an explicit emphasis encoder to better model
prosodic prominence, which together significantly enhance the
controllability of emphasis. This framework not only provides
a novel method for controllable synthesis but also offers an ef-
ficient pipeline for generating high-quality labeled data. The
main contributions of our work are as follows:
(1) We propose an emphasis-controllable F5-TTS framework
that integrates a flexible prosodic modulation module into the
flow-matching backbone.

2. Method
2.1. Overview
The proposed model extends F5-TTS (Figure 1(a)) by incor-
porating an additional Emphasis Encoder as a dedicated condi-
tioning module. The input to the system is a text sequence anno-
tated with emphasis tags (e.g.,<strong>and</strong>).
The text tokens are first converted into token embeddings and
then processed by the Emphasis Encoder to generate emphasis-
aware representations with the same dimensionality as the orig-
inal text embeddings. These emphasis representations are fused
with the original F5-TTS conditioning embeddings and subse-
quently fed into the DiT backbone. The output of the model
is the flow prediction used by the flow-matching framework to
generate the target speech waveform with controllable empha-
sis.
The training pipeline follows a three-stage optimization
process. First, SFT is performed on a manually curated dataset
to learn an initial mapping between text inputs and prosodic
emphasis patterns. Next, DPO is applied to a preference data
set automatically constructed by ranking synthesized samples
according to prominence scores computed using the Wavelet
Prosody Toolkit (WPT) [5], encouraging improved alignment
with prominence-based preference signals. Finally, the model is
refined using Flow-CPS [24], a variant of FlowGRPO [23] that
employs WPT as a reward model to optimize flow-matching
trajectories. By incorporating the Coefficients-Preserving Sam-
pling (CPS) strategy, this stage is designed to further enhance
the intensity and controllability of synthesized emphasis.
2.2. Emphasis Encoder
The Emphasis Encoder maps input text embeddings to
emphasis-aware representations. Rotary positional embeddings
are applied to retain sequential structure, followed by self-
attention layers with residual connections to capture contextual
dependencies. A feed-forward network with layer normaliza-
tion further refines the features through non-linear transforma-
tions.
For tokens annotated with emphasis markers, the corre-
sponding emphasis representations are selectively extracted and
element-wise added to their original text embeddings, forming
a fused conditioning representation. This fused representation
is provided to the DiT backbone as an additional conditioning
signal, enabling the flow-matching process to generate speech
with targeted prosodic emphasis at specified positions.
This design establishes an explicit conditioning pathway
that bridges discrete emphasis annotations and continuous
prosodic variations, thereby improving the controllability and
precision of emphasis realization.
2.3. Supervised Fine-Tuning
The initial stage of our training pipeline focuses on SFT using
a high-quality, manually annotated dataset. During this stage,
both the Emphasis Encoder and the F5-TTS backbone are opti-
mized jointly. The objective is to train the Emphasis Encoder
together with the DiT backbone to associate textual empha-
sis annotations with their corresponding prosodic variations in
speech. The model learns the mapping between textual empha-
sis annotations and corresponding prosodic variations.
2.4. Direct Preference Optimization
The model is initialized from the SFT checkpoint and all train-
able parameters are further optimized using the DPO objective.
Following the SFT stage, we employ DPO to further enhance
the model’s ability to control emphasis in synthesized speech.
For this purpose, the training data is generated by the SFT-tuned
model, using text prompts that are annotated with emphasis in-
formation by DeepSeek [25]. To obtain word-level boundaries
in the synthesized audio, we first apply the Montreal Forced
Aligner [26] to align the speech with the input text. Based
on these boundaries, we leverage the Wavelet Prosody Toolkit
(WPT), which provides objective acoustic measures such as
pitch, energy, and duration to quantify the prominence of the
targeted emphasis words. Based on these prominence scores,
we construct a preference dataset by selecting samples with the
highest and lowest scores, forming positive and negative pairs.
The DPO training objective encourages the model to better cap-
ture the relationship between annotated emphasis cues and the
resulting acoustic prominence, thereby increasing the correla-
tion between intended emphasis and the prominence scores of
the synthesized speech while providing a stronger initialization

3. Experiments
3.1. Setup
SFT.The model is built upon the F5-TTS v1 Base (DiT;
dim=1024, depth=22, heads=16, ff mult=2) and initialized
from pretrained weights. To handle emphasis, an emphasis en-
coder is integrated, sharing the same hidden size and attention
configuration as the base model and consisting of 4 Transformer
layers. The training is conducted on 2.5 hours of manually
annotated Chinese speech data with emphasis labels using the
AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of1×10 −5. All exper-
iments are performed on a cluster of 8 NVIDIA H800 (80GB)
GPUs for a total of 21,500 steps.
DPO.The Flow-DPO stage employs the same architecture and
emphasis encoder as in the SFT stage, initialized from the SFT
checkpoint. We fine-tune the model on a Chinese preference
dataset of 800 pairs generated by the SFT model. The training
objective balances the supervised and preference-based losses
with an SFT loss weight of 0.5 and a DPO loss weight of 1.0,
using a DPO temperature (τ) of 0.5. Optimization is conducted
using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of1×10−5 on
a cluster of 8 NVIDIA H800 (80GB) GPUs for a total of 13,000
steps.
GRPO.The GRPO fine-tuning is conducted on 2,000 Chinese
emphasis-annotated text samples, maintaining the SFT model
configuration. Training involves GRPO-specific hyperparam-
eters:β= 0.2, a0.1clipping range,6samples per prompt,
0.1noise level, and a5.0adversarial clipping maximum. Us-
ing 8 NVIDIA H800 (80GB) GPUs, the model is optimized via
AdamW (LR= 1×10 −4) for 2,000 steps. Throughout train-
ing, the reward increases steadily from 0.7887 to 0.9479.
3.2. Compared Methods
We evaluate our proposed framework against the vanilla F5-
TTS base model and CosyV oice. We selected CosyV oice as the
primary baseline because, in our preliminary evaluations, other
instruction-following models (e.g., Cosyvoice2-3, MiMo [28],
V oxInstruct [29]) and large-scale closed-source systems (e.g.,
Doubao [30], GPT-4o mini, Google API) exhibited relatively
weak emphasis modulation, failing to meet the intensity stan-
dards required for distinct prosodic prominence. In contrast,
CosyV oice demonstrates a more pronounced emphasis effect in
Chinese synthesis, making it the most competitive baseline for
this study. To demonstrate the progressive improvement of our
framework, we compare three variants: F5-SFT, F5-DPO, and
F5-GRPO, representing the model after each respective training
stage.

consistency, enabling the model to produce expressive, control-

3.5. Emphasis on Noun
Emphasis control depends on the coverage of words in the SFT
and DPO training data, as uneven distributions can hinder gen-
eralization. To evaluate GRPO’s effectiveness on underrepre-
sented words, we conducted experiments on nouns using a test
set of 100 Chinese sentences with manually annotated noun em-
phasis, which are particularly challenging due to their low pro-
portion in the training data.
As shown in Table 3, our model achieves higher control
accuracy (ACC) and prominence values on nouns compared
to Cosyvoice and F5-TTS-DPO. While the baseline and DPO-
tuned models show basic controllability, they struggle with the
linguistic diversity of nouns, resulting in inconsistent empha-
sis. By leveraging our proposed architecture and multi-stage
training strategy—including GRPO—the model better general-
izes to underrepresented words, producing more consistent and
accurate emphasis across diverse noun instances.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed a three-stage optimization frame-
work—progressing from SFT to DPO and online RL via Flow-
CPS—to significantly enhance the emphasis controllability of
the F5-TTS model. By integrating a dedicated emphasis en-
coder that fuses prosodic embeddings with text representations
and utilizing the WPT as a reward model, our pipeline achieves
superior prominence levels while maintaining a stable word er-
ror rate and high speaker similarity. This approach effectively
improves generalization across linguistically diverse categories
such as nouns, providing a robust and efficient solution for high-
quality, expressive speech synthesis under low-resource condi-
tions.
