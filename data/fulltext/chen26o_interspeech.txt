Abstract
As conversational agents become widespread, emotionally
aligned responses are essential. Previous work emphasizes
recognition and activeness, but overlooks directional correct-
ness, i.e, emotion consensus (EC). EC evaluates whether the
agent emotion matches the speaker state, yet it is under-
explored in speech LLMs. We proposeContrastive Empathetic
Chain of Thought(CE-CoT), decomposing responses into emo-
tion recognition, neutral response and emotion-aligned revision
to enable implicit contrastive training. Experiments across four
datasets and multiple speech LLMs show consistent and sig-
nificant gains in EC. For instance, BLSP-Emo with proposed
method improves EC by up to 19.1% on MSP-Podcast com-
pared to vanilla one. Notably, when compared to the pretrained
BLSP-Emo model, our method achieves gains of up to 35.2%
on MESC. These results shows our approach can effectively
enhances emotion consensus, leading to more reliable speech
based conversational agents.
Index Terms: speech LLM, empathetic conversational agent,
speech emotion recognition, emotion consensus
1. Introduction
As conversational agents become increasingly integrated into
daily life, the demand for agents with empathetic capabilities
has grown. Beyond task-oriented functions, modern systems
are expected to provide emotionally aware support, including
mental health support [1], medical [2], education [3], and social
companionship [4]. However, empathy in human–machine in-
teraction is a multifaceted concept, requiring not only emotion
recognition but also emotionally aligned, context-appropriate
responses [5, 6]. Thus, developing and evaluating truly empa-
thetic agents becomes a key research direction.
Prior work on empathetic agents evaluates empathy across
several dimensions, including emotion recognition [7] and re-
sponse quality [8]. Common metrics include Speech Emotion
Recognition accuracy (SER Acc.), Emotional Reaction (ER),
and Exploration (Ex). While ER measures emotional engage-
ment, Ex assesses the appropriateness of follow-up questions
that promote deeper interaction [9]. These metrics do not guar-
antee emotional alignment. To address this, Emotion Consen-
sus (EC) was proposed [10] to measure whether the emotion
expressed in the agent’s response aligns with the speaker’s emo-
tional state, reflecting the directional correctness of empathetic
behavior. As illustrated in Fig. 1, an agent may correctly recog-
nize the speaker’s emotion as angry, indicating successful SER,
yet generate a response (e.g., R1) conveying happiness. Such
a response represents failed EC, whereas a response (e.g., R2)

This concern is particularly critical as empathetic agents are in-

vulnerable, user populations.
Therefore, explicitly incorporating EC into training is es-
sential for empathetic speech LLMs [15, 16]. However, to
the best of our knowledge, emotion consensus in empathetic
speech LLMs remains largely unexplored. This challenge is
more pronounced in speech LLMs. Unlike text LLMs that can
rely on explicit emotion labels with corresponding prompt de-
sign, speech LLMs must infer emotions from acoustic signals.
This additional uncertainty makes accurate emotion alignment
of response more difficult. To address this issue, we focus on
explicitly controlling model outputs toward emotion-aligned re-
sponses, a strategy used to control output strategy in Chain-
of-Thought reasoning [17, 18]. Specifically, we proposeCon-
trastive Empathetic Chain-of-Thought(CE-CoT), a prompting
strategy applied during both training and inference to explic-
itly model EC by making it learn to generate responses through
step-by-step reasoning that differentiates emotion-consistent re-
sponses from misaligned ones.
The metric of EC score [10] is derived by using a strong
commercial language model that computes the matching rate
between the responses generated to the emotion of the in-
put speaker. Our results show that CE-CoT significantly en-
hances EC score. Using BLSP-Emo [15], a representative em-
pathetic speech LLM, incorporating CE-CoT into behavioral
alignment leads to up to a 19.1% EC gain on MSP-Podcast
compared with the vanilla setting. Notably, compared to the
pretrained BLSP-Emo baseline, our approach achieves gains
of up to 35.2% on MESC. Similar improvements are observed
for the recently-proposed RE-LLM [16] and the general speech
LLM Qwen2Audio [19], demonstrating consistent gains across

2. Methodology
The overall training process follows a two-step paradigm, as il-
lustrated in Figure 2. For the step 1, it generates the behavioral
alignment target (expected response) using an text LLM, fol-
lowed by step 2 that fine-tunes the speech LLM to align its re-
sponses with this target upon receiving speech input. In the fol-
lowing subsections, we introduce the proposed CE-CoT, each
step in training, and three key terms used throughout the pa-
per: speech input (S), CE-CoT prompt (P), and expected CoT
response (R).
2.1. Contrastive Empathetic Chain of Thought (CE-CoT)
To guide the speech LLM toward generating empathetically
aligned responses, we propose a CE-CoT prompt to guide the
speech LLM in generating empathetically aligned responses.
Inspired by recent CoT techniques [20, 18, 17], our design of
CE-CoT (shown in Part B of Fig. 2) encourages controllable
empathetic behaviors through explicit step-by-step reasoning,
rather than relying solely on implicit emotion representations.
As shown in part A of Figure 2, each utterance in empa-
thetic response generation has three components, i.e., emotion
(e), neutral response (r neu), and revised response (r rev). For-
mally, we denote the CoT response as
R={e,r neu,r rev}(1)
The model firstly identifies the user’s emotion from speech
to establish context and then produces a neutral response that
ignores the detected emotion, serving as a non-empathetic base-
line. Finally, it revises this response by explicitly expressing the
responder’s emotional reaction in the first person and adding an
exploratory question about the user’s feelings or underlying ex-
perience. This final revision targets key empathetic dimensions,
namely Emotional Reaction (ER) and Exploration (Ex).
By constructing both neutral and emotionally aligned re-
sponses, CE-CoT forms an implicit contrastive structure:
r− =r neu, r + =r rev (2)
During training, the speech LLM is optimized to produce
rrev closer tor + and farther fromr −, encouraging the model to
adjust its responses according to the user’s emotional state. The
same formulation is applied during inference to maintain con-
sistency between training and deployed empathetic behaviors.

Through this process, each speech-transcript pair has its

sponse attempts to reflect, selecting from the predefined emo-

3. Experimental Setup and Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
To evaluate the generalizability of our method, we apply it
to multiple speech LLM architectures, including BLSP-Emo,
RE-LLM, and Qwen2Audio. All backbones strictly follow
their original implementations without architectural modifica-
tions for fair comparison. Qwen-7B-Chat [23] is used as the
text LLM to generate expected CoT responses during training,
consistent with the configurations of BLSP-Emo and RE-LLM.
The EC LLM judge in our experiments is Gemini Flash 2.0 [24].
3.2. Dataset
1.IEMOCAP (IEM): IEMOCAP [25] is a widely-used bench-
mark for speech emotion recognition, consisting of approx-
imately 12 hours of dyadic speech from 10 speakers across
five sessions. Following common practice, we use 5,531 ut-
terances from four major emotions—neutral, happy (excited),
angry, and sad—along with their valence-arousal-dominance
annotations. To maintain fair comparison with BLSP-Emo
[15], sessions 1–4 are used for training and session 5 is re-
served for testing.
2.Emotional Speech Dataset (ESD): ESD [26] is a parallel
emotional speech corpus with 350 utterances per speaker
across five emotions from 20 speakers (10 English, 10 Chi-
nese). We use only the English subset and four emo-
tions—neutral, happy, angry, and sad—generating pseudo
valence-arousal labels via a pretrained wav2vec 2.0 model
[27]. The dataset is split into training and testing sets with a
7:3 ratio while maintaining balanced emotion distribution.
3.MSP-PODCAST (MSP-P): MSP-PODCAST [28] is a large
naturalistic emotional speech corpus from online podcasts,
with over 50k training and 30k testing segments annotated for
categorical and dimensional emotions. For comparison with
IEMOCAP, we randomly sample 4,290 training and 1,245
testing utterances, keeping the original train-test split.
4.Multimodal Emotional Support Conversation(MESC):
The MESC dataset [29] is a multimodal emotional sup-
port corpus from the TV series *In Treatment* (Seasons
1–3), containing 1,019 dialogues and 28,762 utterances. Fo-
cusing on empathetic responses under four primary emo-
tions(neutral, happy, angry, and sad). We generate pseudo
valence-arousal features (as in ESD) and retain only client ut-
terances. After filtering, the training set has 9,320 utterances
and the test set 1,206, following the original data split.
3.3. Baseline
To assess how training methodologies affect EC learning, we
compare two baseline groups with different settings, isolating
methodological impact from model scale.
(1) Pretrained Models (No Behavioral Alignment).We first
evaluate several pretrained models without applying any behav-
ioral alignment. These include open-source text and speech
LLMs as well as a commercial model:
•Qwen-7B-Chat[23]: The text-only LLM backbone used in
BLSP-Emo.
•BLSP-Emo (w/o ft.)[15]: The released well-known empa-
thetic speech LLM without additional fine-tuning.
•RE-LLM[16]: A speech LLM that enhance empathetic re-
sponses.
•Qwen2Audio[19]: A strong speech LLM trained for speech-
text multimodal understanding.
•Gemini 2.5 Pro[30]: A commercial LLM included to reflect
real-world deployment scenarios, representing off-the-shelf
LLM usage in everyday applications.
(2) Vanilla Behavioral Alignment.Following prior work
[15, 16], the speech LLM is trained to imitate expected re-
sponses generated from the reported prompting scheme. For
fair comparison, this alignment strategy is applied to BLSP-
Emo, RE-LLM and Qwen2Audio, and the results are reported
asV anilla Behavioral Alignment.

architectures.
3.4.2. Empathetic Effect under Emotion Consensus
To examine the role of EC in empathetic quality, we estimate
its impact on Emotional Reaction (ER) and Exploration (Ex)
scores [9]. We adopt a conditional evaluation: if the responding
emotion does not match the ground-truth (EC = 0), the corre-
sponding ER and Ex scores are set to zero. Conditional scores
are then computed for correct EC, and performance drops rel-
ative to the original scores indicate the effect of mismatched
emotional responses.
As shown in Table 2, although RE-LLM achieves the high-
est original ER and Ex, its conditional ER (0.667) and fine-
tuned BLSP-Emo (0.697) are lower than pretrained BLSP-Emo
(0.763), suggesting that strong but misaligned empathetic re-
sponses can reduce actual empathetic effect. In contrast, both
BLSP-Emo and RE-LLM with CE-CoT show higher condi-
tional ER and Ex than vanilla alignment and pretrained models.
The ER and Ex drop rates further confirm this: for example,
RE-LLM with CE-CoT has an ER drop rate of 26.3%, less than
half that of vanilla alignment, indicating fewer responses are pe-
nalized due to emotional mismatch. These results demonstrate

3.4.3. Case Study
We compare responses to a happy IEMOCAP prompt (“oh then
you had a whole weekend of camping”) under Qwen2Audio
with different settings. Pretrained and vanilla alignment
responses incorrectly reflect sad emotions(as highlighted in
bold), while CE-CoT correctly matches the user’s happy emo-
tion(mark in red). This shows that high ER and Ex scores of
2 are ineffective if the emotion is misaligned. Notably, CE-
CoT responses convey emotional understanding without explic-
itly naming the emotion, and the EC judge accurately assesses
intent from context. This demonstrates the flexibility of CE-
CoT and EC evaluation, though further validation for judging
framework is needed.
3.5. Limitation
Although CE-CoT improves emotion-consistent responses, sev-
eral questions remain. We have yet analyzed internal reason-
ing or the link between speech emotion recognition, reasoning
traces, and response. EC is implemented without exploring al-
ternative measurement, and experiments are limited to single-
turn settings. Future work should explore multi-turn interac-
tions, and richer formulations of EC to capture more nuanced
emotional and strategic dimensions.
4. Conclusion
We propose a contrastive empathetic Chain-of-Thought (CE-
CoT) alignment framework to improve emotion-consistent re-
sponse generation in speech-based LLM agents. By introducing
contrastive reasoning structures, the model learns to distinguish
between aligned and misaligned emotional behaviors, going be-
yond conventional fine-tuning.
Experimental results show that contrastive alignment en-
hances emotion consensus in empathetic responses. This
demonstrates that carefully designed contrastive objectives can
effectively refine high-level social behaviors in multimodal
LLMs.
Overall, our work underscores the value of structured be-
havioral alignment and provides a foundation for future re-
search. Future works will extend the framework to multi-turn
conversations, refine the EC measurement, and explore explicit
prediction of empathetic strategies to further improve controlla-
bility and interaction quality.
