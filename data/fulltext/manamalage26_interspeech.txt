Abstract
Speech-based automatic depression detection (ADD) enables
scalable, non-invasive mental health assessment but raises pri-
vacy concerns due to sensitive speech data. However, jointly en-
suring privacy and performance remains challenging, and quan-
tifying this balance is underexplored. We propose FedMPA, a
Federated Multimodal Prototype Alignment framework that im-
proves performance, privacy, and multimodal consistency for
decentralized speech-based ADD. FedMPA employs modality-
specific prototypes, an Exponential Moving Average-stabilized
global prototype bank, prototype-guided losses, and softmax-
prototype hybrid inference. We introduce a task-specific
Privacy-Performance Score (PPS) to quantify this trade-off, rep-
resenting the first framework to optimize and measure privacy-
performance balance in depression detection. Experiments on
E-DAIC show that FedMPA outperforms FedAvg, FedProx,
FedProto, and FedGPD, achieving a PPS of 0.92 with strong
classification performance.
Index Terms: Depression Detection, ADD, Federated Learn-

1. Introduction
Depression is a globally prevalent mental health disorder that
significantly reduces quality of life [1], making early and ac-
curate detection essential for timely intervention. However, the
sensitive nature of mental health data raises serious privacy con-
cerns, particularly in speech-based systems where identifiable
voice signals limit the feasibility of centralized data collection
and model training.
Prior studies report strong privacy concerns in speech-based
mental health applications, with many users unwilling to adopt
such systems without explicit privacy guaranties [2]. Speaker-
related representations (e.g., i-vectors [3], x-vectors [4], [5],
and speaker embeddings [6]) widely used in automatic depres-
sion detection (ADD) improve performance, but can also ex-
pose speaker identity through automatic speaker identification
[7] and verification models [8]. Privacy breaches may occur
at both the data and the model levels [9], for example through
Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) that determine whether
a sample was used during training [10, 11], posing a serious risk
in medical settings.
To address these concerns, privacy-preserving approaches
have been explored [12, 13, 14], with Federated Learning (FL)
emerging as a promising paradigm [15, 16]. FL enables col-
laborative model training across multiple devices or institutions
without sharing raw data, thereby reducing direct data exposure.

However, applying FL to multimodal speech-based ADD
introduces three key challenges. First, multimodal representa-
tions arise from heterogeneous feature spaces, leading to mis-
aligned client distributions under Non-Independent & Identi-
cally Distributed (non-IID) conditions (e.g., varying speakers,
labels, modalities, and recording characteristics) [17, 18]. Sec-
ond, FL model updates may still leak sensitive acoustic or lin-
guistic information exploitable by MIAs [19, 20]. Third, FL
often suffers performance degradation compared to centralized
training [21, 22], creating a tension between predictive perfor-
mance and privacy that is rarely quantified in prior work.
To address these challenges, we draw inspiration from pro-
totype learning [23, 24, 25], where aligning class-level repre-
sentations provides a natural mechanism for integrating knowl-
edge across heterogeneous clients. In speech-based ADD, in-
dividuals may express depression through different acoustic or
linguistic cues depending on personal context. Motivated by
this intuition, we propose FedMPA, a Federated Multimodal
Prototype Alignment framework that jointly optimizes privacy,
performance, and multimodal consistency. FedMPA lever-
ages modality-specific prototypes, prototype-guided local train-
ing, robust aggregation, and an Exponential Moving Average
(EMA) stabilized global prototype bank to mitigate non-IID ef-
fects. We further quantify the privacy-performance balance us-
ing a task-specific Privacy-Performance Score (PPS) and evalu-
ate FedMPA on the E-DAIC dataset [26, 27]. Our main contri-
butions are:
• A federated multimodal prototype alignment framework for
privacy-aware speech-based ADD.
• Modality-specific class prototypes with an EMA-based
global prototype bank to improve cross-client consistency.
• A repulsion-based prototype loss to maintain class separation
under non-IID conditions.
• Prototype-based similarity weighting for aggregation and a
hybrid inference strategy for robustness.
• A task-specific PPS metric to characterize pri-
vacy–performance trade-offs.
• Comparative analysis with centralized and FL baselines (Fe-
dAvg, FedProx, FedProto, FedGPD) on the E-DAIC dataset.
2. Methodology
2.1. FedMPA architecture
For our analysis, we extend our centralized ALiDeR model [28]
to an FL setting. The proposed FedMPA introduces three core
components: modality-based prototype alignment, prototype-
guided local training, and enhanced server-side aggregation.

modality-specific prototypes and a global EMA-stabilized pro-

conditions. Thus, prototypes act as shared class-level represen-

2.1.3. Enhanced server-side aggregation
Instead of simple averaging (FedAvg [29]), FedMPA
adopted prototype-based similarity weighting combined with
momentum-based aggregation.
Existing similarity-aware federated aggregation methods,
such as FedSim [30] and MOON [31], compute client align-
ment in either gradient or feature space. In contrast, FedMPA
employs prototype-based similarity, leveraging the global mul-
timodal prototypes maintained at the server.
For each clientkat roundt, the server computes a similarity
scoresim k based on the cosine similarity between each client’s
prototype updates and the global prototype distribution. These
scores are converted into normalized aggregation weights using
softplus followed by softmax normalizationα k:

clients whose representations align better with the global proto-

To further stabilize the training process, we employed a
momentum-based update strategy on the server as in [32].
These two enhancements achieve a more stable, representation-
aware global model update, thereby reducing client drift and
improving convergence under non-IID conditions.
2.2. Dataset
To benchmark our proposed FedMPA approach, we use the E-
DAIC dataset, a widely adopted baseline in speech-based ADD
settings [26, 27]. E-DAIC consists of 275 participants (66 de-
pressed and 209 non-depressed), with binary labels based on
PHQ-8 scores. The data was collected through semi-structured
clinical interviews. For consistency with ALiDeR, we use only
the audio and text modalities, following the same preprocessing
and labeling procedures described in [28]. The dataset includes
predefined training (163 participants), development (56 partic-
ipants), and testing (56 participants) sets, and we retain these
splits to ensure that no speaker appears in more than one subset.
To simulate FL, participants were partitioned into four speaker-
level clients using approximately stratified label-based splitting,
resulting in naturally non-IID client distributions.
2.3. Evaluation methodology
We evaluate FedMPA across four aspects: performance-based
metrics, hybrid inference, privacy-based metrics, and the trade-
off between performance and privacy.

2.3.1. Performance-based metrics
We report standard performance metrics commonly used in bi-
nary classification scenarios, including Accuracy (Acc), Preci-
sion (Prec), Sensitivity (Rec), F1 Score (F1), Unweighted Av-
erage Recall (UAR), and Specificity (Spec). All metrics follow
standard definitions and are computed on the held-out E-DAIC
test set.
2.3.2. Hybrid inference
Although the model is trained based on weighted cross-entropy
and prototype regularization, the final model prediction in our
FedMPA is performed using a hybrid inference strategy. This
approach combines the predictions from both the softmax clas-
sifier and the prototype-based classifier.
Softmax outputs capture multimodal interactions but are of-
ten poorly calibrated and may assign high confidence to incor-
rect predictions [33]. Prototype-based classification instead re-
lies on geometric similarity, making it more stable under cross-
client variation, except when samples lie nearly equidistant
from class prototypes. Since softmax uncertainty does not al-
ways reflect prototype ambiguity, uncertain predictions are han-
dled by the prototype classifier for improved reliability.

2.4. Hyperparameter selection
All hyperparameters were tuned on the development set using
UAR as the primary selection criterion, except for those explic-
itly fixed to match prior work for comparability. For theγin
Equation 2, we performed a grid search over{0.7, 0.8, 0.9}and
selectedγ= 0.9, which achieved the best validation UAR. The
modality-specific weightsλ(λ text,λ audio,λ fused) in Equation 4
andβ(β text,β audio,β fused) in Equation 5 were selected from the
grid{0.06, 0.07, 0.08, 0.09}. For bothλandβ, the configu-
ration (0.07, 0.07, 0.08) achieved the best validation UAR and
showed stable training behavior, whereas larger values led to
over-regularization. Theδin Equation 8 was set to 2.0 from the
grid{1.0, 2.0, 3.0}. For the FL configuration, we explored the
number of clients{2, 4, 6, 8, 10}, local epochs{5, 10}, batch
size{4, 8, 16, 32}, and communication rounds{10, 20, 30,
40}. Finally, the experiment results were reported for 4 clients,
5 local epochs, a batch size of 8, and 30 rounds. The server-
side momentum-based aggregation uses a learning rate of 1.0
and a momentum of 0.9, while all clients are optimized with
Adam at a learning rate of1×10 −5. For the hybrid inference
evaluation,τin Equation 10 was tuned in{0.5, 0.6, 0.7}, with
τ= 0.6giving the best validation UAR. The remaining hyper-
parameters and computational setup are set to align with [28] to
maintain consistency.

3.4. Limitations and future work
While FedMPA demonstrates improvements in multimodal
speech-based ADD settings, several limitations remain. First,
our experiments are conducted solely based on the E-DAIC
dataset, which is widely used in literature but represents a semi-
clinical environment. This limits the generalizability of our
findings to broader clinical populations. Thus, future work
should extend the experiments to multiple clinical and real-
world datasets to assess robustness across demographic, linguis-
tic, and recording variations.
Second, FedMPA currently incorporates only audio and
text modalities, aligning with our baseline model ALiDeR. Al-
though these modalities are highly informative in ADD settings,
extending the framework to other modalities may introduce new
challenges related to multimodal alignment and representation
consistency. Therefore, future work should consider further
analysis on multiple modalities that could provide a more com-
prehensive understanding of depression symptoms.
Third, our privacy evaluation focuses on black-box MIAs,
which represent a common but limited threat model. Stronger
white-box or gradient-based attacks may expose additional vul-
nerabilities. Evaluating such attacks could provide deeper in-
sights into the privacy risks within FL multimodal settings. Fi-
nally, incorporating stronger privacy mechanisms, such as dif-
ferential privacy or secure aggregation variants, could further
strengthen protection.
4. Conclusions
This work introduced FedMPA, designed to balance predic-
tive performance and privacy in speech-based ADD. Through
modality-specific prototypes, EMA-stabilized global represen-
tations, and prototype-guided losses, FedMPA effectively mit-
igates client drift and enhances multimodal consistency under
non-IID conditions. Additionally, the proposed hybrid infer-
ence approach effectively combines softmax and prototype pre-
dictions, yielding more reliable outcomes in multimodal set-
tings.
Experimental results on E-DAIC demonstrate that FedMPA
outperforms existing FL baselines, achieving enhanced classifi-
cation performance and a higher privacy-performance trade-off
(PPS). These findings highlight the effectiveness of prototype-
based alignments and hybrid inference strategies in decentral-
ized speech-based ADD settings.
