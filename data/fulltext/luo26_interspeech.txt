Abstract
Pitch estimation (PE) in monophonic audio is crucial for MIDI
transcription and singing voice conversion (SVC), but current
deep learning methods often struggle to balance robust noise
tolerance with computational efficiency. In this paper, we pro-
pose FCPE, a fast context-based pitch estimation model that
employs a modified convolutional architecture with depth-wise
separable convolutions to effectively capture mel spectrogram
features while maintaining low computational cost and robust
noise tolerance. Experiments show that our method achieves
96.79% Raw Pitch Accuracy (RPA) on the MIR-1K dataset, on
par with the state-of-the-art methods. The Real-Time Factor
(RTF) is 0.0062 on a single RTX 4090 GPU, which significantly
outperforms existing algorithms in efficiency.
Index Terms: pitch estimation, fast inference, deep learning
1. Introduction
Pitch estimation (PE) or fundamental frequency (f0) estimation
is crucial for tasks such as MIDI transcription and singing voice
conversion (SVC). V ocal pitch estimation is one of the cases
that is widely used for industrial production. Before the advent
of recent deep learning models, the field of PE was mainly dom-
inated by classical signal processing techniques. These methods
can be categorized into three types.
•Time-domain methods:They operate directly on the sig-
nal waveform to identify its periodic structure. The most well-
known one is the Autocorrelation Function (ACF) [1] method.
•Frequency-domain methods:These methods transform the
signal into the frequency domain to analyze its harmonic struc-
ture. The cepstrum-based pitch determination algorithm [2] is
a classic example designed to separate the glottal source exci-
tation from the vocal tract filter to reveal the fundamental fre-
quency.
•Hybrid methods:To achieve greater robustness, these meth-
ods combine techniques from both domains. The seminal YIN
algorithm [3], for instance, enhances the ACF by incorporating
several error-reduction steps, while Y AAPT [4] explicitly inte-
grates spectral harmonic information with time-domain analysis
to improve tracking accuracy.
The methods above represent the traditional paradigm of
PE, while achieving considerable success, they still struggle
with noisy environments, polyphonic sources, and other issues.
With the development of deep learning (both software and
hardware), related work has greatly enhanced results in PE
tasks. Seminal models such as CREPE [5], DeepF0 [6] and
HARMOF0 [7] have leveraged Convolutional Neural Network

To address these issues, we proposeFCPE (Fast Context-
based Pitch Estimation), a novel model achieving high effi-
ciency without sacrificing accuracy. FCPE employs a modified
convolutional backbone, which leverages depthwise separable
convolutions to efficiently extract features from mel spectro-
grams and offers sufficient contextual coverage to model tem-
poral relationships across frames. In this paper, we demonstrate
that this architectural choice, combined with elaborated train-
ing strategies, jointly enables our model to achieve state-of-the-
art performance. The authors have contributed this model to
community’s VC projects like Retrieval-based V oice Conver-
sion (RVC) and So-vits-svc (SVC) [9, 10] in 2023. This paper
reveals its technical details for the first time.

with the highest probability. Instead, we apply a local argmax

weighted average of pitches around the peak probability bin,
providing a more precise and robust f0 estimate than a simple
argmax operation, thereby generating the final predicted f0.

3. Experiments
To compare FCPE with other models and quantify training
strategies’ contribution to the system, we conduct a series of
experiments. The following parts illustrate the results of the
comparative study and ablation study.
3.1. Comparative study
3.1.1. Accuracy
In order to demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of our
model, we conduct a comparative study against five leading
pitch estimation algorithms: RMVPE, CREPE, PESTO [15],
PM [16], and Harvest [17]. The performance is evaluated on
the MIR-1K [18, 19], V ocadito [20], and TONAS [21] datasets
under various challenging conditions, including clean audio and

evaluation. Specifically, it is re-synthesized from the test set
of the THCHS30 [23] dataset, and we refer to this dataset as
THCHS30-Synth. In this comparison, all deep learning-based
models are evaluated using their publicly available pre-trained
versions [24]. The four datasets contain various vocal data that
cover a wide range from singing to speech, all audio was resam-
pled to 16 kHz for evaluation.

defined as the time required to process an audio stream divided
by the duration of the audio itself. A model with an RTF signif-

processing. The RTF is calculated as follows:

The results in Table 3 offer a clear picture of each com-
ponent’s contribution. First, it is evident that the model trained
withoutnoise augmentationsuffers a dramatic loss in accuracy
under noisy conditions. This is most apparent in the extreme -20
dB white noise (β=0) scenario, where the RPA plummets from
the baseline’s29.75%to a mere6.19%. This stark degradation
unequivocally demonstrates that noise augmentation is the most
critical component for enhancing the model’s robustness.
Second, the significance ofspectrogram maskingis also
clearly demonstrated, especially against structured noise. With
its removal, the model’s RPA on -20 dB pink noise (β=1) drops
to a mere4.81%. Notably, this is even worse than the model
without noise augmentation (6.00%), which strongly supports
our core hypothesis: masking is the key mechanism that com-
pels FCPE to exploit its long-context architecture. This learned
skill of finding reliable pitch cues from the temporal context is
essential for overcoming extreme noise.
Finally, removingkey shiftingreveals an interesting and
important trade-off between model precision and generaliza-
tion. At first glance, the model without this augmentation ap-
pears superior, achieving the highest accuracy on clean data
across all variants. However, our test set (MIR-1K) cannot
cover more general and realistic situations, and the inherent lack
of pitch diversity in our synthesized training data necessitates
key shifting to enhance generalization for real-world scenarios.
As shown in Figure 3, the model trained without key shifting
augmentation mistakenly identifies a high-pitched human voice
segment as unvoiced (no f0), while the model trained with key
shifting augmentation correctly detects the f0 in the same seg-
ment. Further quantitative analysis reveals that the model’s vo-
cal range with key shifting enhancement is29.8%wider (1139
Hz vs 877.2 Hz).

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose FCPE, a fast context-based pitch es-
timation model that reaches state-of-the-art performance. We
also introduce several training strategies to enhance model per-
formance, which are proven effective through ablation studies.
Additionally, we successfully address the data scarcity problem
by utilizing DDSP-resynthesized data, and we find that even
re-synthesized data demonstrates excellent generalization capa-
bilities. This model has been widely adopted in Retrieval-based
V oice Conversion (RVC) and So-vits-svc (SVC).
