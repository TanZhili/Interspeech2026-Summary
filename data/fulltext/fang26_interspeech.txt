Abstract
During large-scale speaker dataset collection, noisy labels are
unavoidable and seriously affect model training. Existing meth-
ods mainly alleviate noisy label effects via sample selection or
label correction, but for open-set noisy samples, corrected la-
bels remain inaccurate. This paper proposes a Temporal En-
sembling Threshold and Neighbor-Aware Label Mixup (TET-
LM) method. This method models the embedding similarity
with a two-component GMM in each epoch and calculates the
ensemble threshold using exponential moving average to distin-
guish clean labels from noisy labels. In addition, we generate
mixed labels through the neighbor-aware mechanism to effec-
tively mitigate the correction bias of open-set noisy samples.
We further design a multi-stage training strategy. Experiments
on V oxCeleb show TET-LM improves system robustness under
various noisy label conditions, with EER and minDCF reduced
by 54% and 42% over standard methods (52% and 40% vs. the
best baseline).
Index Terms: Speaker Verification, Noisy Labels, Embedding
Similarity, Label Correction
1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV), as one of the core tasks in speech pro-
cessing, aims to determine whether two speech segments belong
to the same speaker [1]. With the development of deep learning,
speaker verification methods based on deep neural networks
have achieved significant performance improvements [2, 3, 4].
The core of speaker verification lies in extracting discrimina-
tive speaker features, which relies on training speaker classifi-
cation models with large amounts of high-quality labeled data.
In practice, the collection and annotation of online speech data
inevitably introduce noisy labels [5, 6], making the identifica-
tion and correction of such noisy labels a critical problem in the
field of speaker verification [7].
Speaker representation learning with noisy labels has been
extensively studied [8, 9, 10, 11], and existing approaches can
be broadly categorized into sample selection, label correction,
and selection-then-correction. Sample selection is a straight-
forward and efficient approach, which trains models using only
clean samples to avoid the influence of noisy labels. INLD [9]
and ES-GMM [12] measure sample cleanliness using the cosine
similarity between embeddings and speaker centroids, where
the former applies a fixed threshold to filter noisy labels, while
the latter adopts a two-component GMM to obtain a dynamic
threshold. OR-Gate [13] employs a top-k ranking mechanism

to select high-confidence samples as clean samples. CEC [14]
detects noisy labels by counting the number of mismatches be-
tween predictions and labels across different training epochs
and gradually increases the difficulty of selecting clean sam-
ples through a curriculum learning strategy. Label correction
methods automatically adjust incorrect labels during training.
LNCL [8] incorporates a correction loss into the objective func-
tion and dynamically adjusts the weights between original and
corrected labels using a threshold-based scheme. Selection-
then-correction combines the advantages of both approaches.
LESS [15] iteratively selects clean samples and corrects noisy
labels, thereby integrating learning, selection, and correction,
which improves the robustness of SV systems.
However, in real-world scenarios, noisy labels are not re-
stricted to the closed-set case. Open-set noisy labels are of-
ten mixed into the training data [16, 17, 18], corresponding
to speech samples from unknown speakers whose true identi-
ties lie outside the label setY. For label correction-based SV
methods, since the identities of open-set noisy samples cannot
be recognized, the corrected labels may still negatively affect
the speaker model. Therefore, investigating robust correction
strategies specifically tailored for open-set noisy labels is of
practical importance.
In this paper, we propose aTemporalEnsembling
Threshold and Neighbor-AwareLabelMixup (TET-LM)
method to identify and correct noisy labels. In each epoch,
we compute cosine similarity between sample embeddings and
speaker centers, and use a two-component GMM to derive a
separation threshold, which is temporally ensembled via expo-
nential moving average to distinguish clean and noisy labels.
For noisy samples, we select theKclosest speaker prototypes
as neighbors and re-weight their mixed labels using the mean-
teacher model’s outputs for loss computation. The main contri-
butions are:
• We propose a temporal ensembling threshold method, which
separates clean labels and noisy labels using the ensemble
threshold to avoid the fluctuation problem of a single two-
component GMM threshold.
• We propose a neighbor-aware label mixup method, which
adapts to the distribution of open-set noisy samples by re-
weighting the mixed labels ofKneighbors.
• We design a multi-stage training method to adapt to different
noisy label scenarios, and conduct extensive experiments to
demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

2.4. Training Process and Loss Functions
Our training procedure is divided into three stages, with the
stopping epochs set ast 1,t 2, andt 3, respectively.
Stage 1: Warm-up.Leveraging the memorization effect of
deep models, we first perform warm-up training on the entire
dataset to enable the model to acquire basic speaker discrimi-
nation ability. At the end of the warm-up stage, the temporal
ensembling threshold¯τt1 and the mean-teacher model parame-
ters[Θ′,Ψ′]are initialized asτ t1 and[Θ,Ψ], respectively.
Stage 2: Learning from Clean Labels.In this stage, the
temporal ensembling threshold is used to separate samples into
clean and noisy sets. Only the clean setD clean is used for train-
ing, further improving the model’s discriminative capability.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets and Experiment Setup
Datasets.We evaluated the performance of the proposed
method in real-world scenarios on V oxCeleb1 [25] and V ox-
Celeb2 [26]. V oxCeleb1 contains 1,211 speakers and 148,642
utterances, while V oxCeleb2 contains 5,994 speakers and
1,092,009 utterances. To assess performance under different
noisy label conditions, we randomly selected 1,000 speakers
from V oxCeleb1 and added symmetric and asymmetric noisy
labels at different ratios (10%–50%). The labels of the remain-
ing speakers were randomly flipped into the label set of these
1,000 speakers. Similarly, 5,000 speakers were selected from
V oxCeleb2 with the same procedure. The construction of sym-
metric and asymmetric noisy labels is as follows:
•Sym-30%: Each sample has a 30% probability of being ran-
domly flipped to another speaker label.
•Asym-20%: Each sample has a 20% probability of being
flipped to the label of the next speaker (the last speaker is
flipped to the first speaker label).
To increase the difficulty of identifying noisy labels, the se-
lected noisy samples were flipped only to speaker labels of the
same gender. We used the clean versions of V ox1-O, V ox1-E,
and V ox1-H as the evaluation sets1.
Training details.For all experiments, the input features are
80-dimensional log Mel filterbanks. The speaker embedding
extractor is an ECAPA-TDNN [27] with 1024 channels, and
the speaker embedding dimension is 192. The loss function is
AM-Softmax [19] (hyperparametersm= 0.2,s= 30). The
initial learning rate is 0.001, decayed by 0.97 every epoch, and
the optimizer is Adam [28]. Training is conducted on a single
GeForce RTX 3090 GPU with a batch size of 256. The hyper-
parameters in our method are set as follows:α= 0.5,β= 0.9,
µ= 0.4,K= 10. For V oxCeleb1:t 1 = 5,t 2 = 40,t 3 = 80;
for V oxCeleb2:t1 = 5,t 2 = 60,t 3 = 100.
Evaluation Metrics.We use the Equal Error Rate (EER) and
the minimum Detection Cost Function (minDCF,Ptarget = 0.01)
to evaluate SV model performance.
Baselines.We compare TET-LM with the following noisy-
label-robust SV methods: LNCL [8], OR-Gate [13], CEC [14],
LESS [15], and ES-GMM [12], and directly training on the
noisy-labeled dataset is also used as a simple baseline (denoted
as Standard).

3.2.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art
We further compared TET-LM with the current state-of-the-art
methods, as shown in Table 2. On the V oxCeleb1 and V ox-
Celeb2 datasets, TET-LM shows significant advantages under
both symmetric and asymmetric noise scenarios. In particular,
under high noise ratios (such as Sym-50% and Asym-40%), the
EER and minDCF values of TET-LM are significantly lower
than those of other methods, which indicates stronger robust-
ness in handling extreme noise conditions. In addition, the per-
formance of TET-LM in the clean scenario is also close to or
better than other methods, showing that it not only performs
well in noisy environments but also maintains stable perfor-
mance on clean data. Overall, on average, compared to the
standard methods, TET-LM achieves relative reductions of 54%
and 42% in EER and minDCF, respectively, while the best base-
line ES-GMM achieves relative reductions of 52% and 40%,
demonstrating the superior overall performance of TET-LM.
4. Conclusions
To address the challenges posed by open-set noisy labels in
speaker verification, this paper proposes a Temporal Ensem-
bling Threshold and Neighbor-Aware Label Mixup method.
The temporal ensembling threshold effectively separates clean
and noisy labels. In addition, our experiments show that nei-
ther hard labels (one-hot labels) nor soft labels (predicted prob-
ability) provide an optimal solution for handling open-set noisy
samples. We proposed neighbor-aware label mixup to effec-
tively resolve the label correction problem for such samples.
Furthermore, we design a multi-stage training strategy to en-
hance the speaker model’s adaptability under different noisy la-
bel scenarios. Extensive experiments on the V oxCeleb datasets
demonstrate that TET-LM exhibits significant robustness across
various noisy label conditions.
