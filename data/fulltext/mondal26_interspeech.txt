Probing LoRA-to-LoRA Cross-Lingual Transfer for Unseen Low-Resource
Conditions in Whisper-Based ASR
Hirak Mondal, Spandan Dey, Gopal Agrawal
Samsung R&D Institute, Bangalore, India
hirak.mondal@samsung.com, spandan.dey@samsung.com, gopal.kumar@samsung.com
Abstract
Low-resource Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) remains
a challenge despite large-scale pre-training, especially for lan-
guages with minimal representation. We investigate LoRA-
to-LoRA transfer using Whisper, where a Low-Rank Adapta-
tion (LoRA) module trained on a donor language, even one
under-represented in the base model, initializes adaptation for
a low-resource recipient. Unlike full fine-tuning, this approach
preserves pretrained parameters, ensuring stable, parameter-
efficient cross-lingual transfer. We propose a two-stage donor
selection strategy that filters candidates by genealogical relat-
edness and identifies the optimal donor through orthographic-
distributional similarity. Results demonstrate that donor-
informed initialization consistently outperforms recipient-only
adaptation, yielding relative Word Error Rate (WER) reductions
of up to17%. Our findings indicate that leveraging linguisti-
cally and orthographically aligned donors is critical for effective
low-resource ASR.
Index Terms: low-resource ASR, cross-lingual transfer, LoRA,
Whisper, parameter-efficient fine-tuning
1. Introduction
Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) has undergone a pro-
found transformation over the past decade, evolving from hy-
brid Hidden Markov Model-Gaussian Mixture Model (HMM-
GMM) pipelines [1] to the current era of large-scale speech
foundation models. Early neural frameworks transitioned
through CTC, attention, and transducer-based architectures [2–
4], eventually leading to massive weakly supervised scaling.
Models such as Whisper [5], SeamlessM4T [6], and the Ca-
nary series [7] now demonstrate performance that approaches
or surpasses human transcription accuracy across nearly 100
languages, marking a shift from language-specific pipelines to
universal pre-trained backbones.
However, this broad coverage hides a difficult reality. Of
the 7,000+ languages worldwide [8], the vast majority remain
low-resourcewith minimal transcribed data. This exclusion
creates a significant technological equity gap, effectively bar-
ring speakers of under-represented languages from accessing
vital digital services in healthcare, education, and governance
[9]. Furthermore, without robust ASR systems to facilitate tran-
scription and documentation, many endangered languages face
an accelerated risk of digital extinction, as they are sidelined in
an increasingly voice-driven technological landscape [10].
The adaptation of these models to low-resource regimes fol-
lows several paths, each with distinct trade-offs. While full
fine-tuning often minimizes Word Error Rate (WER), its high
computational cost and susceptibility to catastrophic forget-
ting [11, 12] remain problematic. Continual learning strategies,
including experience replay [13, 14], gradient-based constraints
[15], and parameter isolation [16–18] have been investigated to
mitigate these issues, yet the fundamental tension between sta-
bility and plasticity persists [19–21].
Recent research has explored parameter-efficient fine-
tuning (PEFT) like LoRA [22] for direct adaptation [12], but the
potential of transfer-guided donor mechanisms remains largely
unexplored for unseen languages. While Song et al. [23] inves-
tigated LoRA-based transfer, their study was limited to high-
resource anchors already present in pre-training data. Other ap-
proaches, such as LM-based output correction [24], face statis-
tical intractability in extreme low-resource settings. Notably,
while Joshi et al. [25] introduced a rare and vital “Bhojpuri Ru-
ral Woman” dataset, their methodology relies on training from
scratch. Such an approach is not only computationally expen-
sive but also increasingly impractical for large-scale multilin-
gual models, as it fails to leverage the powerful linguistic priors
already embedded in pre-trained backbones and is highly sus-
ceptible to catastrophic forgetting [17].
In this work, we move beyond high-resource donor
paradigms to investigate transfer learning dynamics when even
the donor language is under-represented in the original pre-
training data. We propose a “bootstrapping” strategy using se-
quential LoRA-to-LoRA initialization: training a LoRA adapter
on a donor language before transferring those weights to a recip-
ient. This setup addresses three critical questions: (i) the influ-
ence of donor visibility in pre-training on transfer effectiveness;
(ii) the role of cross-lingual representation similarities in the
encoder-decoder architecture; and (iii) the advantages of staged
adaptation over single-stage LoRA for low-resource language
pairs. By jointly evaluating empirical adaptation performance
and orthographic-distributional similarity through genealogical
relatedness, we demonstrate that our staged LoRA-to-LoRA
transfer consistently outperforms single-stage baselines. These
results establish the proposed method as a robust strategy for
parameter-efficient cross-lingual adaptation, particularly when
orthographic alignment and shared genealogy are leveraged to
bridge languages under-represented or absent from the model’s
original pre-training.

3.3. Orthographic Similarity-Driven Donor Selection
We adopt a two-stage donor selection strategy grounded in lin-
guistic structure and distributional similarity. First, we restrict
the donor search space by genealogical relatedness; candidate
donors must belong to the same linguistic family as the re-
cipient. This serves as a structural prior, ensuring baseline
compatibility in phonological and morpho-syntactic character-
istics. Second, within this constrained set, we select the donor
based on orthographic distributional similarity. The motiva-
tion for this is rooted in the decoder’s operation over discrete
token sequences, where it models the conditional probability
pθ(yt|y <t,X)over a fixed vocabularyV. Since the empir-
ical token distribution of a language fundamentally shapes the
statistical structure of decoder gradients, minimizing the ortho-
graphic divergence between the donor and recipient ensures that
the initialized LoRA parameters are already tuned to a similar
gradient manifold. By selecting a donor that shares a signif-
icant token overlap or distributional profile with the recipient,
we facilitate a more stable and efficient transfer of the decoder’s
linguistic modeling capabilities.

4.2. Donor Selection
As described in Section 3, donor languages are selected
based on genealogical relatedness and the minimization of
Jensen–Shannon (JS) divergence between empirical token dis-
tributions. By employing this quantitative framework, we en-
sure that our donor-recipient pairings are derived from measur-
able orthographic alignment rather than heuristic intuition.
4.3. Training Configuration
All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A10 GPU
(24GB VRAM). We evaluate the effectiveness of language-
specific adapters by training LoRA modules on top of the
Whisper-small backbone, as well as exploring LoRA-to-LoRA
transfer where recipient weights are initialized from a donor
LoRA and subsequently adapted. In all cases, the AdamW
[28] optimizer is used with a peak learning rate of10 −4 for
10epochs.
Following the findings of [23], which identified a rank of
r= 32as optimal for speech adaptation, we adoptedr= 32for
all LoRA modules. This selection was further validated by our
preliminary experiments on Hindi data, wherer= 32consis-
tently yielded superior performance compared to lower ranks.
To ensure a high-capacity adaptation, LoRA adapters are ap-
plied to the attention mechanism’s query (Wq), value (Wv), and
output (Wout) projections, as well as the fully-connected layers
(Wfc ) within the feed-forward blocks of both the encoder and
decoder. During the testing stage, a beam width of5is em-
ployed for decoding.

While the absolute Word Error Rates (WER) are higher than

6. Conclusion
In this work, we investigate LoRA-to-LoRA transfer for low-
resource languages in Whisper-based ASR. Unlike prior re-
search focusing on pre-trained anchors, we address a stricter,
more realistic setting where recipient languages are absent from
the original pre-training phase and are available only with lim-
ited labeled data. We demonstrate that donor-initialized transfer
consistently outperforms recipient-only adaptation. These sta-
ble gains suggest that transfer provides a superior optimization
starting point rather than mere regularization. Furthermore, our
results confirm that donor selection can be guided by measur-
able distributional properties, offering a principled alternative
to heuristic pairing.
These findings establish that parameter-efficient transfer re-
mains effective even for languages without prior exposure in the
base model, offering a scalable path for ASR expansion without
massive retraining. Future work will investigate the universal-
ity of this distributional alignment across diverse typologies and
explore cross-modal metrics to further refine donor selection.
