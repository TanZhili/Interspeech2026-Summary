Semi-supervised learning and Large Language Model

Abstract
The efficacy of Wake-up Word (WUW) detection is often con-
strained by poor generalization to unseen negative samples, ex-
acerbating the trade-off between wake-up rate and false acti-
vation rate. To address this limitation, we introduce ADALA,
a novel WUW detection framework that integrates ADAptive
semi-supervised learning with a LArge language model. Specif-
ically, a Large Language Model (LLM) is fine-tuned via Re-
inforcement Learning (RL) to dynamically generate new and
effective hard-negative samples. These synthetically gener-
ated negatives are subsequently combined with real-world data
within a semi-supervised learning scheme to continuously en-
hance the WUW model. Experimental results validate our ap-
proach, demonstrating that ADALA achieves a superior perfor-
mance equilibrium, significantly reducing the false activation
rate (3.19%) on datasets of phonetically similar words while
preserving a high wake-up rate (90.43%).
Index Terms: Wake-up Word Detection, Adversarial Training,
Large Language Model, Proximal Policy Optimization
1. Introduction
With the exponential growth of the Internet of Things (IoT),
Wake-up Word (WUW) detection has become a cornerstone of
modern human-computer interaction. Its applications are in-
tegral to a diverse range of daily scenarios, from smartphones
and smart speakers to wearables and vehicles [1], with recent
analyses indicating over 153 million voice assistant users in the
U.S. alone [2]. As WUW technology becomes more pervasive,
its inherent always-on nature makes it challenging to achieve a
high wake-up rate with an extremely low false activation rate, a
fundamental requirement for a reliable user experience.
The technical root of this challenge lies in the task’s in-
herent open-set problem: a model with finite parameters must
reject a virtually infinite set of unseen targets [3, 4, 5]. To ad-
dress this, current research has shifted towards sophisticated
data strategies. One line of work focuses on semi-supervised
learning (SSL) that leverages large amounts of unlabeled au-
dio via consistency regularization [6, 7, 8]. Another direc-
tion involves data synthesis, where hard negatives are gener-
ated from scratch, for instance, by systematically editing a key-
word’s graphemes [9, 10]. Furthermore, adversarial training is
employed to bridge the domain gap between synthetic and real
samples by forcing the model to learn domain-invariant fea-
tures [11, 12].
While effective, these strategies share a common limitation:
poor generalization on unseen hard negatives [13]. This is be-

To address these limitations, we propose a novel framework
named ADALA (Adaptive Semi-supervised and Large-model
Learning), aimed at improving the wake-up rate while main-

novation is the use of a Large Language Model (LLM) to dy-
namically generate training samples, where the LLM itself is
continuously optimized via Reinforcement Learning (RL) [15]
based on the WUW model’s performance. This interactive pro-
cess is guided by a reward function that incentivizes the LLM
to generate phrases that successfully confuse the WUW model.
This enables an adaptive data curation that continuously gener-
ates high-value training samples at the WUW model’s decision
boundary. These dynamically generated samples are then uni-
fied with real-world data under a semi-supervised framework to
refine the model. To summarize, this paper makes the following
contributions:
•LLM-based Semantic Generation:We utilize a LLM to
generate diverse boundary samples at the semantic and pho-
netic levels.
•RL-driven Closed-Loop Optimization:We formulate an
RL problem to dynamically optimize the LLM with real-time
model feedback, enabling the generation of high-quality ad-
versarial examples.
•Adaptive Semi-Supervised Fusion:We employ a semi-
supervised framework to seamlessly integrate the generated
boundary samples with real-world data for synergistic model
optimization.
2. Methodology
Our proposed ADALA framework comprises two core modules.
A WUW generation module utilizes a RL loop to optimize an
LLM to dynamically generate hard negatives. Subsequently,
a training module integrates these synthetic samples with real-
world data under a semi-supervised learning scheme to refine
the WUW model.

This module operates as a closed-loop system to dynamically
generate hard negative samples, implemented within an Actor-
Critic reinforcement learning framework as shown in Fig. 1.

To steer its vast generative space, the policy of the Actor is first
primed with expert knowledge, a set of linguistic priors such
as syllable count and phonetic clarity and real-world false-wake

ensures the generation of more highly similar samples with true

semantic knowledge to explore conceptual variations, generat-

(CosyV oice2-1.5B [17]), which serve as the inputs for the Critic
and reward evaluation. Working in concert with the Actor, the
Critic is a separate value network that estimates the potential of
a generated phrase to become a successful hard negative. This
creates an iterative Generation-Feedback-Update cycle where
the LLM continuously co-evolves with the WUW detector, ex-
plicitly correcting the limitations of two-stage static generation.
Both networks are trained in tandem, guided by a reward func-
tion.
The reward function is meticulously designed to guide the
Actor toward the WUW model’s decision boundary. The re-
ward signal is determined by the joint evaluation of two arbiters:
the target WUW model and an external ASR verifier (FunASR-
LargeV3-Turbo [18]). These arbiters assess each synthesized
audio sample, and the outcome of their evaluation dictates the
reward signal as follows:
• A positive reward (+1 to +5) is granted if the audio triggers
a false activation, confirmed when the WUW model fires but
the external ASR identifies a different phrase.
• A strong negative penalty (-5) is applied if the WUW model
fires and the ASR also identifies the audio as the true wake-
up word.
• Trivial, non-triggering samples receive a minor negative
penalty (-1) based on phoneme-level deviations to discour-
age irrelevant generations.
To further stabilize the RL training, these granular rewards are
effectively distributed across the generation steps using Gener-
alized Advantage Estimation (GAE).

2.2. Wake-up Word Model Training
The WUW model shares a similar architecture in [19]. The
ultimate goal of training is to create a WUW model that is
both highly responsive to the correct wake-up command and ex-
tremely resistant to false activations. To achieve this, our train-
ing strategy combines signals from labeled, unlabeled, and our
adaptively generated synthetic data.

3. Experiment
3.1. Dataset
All experiments were conducted in an anechoic chamber with a
background noise level of 45 dB(A). We utilized a large-scale,
in-house Mandarin dataset totaling 1430 hours, comprising both
labeled and unlabeled data. Specifically, this dataset features
a strictly balanced demographic and acoustic distribution: a
1:1 gender ratio, a 1:2:1 age distribution (Senior:Adult:Child),
and a 1:2:1 speech rate ratio (Fast:Medium:Slow). The target
wake-up word for all experiments is ”xiao fei xiao fei”. The
evaluation suite consists of test sets representing diverse real-
world scenarios, including quiet environments, varied speech
rates (fast: 500-800ms; slow: 1200-2000ms), dual-speaker in-
terference, and accented speech. To specifically challenge the
model’s discriminative capabilities, we included two sets of
acoustically similar false-trigger words: one recorded offline in
a controlled setting (SW-Rec) and another collected from online
user logs (SW-Log).
3.2. Implementation Details
Our framework integrates Qwen2.5-14B as the LLM genera-
tor, FunASR-LargeV3-Turbo as the external ASR verifier, and
CosyV oice2-1.5B as the TTS engine. The training process be-
gins with the LLM generator, which is fine-tuned via Reinforce-
ment Learning for 50,000 iterations using the AdamW opti-
mizer with a learning rate of 3e-5 and a batch size of 64. To pro-
vide context on computational efficiency, this LLM fine-tuning
process takes approximately 40 hours on a platform equipped
with eight Tesla A800-80GB GPUs, efficiently yielding 10,000
high-quality, dynamically generated hard-negative samples.
Following the generation phase, the WUW model is trained
for 100,000 iterations on long concatenated utterances, approx-
imating a batch size of 16. Initially, we use the AdamW op-
timizer before switching to SGD with a learning rate of 1e-4.
In this phase, labeled data, unlabeled data, and the newly syn-
thesized dynamic samples are trained simultaneously within the
same mini-batches, rather than in sequential stages. This paral-
lel training strategy ensures continuous alignment between the
semantic variations and real-world acoustics, requiring approx-
imately 50 hours to converge.
3.3. Evaluation Methodology
Our evaluation protocol employs a fixed decision threshold for
each model to ensure a fair and consistent comparison. Each
model is calibrated to a strict commercial standard operating
point, corresponding to one false activation per 48 hours on
a general negative set. This setup ensures a fair and consis-
tent comparison in our long-utterance streaming task, since an
early detection can influence subsequent processing. With this
threshold established, we assess performance using two primary
metrics. The Wake-up Rate (WR) is measured across a suite of
diverse and challenging conditions (e.g., fast/slow speech, ac-
cented speech) to evaluate overall sensitivity and robustness.
The False Activation Rate (FAR) is specifically evaluated on
the SW-Rec and SW-Log sets to measure discriminative power
against confusable phrases. We compare our ADALA frame-
work with two representative baselines. The first is a classic
Hard-Negative Mining approach that focuses training on chal-
lenging samples. The second, GraphemeAug, is a state-of-the-
art method that synthesizes hard negatives using pre-defined
grapheme editing rules.

3.5. Ablation Study
The ablation study in Table 2 further isolates the performance
gains contributed by each core component. Evaluating the im-
pact of Semi-Supervised Learning, we observe that introduc-
ing the SSL framework provides the largest boost to the Av-
erage Wake-up Rate, increasing it from the baseline’s 86.36%
to 90.44%. This improvement is primarily attributed to the
consistency regularization applied over a large-scale unlabeled
dataset, which forces the model to learn invariant representa-
tions and dramatically enhances its generalization capabilities
across diverse acoustic conditions, effectively preventing over-
fitting to the synthetic samples.
Separately, the distinct value of dynamic generation is ev-
ident when examining the RL-based LLM Generation mod-
ule. Introducing this module yields the greatest reduction in
ASW-FAR, lowering it drastically from 9.17% to 3.54%. This
confirms that unlike static generation, our RL mechanism suc-
cessfully co-evolves with the detector in an iterative cycle, dy-
namically synthesizing targeted hard negatives that refine the
model’s fine-grained discriminative power. Ultimately, the full
ADALA framework is not a mere combination of components,
but an adaptive integration via Reinforcement Learning. It syn-
ergizes the high sensitivity brought by SSL with the precise
boundary-defining capabilities of the RL generator, achieving
the optimal performance equilibrium.
3.6. Cross-Lingual Generalization
To rigorously evaluate the cross-lingual generalization capabil-
ities of the ADALA framework, we extended our testing to
an English wake-up word, ”Hello Wikka”. Unlike the large-
scale Mandarin corpus, this newly collected dataset serves as a
targeted benchmark for language transferability. It comprises
2,280 examples and maintains a strictly balanced distribution
across demographics, including a 1:1 gender ratio and a 1:2:1
age distribution across Senior, Adult, and Child categories. Fur-
thermore, the acoustic conditions are controlled with a 1:2:1
speech rate ratio among Fast, Medium, and Slow speech.
As summarized in Table 3, the adaptive data curation gener-
ated by our reinforcement learning loop seamlessly transfers to
a new linguistic domain without requiring any structural mod-
ifications to the underlying architecture. ADALA achieves a
superior performance equilibrium, delivering a 90.42% average
Wake-up Rate alongside a remarkably low 1.67% average False
Activation Rate. It consistently and significantly outperforms
traditional methods, surpassing the supervised Hard-Negative
Mining baselineand the rule-based GraphemeAug approach.
These compelling results confirm that the proposed closed-loop
generation framework is effectively language-agnostic, demon-
strating robust generalization across diverse phonetic land-
scapes.

4. Conclusion
We introduced ADALA, an adaptive framework that integrates
an RL-optimized large language model with semi-supervised
learning for robust wake-up word detection. By establishing
an iterative Generation-Feedback-Update cycle, the LLM con-
tinuously co-evolves with the detector to dynamically synthe-
size boundary-defining hard negatives based on granular reward
feedback. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that ADALA sig-
nificantly improves the wake-up rate to 90.43% while maintain-
ing a highly competitive false activation rate of 3.19%. Fu-
ture work will explore expanding the framework to multilingual
scenarios and comparing current TTS-based synthesis pipelines
with emerging direct audio generation models to further bridge
the acoustic domain gap.
