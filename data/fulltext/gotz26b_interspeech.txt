Abstract
We present the Scene Generator of the Treble SDK, an inter-
active workflow for constructing realistic audio scenes from
room-acoustic simulations, clean source material, and source
or receiver definitions. The system supports controlled manual
scene design as well as automated bulk generation. A scene is
represented as a lightweight recipe containing tracks, source-to-
impulse-response mappings, listener configuration, metadata,
and target definitions. Audio is rendered only on demand,
enabling fast inspection, serialisation, sharing, and scalable
dataset generation. In the demonstration, participants build a
multi-speaker conversational scene with background noise, vi-
sualise it as a timeline and 3D room view, render the scene,
inspect targets and transcripts, and then generate many ran-
domised scenes from compact rules.
Index Terms: audio scene generation, acoustic simulation,
room impulse responses, spatial audio, speech datasets
1. Introduction
Speech and audio systems increasingly operate in shared acous-
tic spaces: meeting rooms, homes, vehicles, immersive environ-
ments, and hands-free interfaces. In these settings, recorded
signals depend on room geometry, materials, source and re-
ceiver positions, listener or device characteristics, background
noise, and interactions between multiple, potentially overlap-
ping speakers. Collecting real recordings that cover this vari-
ability is expensive, difficult to control, and hard to scale [1, 2].
Physically grounded acoustic simulation provides a comple-
mentary path, enabling controlled experimentation and large-
scale generation of diverse datasets.
Room impulse responses (RIRs) are widely used to turn
clean audio into reverberant audio for training and evaluating
speech systems. Recent work shows that the realism of syn-
thetic acoustic data can affect downstream performance in far-
field keyword spotting and speech recognition [3, 4]. Addition-
ally, physically accurate room-acoustic simulation can provide
evaluation insights comparable to those obtained from real mea-
surements [5].
However, many modern audio tasks require more than iso-
lated RIRs. Speech enhancement and separation systems need
mixtures, separated targets, labels, and background noise [6, 7].
Spatial audio and immersive applications additionally require
advanced listener representations and device rendering [8, 9].
This motivates tools that turn acoustic assets into complete,
metadata-rich scenes rather than disconnected files.
The Treble SDK is a Python interface to a powerful
and physically accurate acoustic simulation engine, provid-

2. Scene generation workflow
Figure 1 summarises the workflow. The system’s input con-
sists of a dataset of RIRs (IR collection), clean audio collec-
tions, and user-defined rules or metadata. The IR collection de-
fines available source-receiver paths. Clean audio collections
provide source content, such as speech or noise. Scene and
device/listener rules detail the scene setup, including conversa-
tional structure, overlap conditions, source groups, level ranges,
device settings, source and listener orientation, and target defi-
nitions.
A central design choice is the separation of scene defini-
tion from audio rendering. During construction, a scene is
a lightweight recipe containing timed audio blocks, mappings
from tracks to RIRs, listener configuration, and metadata. No
convolution or mixing is performed at this stage. This “lazy”
representation makes scene creation fast, portable, and serial-
isable. When audio is required, the scene is rendered by con-
volving each source track with the corresponding room and de-
vice response and mixing the result. The same scene can be
rendered, inspected, stored, reconstructed, or used to generate
different targets.
The workflow supports both manual construction and au-
tomated bulk generation. Manual construction gives full con-
trol over source content, spatial assignment, listener choice,
and target extraction. Bulk generation instead uses rule objects
and source groups to create many valid scenes automatically by
varying receiver positions, source assignments, speaker identi-
ties, timing patterns, levels, listener orientations, and noise con-
ditions accordingly.

4. Relevance and Conclusion
The Scene Generator addresses a practical gap between acoustic
simulation and audio machine-learning workflows. Researchers
and product developers often have access to RIRs, clean au-
dio, and model training code, but still need substantial custom
scripting to construct realistic mixtures, define targets, manage
metadata, and scale from one example to many. The Treble
SDK Scene Generator packages these steps into a reproducible
workflow centred on scene recipes and on-demand rendering.
The Show and Tell format is well suited to this system be-
cause the workflow is inherently interactive. Participants can
inspect the notebook, modify scene rules, visualise source and
listener configurations, listen to rendered outputs, and see how
individual recipes scale to collections. The demonstration high-
lights how physically grounded acoustic simulation can be used
not only to produce audio, but also to create and share struc-
tured datasets for developing and evaluating robust speech and
audio systems in realistic acoustic environments.
