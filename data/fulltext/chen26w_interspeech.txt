T-ORR: Text-Anchored Orthogonal Residual Rectification for Robust
Multimodal Sarcasm Detection
Qi Chen1, Junyi Chen2, Jiabin Han1, Hongjiao Yang1
1 Tianjin Foreign Studies University, China

Abstract
Sarcasm relies on a covert contradiction between intended con-
notation and literal text. In Multimodal Sarcasm Detection
(MSD), conventional fusion models often obscure sparse in-
congruity cues by entangling features within a joint space. We
propose Text-Anchored Orthogonal Residual Rectification (T-
ORR), reframing MSD as a signal separation problem. T-ORR
utilizes Dynamic Locality-Constrained Alignment to adaptively
focus non-verbal streams on the specific words they modify. A
Structure-Preserving Geometric Decomposition then employs
QR projection to disentangle these signals into semantic Res-
onance and modality-specific Dissonance. Rather than stan-
dard fusion, a Contrastive Incongruity Routing (CIR) mecha-
nism models the semantic distance between literal and incon-
gruous states. Evaluations on MUStARD and MUStARD++
yield state-of-the-art results, suggesting geometric decoupling
effectively isolates multimodal incongruities.
Index Terms: sarcasm detection, multimodal fusion, orthogo-
nal decomposition, dynamic alignment
1. Introduction
Human communication integrates foundational linguistic se-
mantics with acoustic and visual delivery cues [1]. While these
modalities are generally congruent in straightforward commu-
nication [2], sarcasm introduces intentional incongruity [3]. Of-
ten used to mock expectations or express implicit sentiment [4],
sarcasm challenges computational models because it relies on
overlapping and potentially contradictory signals.
To conceptualize this multimodal incongruity, Figure 1
illustrates a representative scenario. The speaker utters the
phrase, “Oh, yes, that’s abrilliantidea.” Evaluated purely
on lexical content, the statement indicates strong agreement
and positive sentiment. However, the visual modality reveals
a rigidly deadpan facial expression, while the acoustic stream
lacks the expected energetic prosody. The sarcastic intent is
not encoded within any single channel. Rather, it emerges ex-
plicitly from the targeted divergence between the literal seman-
tics of “brilliant” and the suppressed non-verbal delivery. This
structural mismatch underscores a critical limitation of standard
feature fusion: indiscriminately mixing these vectors into a joint
representation risks neutralizing the exact contradiction that de-
fines the sarcasm.
Historically, computational modeling of this incongruity
began with unimodal analyses. Early research primarily tar-
geted textual features, exploring rule-based heuristics [5, 6]
and surface-level linguistic mismatches [7, 8]. The prolifera-
tion of social media later facilitated large-scale distant super-
vision [9, 10]. Nevertheless, relying solely on text is often
insufficient, as many sarcastic utterances lack explicit linguis-

Consequently, subsequent studies incorporated speaker context
and isolated unimodal acoustic-prosodic or visual traits (e.g.,
reduced tempo or deadpan expressions) as reliable indicators of
sarcasm [12–14].
Recognizing the adversarial nature of these signals, re-
cent advances have shifted towards multimodal joint modeling.
Early approaches fused deep visual features with linguistic rep-
resentations [15], culminating in the introduction of the MUS-
tARD conversational dataset [4], which validated that audio-
visual-text co-occurrences significantly outperform unimodal
baselines. Subsequent research explored hierarchical architec-
tures [16], attention modules [17, 18], and graph-based model-
ing [19]. Recent state-of-the-art architectures, such as the Dual
Incongruity Perceiving (DIP) network [20], have specifically
addressed factual and affective inter-modal incongruities.
Despite this progress, isolating transient and nuanced mul-
timodal signals remains an ongoing technical challenge. A
fundamental limitation persists: existing cross-modal solutions
predominantly project multimodal features into a shared con-
tinuous space for fusion. This paradigm inherently risks entan-
gling modality-specific nuances (e.g., a momentary sneer) with
task-irrelevant background noise, hindering the model’s ability
to cleanly isolate the targeted conflict.
To bridge this gap, we propose T-ORR, formulating MSD
fundamentally as a signal separation and adaptive rectification
problem rather than a feature fusion task. Guided by three ge-
ometric principles—focusing on momentary cues, decoupling
conflicting signals via orthogonal mapping, and rectifying pre-
dictions through conflict routing—T-ORR ensures that textual
and non-verbal incongruities are rigorously isolated.

2. Methodology
The design of T-ORR is inspired by the human cognitive pro-
cessing of sarcastic discourse. When human listeners encounter
potential sarcasm, they do not merely perform a mathemati-
cal average of the semantic, acoustic, and visual inputs. In-
stead, they unconsciously anchor their interpretation on the spo-
ken words (text) and actively scan the non-verbal channels for
anomalies—such as an unexpected pitch drop or a mismatched
facial expression—that specifically modify those words. This
cognitive asymmetry dictates that the text serves as the stable
coordinate system, while audio and video function as dynamic
modifiers. As illustrated in Figure 2, T-ORR operationalizes
this cognitive process through a strict sequence of temporal
alignment, geometric isolation, and logical conflict routing.
We employ frozen pre-trained backbones to extract gener-
alized semantic representations across modalities. Textual fea-
turesH t∈R L×D are derived from DeBERTa-v3-Large [21],
whereLdenotes the sequence length andDis the embedding
dimension. Similarly,H a∈R Ta×D andH v ∈R Tv×D rep-
resent acoustic features from WavLM [22] and visual features
from DINOv2-Large [23], respectively. Since non-verbal se-
quences typically contain substantially more time steps than text
(Ta,T v≫L), establishing precise temporal alignment consti-
tutes a primary structural challenge.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
We evaluated T-ORR on the MUStARD dataset [4] and its
extension, MUStARD++ [27]. Following a strict Speaker-
Independent protocol [28], test set speakers remained unseen
4702
during training to assess generalization. All foundational back-
bones were kept frozen to isolate the efficacy of the fusion strat-
egy. We report Precision (P), Recall (R), and F1-score (F1) for
MUStARD, and F1-score for MUStARD++.
Implementation Details:The model was implemented in
PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU. To en-
sure uniform feature extraction, raw audio was resampled to
16kHz and processed into 10ms frames, while video frames
were extracted at 15 FPS. Text was tokenized with a maximum
sequence length ofL= 50. During training, we utilized the
AdamW optimizer with a batch size of 32 and a weight decay
of1e−4. The learning rate was set to1e−4for the newly
initialized fusion layers and projection heads. The balancing
coefficients for the geometric constraints were empirically set
toλ 1 = 0.5andλ 2 = 0.1following a grid search on the val-
idation set. Training proceeded for a maximum of 30 epochs,
with early stopping triggered after 5 epochs without validation
loss improvement.

We analyzed internal activation behaviors to observe signal
separation and alignment. During the evaluation of a sarcas-
tically drawn-out utterance, the dynamic alignment algorithm
predicted a temporal shiftµ i that aligned the attention win-
dow with the delayed acoustic anomaly, whereas linear map-
ping missed this specific variation.
Furthermore, analysis of the token-wise gate activations

In terms of failure cases, we observed that T-ORR struggles
when sarcastic intent is reliant on external world knowledge
rather than observable multimodal incongruity. For instance, ut-
terances delivered with completely sincere prosody and neutral
facial expressions—where the sarcasm stems purely from con-
textual absurdity not present in the dialogue history—bypassed
our CIR mechanism. In such instances, the dissonance compo-
nentF disc correctly registered near zero, causing the literal state
Hlit to dominate and resulting in a false negative prediction.
4. Conclusion
This paper introduced T-ORR for multimodal sarcasm detec-
tion. By framing the task as a signal separation problem, we
integrated Dynamic Locality-Constrained Alignment to handle
temporal variance, alongside a Decoupled Topology Geometric
Decomposition to mathematically separate congruent semantics
from modality-specific nuances. Additionally, Contrastive In-
congruity Routing models the semantic divergence between lit-
eral and incongruous states. Experimental results on the MUS-
tARD datasets suggest that geometrically decoupling sarcastic
modifiers provides a robust pathway for multimodal incongruity
detection.
