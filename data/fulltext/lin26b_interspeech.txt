The Role of Context and Prosody in the Understanding of English Irony by
Chinese L2 Learners
Yinan Lin, Shanpeng Li
MIITKeyLabforLanguageInformationProcessingandApplications,SchoolofForeignStudies,

Abstract
Irony conveys a meaning opposite to its literal expression and
is frequently used for humorous, critical, or satirical purposes.
Because of its ambiguity, ironic interpretation relies heavily
on contextual cues and prosodic features. However, the
interplaybetweencontextandprosodyinsecondlanguage(L2)
irony comprehension remains unclear. This study investigates
their relative contributions and examines whether language
proficiency affects Chinese L2 learners’ ability to interpret
irony. A perception experiment was conducted using English
materials with three contextual and three prosodic conditions.
Results showed that context played a dominant role in irony
comprehension, while prosody contributed independently
when contextual information was limited. Language
proficiency had only a minor effect. The findings support the
Parallel Constraint Satisfaction Model and offer implications
forironyresearchandEnglishlanguageteaching.
Index Terms: irony understanding, context, prosody, Chinese
Englishlearners
1. Introduction
Irony involves a contrast between what is said and what is
meant, often arising from a mismatch between expectations
and reality, including sarcasm, hyperbole, understatement,
rhetorical questions, and jocularity [1]. It has been widely
examined from its structural, cognitive, and pragmatic
perspectives [2-7]. Although it is common in everyday
communication, irony poses challenges for L2 learners.
Previous research suggests that factors such as language
proficiency [8] and cultural background [9] may influence
irony comprehension. Contextual information and prosodic
cues have also been shown to facilitate irony interpretation in
L2 [10-12]. However, their relative contributions remain
unclear, and learners may rely on them differently depending
onexperienceandindividualdifferences[12].
Thepresentstudyexaminesthejointeffectsofcontextand
prosody on irony comprehension by Chinese learners of
English. It also investigates whether language proficiency
modulates irony perception in L2.By analyzing both response
patterns and reaction times under systematically manipulated
contextual and prosodic conditions, the study aims to clarify
whichcuesplayamoredecisiveroleandhowtheyinteract.In
doing so, it seeks to contribute to research on L2 pragmatic
processingandinformEnglishlanguageteaching.
1.1. The Interplay between Context and Prosody in
Irony Understanding of L2
Irony comprehension requires listeners to use contextual
information to infer a meaning that diverges from the literal
utterance [13]. Major theoretical accounts emphasize the
central role of contextual incongruity [14-16], and empirical
evidence shows that greater context-utterance mismatch
increases the likelihoodofironic interpretation[17-20]. Atthe
same time, prosody conveys affective stance and speaker
intention, and thus can signal irony [21, 22]. Some studies
suggest that listeners can distinguish ironic from non-ironic
utterances based on prosodic cues alone [23-25], whereas
others argue that prosody is insufficient without contextual
support[26].
When both cues are available, findings diverge. Some
researchers maintain that contextual information plays a
primary role [12, 19, 20], while others propose that prosody
provides a cognitively economical route to irony detection
[21], [27]. Although these issues have been widely examined
in native speakers, their interaction in L2 remains
underexplored. L2 learners may rely more on bottom-up cues
due to limited pragmatic knowledge [9], and cross-linguistic
differences in prosodic realization may further complicate
interpretation [24], [28-31]. Few studies have systematically
compared the relative weight of contextual and prosodic cues
inL2ironycomprehension.
1.2. Understanding Irony by L2 Learners
L2 learners often struggle with irony [9]. Multiple factors,
including irony type, cultural background, language
proficiency, context, and prosody, have been reported to
influenceperformance[9,32,33].
The role of language proficiency remains controversial.
Some studies report that higher proficiency facilitates
recognition of mismatches between literal meaning and
context, thereby improving irony comprehension [34]. In
contrast, other research finds weak or no correlations between
proficiencyandironydetection[32,35].Thesemixedfindings
suggest that proficiency may not be the decisive factor in L2
ironyprocessing.
To address these gaps, the study investigates the irony
processingof Chinese Englishlearners inL2. Specifically,the
studyaddressesthefollowingquestions:
● What are the relative contributions of context and prosody
inunderstandingironyinL2(English)?
● WhatistherelationshipbetweenChineseEnglishlearners’
languageproficiencyandtheirabilitytointerpretirony?
Firstly,itisexpectedthatintheironyunderstandingofL2,
the context plays a dominant role and the stimuli in the strong
incongruent context will be judged as much more ironic than
thoseintheambiguouscontextandthenoincongruentcontext.
Itis also expectedthat theprosody promotesthe perception of
irony: if the context is ambiguous, the stimuli in ironic
prosody will be perceived as much more ironic than those in
neutral prosody and sincere prosody. Moreover, it is
hypothesized that language proficiency is not a key factor in
ironyunderstanding.

2.2. Materials
2.2.1. Design of Contexts and Target Sentences
A total of 61 declarative sentences were designed for the
experiment as target sentences. They were all literal praising
(mean length = 5.1 syllables). They adhered to a fixed
sentence structure of “X+be+really+adj.” (e.g., Your paper is
really well-written!).
Each sentence was embedded in three types of contexts:
strong incongruent, ambiguous and non-incongruent, thus
eliciting ironic, ambiguous, and sincere interpretations,
respectively. In the strong incongruent condition, contextual
information directly conflicted with the literal statement,
creating a clearly ironic scenario. In the non-incongruent
condition, context and statement aligned, leading to a sincere
reading. In the ambiguous condition, the neutral context
allowed multiple interpretations. All contexts were stylized to
ensure clear experimental contrasts rather than to fully mirror
everyday conversation. According these scenarios, each target
sentence was produced with three prosodies: ironic, neutral,
and sincere. The materials were developed following the
Discourse CompletionTask paradigm,resultingin 549stimuli
pairs(61sentences×3contextconditions×3prosodies).
2.2.2. Stimuli Recording, Processing and Evaluating
An AI voice generatortool TTSMAKER(https://ttsmaker.cn),
was used to generate the context audio, using a male voice of
American English withneutralemotion, to strictlykeep voice,
rate and prosody constant across context conditions and
thereby isolate contextual incongruity as the only manipulated
variable. All AI-generated sound files were uniformly
processed into WAV files, 44.1kHz and 16 bits using Adobe
Audition2020.
The English target sentences were recorded by a female
nativeEnglishspeaker(age=22). First,shewasaskedtoread
the target sentences with a neutral prosody without showing
contextual information. Then, given the contextual
information, she was asked to read the target sentences with a
proper prosody (ironic or sincere) according to her
interpretations of the context and the sentence. She was
neither directed nor given any feedback. All the audios were
saved in the 44.1kHz,16-bit, WAV files and were normalized
to70dBusingPraat.
Tomakesurealltheaudiotargetsentenceswereperceived
as the intended prosody, a perceptual validation test was
performed. Four students (4 females, mean age = 22.23, SD =
0.96) were engaged. They needed to rate all stimuli, judge the
degree of criticism or praise of every sentence on a 5-point
Likert scale. Based on the test results, 7 items with the lowest
ratings per prosody (for neutral ones, items with the lowest
and highest) were excluded, yielding a total of 486 English
stimuli (54 scenario × 3 context × 3 prosody) for the formal
experiment.
2.3. Experimental Procedure
The whole perception experiment was carried out in a
computer classroom under a quiet environment, lasting about
40minutes. They were given instructions to listen carefully to
each stimulus and make judgment as quickly as they could,
andtheyneededtotakeintofullconsiderationalltheavailable
information. Stimuli were presented using the professional
psychology software PsychoPy (Version 2024.1.4) [37] in
OX83-355computersandECD-69headphones.
There was a practice session before the formal one. It
consisted of 9 trials to make the participants more familiar
with the experiment. In formal session, therewas a total of 54
stimuli for each participant, with 6 stimuli allocated to each
condition. During the experiment, they first heard an audio of
context with the text presented on the screen. After a 500 ms
interval with a red cross and a prompt sound, the sound of
target sentence was played without the text presented. Then,
participants were required to make a judgment between irony
and sincerity, using the two-alternative forced choice
paradigm. They respond by pressing the key “f” (“critical and
negative”), or “j” (“praising and positive”) without time limit.
Besides the judgment question, to make participants focus on
the experiment, a third of trials followed an additional
comprehensiontaskrelatedtothecontextinformation.
2.4. Data Coding and Statistical Analysis
The proportions of ironic response and reaction times (RT)
were submitted to statistical analysis. R was used to run the
Generalized Linear Mixed Model (GLMM). Context, prosody
and level were set as fixed effects, participants and sentences
were set as random effects. Through the Type II Wald chi-
square test, the main effects and interaction effects were
obtained. If a significant interaction effect was observed,
Tukey-HSD pairwise comparisons were further performed
usingtheemmeansfunctionoftheemmeanspackage[38].
Furthermore, to comparethe differences between different
conditions, a compact letter display (CLD) was created using
the CLD function of the multcompView package [39].
Conditions with labels that contained at least one letter in
common (e.g., a and ab) suggested that there were no
significant differences (p ≥ 0.05) between them, and
conditions that had different letter labels (e.g., a and b)
showedthatthereweresignificantdifferencesbetweenthem.

4. Discussion
Thestudyaimedtoinvestigatetherole ofcontextandprosody
in the interpretation of irony in L2. The results showed that
both context and prosody play an important role in irony
perception in L2. In the following section, we will thoroughly
discuss the results and details concerning each research
question.

4.1. The Relative Contributions of Context and
Prosody in L2
4.1.1. Dominance of Context
According to the data, context significantly influences the
perception of irony.The proportion of ironic responsewas the
highest in strong incongruent context, followed by ambiguous
contextandnoincongruentcontext,meaningthatthemorethe
incongruence exists between the contextual information and
the literal meaning of the target sentence, the more likely a
stimulusistobe judgedasirony.This resultisconsistent with
the Allusion Pretense Theory [16], which claims that irony
works by alluding to a failed expectation. Thus, a more failed
expectationwillevokeamoreinclinedironicresponse.
As we can see in the result, under the strong incongruent
context, even the sincere prosody led to a high proportion of
irony; Under no incongruent context, even the ironic prosody
led to a low proportion of irony. Moreover, in the three
prosodies, the differences between each context type were
mostly significant, especially between strong incongruent
context and the other two kinds of context. So it can be
concludedthatwhenitcomestotheironyinterpretationinL2,
context is a more foundational cue than prosody, which
coincideswiththefindingsofotherauthors[17-20]whiletheir
researchmostlyfocusedontheironyinterpretationinL1.
4.1.2. Complementary Role of Prosody
The prosody also plays a vital role in irony interpretation. No
matterwhatkindofcontextthestimuluswasin,theproportion
of ironic response was the highest in ironic prosody, followed
by neutral prosody and sincere prosody. Prosody can enhance
the perception of irony, especially when the context doesn’t
provide enough information. The data showed that in an
ambiguous context, ironic prosody elicited a 75.5% ironic
response whereas neutral prosody yielded 29.5% and sincere
prosody only10.9%.Sincethecontextual informationaloneis
insufficient to disambiguate the speaker’s intent, the prosody
comes to serve as a powerful cue. It can be verified by the
differencesbetweeneachprosodycondition.
Under a strong incongruent context and a no incongruent
context, the differences between prosodic conditions were
smaller, indicating that prosody’s influence decreases as
contextual impact increases. This aligns with the above
conclusion that prosody serves as a secondary cue, enhancing
irony perception primarily in contexts where the information
is weak or unclear [11, 12]. In addition, in the ambiguous
context, neutral prosody resulted in slower RT compared to
ironic or sincereprosody,suggesting thatprosody canliterally
speed up irony perception. As Bryant and Fox Tree [27]
proposed, listeners were able to perceive irony based only on
prosodicclues.
All in all, when the contextual information was sufficient,
prosodyenhancedordiminishedtheperceptionofirony;when
the contextual information was insufficient, prosody provided
a stronger impact on irony detection. This is in line with the
Parallel Constraint Satisfaction Model, proposed by Pexman
[5],thatvariouskindsofclues,includingprosody,incongruity,
knowledge of the speaker, etc., are processed quickly and
simultaneously until there is enough evidence to support an
ironic interpretation. It is also consistent with Relevance
Theory, which maintains that complicated mental-state
attributionisaprerequisiteforinterpretingutterances[40].
4.2. The Relationship between Language Proficiency
and Irony Interpretation
According to the data, there was not a statistically significant
relationship between the English level and the ironic
proportion. It seems that language proficiency is not a key
factor in irony interpretation for Chinese English learners,
which is consistent with the findings of the study on Iraqi
learners [35] and English learners [41]. Meanwhile, there was
no significant interaction effect among Context, Prosody and
Level, indicating that context, prosody, and level do not work
in a sophisticated and complementary way to influence the
ironyperception.
However, a significant interaction effect was found
between context and level. Post hoc tests revealed that the
significant level difference only appeared in the strong
incongruent context condition, in which participants at a high
English level judged stimuli to be slightly more ironic than
thoseatalowEnglishlevel.Similarly,theinteractionbetween
prosodyandlevel was significant,but thelevel difference was
only significant for ironic prosody, not for neutral prosody or
sincere prosody, implying that individual variations in
language proficiency might only become apparent when the
ironyisparticularlysalient[42].
The small effects of level differences and the lack of a
consistent pattern across context conditions and prosody
conditions suggest that level may be a relatively minor factor
in irony perception compared with context and prosody. This
discovery has important implications for future experimental
design since it implies that language proficiency control may
not be a primary concern for irony perception research
targetingChineseL2Englishlearners[32].
5. Conclusions
This study mainly investigated the roles of context and
prosody in interpreting irony in L2 (English). It has been
found that context plays a dominant role in interpreting irony
andsometimesprosodycanserveasanindependentrolewhen
the contextual information is insufficient. It seems that the
irony perception in L2 follows the parallel constraint
satisfaction model. Moreover, language proficiency seems to
be a relatively minor factor in irony perception in L2. Taken
together, this study extends the literature on interpreting irony
byhighlighting the effect of context and prosody in L2. In the
further research, it would be useful to examine the ironic
compliments in L2 whose target sentence is literal blaming.
Moreover, the study was only based on behavioral
experimental data. Future research could add additional
measures and technologies, like eye-tracking or neural
imaging, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of
ironyinterpretation.
