Diffusion Bridge Learning Between Overfitted and Underfitted
Representations for speech emotion recognition
Shi-wook Lee ID
National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST), JAPAN
s.lee@aist.go.jp
Abstract
Robust recognition of human emotions from speech remains
difficult under domain and language shift because neural en-
coders often overfit to dataset-specific acoustic cues. This
paper proposes a diffusion-based representation-space training
framework that reshapes learned representations toward class-
centered geometry. The model introduces class prototypes as
anchors and learns stochastic sample–prototype transformations
using a conditional denoising diffusion process with paired
alignment, cycle consistency, and classifier-based regulariza-
tion. Experiments on cross-lingual emotion recognition be-
tween English and Japanese show consistent improvements in
weighted average recall. The proposed method achieves up
to +6.42 percentage points in English-to-Japanese evaluation
and +4.00 percentage points in Japanese-to-English evaluation,
demonstrating improved robustness to cross-language domain
shift.
Index Terms: speech emotion recognition, diffusion-bridge,
out-of-distribution generalization, cross-language
1. Introduction
Speech emotion recognition (SER) aims to infer human af-
fective states from speech and is a key component of em-
pathic human–machine interaction [1]. Recent advances in self-
supervised learning (SSL) have substantially improved speech
representation learning. Large-scale pretrained models such as
wav2vec 2.0 [2], HuBERT [3], and WavLM [4] now provide
strong transferable encoders and have become standard back-
bones for SER, either as frozen feature extractors or via rep-
resentation fine-tuning [5]. Despite these gains, robust SER
under domain shift remains challenging: emotional cues are
subtle and entangled with speaker traits, recording conditions,
and linguistic content, and available emotion corpora are typi-
cally small and heterogeneous [6, 7]. As a result, modern deep
models can achieve high in-domain performance while relying
on dataset-specific artifacts or spurious correlations, leading to
poor generalization to unseen corpora.
A useful lens to understand and mitigate this behavior is
the representation dynamics of deep models during training.
Prior work suggests that learned representations evolve from
relatively smooth and generic structures in early training to
increasingly specialized and task-discriminative geometry in
later training [8, 9]. In SER, this transition is particularly pro-
nounced: early-stage representations often exhibit better robust-
ness but limited class separation, whereas late-stage represen-
tations can become sharply discriminative yet overly aligned
with corpus-specific factors. These observations imply a com-
plementary trade-off between robustness and discriminability
across training stages. However, existing SER approaches typi-
cally rely on a single checkpoint or a single fine-tuning regime,
implicitly committing to one side of this trade-off.
In this paper, we ask a simple question:Can we explic-
itly connect underfitted (early) and overfitted (late) representa-
tions to combine their complementary strengths?To this end,
we proposeDiffusion Bridge Learning, a representation-space
adaptation framework that learns stochastic transformations be-
tween representations extracted from different stages of train-
ing. Our key idea is to treat early- and late-stage representa-
tions as two endpoints and learn a bridge that reshapes the ge-
ometry of the representation space toward a more class-centered
organization while preserving emotion semantics. Rather than
directly translating between early- and late-stage representa-
tions, we introduce class prototypes as intermediate anchors
and learn sample–prototype bridges within each representa-
tion space. This prototype-mediated design stabilizes stochastic
translation while still connecting complementary training-stage
geometries.
Our approach is inspired by diffusion-based generative
modeling [10, 11] and, more specifically, by diffusion bridge
models that construct stochastic paths between paired endpoint
distributions [12, 13]. Unlike conventional diffusion models
designed for data generation, we use a conditional denoising
diffusion process directly in representation space as a learning
mechanism for encoder adaptation. Concretely, we introduce
class prototypes as anchor points in the representation space
and learn bidirectional sample–prototype transformations using
a shared conditional denoiser. To ensure the learned transforma-
tions are both faithful and stable, we impose paired alignment
and cycle-consistency constraints, and we jointly train an emo-
tion classifier with endpoint supervision. Moreover, to preserve
discriminative decision structure during stochastic translation,
we introduce a logit-pair Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization
that matches softened class-posterior distributions between end-
points and their translated counterparts.
Our method is related to data augmentation and domain
generalization approaches such as Mixup [14], manifold-based
augmentation [15], and adversarial invariance learning [16, 17].
In contrast, rather than modifying inputs or enforcing do-
main invariance explicitly, Diffusion Bridge Learning learns
a stochastic geometry transformation that bridges complemen-
tary representation structures arising along the training trajec-
tory. This viewpoint provides a principled mechanism to mit-
igate encoder overfitting by smoothing over-specialized direc-
tions while maintaining emotion-relevant decision boundaries.
The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• We analyze training-stage-dependent representation charac-
teristics in SER and identify complementary properties be-
tween underfitted (early) and overfitted (late) representations.
• We proposeDiffusion Bridge Learning, a prototype-guided

ganization and out-of-domain generalization.
3. Experimental Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
Cross-lingual speech emotion recognition (SER) provides a
challenging benchmark for evaluating out-of-domain general-
ization. In this study, we assess the proposed framework
using three emotional speech corpora: the Japanese Twitter-
based Emotional Speech (JTES) [18], the Interactive Emotional
Dyadic Motion Capture (IEMOCAP) dataset [19], and MSP-
IMPROV [20]. Table 1 summarizes the number of utterances in
each dataset.

To investigate the effect of domain shift, we conduct cross-
lingual SER experiments between English and Japanese. The
two English datasets (IEMOCAP and MSP-IMPROV) are com-
bined to form a single training domain, while JTES is used
as the Japanese corpus. Accordingly, we perform two cross-
lingual evaluation scenarios:
• English→Japanese: models are trained on the English
datasets (IEMOCAP + MSP-IMPROV) and evaluated on
JTES.
• Japanese→English: models are trained on JTES and evalu-
ated on the English datasets.
This experimental design involves two linguistically dis-
tant languages, which provides a challenging benchmark for
studying cross-domain and cross-lingual generalization in SER.
We evaluate classification performance using Weighted Average
Recall (W AR), which measures the overall accuracy weighted
by the number of samples in each class. For representation
learning, we employ the pretrained HuBERT Large model as
the backbone encoder [21]. The overall SER training pipeline
follows the configuration used in the Speech Processing Univer-
sal PERformance Benchmark (SUPERB) [5].
3.2. Cross-lingual Evaluation and Bridge-weight Analysis
We evaluate cross-lingual generalization in two directions:
training on English (IEMOCAP + MSP-IMPROV) and testing
on Japanese (JTES), and vice versa. All values are reported
aspercent WAR(%). For each setting, we run five trials with
different random seeds and report mean±standard error (SE).
Statistical significance is assessed by a pairedt-test between
AfterandBeforeacross the same five trials. We vary the bridge-
loss weights(λ pair,λ cycle)while keeping other loss weights
fixed (i.e.,λ eps,λ cls,λ cls-tr,λ KL are fixed to1.0). We compare
three representation-pair configurations: (i) underfit–underfit,
(ii) overfit–overfit, and (iii) overfit–underfit. In the mixed set-
ting, we separately report improvements for the overfit-side
classifier (A) and the underfit-side classifier (B) asoverfit (A)–
underfit and overfit–underfit (B).

4. Conclusions
We presentedDiffusion Bridge Learning, a representation-space
adaptation framework for robust speech emotion recognition
that leverages training-stage complementarity between over-
fitted (late-stage) and underfitted (early-stage) representations.
Rather than directly translating across stages, Diffusion Bridge
Learning adopts a prototype-mediated design and learns bidi-
rectional sample–prototype transformations via a conditional
denoising diffusion model. Paired alignment and cycle consis-
tency stabilize stochastic translation, while cross-entropy and
logit-pair KL regularization preserve emotion-discriminative
decision structure during geometry reshaping.
Across cross-lingual speech emotion recognition in both di-
rections (English to Japanese and Japanese to English), Diffu-
sion Bridge Learning yields consistent and statistically signif-
icant improvements in out-of-domain weighted average recall.
These improvements are consistent with the view that diffusion-
based stochastic projection encourages more class-centered rep-
resentation geometry and reduces over-specialized directions
in the representation space. The strongest gains arise when
bridging heterogeneous representations (overfit–underfit), par-
ticularly on the underfitted-side classifier, indicating improved
discriminability without sacrificing robustness. Under strong
bridge constraints, Diffusion Bridge Learning achieves up to
+6.42percentage points on English to Japanese and+4.00
percentage points on Japanese to English, with improvements
saturating around(λ pair,λ cycle) = (10,1). These results high-
light that prototype-guided diffusion bridging is a practical and
effective mechanism for mitigating encoder overfitting and im-
proving cross-language generalization in speech emotion recog-
nition.
Future work will extend Diffusion Bridge Learning beyond
sample–prototype mappings toward direct cross-domain trans-
formations, explore more efficient diffusion schedules for faster
training and inference, and further analyze when bridging is
most beneficial using geometric diagnostics such as anisotropy
and curvature.
