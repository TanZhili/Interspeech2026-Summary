Abstract
We present VisionSFX, a real-time, vision-grounded video-
to-audio (V2A) workflow that generates editable, binaurally-
rendered audio from silent video. Conventional V2A systems
output a single mono mixture of all sources, which precludes
the per-source editing and spatial control routinely required
in film post-production, game engines, and AR/VR. End-to-
end binaural systems can produce spatialized output but rely
on large paired video–binaural training corpora. VisionSFX
adopts a training-free route. A vision-language model first de-
composes the scene into discrete sound events. Each event is
generated independently by a pretrained V2A model within its
own temporal window, while ambience is produced separately
by a video-agnostic text-to-audio model. The resulting tracks
are then spatialized post-hoc using optical-flow centroids with
ego-motion correction, monocular depth, and binaural render-
ing. The pipeline requires no training, fine-tuning, or paired
binaural data. Because each source is generated and stored sep-
arately, editing one—through re-prompting, repositioning, or
timeline injection—leaves all others unchanged. At the booth,
visitors upload a video clip and watch a per-source timeline
populate within approximately one minute. They then drag
sources around in 3D space, rewrite their prompts, or inject new
events, with each change reflected on headphones in real time.
Index Terms: video-to-audio, event-wise generation, binaural
rendering, vision-language model, real-time demonstration
1. Introduction
Real-world scenes contain multiple sound sources, and pro-
fessional audio workflows—in game engines, film post-
production, and AR/VR—require access to each source indi-
vidually. However, most video-to-audio (V2A) models, such
as MMAudio [1] and FoleyCrafter [2], output a single mono
waveform that mixes all sources. To remedy this limitation, a
step-by-step V2A method [3] generates per-event tracks using
a trained negative audio guidance module that augments a pre-
trained V2A backbone. Nevertheless, the quality is still limited
depending on playback environments since the generated audio
is mono. In parallel, several end-to-end V2A methods [4, 5]
generate stereo or binaural audio directly but commit to a fixed
output format determined by their training data. In addition,
substantial training data were required to model the end-to-end
V2A; for instance, ViSAudio [4] required constructing a dedi-
cated 97K-pair video-binaural corpus for this purpose.
In contrast, we propose a fundamentally different ap-
proach that combines off-the-shelf pretrained models (a vision-
language model (VLM), a V2A generator, and monocular

and spark discussion on the future directions of compositional,

3. Demonstration
The booth includes a dual-screen laptop, headphones for bin-
aural playback, and a LAN router connecting the laptop to the
NVIDIA DGX Spark under the table. We present the UI (Fig.
2) so visitors can experience VisionSFX through the following
steps.
Step 1: Upload.The visitor uploads a video via theUpload
Videobutton. If the video already contains audio, our workflow
strips it and retains it as a reference for comparison with the
generated audio.
Step 2: V2A Generation.The full pipeline—event-wise
scene decomposition, per-source audio generation, and HRTF
rendering—runs automatically. Within approximately 1 min for
a 10 s clip, the timeline is populated with independently gener-
ated sources.
Step 3: Audio Editing.After generation, visitors can explore
the following editing features:
•Prompt editing:Clicking a source shows its auto-generated
text prompt, which can be rewritten; pressingGenregener-
ates only that source.
•Timeline injection:Dragging across an empty region on the
timeline creates a new sound event for that span.
•Spatial controls:Azimuth and elevation sliders reposition
a source laterally, while the top-down depth view adjusts its
perceived distance, all in real time (≈0.3 s).

Step 4: Rendering.TheRenderbutton opens an export dialog

or separate per-source track files. Several codecs and container

Key takeaway.Visitors will experience three properties first-
hand: (i) editing one source leaves all others unchanged;
(ii) repositioning a source in 3D space is instantaneous as no
audio is regenerated; and (iii) binaural rendering produces spa-
tial depth that a mono waveform cannot convey.
