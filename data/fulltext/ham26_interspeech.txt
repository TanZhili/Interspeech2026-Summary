Abstract
Recent speaker verification (SV) systems often employ hy-
brid architectures that combine spectral–temporal modeling
with temporal aggregation. However, approaches such as
ReDimNet perform global dependency modeling after col-
lapsing the spectral–temporal grid into a temporal representa-
tion, which removes the explicit spectral–temporal structure.
We propose Continuous 2D Spectral–Temporal Transformer
(C2D-ST), which maintains the spectral–temporal representa-
tion throughout the backbone and performs global dependency
modeling directly on the spectral–temporal grid, with temporal-
only modeling applied only in a final stage. Experiments on
V oxCeleb show that C2D-ST achieves competitive performance
with strong parameter efficiency, reaching 0.507 average EER
with 6.9M parameters.
Index Terms: speaker recognition, speaker verification
1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV) aims to determine whether two
speech recordings belong to the same speaker by extract-
ing speaker-discriminative representations from speech signals.
Most modern SV systems[1][2][3][4][5][6][7] operate on time–
frequency representations such as log Mel filterbanks, where
both spectral and temporal information contribute to speaker
identity modeling.
Recent SV systems such as [3][4][5][6][7] adopt hybrid ar-
chitectures that combine spectral–temporal and temporal mod-
eling. In particular, ReDimNet [7] repeatedly reshapes interme-
diate representations between spectral–temporal and temporal
formats during stage-wise processing.
While this paradigm has demonstrated strong performance,
collapsing the frequency axis removes the explicit spectral–
temporal grid structure before global dependency modeling is
applied.
These observations raise several structural questions for
speaker representation learning: whether global dependency
modeling should be performed after collapsing the spectral–
temporal grid or directly on the spectral–temporal representa-
tion, and whether modeling dependencies across both spectral
and temporal dimensions can improve speaker representation
learning.
To address these questions, we proposeContinuous 2D
Spectral–Temporal Transformer (C2D-ST)1, a speaker ver-
ification architecture that performs global dependency model-
ing directly on the spectral–temporal grid. The proposed model

bone that preserves the spectral–temporal grid structure. Within

2.1. Overall Architecture
The overall architecture of C2D-ST is illustrated in Fig. 1. The
model consists of the following components:
C2D-ST consists of a spectral–temporal attention back-
bone, stage-wise feature aggregation, a 1D projection layer, a
final 1D temporal modeling stage, attentive statistics pooling
(ASP)[10], and an embedding projection.
The input acoustic feature is a spectral–temporal represen-
tation
X∈R B×C×F×T ,
whereBdenotes the batch size,Cthe channel dimension,
Fthe number of frequency bins, andTthe number of temporal
frames.
The backbone consists of five spectral–temporal attention
stages containing a total of 16 transformer[11] layers. Each
spectral–temporal stage consists of Neighborhood Attention
layers followed by a stage-final Axial Attention layer. Thus,
local and global dependencies are modeled within the spec-
tral–temporal grid without collapsing the frequency axis.
We use RoPE[12] as positional encoding for Axial At-
tention in the Spectral–Temporal Attention Backbone and for
Global Attention in the Final Temporal Modeling Stage.
The outputs of multiple backbone stages are converted into
temporal representations. These representations are aggregated
using learnable scalar weights following the stage-wise aggre-
gation strategy introduced in ReDimNet.
After aggregation, a one-dimensional projection layer ad-
justs the channel dimension before the representation is pro-
cessed by a final temporal modeling stage. The resulting fea-
tures are then passed to attentive statistics pooling to produce
speaker embeddings.
2.2. Spectral–Temporal Attention Backbone
The spectral–temporal backbone operates on the two-
dimensional feature map
X∈R B×C×F×T .
In contrast to architectures that collapse the spectral dimen-
sion early in the network, the proposed backbone preserves the
spectral–temporal grid representation throughout all backbone
stages.
Each stage consists of transformer layers composed of at-
tention modules and feed-forward networks. Two types of at-
tention mechanisms are employed:
• Neighborhood Attention for local spectral–temporal model-
ing
• Axial Attention for global spectral and temporal dependency
modeling
Both mechanisms operate directly on the spectral–temporal
grid.
The internal stage structure is based on a modified
ReDimNet-style design, where neighborhood attention layers
capture local spectral–temporal dependencies and an axial at-
tention layer models global dependencies.

3. Experimental Setup
Our training and evaluation pipeline is based on an adapted ver-
sion of ESPnet-SPK [16].
3.1. Datasets
The model is trained on the V oxCeleb2[17] development set
and evaluated on the V oxCeleb1 benchmark, including the
V oxCeleb1-O, V oxCeleb1-E, and V oxCeleb1-H test protocols.
3.2. Input Features
Speech signals are sampled at 16 kHz and converted into 80-
dimensional log Mel filterbank features using a 25 ms window
and a 10 ms hop size. The FFT size is 512.
3.3. Training
The model is pretrained from scratch on V oxCeleb2 using ran-
dom 3-second speech segments. Pretraining is performed for 20
epochs using AdamW with cosine learning rate scheduling, an
initial learning rate of 0.008, and a weight decay of 0.05. Large-
margin finetuning (LMFT) [18] is subsequently applied for two
epochs using 6-second speech segments with an initial learning
rate of 0.0003 and the same weight decay.
3.4. Data Augmentation
During pretraining, we use speed perturbation[19] with factors
of 0.9 and 1.1, while MUSAN noise[20] and simulated room
impulse responses[21] are applied with probability 0.5. All aug-
mentation is disabled during LMFT.
3.5. Loss Function
SphereFace2[22] (C-type) loss is used for training. The mar-
gin is set to 0.2 during pretraining and increased to 0.3 during
LMFT.
3.6. Evaluation
Model performance is evaluated using Equal Error Rate (EER)
and minimum Detection Cost Function (minDCF). Adaptive
score normalization (AS-Norm) [23] is applied with a cohort
size of 500 using the V oxCeleb2 development set. The QMF
setting follows the quality-measure based scoring setup in [24],
except that SNR-based quality features are not used. For all re-
ported results other than Table 1, LMFT, AS-Norm, and QMF
are applied. During inference, full-length utterances are used
for speaker embedding extraction. Minimum detection cost
function (minDCF) is computed withP target = 0.01,C miss = 1,
andC fa = 1.
4. Results and Analysis
This section presents the experimental results and analyzes the
effectiveness of the proposed C2D-ST architecture. All experi-
ments follow the training and evaluation protocols described in
Section 3.
4.1. Overall Performance
Table 1 compares the proposed model with several recent
speaker verification architectures on the V oxCeleb1 benchmark.
The proposed C2D-ST model achieves competitive perfor-
mance with only 6.9M parameters. Without large-margin fine-
tuning (LMFT) and quality measure function (QMF), the model
achieves an average EER of 0.627. After LMFT, the average
EER improves to 0.517, and the final configuration with both
LMFT and QMF achieves an average EER of 0.507.
Compared with ReDimNet-B6, which has 15.0M pa-
rameters, C2D-ST achieves lower average EER/minDCF of
0.507/0.051 versus 0.633/0.059 while using less than half
the parameters. This suggests that maintaining the spectral–
temporal grid during backbone modeling can support effective
speaker representation learning with improved parameter effi-
ciency.
Compared with ECAPA2, which has 27.1M parameters,
C2D-ST achieves lower average EER/minDCF with fewer than
one-third of the parameters. Specifically, C2D-ST achieves
an average EER/minDCF of 0.507/0.051, compared with
0.617/0.062 for ECAPA2, and improves the V oxCeleb1-H EER
from 0.99 to 0.79. These results suggest that C2D-ST can
achieve competitive performance with substantially fewer pa-
rameters than ECAPA2.
4.2. Effect of Spectral–Temporal Global Modeling
To analyze the effect of spectral–temporal global modeling,
we evaluate variants that modify the axial attention mechanism
used in the backbone.
When axial attention is removed and replaced with neigh-
borhood attention, the average EER increases significantly
from 0.507 to 0.693. This result indicates that local spectral–
temporal modeling alone is insufficient and that global depen-
dency modeling is important for speaker verification.
We also evaluate a variant where axial attention is applied
only along the temporal axis while keeping the transformer fea-
ture dimension and number of attention heads unchanged. In
this case, the average EER increases from 0.507 to 0.533. This
degradation suggests that modeling global dependencies along
the frequency axis also contributes to speaker representation
learning.

4.3. Global Modeling With and Without Spectral Collapse
To examine the impact of performing global modeling after col-
lapsing the spectral dimension, we replace axial attention with
a 1D transformer block applied to the temporal representation.
This variant follows the hybrid modeling strategy used in
ReDimNet-style architectures, where the spectral axis is merged
into the channel dimension followed by a 1D transformer block
with pre- and post-linear projections for temporal modeling.
For a controlled comparison, the 1D transformer block uses
the same feature dimension and number of attention heads as
the corresponding axial attention block.
Compared to the proposed model, this design increases the
number of parameters by 2.8M.
As shown in Table 2, this variant increases the average EER
from 0.507 to 0.557. The result suggests that performing global
dependency modeling directly on the spectral–temporal grid
can achieve better EER with fewer parameters than performing
global modeling on a collapsed temporal representation.
4.4. Attention vs Convolution
For the convolutional variants, each selected Neighborhood
Attention transformer block is replaced with two ConvNeXt-
style[25] blocks using the same kernel size and an expansion
ratio of 3 to keep a similar parameter budget.
As shown in Table 4, replacing backbone neighborhood at-
tention with convolution blocks increases the average EER from
0.507 to 0.580, indicating that attention-based local model-
ing better captures spectral–temporal dependencies in the back-
bone.

4.5. Effect of Positional Encoding
We further examine the impact of positional encoding used in
the neighborhood attention layers.
The proposed model employs both relative position bias
(RPB) and relative positional encoding (RPE) to incorporate
positional information in the spectral–temporal grid. To eval-
uate the contribution of RPE, we remove it while keeping RPB
unchanged.
As shown in Table 3, removing RPE slightly degrades the
overall performance, increasing the average EER from 0.507 to
0.513.
These results indicate that while RPB already provides
strong positional information for local attention, the additional
RPE helps the model better capture spectral–temporal posi-
tional relationships.
5. Conclusion
We proposed C2D-ST, a speaker verification architecture that
maintains the spectral–temporal structure throughout the back-
bone and performs global dependency modeling directly on the
spectral–temporal grid.
Experiments on V oxCeleb show that C2D-ST achieves
competitive performance with 6.9M parameters, including an
average EER/minDCF of 0.507/0.051. Ablation studies further
show that spectral–temporal global modeling improves perfor-
mance and parameter efficiency compared to the collapsed tem-
poral global-modeling variant.
These results suggest that preserving the spectral–temporal
representation throughout the backbone and performing global
dependency modeling directly on the spectral–temporal grid is
an effective design choice for this hybrid SV architecture.
