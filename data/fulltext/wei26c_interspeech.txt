Abstract
To address multiuser interference in in-vehicle personal sound
zones (PSZ), this paper proposes a perceptually weighted
acoustic multi-user MIMO (MU-MIMO) precoding framework.
Traditional spatial audio methods often neglect human psychoa-
coustics, treating cross-talk merely as uniform physical energy.
To overcome this, we integrate a psychoacoustic masking model
into the Weighted Minimum Mean Square Error (WMMSE) cri-
terion. The algorithm dynamically assigns stronger distortion
penalties to perceptually sensitive time-frequency regions while
relaxing constraints in zones heavily masked by target speech.
By actively coordinating precoding filters, this method mini-
mizes the perceptually weighted MSE, jointly balancing inter-
user interference and target-signal distortion. Validated using
measured impulse responses from a real car cabin, the approach
shows improvements in objective speech quality over baselines,
offering a scalable solution for smart cockpit audio.
Index Terms: in-vehicle personal sound zones, perceptual
weighting, sound zone control
1. Introduction
Personal sound zones (PSZ) enable independent audio delivery
to multiple listeners sharing a common acoustic environment
[1, 2]. Deploying such systems in automotive cabins, however,
remains challenging due to adverse acoustic conditions. The
cabin is a compact, reflective enclosure that gives rise to a dense
and reverberant sound field with complex multipath propagation
[3, 4]. This environment leads to two critical issues. First, audio
intended for one listener leaks into other seats through direct
paths and boundary reflections. Second, the acoustic transfer
functions exhibit pronounced frequency-selective fading. Fur-
thermore, spatial degrees of freedom are limited by constrained
loudspeaker placements and passenger proximity [5]. Conse-
quently, the automotive setting amplifies the core difficulties of
PSZ realization and demands algorithms capable of robust in-
terference management under highly adverse and spatially re-
stricted conditions.
Conventional spatial audio techniques, such as acoustic
contrast control and pressure matching [6, 7, 8, 9], primarily
optimize spatial energy distribution. These methods generally
treat crosstalk as uniformly detrimental acoustic energy, lack-
ing a perceptually guided mechanism for coordinated suppres-
sion. Subsequent developments, including variable span linear
filters [10, 11, 12] and the V AST framework [13, 14, 15], have
improved acoustic separation, yet still do not systematically in-
corporate perceptual characteristics of human hearing. Notably,

a fundamental requirement in multiuser PSZ scenarios.
Coordinating multiuser interference is a central problem in
wireless communications. In multiuser MIMO systems, zero-
forcing and MMSE precoding are routinely employed for active
spatial interference management. Among these, the weighted
minimum mean square error (WMMSE) algorithm [16] trans-
forms nonconvex sum-rate maximization into tractable sub-
problems. A key advantage of the WMMSE framework lies
in its ability, through user-specific weighting matrices and re-
ceive equalizers, to match target channels and synthesize nulls
toward unintended users [17, 18, 19]. This characteristic ren-
ders the framework naturally suited for PSZ applications requir-
ing simultaneous control of acoustic errors in multiple zones.
Nevertheless, adapting WMMSE directly to the acoustic do-
main poses significant theoretical and technical challenges. It
requires bridging the gap between narrowband stationary chan-
nel models and broadband dynamic speech signals, while main-
taining algorithmic robustness in a low-rank, highly reverberant
cabin environment.
Effectively adapting WMMSE for acoustic PSZ necessi-
tates a frequency-domain parallel processing architecture that
respects the properties of human auditory perception. The au-
ditory system spans 20 Hz to 20 kHz, across which distinct
physical behaviors manifest [3, 20, 21]. More importantly, the
human ear does not perceive acoustic errors uniformly. Per-
ceptual models based on psychoacoustic masking thresholds

based on measured impulse responses. The measured impulse
responses were collected in a sedan using a loudspeaker array
and a microphone array. The transmit array consists ofN L = 7

cabin, while the receiving microphone array comprises four

ing in a total of64receiving sensors, as shown in Fig.1. To
account for measurement variability, the ATFs were measured
three times under identical conditions, and all results reported

4.2. Evaluation Results
This section evaluates the proposed perceptually weighted
WMMSE method (MU MIMO Perceptual) against sound zone
control algorithms, including ACC(True)/PM(True) as a base-
line under undisturbed conditions, conventional ACC-PM with
κ= 0.7[9], narrowband V AST withµ= 1,V= 7[12], worst-
case RACC withγ B≈ε B,γ D≈ε D [31], and POTDC RACC
with parametersα I,α u,η=ε B, andγ D [32].
To quantitatively assess speech quality, we employ two
metrics: STOI (Short-Time Objective Intelligibility), and
ViSQOL (Virtual Speech Quality Objective Listener) [33],
where higher values indicate better intelligibility or perceived
quality. These metrics provide a comprehensive view of intelli-
gibility and perceptual fidelity.
Table 1 summarizes the STOI and ViSQOL scores for each
algorithm. Among conventional methods, PM (0.855/4.184)
and ACC PM (0.854/4.185) outperform ACC (0.819/4.086), in-
dicating that combining pressure matching with contrast con-
trol improves fidelity. V AST NF (0.855/4.184) performs simi-
larly to PM but offers no additional perceptual gain in this envi-
ronment. The robust methods, wc RACC and POTDC RACC,
exhibit degradation, with POTDC RACC achieving the lowest
ViSQOL, suggesting that overly conservative robustness con-
straints impair audio quality.
In contrast, unweighted MU MIMO NoWeight achieves
superior STOI (0.857) and ViSQOL (4.183) compared
to baselines, confirming that multiuser optimization miti-
gates inter-user interference. With perceptual weighting,
MU MIMO Perceptual attains the highest scores across met-
rics, demonstrating that incorporating psychoacoustic masking
enhances perceived quality while preserving intelligibility. This
suggests that perceptual weighting provides consistent gain over
the unweighted MU-MIMO baseline. These results validate the
effectiveness of the proposed framework in automotive cabin
environments.
Beyond speech quality, we examine the trade-offs among
acoustic contrast(AC), normalized reconstruction error (NRE),
and acoustic efficiency (AE) [32]. Fig. 2 compares performance
of algorithms across the 100–8000 Hz frequency range. While
conventional methods such as ACC and PM achieve high acous-
tic contrast at frequencies, this often comes at the expense of
reconstruction accuracy or radiation efficiency. Robust meth-
ods, including wc RACC and POTDC RACC, offer improved
robustness but suffer from acoustic-contrast degradation due to
conservative constraints. In contrast, the proposed MU-MIMO
framework strikes a balance among these three metrics across
the frequency spectrum. Experimental results demonstrate that,

5. Conclusion
This paper presents a perceptually weighted MSE framework
for automotive personal sound zones. By integrating psychoa-
coustic masking into spatial filter design, the method minimizes
the perceptually weighted MSE with stronger penalties in per-
ceptually sensitive regions. Unlike conventional bright-dark
zone paradigms, it minimizes weighted reconstruction and in-
terference errors across multiple zones. The approach adapts
the weighted MSE precoding methodology to reverberant cabin
environments, offering a solution for enhanced perceived audio
quality. Future work will focus on further improving inter-user
interference mitigation and algorithm robustness under practi-
cal uncertainties.
