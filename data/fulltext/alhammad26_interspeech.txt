Interpretable Frequency-Band Attention with Gated SSL Fusion
for Audio Deepfake Detection
Abeer Alhammad1, Abdullah Aldahlawi1
1 Thaka, Advanced AI and Information Technology
Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
{a.alhammad, aldahlawi}@thakaait.net
Abstract
Synthetic and manipulated speech threatens automatic speaker
verification. Self-supervised (SSL) front-ends achieve low error
rates but operate as black boxes, revealing little about where in
the spectrum spoofing artifacts reside. We propose BandMIL,
a band-aware multiple instance learning (MIL) framework that
augments SSL representations with interpretable frequency-
band analysis. Overlapping windows are split into eight band-
specific spectrogram images, encoded by a shared convolutional
encoder combined with handcrafted per-band features, and
pooled via band attention. Gated fusion adaptively integrates
these cues with WavLM representations, and MIL aggregates
window predictions into an utterance-level decision without
truncation. On ASVspoof 2019 LA, BandMIL achieves 1.28%
EER and 0.0331 min t-DCF, improving over SSL-only (1.73%
EER). Per-attack analysis shows the band and SSL branches are
complementary, while band attention reveals which frequencies
drive each decision.

1. Introduction
Automatic speaker verification (ASV) systems are vulnerable
to spoofing attacks generated by text-to-speech (TTS) synthe-
sis and voice conversion (VC) algorithms [1]. The ASVspoof
challenge series has driven significant progress in developing
countermeasures (CMs), with recent systems achieving sub-1%
Equal Error Rate (EER) on the ASVspoof 2019 logical ac-
cess (LA) benchmark [2]. Despite these advances, most high-
performing detectors analyze the full-band signal holistically
and lack explicit modeling of frequency-localized spoofing ar-
tifacts.
Two dominant paradigms have emerged. End-to-end archi-
tectures such as AASIST [2] and RawNet2 [3] learn represen-
tations directly from raw waveforms, achieving strong overall
performance. Self-supervised learning (SSL) front-ends such
as wav2vec 2.0 [4] and WavLM [5] have further improved ro-
bustness by leveraging large-scale pre-trained speech represen-
tations [6, 7, 8]. However, these systems typically operate as
monolithic architectures that provide limited insight intowhere
in the frequency spectrum spoofing artifacts reside.
Understanding frequency-level detection cues is valuable
for multiple reasons: it provides researchers with actionable
knowledge about spoofing algorithm weaknesses, enables tar-
geted analysis in deployment, and builds trust in CM decisions.
Chettri et al. [9] proposed a joint sub-band CNN framework
and found the lowest and highest 1 kHz bands most discrimi-
native for replay detection on ASVspoof 2017. Tak et al. [10]
demonstrated that a bank of sub-band classifiers with non-linear
score fusion can outperform complex neural architectures on
ASVspoof 2019 LA. Yet existing sub-band methods either com-
bine per-band models through concatenation or score-level fu-
sion, or lack a complementary representation for attacks that do
not leave frequency-localized traces.
We proposeBandMIL, a two-branch architecture that com-
bines explicit frequency-band analysis with SSL representations
through adaptive gated fusion. The band branch decomposes
the spectrogram intoK=8overlapping frequency bands, en-
codes each with a shared ResNet-18 [11], and aggregates them
via learned attention that reveals which frequency regions are
most informative. The SSL branch processes the raw wave-
form through WavLM-Large to capture global temporal pat-
terns. A gated fusion module learns to adaptively weight the
two branches per input, enabling the model to suppress band-
level features when spectral cues are absent and rely more on
SSL, or vice versa. Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) aggre-
gates window-level predictions into an utterance-level decision,
accommodating variable-length audio and modeling the tempo-
ral sparsity of spoofing artifacts.
To our knowledge, BandMIL is the first system to combine
explicit frequency-band decomposition with self-supervised
representations through adaptive gated fusion for spoofing de-
tection. Our contributions are:
1. An interpretable two-branch architecture where per-band at-
tention weights reveal which frequency regions drive detec-
tion for each utterance—a capability absent in monolithic
SSL and end-to-end approaches—while retaining competi-
tive accuracy (1.28% EER) on ASVspoof 2019 LA.
2. A per-attack analysis demonstrating complementary detec-
tion mechanisms: band decomposition captures frequency-
localized artifacts in most TTS/VC attacks, while SSL rep-
resentations compensate for attacks that lack frequency-
localized traces, with gated fusion reducing EER from
1.73% (SSL-only) to 1.28% and providing interpretable fre-
quency analysis.
3. A MIL framework for utterance-level spoofing detection that
handles variable-length audio without truncation and pro-
vides per-window localization of spoofing artifacts.

2.2. Self-supervised representations for anti-spoofing
Self-supervised speech models such as wav2vec 2.0 [4] and
WavLM [5] are effective front-ends for spoofing detection.
Prior work pairs these SSL front-ends with anti-spoofing back-
ends such as AASIST [2] (used, e.g., with a wav2vec 2.0 front-
end [6]) and attentive pooling or fusion strategies [7, 8], but
these systems do not explicitly model the relative importance of
frequency sub-bands.
2.3. Frequency-band and sub-band analysis
Motivated by frequency-localized spoofing artifacts, sub-band
modeling has been explored by jointly training per-band sub-
networks whose features are combined before a shared classi-
fier [9], or by non-linear fusion of the scores of independently
trained per-band classifiers [10], with extensions that exploit
complex-spectrogram (magnitude and phase) information [13].
However, many approaches either fuse bands post-hoc or lack
a complementary global representation for attacks with weak
spectral artifacts.Our approachdiffers by: (i) learning explicit
bandimportancevia band-level attention (rather than equal-
weight feature concatenation [9] or post-hoc score fusion [10]);
(ii) integrating band cues with SSL features using gated fusion
that adapts per input; and (iii) using MIL to aggregate over-
lapping windows into utterance-level decisions, reflecting the
temporal sparsity of spoofing artifacts.
3. Proposed Method
Figure 1 illustrates the proposed BandMIL system. A variable-
length utterance is segmented into overlapping 4 s windows
(2 s hop). Each window is processed by two complemen-
tary branches: a band-level encoder that analyzes frequency-
localized spectral artifacts, and a self-supervised (SSL) encoder
that captures global temporal patterns. The branches are com-
bined via gated fusion, and window-level predictions are aggre-
gated into an utterance-level decision through Multiple Instance
Learning (MIL).
3.1. Band-Level Encoder
For each window, we compute an STFT (1024-point FFT,
160-sample hop, Hann window) and convert the magnitude
spectrogram to dB scale. The 0–8 kHz range is divided into
K=8overlapping bands (25% overlap). Each band is rendered
as a64×256grayscale image using global dB normalization
([−80,0]dB→[0,255]), which preserves cross-band energy
relationships important for detecting abnormal high-frequency
patterns.
A shared ResNet-18 [11] (modified for single-channel in-
put) encodes each band image into a 512-d vector. This vector
is concatenated with 24 per-band features (capturing spectral,
energy, phase, and temporal characteristics) and then projected
tod=256via a linear layer with LayerNorm and GELU.

The raw waveform of each window is processed by WavLM-

A learned gate adaptively balances the two branches. A two-

(1−g)⊙h ssl, enabling the model to suppress either branch de-

set EER.
4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Dataset
We evaluate on the ASVspoof 2019 LA partition [1], which
contains bona fide and spoofed utterances generated by 6 known
attacks (A01–A06) in the training and development sets, and
13 attacks (A07–A19) in the evaluation set.
4.2. Evaluation metrics
We report Equal Error Rate (EER) and minimum tandem de-
tection cost function (min t-DCF) [16] following the official
ASVspoof 2019 LA evaluation protocol with the standard cost
model (Pspoof=0.05,C fa=10,C miss=1). Per-attack EER is re-
ported for the evaluation set to assess generalization to unseen
attacks.

4.3. Baselines
We compare with the following published systems: the CQCC-
GMM and LFCC-GMM baselines from the ASVspoof 2019 LA
challenge [1], RawNet2 [3], RawGAT-ST [12], AASIST [2],
and XLS-R-AASIST [17]. All baseline results are taken from
their respective publications.
4.4. Ablation configurations
To isolate each branch’s contribution, we evaluate three config-
urations, all sharing the training recipe of Sec. 3.4:
•Band-only:ResNet-18 band encoder with attention pooling
overK=8bands, combined with handcrafted features. No
SSL component.
•SSL-only:WavLM-Large (last 12 layers fine-tuned) with a
projection head. No band encoder or handcrafted features.
•Full (BandMIL):Both branches combined via gated fusion
(Sec. 3.3).
All configurations share the same optimizer, schedule, loss,
and training budget, with branch-specific augmentations ap-
plied only where the corresponding branch is present. This en-
sures a controlled ablation in which any performance gap re-
flects the presence or absence of each branch rather than differ-
ences in the training procedure.

5.3. Discussion
BandMIL delivers interpretable detection while maintaining
competitive accuracy, with band attention (Figure 2) high-
lighting high-frequency bands (B8) as the dominant cue for
most attacks. While XLS-R-AASIST [17] achieves lower EER
(0.22%), BandMIL adds frequency-band interpretability useful
in deployment settings requiring auditability.

6. Conclusion
We presented BandMIL, a band-aware multiple instance learn-
ing framework combining interpretable frequency-band anal-
ysis with self-supervised representations for audio deepfake
detection. Unlike monolithic approaches, BandMIL exposes
per-band attention weights that reveal which frequency re-
gions drive detection, and gated fusion adaptively integrates
both branches, achieving 1.28% EER and 0.0331 min t-DCF
on ASVspoof 2019 LA—improving over SSL-only detection
(1.73% EER) while adding frequency-level interpretability.
Per-attack analysis confirms that band and SSL cues are
genuinely complementary: the band branch resolves attacks
leaving frequency-localized traces, while SSL representations
carry those voice-conversion attacks where such traces are
weak, and the gate shifts weight accordingly.
Fusion is not uniformly beneficial, however: on three of
the thirteen evaluation attacks it underperforms both individual
branches, suggesting that a single global gate cannot always de-
termine which branch to trust. Future work will explore attack-
aware gating and broaden evaluation beyond ASVspoof 2019
LA to ASVspoof 2021 [18], ASVspoof 5 [19], and in-the-wild
data [20], assessing generalization to more diverse and recent
attacks.
