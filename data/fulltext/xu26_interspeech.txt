Abstract
Functional link network (FLN) based active noise control
(ANC) algorithms offer strong nonlinear modeling capabilities
but inevitably introduce redundant basis functions during ex-
pansion, leading to inefficient adaptation and degraded steady-
state performance. To improve the performance, we propose
a sparsity-aware robust nonlinear ANC algorithm that incor-
porates a reweighted zero-attracting (RZA) regularization term
into the minimum-output-variance constrained filtered-s least
meanp-power (MOV-FsLMP) framework. This mechanism
acts as an online feature selector, effectively suppressing re-
dundant coefficients induced by FLN expansion while pre-
serving the inherent robustness against impulsive disturbances.
Extensive simulations underα-stable impulsive noise condi-
tions demonstrate that the proposed algorithm consistently out-
performs the conventional MOV-FsLMP, achieving a 1–2 dB
improvement in steady-state mean square error (MSE) and a
higher active noise reduction (ANR) under identical configura-
tions, validating its practical effectiveness for nonlinear ANC
applications.

1. Introduction
Active noise control (ANC) has become an indispensable tech-
nology for mitigating low-frequency acoustic noise in various
industrial and consumer applications [1, 2]. The conventional
Filtered-x Least Mean Square (FxLMS) algorithm [1] is widely
adopted due to its simplicity. However, in practical compact
devices, electro-acoustic transducers (e.g., loudspeakers) often
operate near saturation limits, introducing significantnonlin-
ear distortion[3, 4] that severely degrades the performance of
linear controllers [5].
To address nonlinearities, the functional link neural net-
work (FLNN) based filters, such as the Filtered-s LMS
(FsLMS) algorithm [6, 7], have emerged as a preferred solu-
tion due to their computational efficiency and ability to model
nonlinear mapping through trigonometric or polynomial ex-
pansions. Furthermore, real-world acoustic environments are
often contaminated by non-Gaussian noise such as impulsive
noise. To combat this, robust algorithms based on the least
meanp-power (LMP) criterion have been developed [8, 9, 10].
Recently, integrating these aspects, the minimum output vari-
ance (MOV) constrained FsLMP (MOV-FsLMP) algorithm [11]
was developed. By imposing a constraint on the control sig-
nal power, MOV-FsLMP effectively prevents system divergence

caused by output saturation [12] while maintaining robustness
against impulsive disturbances [13].
However, a critical issue remains unaddressed in the afore-
mentioned state-of-the-art methods: parameter redundancy.
The polynomial or trigonometric expansion in FLN is typically
structure-agnostic, generating a high-dimensional feature space
where many basis functions may contribute negligibly to the
target physical nonlinearity [14]. This over-parameterization
induces excess MSE due to gradient noise accumulation and
degrades convergence and tracking performance by wasting de-
grees of freedom on irrelevant coefficients [15, 16].
To further improve the performance, this work proposes a
sparsity-aware MOV-FsLMP algorithm. Motivated by the phys-
ical observation that the nonlinearity of secondary paths is of-
ten sparse (i.e., dominated by specific low-order harmonics),
we introduce a reweighted zero-attracting (RZA) regularization
[17] into the robust MOV framework. The RZA mechanism
functions as an online feature selector, adaptively distinguish-
ing between significant physical nonlinear terms and redundant
expansions.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as fol-
lows.
• We identify structural redundancy as a key factor limiting the
performance of conventional FLN-based robust ANC algo-
rithms.
• We formulate a unified cost function that integrates impulsive
reduction (Lp-norm), output constraint (MOV), and sparsity
promotion (RZA), deriving a robust update rule that effec-
tively suppresses redundant coefficients.
• Simulation results underα-stable impulsive noise demon-
strate that the proposed method effectively alleviates the pre-
cision–stability trade-off.

Although the integration of the RZA penalty introduces addi-

is negligible and is well-justified by the significant gains in sta-
bility and noise reduction performance demonstrated in Section
5.
From a mechanistic perspective, the proposed update term
in (9) acts as a variable-strength attractor. For redundant coef-
ficients where|w i|≈0, the denominator1 +ε|w i|≈1, and
the penalty force is maximized (≈ρ). For active physical co-
efficients where|w i|≫1/ε, the denominator becomes large,
diminishing the penalty force. This mechanism effectively per-
forms online feature selection, pruning the FLN structure dy-
namically.

5. Simulation Results
5.1. Simulation Setup
In this section, simulations were conducted to evaluate the per-
formance of the proposed sparsity-aware MOV-FsLMP algo-
rithm against the standard FxLMS, FsLMS, and the baseline
MOV-FsLMP [11]. All results were obtained by averaging over
50independent Monte Carlo runs.
5.1.1. Acoustic Paths and Noise Model
The reference noisex(n)was generated using a symmetricα-
stable (SαS) distribution with a characteristic exponentα=
1.6and dispersionγ= 0.1, simulating a highly impulsive noise
source. To strictly evaluate the nonlinear modeling capability,
the primary path was modeled by a synthetic nonlinear interac-
tion with memory. The primary noised(n)at the error micro-
phone was generated according to the same difference equation
as in [11], given by
d(n) =x(n−2)+0.08x 2(n−2)−0.04x3(n−1)+v(n),(15)
wherev(n)is a background white Gaussian noise floor with a
variance of10 −4 (amplitude scaled by0.01). The secondary
pathS(z), representing the electro-acoustic transfer function
from the secondary loudspeaker to the error microphone, was
modeled as a non-minimum phase linear FIR filter ofS(z) =
z−2 + 0.5z−3 [11].
5.1.2. Algorithm Configuration
A trigonometric FLN expansion with orderP= 3was em-
ployed for all nonlinear controllers, resulting in an expansion
length ofL=M(2P+ 1). The step sizes were carefully tuned
for each experiment to ensure fair comparison. The key param-
eters for the proposed algorithm were set toρ rza = 2e−4and
ε= 20to promote sparsity.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we addressed the problem of parameter redun-
dancy in FLN-based ANC systems. We identified that the blind
expansion of nonlinear basis functions introduces structural re-
dundancy, which not only elevates the steady-state excess er-
ror but also severely limits the stability bound of the adaptation
step size. By integrating a RZA regularization term into the
robust MOV constraint framework, the proposed method func-
tions as an online feature selector that dynamically prunes re-
dundant coefficients. The simulation results demonstrated that
the proposed algorithm achieves a steady-state noise reduction
improvement of1–2 dBover the baseline and improves the sta-
bility region of the filter. Future work will focus on validat-
ing the proposed algorithm on hardware platforms with real-
time acoustic paths and extending the sparsity mechanism to
V olterra-based ANC systems.
