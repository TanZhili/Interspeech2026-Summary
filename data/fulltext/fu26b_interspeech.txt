Abstract
Generative speech enhancement has shown significant advance-
ments in improving speech quality in noisy environments. How-
ever, its iterative nature suffers from high inference computa-
tional complexity. In this paper, we proposeEffDiffSE+,
an efficient single-iteration diffusion-based speech enhance-
ment model based on a condition DNN, a bridge DNN, and
our novel learnable Schr ¨odinger bridge (SB). Our contribu-
tions are threefold. First, we propose asingle-iteration SB
with Gaussian distribution initializationfor the reverse pro-
cess. Second, an auxiliary network is proposed to provide
learnable adaptivity to the bridge DNN initial state estima-
tion. Third,topology improvementsare presented, constitut-
ing ourEffDiffSE+to consistently improve model perfor-
mance. The proposedEffDiffSE+model excels top open-
source time- and frequency-domain diffusion baseline methods
in PESQ, POLQA, NISQA, UTMOS, ESTOI, LPS, SBScore,
SpkSim, and subjective MOS, clearly achieving an overall top
rank.
Index Terms: single-iteration diffusion, speech enhancement,
learnable Schr¨odinger bridge
1. Introduction
Diffusion-based generative speech enhancement (SE) models
have recently gained widespread attention [1, 2], in which two
processes between the clean speech distribution and the noisy
signal prior distribution are measured. The forward process
transforms the clean speech distribution into a known noisy sig-
nal (usually a Gaussian noise) prior distribution, while the re-
verse process aims at sampling the clean speech distribution
starting from the prior distribution. A deep neural network
(DNN) is commonly trained to guide the reverse process. Re-
cent successful diffusion-based SE methods are [3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8].
A hybrid approach combining discriminative and genera-
tive methods takes the pre-enhanced speech from the discrim-
inative network as input to or condition for the generative net-
work, providing better performance on both residual noise com-
pression and speech component reconstruction [9, 10, 11]. Most
importantly, starting from or conditioned on the known pre-
enhanced speech can significantly reduce the number of iter-
ations in the reverse process, reducing the computational com-
plexity to a large margin [12, 13].
Schr¨odinger bridge (SB)-based diffusion, whose goal is to
describe the data-to-data process, starts the reverse process di-
rectly from the observed noisy speech data, instead of a noisy
prior [8]. SB-based SE models have reported promising per-
formance among diffusion-based SE models [6, 14]. However,
solving the vanilla SB still requires executing a costly multi-
iteration reverse process for simulating the stochastic processes,
which limits its efficiency and applicability [15, 16].
In this paper, we proposeEffDiffSE+, an adaptive and
efficient single-iteration SB-based SE model. Our contributions
are threefold. First, a Gaussian distribution initialization, in-
stead of the noisy speech data, is proposed as the initial state
in the reverse process of SB to achieve matched conditions in
both training and inference. Second, an adaptive mean and stan-
dard deviation estimation is realized by an auxiliary network to
further improve the initial state estimation. Third, we demon-
strate the effectiveness of the topology improvements on the
noise-consistent Langevin dynamics basedEffDiffSE[13],
including a sub-band down/upsampling strategy with sub-pixel
convolution, a learnable periodic activation (Snake), and an ef-
fective interaction module for condition information processing.
The results in terms of both intrusive and non-intrusive met-
rics demonstrate the outstanding performance of the proposed
EffDiffSE+among the most popular generative SE models,
with a sizeable model and the lowest computational complexity.

2.3. ProposedEffDiffSE+Topology and Losses
As shown in Fig. 1, during training, the condition DNN takes
noisy speechYas input to estimate the enhanced speech ˆXcond
and speech conditionsSCi,i∈I={1,2,3,4}, where 4 is the
number of the downsampling and upsampling blocks in both
condition DNN and bridge DNN. The condition DNN is sub-
ject to a psychoacoustic lossJ cond( ˆXcond,X)by Braun et al.
[20]. The bridge DNN takes eitherX t calculated by Gaussian
distribution initialization (gray block and (7)), orX SB
t calcu-
lated by the auxiliary network initialization (yellow module and
(9)), together with noise conditionsNCi,i∈I={1,2,3,4},
as inputs of the bridge DNN to directly estimate the enhanced
speech ˆX, benefiting from the property of SB [6, 8]. An auxil-
iary loss
Jaux( ˆX,X) =J cond( ˆX,X) +J L1(ˆx,x)(10)
is introduced, whereJ L1(ˆx,x)operates on time-domain wave-
forms. The network topologies of condition DNN, bridge DNN,
and noise embedding modules are adopted from [13].
Further topology upgrades from the baselineEffDiffSE
[13] are introduced to specify the proposedEffDiffSE+:
(i) Sub-Pixel Convolution, which is adopted to substitute the
deconvolution layer to mitigate checkerboard artifacts in the en-
hanced speech. Sub-pixel convolution [21] is a learnable up-
sampling operation that rearranges feature maps from the chan-
nel dimension into the frequency dimension through a pixel-
shuffling mechanism.
(ii) Snake Activation, which is adopted to substitute all PReLU
activations. The Snake activation [22], achieving monotonicity
and periodicity at the same time, is proven to be effective in

modeling periodic features. A learnableαis adopted to control
the amplitude and frequency by the activation function:
Sα(x) =x+ 1
α sin2(αx).(11)
An additional learnableβin the SnakeBeta activation [23] al-
lows decoupling frequency and amplitude modeling:
Sα,β(x) =x+ 1
α tanh(βsin2(αx)).(12)
(iii) Interaction Module, which is adopted to better model the
bottleneck features, to assist the condition DNN to provide more
clues of clean speech to the generation process in the bridge
DNN. As shown in Fig. 1, an interaction module [24, 25] is first
applied between the condition encoder outputh cond and bridge
encoder outputh SB, within which the discriminative branch can
provide clues of the degraded speech to the current diffusion
stateXt. The block diagram of the interaction module is shown
in Fig. 2 (b). Signalsh cond andh SB are first concatenated and
then fed into a cascade of convolution layer, layer normaliza-
tion (LN), and sigmoid activation to estimate the mask, which
is further used to filterh cond to estimate ˆhSB.
(iv) Sub-band Conv/DeConv Block, which is adopted within
downsampling blocks (DSBlocks) and upsampling blocks (US-
Blocks) to substitute downsampling convolution layer and up-
sampling transpose convolution layer, respectively, to extract
band-aware features [25]. As shown in Fig. 2 (c), the input fea-
tures are first split into low- and high-band (blue and red boxes,
respectively) with bandwidth ratio 1:3. Then they are fed into a
convolution layer without downsampling (stride 1) and a down-
sampling/upsampling convolution layer (stride 3), respectively,
leading to an overall 2-times downsampling/upsampling. The
sub-band Conv block is used to substitute the downsampling
convolution layer in the first two DSBlocks of the condition en-
coder and the bridge encoder, while the sub-band DeConv block
is used to substitute the upsampling transpose convolution layer
in the last two USBlocks of the condition decoder and the bridge
decoder, for topology details [13].
3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets and Metrics
We train and evaluate our proposedEffDiffSE+on the
large-scale diverse-source URGENT 2024 Speech Enhance-
ment Challenge data splits [26], however, excluding the Com-
monV oice 11.0 English portion [27] during training and valida-
tion, due to its occasional background noise. For training and
validation datasets (Dtrain,D val), we mainly follow the strate-
gies in the challenge [26], in which three kinds of distortions
are adopted: additive noise with and without reverberation, and
additive noise with clipping. We do not employ bandwidth lim-
itation distortion from the challenge inD train andD val. The
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) range is [-5, 20] dB. An active
speech level with a range [-36, -16] dB is adopted before mixing
speech and noise data [28]. All waveforms are downsampled to
16 kHz. We configure a 634.5 hD train and a 32.7 hD val. As
the test setD test, we adopt the officially published non-blind
test set of the challenge and downsample to 16 kHz, which con-
tains 1000 waveforms.
We employ intrusive SE metrics, including perceptual eval-
uation of speech quality (PESQ) [29, 30], extended short-
time objective intelligibility (ESTOI) [31], and SDR [32], non-
intrusive SE metrics, including DNSMOS [33], NISQA [34],

3.2. Model Training
We train the proposedEffDiffSE+model with 16 kHz data.
Framing ofX,N,Y,andZis performed with a square-root
Hann window and a DFT frame length ofK= 512samples
with a 25% frameshift. The condition DNN contains an encoder
with one downsampling convolution layer and four DSBlocks,
a decoder with one upsampling convolution layer and four US-
Blocks. A two-layer GRU block is applied between the encoder
and decoder. The bridge DNN shares a similar architecture to
the condition DNN. More details of architecture and hyperpa-
rameters can be found in [13]. The model is trained on a single
Nvidia RTX 3080 Tiwith a batch size of 16 for 1 million
steps. We adopt some popular generative speech enhancement
models as baselines, includingSGMSE+[4],BBED[5],SB[6],
CRP[12],CDiffuSE[7],StoRM[11],Universe++[10],
andEffDiffSE[13]. All these models are trained for 1 M
steps on the same datasetD train asEffDiffSE+for fair com-
parison with the best setups in the corresponding papers. We
also perform inference with the number of steps of the best per-
formance in the corresponding papers. ForCRP, we use theSB
model trained by 950 K steps as the first stage model to deliver
the second stage training for 50 K steps, according to the ratio
of the number of training steps of the two stages in [12].

4. Results and Discussion
4.1. Ablation on Validation Set
In Table 1, we perform an ablation study onDval, with all mod-
els trained for 300 K steps onD train. Here, 1marks the
noise-consistent Langevin dynamics-basedEffDiffSE[13].
For 2, we train SB with the vanilla marginal distribution (1),
where clean speechX 0 is used, witht∈ U(0,1). We in-
fer by ODE initialization withT= 1.0(3) for 4 and 1 re-
verse iterations, respectively, observing that in our case, 4 it-
erations don’t come with a benefit. For 3, we train SB with
the conditioned marginal distribution (7), where the condition
DNN ouputX cond is used, witht∈ U(0,1). First, we em-
ploy a single-iteration reverse process by ODE initialization
withT= 1.0(3), showing clear improvements. Next, we in-
vestigate the optimalT ′ <1.0for the single-iteration reverse
process with our proposed Gaussian distribution initialization
(8). A balanced PESQ and DNSMOS performance is achieved
withT ′ = 0.3. Thus, for 3, we also infer with Gaussian dis-
tribution initialization (8) withT ′ = 0.3for a single reverse
iteration, giving further slight improvements. As concluded in
[13], a totally matched initial state of the reverse process in both
training and inference has a considerable benefit on the model
performance. Thus, for 4, we train SB with amatchedGaus-
sian distribution initialization (7) witht= 0.3, and infer with
Gaussian distribution initialization (8) withT ′ = 0.3for a sin-
gle reverse iteration, yielding slight improvements in PESQ.
For 5, both training and inference use the proposed aux-
iliary network and its initialization (9) for a single iteration,
giving us slight advantages in PESQ and DNSMOS. Based
on 5, we further investigate the effectiveness of each topol-
ogy upgrade (Section 2.3), respectively, including sub-pixel
convolution—resulting in better PESQ, Snake activation (11)—
yielding better PESQ and DNSMOS, SnakeBeta activation
(12)—all metrics improve, interaction module (Fig. 2 (b))—we
see a slight PESQ improvement, and sub-band Conv/DeConv
block (Fig. 2 (c)). The latter shows great improvements over all
metrics. With the auxiliary network and the effective topology
upgrades mentioned above (sub-pixel Conv, SnakeBeta, interac-
tion module, and sub-band Conv/Deconv), the performance of
our ultimate proposalEffDiffSE+is shown in 6, excelling
all other ablations onD val shown in Table 1.

Table 2 shows the performance comparison among the proposed

In Table 2, some of the baselines stand out with distinc-

top overall rank of 1.27, proving significant benefits over the

overall rank 3.09.
5. Conclusions
We proposeEffDiffSE+, a hybrid discriminative and gen-
erative SB-based speech enhancement model with a relatively
small model size (9.40 M) and low computational complexity
(3.84 GMAC/s), the lowest among all investigated approaches.
In inference, a single-iteration reverse process is supported by
a learned Schr ¨odinger bridge (SB) and learned activations. An
auxiliary network estimates the mean and standard deviation in
the SB. We excel all investigated baselines on most intrusive
and non-intrusive metrics, achieving a top subjective MOS of
3.95 and the clearly top overall rank.
