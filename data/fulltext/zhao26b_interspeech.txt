Abstract
This study examined age-related variation in the F0 realization
of prosodic focus across adulthood in Jianghuai Mandarin. A
focus production task was conducted with young, middle-aged,
and older adult speakers, who produced sentences with neutral
focus and three types of narrow focus. The results show that
Jianghuai Mandarin exhibits the canonical tri-zone F0 pattern,
reflected in both mean F0 and F0 excursion, and that this global
pattern is largely preserved across age groups. However, fine-
grained F0 adjustments, i.e., the contrast between narrow and
neutral focus, show age-related variation. In particular, older
speakers demonstrate greater post-focus compression in mean
F0 compared with young and middle-aged speakers. These
findings indicate that prosodic focus across adulthood is char-
acterized by stable global F0 patterning but age-sensitive fine-
grained F0 adjustment.
Index Terms: prosodic focus, F0, speech aging
1. Introduction
Fundamental frequency (F0) is commonly recognized as the pri-
mary acoustic cue for signaling intonation in speech, playing a
central role in marking prosodic structures such as boundary
tones and sentence modality (e.g., questions vs. statements).
Through systematic modulation of a series of F0 cues, speak-
ers convey a wide range of linguistic and paralinguistic infor-
mation, enabling listeners to segment continuous speech, infer
speaker intentions, and construct interpretations. One crucial
linguistic function supported by F0 modulation is the expression
of prosodic focus. Prosodic focus highlights information that
is new, contrastive, or important within an utterance, thereby
guiding listeners’ attention and facilitating efficient information
exchange. For example, in response to the question “What does
Tom like?”, cheese is realized as the focused item in “Tom likes
CHEESE”, typically accompanied by enhanced F0 to indicate
its discourse-relevant prominence.
Given the central role of F0 in signaling prosodic focus, un-
derstanding how speakers maintain or adapt this prosodic func-
tion across the adult lifespan is of particular importance. Aging
is associated with well-documented changes in the speech pro-
duction system, which may affect the realization of F0 cues.
However, whether and how these age-related changes influence
speakers’ ability to use F0 to signal prosodic focus remains in-
sufficiently understood.
1.1. Tri-zone F0 pattern of prosodic focus
Speakers may produce utterances with or without a specific fo-
cus, resulting in distinct intonational patterns. Utterances with-

out a designated focus, typically referred to as having broad
(neutral) focus, are generally regarded as the default prosodic
pattern of intonation [1, 2]. In such utterances, F0 usually shows
a downdrift over the course of the intonational phrase due to the
progressive release of lung volume, a phenomenon known as
declination[3]. This declination pattern, however, can be at-
tenuated, blocked, or even reversed in sentences containing a
narrow focus. Acoustically, narrow-focus utterances exhibit a
characteristic tri-zone F0 pattern: relative to neutral-focus sen-
tences, F0 is raised on the focused (on-focus) constituents, re-
duced in the post-focus region (post-focus compression, PFC),
and largely unchanged in the pre-focus region [1, 2, 4].
Previous research on the F0 realization of prosodic focus
has employed a range of F0-related cues. Most studies extract
F0 values at specific points along the F0 contour. Among these,
mean F0 is the most widely used and fundamental index of F0
realization. To assess whether a language exhibits a tri-zone
pattern, many studies compare the mean F0 of key constituents
in narrow-focus sentences with that in neutral-focus sentences
[1, 2, 4]. In addition to mean F0, other point-based measures,
such as maximum F0, minimum F0, and F0 span or excursion
(defined as the difference between maximum and minimum F0),
have also been commonly adopted [4, 5, 6]. These measures
have likewise been shown to exhibit the tri-zone pattern. Be-
yond these, researchers have examined specific contour land-
marks, including the F0 offset of pre-focus constituents and the
F0 onset of post-focus constituents [7]. Finally, global contour
properties, such as F0 shape, have been quantified using met-
rics like mean absolute slope derived from adjacent F0 values at
normalized time points [8].
The tri-zone F0 pattern has been documented in a wide
range of languages, including English [9], Japanese [10], Turk-
ish [11], Hindi [12], Arabic [4], and Mandarin [2]. That said,
even within the same language family, evidence for a strict tri-
zone pattern is not always consistent, particularly with respect
to the presence of PFC. While robust PFC has been repeatedly
reported in Standard Mandarin, it is weak or absent in Taiwan
Mandarin [13], Chongqing Mandarin [14], and Min [15], Can-
tonese [16]. These studies have proposed that languages can be
broadly classified as +PFC languages, in which PFC is present,
and−PFC languages, in which PFC is absent. Despite this
growing body of work, little is known about how prosodic focus
is encoded through F0 realization in Jianghuai Mandarin.
1.2. Age-related changes in F0 production

at a certain stage of adulthood and to decline gradually there-
after. The vocal and motor systems are no exception, and age-
related changes in these systems may directly affect F0 pro-

of structural alterations occur in the vocal apparatus, includ-

degeneration in the respiratory, laryngeal, and resonant subsys-

been widely reported. Older speakers tend to produce larger
F0 ranges than younger speakers [23]. Moreover, changes in
overall F0 level are sex-dependent: older females often show
reduced F0, whereas older males may exhibit higher, stable, or
lower F0 levels at different stages of aging [17, 18]. These di-
vergent patterns are commonly attributed to age-related histo-
logical and hormonal changes in the laryngeal system [19, 24].
Despite these age-related voice changes, older speakers ap-
pear to largely preserve their ability to use F0 cues effectively
to convey prosodic information in order to express linguistic
functions, although research in this area is far more limited than
studies focusing on vocal aging per se. For example, younger
and older adults have been shown to exhibit comparable pro-
ficiency in producing intonational boundaries [25]. Similarly,
both age groups are able to produce affective prosody, with no
overall difference in mean F0 [26]. Specifically, both younger
and older speakers produce higher mean F0 in sentences spo-
ken with a happy voice than with a sad voice in a reading task,
whereas such differences are absent in a repetition task. Further,
no age-related differences in mean F0 were found when distin-
guishing syntactically ambiguous sentences [27]. Age-related
differences, however, have been reported in the use of F0 to
signal certain linguistic contrasts. For instance, older speakers
show significantly higher mean F0 on stressed syllables than on
unstressed syllables when marking lexical stress, a strategy not
observed in younger speakers [27].
Based on current evidence, research on age-related changes
in F0 realization has suggested that aging may influence how F0
is used to express linguistic functions. However, previous stud-
ies on the tri-zone pattern of prosodic focus have predominantly
relied on young adult speakers. To address this gap, the present
study recruited adults across different age groups to examine the
F0 realization of prosodic focus. By doing so, the study con-
tributes to the theoretical understanding of prosodic focus con-
figuration in Jianghuai Mandarin and clarifies how age-related
changes may reshape the acoustic implementation of focus.
1.3. Current study
This study aimed to investigate age-related influences across
adulthood on the F0 realization of prosodic focus in young,
middle-aged, and older speakers of Jianghuai Mandarin.
Jianghuai Mandarin is a regional variant of Mandarin Chinese
spoken by a population over 70 million people between the Huai
and Yangtze Rivers and features a five-tone system [28].
Specifically, we addressed the following questions: (1)
whether a tri-zone F0 pattern of prosodic focus is present in
Jianghuai Mandarin, and (2) if so, whether this F0 realization,
particularly its patterning and adjustment, undergoes systematic
changes across adulthood.
2. Method
2.1. Participants
Sixty healthy native speakers of Jianghuai Mandarin took part in
the current study, evenly divided into three gender-matched age
groups: young (20–30 years, M = 23.20), middle-aged (40–50
years, M = 44.50), and older (60–70 years, M = 65.10), with 20
participants (10 females) in each group. All participants were
lifelong residents of Guannan County (Jianghuai Mandarin re-
gion [28]), with secondary education, no relevant medical his-
tory, no habitual smoking or drinking, and predominant daily
use of Jianghuai Mandarin. All provided informed consent and
received a nominal fee.
2.2. Materials
The materials comprised five answer frames and four ques-
tion types. All frames followed an SA VO structure (sub-
ject–adverbial–verb–object) and consisted of 10 syllables: a
trisyllabic S, a trisyllabic A, a monosyllabic V , and a trisyl-
labic O, with S, A, and O serving as the three key constituents.
Each answer frame contained only one lexical tone, and the five
frames together represented the five lexical tones of Jianghuai
Mandarin. Within each frame, syllables were controlled for
tone. Four question types (“what happened,” “who,” “when,”
and “what”) were used to elicit sentences with neutral focus
(NF) or narrow focus on the subject (initial focus, IF), adverbial
(medial focus, MF), or object (final focus, FF), respectively.
2.3. Procedure
Recordings were conducted in a soundproof booth using a head-
worn professional microphone, positioned at a fixed distance of
approximately 5 cm from the participant’s mouth. Stimulus pre-
sentation and recording were controlled with xRecorder [29].
Twenty prompt questions (4 question types × 5 answer frames)
were displayed in a random order. The sampling rate was set
at 22.05 kHz, and the sampling format was one channel. All
responses were automatically recorded and saved as W A V files.
A total of 1,200 recordings were collected.
2.4. Measurement
Speech signals were annotated and analyzed in Praat using
ProsodyPro [30] by sampling nine equally spaced points per
syllable. F0 (Hz) was converted to semitones (St) using a sex-
specific reference (Fr = 64 Hz for females, 55 Hz for males
[31]). Analyses focused on the three key constituents only.
Mean F0 and F0 excursion (calculated by the difference be-
tween the maximum F0 and the minimum F0) were computed
per constituent to estimate F0 patterning, and narrow-minus-
neutral focus differences were calculated for both measures to
capture F0 adjustment [2, 4, 15].
2.5. Statistical analysis
Data analysis was carried out in R. Linear mixed-effect (LME)
models were fitted to examine F0 pattern (Group × Constituent
× Focus) and F0 adjustment (Group × Constituent × Focus

4. Discussion
4.1. Preserved F0 patterning across adulthood
Results from the mean F0 patterning analysis showed that all
participants exhibited a higher mean F0 in on-focus regions
(i.e., the Subject in IF, the Adverbial in MF, and the Object in
FF), a lower mean F0 in post-focus regions (i.e., the Adverbial
and Object in IF and the Object in MF), and largely unchanged
mean F0 in pre-focus regions (i.e., the Subject in MF and the
Subject and Adverbial in FF), relative to the corresponding con-
stituents in neutral-focus sentences. These results indicate that
prosodic focus in Jianghuai Mandarin exhibits the canonical tri-
zone F0 pattern, which is generally preserved across adulthood.
In other words, Jianghuai Mandarin can be classified as a +PFC
language, similar to Standard Mandarin.
Meanwhile, results on F0 excursion revealed a larger excur-
sion size in on-focus regions (i.e., the Subject in IF, the Adver-

bial in MF, and the Object in FF), a reduced excursion size in
post-focus regions (i.e., the Object in IF and MF), and largely
unchanged excursion size in pre-focus regions (i.e., the Subject
in MF and the Subject and Adverbial in FF), relative to the cor-
responding constituents in neutral-focus sentences. Although
the Adverbial, i.e., the first post-focus constituent following the
on-focus region, did not show a significant reduction in excur-
sion size, the overall pattern of results nevertheless indicates
that F0 excursion generally conforms to the canonical tri-zone
pattern and that this pattern is preserved across age groups.
The F0 patterning findings indicate that adult age does not
affect the tri-zone realization of prosodic focus in Jianghuai
Mandarin. This result is consistent with previous research
showing that older speakers largely preserve their ability to use
F0 cues effectively to convey prosodic information for linguis-
tic functions [26, 27]. Together, these findings suggest that F0
serves as a stable cue for marking prosodic focus across adult-
hood. In this sense, F0 patterning may reflect a phonologized
prosodic representation that is robust to age-related changes.
4.2. Altered F0 adjustment across adulthood
Comparisons of mean F0 differences across three constituents
revealed a consistent alignment across adulthood. Specifically,
mean F0 differences were consistently positive in on-focus re-
gions, negative in post-focus regions, and close to zero in pre-
focus regions. This pattern mirrors the tri-zone F0 pattern ob-
served in the patterning analysis. Moreover, no significant dif-
ferences were found between the two post-focus regions (i.e.,
the Adverbial and Object in the IF vs. NF contrast) or between
the two pre-focus regions (i.e., the Subject and Adverbial in the
FF vs. NF contrast) within the same sentence.
Similar to mean F0 differences, F0 excursion differences
showed positive values in on-focus regions, negative values in
post-focus regions, and values close to zero in pre-focus re-
gions. Within the same sentence, no significant difference in
excursion size was observed between the two pre-focus con-
stituents (i.e., the Subject and Adverbial in the FF vs. NF con-
trast). However, unlike mean F0 differences, F0 excursion dif-
ferences revealed a significantly larger excursion reduction in
the second post-focus constituent than in the first post-focus
constituent, namely, a greater difference in the Object than in
the Adverbial in the IF vs. NF contrast.
Different from F0 patterning, mean F0 difference in the
analysis of F0 adjustment reported the effect of age group. More
specifically, older adults exhibited more negative mean F0 dif-
ference in the post-focus regions than both young and middle-
aged adults, i.e., the Adverbial and Object in the IF vs. NF con-
trast as well as the Object in the MF vs. NF contrast. Apart from
this, no significant group difference was found. This indicates
that older adults compressed more than their younger counter-
parts in the post-focus regions. Age-related deviations were
observed in post-focus regions rather than in the on-focus re-
gion, which may reflect an age-related shift in focus realization
strategies, potentially associated with physiological changes in
older adults [17, 18, 19, 20]. Together with the F0 patterning
results, this suggests that although the structural organization of
focus-related F0 patterning is preserved across the adult lifes-
pan, whereas the fine-grained modulation of F0 cues, particu-
larly the mean F0 compression in the post-focus regions, may
undergo age-related changes.
The age-related shifts in F0 adjustment in our study, i.e.,
the larger PFC in older adults, represent one of the central find-
ings. This pattern is broadly consistent with prior evidence that
older speakers tend to rely more heavily on F0 contrast when
signaling linguistic prominence, such as lexical stress [27]. In
that study, older adults exhibited greater F0 differentiation be-
tween stressed and unstressed syllables than younger adults, a
pattern interpreted as reflecting age-related changes in laryngeal
physiology. A similar account may also apply to the current
findings. Specifically, although stiffening of certain laryngeal
tissues may restrict overall F0 flexibility, decreased tension in
other vocal tissues and increased laxity of the laryngeal car-
tilages may facilitate opposite performance. When combined
with reduced precision in voluntary control of laryngeal ten-
sion, these changes may lead older adults to over-compress F0
in post-focus regions, resulting in a larger PFC effect.
Importantly, the present findings indicate that this age ef-
fect is not manifested as a uniform amplification of F0 across
all focus-related regions. These suggest that PFC may play an
even more central role within the prosodic system. While on-
focus F0 enhancement and pre-focus stability were robustly pre-
served across age groups, age-related modulation emerged most
clearly in post-focus regions. This asymmetry indicates that
PFC may constitute a particularly sensitive locus of prosodic
control, where fine-grained F0 regulation is required and where
subtle changes in speech motor control are most likely to sur-
face. In this sense, PFC may not merely be a typological marker
of different languages, but also a window into the dynamics of
prosodic planning and execution [2, 4].
4.3. Limitations and future directions
This study expands the existing tri-zone F0 pattern of prosodic
focus to include older adults in the relatively unexplored lin-
guistic context of Jianghuai Mandarin. However, several unre-
solved questions warrant attention in future research endeav-
ors. Firstly, it is essential to consider gender differences as
a between-subject variable, necessitating the recruitment of a
larger participant pool due to recognized biomechanical dis-
tinctions in the vocal mechanism. Secondly, while our study
