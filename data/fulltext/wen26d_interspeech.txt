Abstract
Silent Speech Recognition (SSR) decodes linguistic intent
from non-acoustic biological signals, where Ultrasound Tongue
Imaging (UTI) serves as a pivotal non-invasive modality. How-
ever, imaging artifacts and complex temporal dependencies
make extracting robust features from UTI sequences challeng-
ing. To address these issues, we propose a framework for learn-
ing physics-aware and context-rich representations. Specifi-
cally, a temporal clustering strategy is employed to downsample
sequences while preserving critical context, complemented by
a physics-aware augmentation that enforces model robustness
against imaging perturbations. Furthermore, temporal masking
and a hybrid architecture integrating 3D convolutional networks
with bidirectional recurrent layers are utilized to promote spa-
tiotemporal representation learning. Our method significantly
outperforms baselines, validating that modeling physical con-
sistency and structural completeness is essential for advancing
robust UTI-based SSR.

1. Introduction
Silent Speech Recognition (SSR) serves as a transformative
technology that decodes linguistic intent from non-acoustic bi-
ological signals, facilitating robust communication in noise-
intensive, privacy-critical, and assistive healthcare environ-
ments [1, 2, 3]. Among various modalities, Ultrasound Tongue
Imaging (UTI) has emerged as a key enabling technology for
silent speech recognition due to its ability to non-invasively vi-
sualize the vocal tract dynamics in real time. Unlike surface
electromyography or sensors susceptible to skin-contact inter-
ference [4, 5], UTI directly captures spatiotemporal evolution
of the vocal tract configuration, offering a unique basis for de-
coding articulatory dynamics [6, 7].
Despite these merits, two fundamental bottlenecks impede
the extraction of reliable speech representations from raw UTI
sequences [8]. Specifically, 1)Acquisition-Related Physical
Variability:The ultrasound imaging process is influenced by
physical factors. Speckle noise arises from acoustic wave re-
flections, while subtle displacements occur in probe placement.
These physics-induced imaging variations cause the same utter-
ance to exhibit different visual patterns across acquisition con-
ditions. If the model fails to maintain robustness against such

physical variability, phonetically identical units may be mapped
to disparate regions in the representation space, undermining
representation stability [9, 10]. 2)Complex Temporal Depen-
dencies:The phenomenon of co-articulation dictates that artic-
ulatory movements do not occur in isolation but are governed
by continuous contextual constraints [11]. Conventional mod-
eling approaches that treat ultrasound frames as independent or
weakly temporally dependent visual units often fail to capture
long-range dynamic dependencies across frames, resulting in
ambiguous or incomplete representations that impair the dis-
crimination of fine-grained linguistic units [12, 13].
To address these challenges, this paper proposes aphysics-
awareandcontext-richrepresentation learning framework an-
chored by two complementary principles:Consistencyand
Completeness. Consistency is achieved through augmentation
strategies that simulate physical imaging variations (probe dis-
placement and speckle noise), enabling the model to maintain
robustness across varying acquisition conditions [14, 15]. Com-
pleteness is pursued via temporal clustering to downsample se-
quences while preserving articulatory context, temporal mask-
ing to promote context-rich learning, and a hybrid architecture
integrating 3D convolutional networks with bidirectional recur-
rent layers to synergistically capture local spatiotemporal dy-
namics and long-range phonetic context [16, 17, 18]. The pri-
mary contributions of this work are three-fold:
• A temporal augmentation framework integrating clustering-
based temporal downsampling and physics-inspired tempo-
ral augmentation to construct compact UTI sequences and
improve robustness to acquisition variability and context de-
pendence.
• A hybrid network architecture combining 3D CNNs and bidi-
rectional recurrent layers to capture local spatiotemporal dy-
namics and long-range phonetic dependencies from compact
and context-rich ultrasound frames.
• Extensive experiments on the UXTD corpus demonstrat-
ing consistent gains in overlap and unseen-speaker settings,
meanwhile validating the effectiveness of learning physics-
aware and context-rich representations for robust ultrasound-
based SSR.

2.1. Clustering-Based Temporal Downsampling
Raw UTI sequences are captured at a high frame rate (e.g. ap-
proximately 121.5 fps in UXTD). While such high temporal res-
olution preserves fine-grained articulatory motion, it also intro-
duces substantial temporal redundancy and increases computa-
tional cost. Moreover, excessively long input sequences may
hinder sequence modeling under limited training data [19, 20].
To obtain compact yet informative input sequences, we per-
form an offline temporal downsampling procedure. For each
utterance, the UTI frames are temporally clustered using thek-
means algorithm [21]. The original frame sequence, denoted
asX origin ={x 1,x 2,...,x T}, x t∈R H×W,is partitioned
into clusters based on temporal proximity, and one representa-
tive frame is selected from each cluster. The selected frames
are then reordered according to their original temporal order to
form a fixed-length sequence.
This downsampling strategy reduces temporal redundancy
while preserving key articulatory patterns and contextual in-
formation required for sentence-level SSR. The resulting se-
quences serve as inputs to all models in our experiments.

2.3. Spatiotemporal Context Modeling
Following temporal downsampling and physics-inspired aug-
mentation, each utterance is represented as a compact sequence
that preserves key articulatory events while reducing redun-
dancy. These sequences serve as inputs to our spatiotemporal
modeling module, which is designed to capture both local mo-
tion dynamics and long-range contextual dependencies.
Given the continuous nature of articulatory movements
and the influence of co-articulation, linguistic realizations are
shaped by surrounding phonetic context. To model such depen-
dencies, we adopt a hybrid architecture combining 3D-CNNs
with bidirectional recurrent layers. The 3D-CNNs frontend ex-
tracts local spatiotemporal features from neighboring frames,
capturing fine-grained articulatory dynamics. The bidirectional
recurrent layers then aggregate these features along the tem-
poral dimension to model global contextual information, pro-
ducing context-rich representations for end-to-end SSR training
with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) loss [18].
3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Dataset and Settings
The UXTD corpus contains UTI data from 58 typically de-
veloping children speakers with corresponding textual annota-
tions [23]. The corpus includes sentence-, word-, and phoneme-
level data. In this work, we focus on the Type-A word-level
data for evaluation. To comprehensively evaluate model perfor-
mance, two evaluation protocols are considered:
• Overlap setting: The training and test sets share the same
speakers. For each speaker, 10% of utterances are randomly
selected for testing and the remainder for training (Speaker
15M is excluded due to insufficient data).
• Unseen setting: Training and test speakers are strictly dis-
joint. 8 speakers are randomly selected for testing and the
remaining 50 are used for training.
The unseen setting corresponds to the commonly adopted
speaker-independent evaluation protocol in speech recognition
studies [24, 25], while the overlap setting provides a comple-
mentary within-speaker evaluation.
3.2. Evaluation Metrics
Word Error Rate (WER) and Character Error Rate (CER) are
used as evaluation metrics. Both metrics are calculated based on
edit distance to measure the discrepancy between the predicted
text and the ground-truth transcription. WER is computed at
the word level, whereas CER is computed at the character level.
Lower values indicate better recognition performance.
3.3. Baselines and Implementation Details
To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method,
we selected three representative spatiotemporal modeling
baselines for comparison: Spatiotemporal-GRU (ST-GRU),
Spatiotemporal-LSTM (ST-LSTM), and Spatiotemporal-
Transformer (ST-Transformer). These models were designed
following the architectures in [26, 27, 28] and re-implemented
under the same experimental settings to maintain consistency in
data processing, training strategies, and optimization protocols.
All models are implemented in PyTorch. The raw ultra-
sound sequences are first processed using the clustering-Based
temporal downsampling procedure described in Section 2.1.
The preprocessed fixed-length and temporally ordered frames
are resized to a unified spatial resolution of64×128. The Adam
optimizer is adopted with a learning rate of1×10−5 and a batch
size of 32. A dropout rate of 0.5 is applied for regularization.
All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090
GPU. Data augmentation is applied only during training, in-
cluding random spatial shifts (±5%), additive zero-mean Gaus-
sian noise (σ= 0.01), and temporal masking (p= 0.3) that
randomly zeros consecutive frames.

4.2. Ablation Study
To quantify key component contributions, we conducted abla-
tion experiments (Table 2). As temporal downsampling is a
fixed preprocessing step applied uniformly, we focus on data
augmentation and the 3D-CNNs within spatiotemporal mod-
eling. Removing the physics-inspired augmentation strategy
leads to a significant increase in error rates, indicating that the
absence of simulated acquisition variations affects the model’s
adaptability to physical imaging noise. Replacing the 3D con-
volutional frontend with 2D-CNNs also degrades performance,
suggesting that the lack of local spatiotemporal dynamics im-
pacts recognition effectiveness. For a more detailed ablation
study of the augmentation strategy, please refer to the supple-
mentary material. These performance differences suggest that
the augmentation strategy and 3D modeling may contribute
to representation consistency and completeness respectively,
which we will further investigate in the subsequent represen-
tation analysis.

5. Conclusion
This paper proposes a physics-aware and context-rich repre-
sentation learning framework for UTI-based SSR. We intro-
duce a physics-inspired augmentation strategy simulating probe
displacement and speckle noise to improve representation con-
sistency and combine clustering-based temporal downsampling
with a hybrid architecture of 3D CNNs and bidirectional recur-
rent layers for capturing local dynamics and long-range depen-
dencies to enhance completeness. Experiments show relative
WER reductions of 36.5% (overlap) and over 50.4% (unseen)
against best baselines. Ablation and representation analyses
validate that our method maintains consistency against imaging
variations while achieving a discriminative, context-rich repre-
sentation space. Future work will explore self-supervised pre-
training and large-scale data scenarios to further enhance model
generalization across diverse speakers and vocabularies.
