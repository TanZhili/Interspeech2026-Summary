Abstract

1. Introduction
Lexical tone research often treatsf0 as the primary acoustic cor-
relate, so reliable estimation of periodicity is methodologically
central. This assumption is stressed in checked tone contexts
and strongly non-modal phonation, where irregular excitation
and rapid offset dynamics can make the waveform compatible
with multiple candidate periods, or with none that is uniquely
justified. Talkin [1] identifies period selection under irregular
excitation as a core difficulty for pitch tracking, and work on
YIN similarly treats strongly irregular intervals as problematic
for defining a unique referencef 0 [2, 3]. In such regimes, ap-
parent late rhymef 0 structure may partly reflect tracker specific
resolutions of ambiguity rather than the intended tonal target.
Checked categories also motivate a broader theoretical
point: tonal contrasts can be distributed across cues. Typolog-
ically, checked systems frequently co-pattern with short dura-
tion and non-modal phonation (e.g., Burmese) [4, 5]. In Sinitic
varieties, the checked (entering,r `ush¯eng) category is widely
linked to historical oral stop codas [p, t, k] [6, 7, 8]. Stop-final
rhymes constrain timing and may be associated with short dura-
tion and laryngealisation. When historical codas weaken or are
restructured, checked tone categories may become more similar
to unchecked tones in their surface realisation [9, 10].
Huoji1 provides a focused setting for testing these issues.

2. Method
2.1. Data collection
Fieldwork recordings were obtained from 17 native speakers
(23–79 years), all born and raised in Huoji. Recordings were
made in a quiet indoor setting using a USB cardioid condenser
microphone, digitised at 24-bit and 48 kHz (W A V). Speakers2
were sampled from four Huoji localities adjacent to Mandarin-
speaking areas (Figure 1). Each speaker produced a fixed
wordlist of 70 monosyllabic citation forms (Suppl. Sec. A)
in isolation, comprising 14 lexical items in each tone cate-
gory (T1–T5). Items were repeated five times, yielding 5,950
citation-tone tokens. The wordlist was compiled from a di-
alect survey character inventory and prioritised minimal or near-
minimal tonal contrasts.

2.2.1. Segmentation of tone-bearing units
TBUs were segmented in Praat for time-aligned extraction of
f0, duration, and voice-quality measures. Following [15, 16],
the TBU was defined as the vowel nucleus plus any post-vocalic
voiced material (e.g., glide, final nasal), excluding onset conso-
nants. For Huoji monosyllables (C)(G)V(C), only the glide (if
present), main vowel, and final [N] (if present) were included;
any final [P] closure was excluded unless it contained voicing.
TBU onset was placed at the earliest post-onset periodic en-
ergy (typically after burst/frication), and TBU offset at the end
of the last voiced interval; non-periodic reverberant tails were
not included. Boundaries were cross-checked by two trained
phoneticians. TBUs were then used to compute TBU duration,

2.2.2. Challenges forf 0 tracking in checked tone glottalisation
Checked tone rhymes pose a principled difficulty for anyf 0
analysis that assumes a unique, recoverable period throughout
the vowel. Near rhyme offset, T5 tokens often show irregular
excitation, including cycle-to-cycle variability, pulse skipping,
diplophonia, and abrupt laryngeal constriction. Such irregular-
ity ranges from weakly organised subharmonic structure to fully
aperiodic vibration (Suppl. Sec. B). In the more structured
cases, excitation remains sufficiently patterned that the wave-
form can be compatible with multiple candidate periods. Under
these conditions, pitch doubling is not merely incidental track-
ing error but a predictable consequence of competing periodic
interpretations under reduced quasi-periodicity [1, 2, 3].
Letτdenote a candidate glottal period, withf 0 = 1/τ
[13]. When excitation remains weakly organised, periodicity-
based trackers may exhibit competing optima atτandτ/2;
stable locking toτ/2yields an octave-elevated estimate2f 0
even if the intended tonal target is unchanged [2, 3]. Impor-
tantly, such octave-elevated estimates should not automatically
be interpreted as a perceived pitch rise. Irregular phonation
can affect perceived voice quality and pitch strength as well as
instrumentalf 0 estimation [17, 18]. Figure 2 illustrates this
structured ambiguity in a representative token ([p h5P]‘beat’):
the auditory impression remains perceptually level, yet the ex-
tracted contour shows an apparent octave rise consistent with
locking toτ/2. This example represents one regime of irregular
phonation in which competing periodic interpretations remain
possible. In more strongly aperiodic intervals, no stable pe-
riod may be physically well defined, and tracker output reflects
algorithm-specific responses to non-periodic excitation rather
than resolution of competing periods. These considerations mo-
tivate a cross-algorithm diagnosis off 0 in checked tones. Com-
paring estimators with different operational definitions of peri-
odicity allows us to distinguish algorithm-dependent behaviour
at rhyme offsets from tonal patterns that remain stable across
methods, and to supplementf 0 with phonation measures that
remain informative when periodic organisation weakens.

2.2.3. Cross-algorithm diagnosis off 0 in checked tone
Taken together, period ambiguity at checked tone rhyme offsets
means that an apparently well-formedf 0 contour may partly
reflect how a particular algorithm resolves irregular excitation
rather than the underlying tonal target. To separate algorithm-
dependent behaviour from substantive patterns, we used a con-
trolled multi-tracker design comparing three waveform-based
(time-domain) estimator families that operationalise periodicity
differently: Praat autocorrelation (To Pitch (ac)) [13, 20], YIN
(difference-function family) [3], and REAPER (epoch-based,
i.e., glottal closure instant–based) [21, 1]. Importantly, none of
these trackers infersf 0 from a spectral-domain harmonic tem-
plate; all diagnose periodicity directly from the time-domain
signal (via correlation, difference functions, or pulse timing).
To minimise confounds from divergent defaults, all trackers
were run with the same search bounds (floor 30 Hz, ceiling
500 Hz) and with settings documented in the analysis scripts.
This follows the general principle that pitch-tracker compar-
isons should minimise differences in parameterisation across
algorithms [22]. Under these controls, between-tracker diver-
gences, especially near rhyme offset, are interpreted primarily
as differences in how each algorithm resolves period ambigu-
ity under irregular excitation. This motivates treating offset
behaviour as a diagnostic forf 0 reliability, and motivates the
inclusion of independent phonation measures alongsidef 0 in
subsequent modelling.
Praat (autocorrelation):f 0 was extracted with Praat “To
Pitch (ac)” [13, 20] using a 10 ms time step. The low floor en-
larges the effective analysis window (useful for low-f0 regimes)
but can increase ambiguity and reduce temporal specificity at
checked offsets [20]. Pilot checks on T5 tokens guided minor
adjustments to reduce systematic octave errors near rhyme off-
set.
YIN (difference function):YIN was computed follow-
ing [3]. By locating minima in a normalised difference func-
tion, YIN can reduce some subharmonic confusions, but it can
still return competing candidates when periodicity weakens; re-
maining offset divergences are therefore informative about al-
gorithmic assumptions under aperiodic excitation [2].

3. Results
3.1.f 0-only tonal space across trackers
Because checked tone offsets can yield ambiguous periodicity,
we do not treat any single pitch tracker as a gold-standard mea-
sure of “true”f 0 (Section 2.2.3). Instead, Praat (ac), YIN, and
REAPER are used as three established, waveform-based esti-
mators that implement different decision rules for resolving pe-
riod ambiguity. This design allows us to test whether the in-
ferred tonal geometry fromf 0-only representations is stable un-
der reasonable variation in the operational definition off 0.
Figure 3 shows that tracker differences concentrate near
rhyme offset, where glottalisation and reduced periodicity make
the period underdetermined. We therefore use MDS as a ro-
bustness probe. Figure 4 shows a largely consistent topology
across trackers, with the main variation involving T5: the T1–
T5 distance increases from Paat to YIN / REAPER. Crucially,
T5 remains the nearest neighbour of T1 in all threef 0-only
spaces. Thus, tracker choice modulates the degree of separation
but does not change the qualitative pattern of T1–T5 compres-
sion, motivating the multi-cue analyses below.

4. Conclusion
This study shows that checked tone rhyme offsets can be ir-
regular enough thatf 0 traces alone will be weak evidence for
tonal identity. Across three waveform-based trackers (Praat au-
tocorrelation, YIN, REAPER) with harmonised low-f0 settings,
thef 0-only tonal geometry is stable: T5 remains closest to T1,
and tracker disagreements cluster near offsets where periodic-
ity is weak. Changing the tracker affects separation magni-
tude, but not the core T1–T5 compression inf 0 space. Adding
phonation-sensitive measures expands the tonal space and sep-
arates T5 more clearly from T1. In particular, HNR05 and

1 −H ∗
2 )form an interpretable phonation-sensitive dimen-
sion that remains informative exactly wheref0 becomes fragile.
Overall, the production results favour a cue-bundle account of
checked tones in Huoji rather than anf 0-only account. A key
limitation is that the evidence is production-based: we provide
no ground-truth reference for offset regionf 0 and do not di-
rectly test how listeners integratef 0 and phonation in categori-
sation. The acoustic pattern nonetheless motivates a percep-
tion experiment: if listeners use multiple co-varying cues,f 0
alone may be insufficient for robust categorisation, especially
under offset-adjacent irregularity. Future work will therefore (i)
establish a perceptual baseline for T1–T5 identification under
compressed or ambiguousf 0, and (ii) jointly manipulatef 0 and
phonation to test whether voice-quality cues support categori-
sation when offset-adjacentf 0 becomes unreliable.
