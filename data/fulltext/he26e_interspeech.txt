Audio-DeepThinker: Progressive Reasoning-Aware Reinforcement Learning
for High-Quality Chain-of-Thought Emergence in Audio Language Models

Abstract
Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) excel at perception
but lack grounded reasoning. Existing methods rely on su-
pervised chain-of-thought (CoT) data or coarse Reinforcement
Learning (RL) rewards that do not directly evaluate reason-
ing quality, yielding chains logically ungrounded in audio.
To bridge this gap, we propose Audio-DeepThinker with two
ideas. First, a hybrid reasoning similarity reward combining
an LLM evaluator assessing logical path alignment and key
step coverage with embedding similarity enforcing semantic
alignment with reference chains. Second, a progressive two-
stage curriculum enabling CoT to emerge via pure RL explo-
ration (RL-Zero). Stage 1 uses the hybrid reward on founda-
tional audio QA, while Stage 2 shifts to boundary cases with an
LLM-only reward for reasoning diversity. Audio-DeepThinker
achieves state-of-the-art on MMAR (74.0%) and MMAU-Test-
Mini (78.5%), winning 1st Place in the Interspeech 2026 Audio
Reasoning Challenge (Single Model Track).
Index Terms: Audio Understanding, Reinforcement Learning,
Large Audio-Language Models, Chain-of-Thought Reasoning
1. Introduction
In the audio domain, Large Audio-Language Models
(LALMs) [1, 2, 3, 4] have made significant progress in audio
comprehension, yet they primarily operate as perception-and-
answer systems without explicit reasoning process. To bridge
the gap between perception and reasoning, prior works have
explored two paradigms. Supervised approaches such as the
Audio Flamingo [5, 6] construct curated Chain-of-Thought
(CoT) demonstrations and train models to imitate them. While
effective, these methods are fundamentally limited by the
quality and diversity of human-authored reasoning, and cannot
discover novel reasoning strategies beyond the training data.
Reinforcement Learning (RL) based approaches offer a more
flexible alternative. Early methods such as R1-AQA [7], Omni-
R1 [8], and AudioMCQ [9] apply GRPO [10] with accuracy
and format rewards, demonstrating that RL can improve audio
QA performance. More recent works have begun to incorporate
reasoning-related signals. For example, Audio-Thinker [11]
introduces adaptive rewards to guide when the model should
reason, and CESAR [12] introduces a comprehensive suite to
reward structured patterns and causal logic.
Despite these advances, two fundamental challenges re-
main:(i) how to ensure that the generated reasoning chains
are genuinely grounded in the audio content, rather than be-
ing formally well-formatted yet semantically decoupled from

the actual acoustic evidence; and(ii) how to fundamentally im-
prove the quality of the reasoning process, ensuring that gener-
ated thinking chains are logically coherent, well-structured, and
free from misleading or spurious inference steps. While these
recent methods, such as Audio-Thinker [11] and CESAR [12],
have made initial strides toward reasoning supervision, they do
not assess the content of the reasoning chain at a fine-grained
level: whether the intermediate steps are grounded in specific
acoustic evidence and whether the logical path is analytically
sound. This limitation arises because the reasoning chains are
generated solely by the model itself, without any ground-truth
reference chains to serve as a basis for evaluating their quality.
To this end, we propose Audio-DeepThinker, a framework
that addresses both challenges through two complementary in-
novations.First, we develop a hybrid reasoning similarity re-
ward that provides direct, fine-grained supervision over both
the audio grounding and logical quality of generated reason-
ing. This reward combines an LLM evaluator assessing logical
path alignment, key step coverage, and analytical depth, with
embedding similarity enforcing semantic alignment against ref-
erence reasoning chains. Since the reference chains are derived
from detailed audio captions, aligning with them inherently en-
courages the model to ground its reasoning in acoustic content.
Critically, this reward is applied only when the answer is cor-
rect, shifting the objective from “finding any path to the answer”
to “constructing a logically rigorous and faithfully grounded
reasoning chain”.Second, to fully leverage this reward sig-
nal, we propose a progressive two-stage RL curriculum that en-
ables high-quality CoT to emerge from scratch via exploration.
Stage 1 fosters basic reasoning patterns on foundational audio
QA with comprehensive reward supervision, while Stage 2 re-
fines these on acoustically challenging boundary cases with a
streamlined reward encouraging diverse reasoning strategies.
The emergence of reasoning suggests that the bottleneck in au-
dio reasoning lies not in model architecture, but in the lack of
reward signals providing fine-grained reasoning supervision.
Our main contributions are summarized as:
• We propose a hybrid reasoning similarity reward that directly
supervises the quality of generated reasoning chains by com-
bining LLM-based logical evaluation with embedding-based
semantic alignment, jointly addressing the dual challenges of
reasoning soundness and audio grounding that existing RL
methods leave unresolved.
• We propose a progressive two-stage RL curriculum that en-
ables high-quality CoT reasoning to emerge from scratch
without supervised reasoning demonstrations. Stage 1 estab-
lishes foundational reasoning patterns, while Stage 2 refines
performance on challenging boundary cases.
• The proposed Audio-DeepThinker achieves state-of-the-art

2. Method
2.1. Overview
As illustrated in Fig 1, our framework consists of three
components: (1) a data construction pipeline that generates
high-quality CoT annotations without human labeling, (2) a
reasoning-aware multi-reward design centered on a hybrid sim-
ilarity reward providing fine-grained supervision on both an-
swer correctness and reasoning quality, and (3) a progressive
two-stage training paradigm that first establishes foundational
reasoning patterns, then refines them on challenging cases.
2.2. Data Construction Pipeline
A practical challenge in training audio reasoning models is the
scarcity of high-quality chain-of-thought annotations. Rather
than relying on expensive human labeling, we construct train-
ing data through an automated three-step pipeline. Each train-
ing sample ultimately consists of a questionq, a ground-truth
answera ∗, and a reference reasoning chainr ∗.
Step 1: Audio Captioning. Given an audio inputx, we use
Qwen3-Omni-Captioner [13]f cap to generate a detailed textual
descriptionc=f cap(x).
Step 2: Question-Answer Generation. For datasets that
lack question-answer annotations, we employ Qwen3-235B-
A22B-Instruct-2507 [14] to generate(q,a ∗) =f qa(c)from the
caption; for datasets that already provide question-answer pairs,
we directly adopt the original(q,a ∗).
Step 3: Chain-of-Thought Generation. Based on the ob-
tained question-answer pair(q,a ∗)and the captionc, we use
DeepSeek V3.1 [15] to generate a reference reasoning chain:
r∗ =f cot(c,q,a ∗). The detailed prompt template is provided
in Supplementary Material A.1.
Through this pipeline, we obtain two stage-specific train-
ing sets.D 1 consists of 39,412 samples from A VQA [16], pro-
viding well-structured queries for initial reasoning acquisition.
D2 comprises 29,483 samples drawn from AudioMCQ [9],
AudioSet [17], MusicBench [18], IEMOCAP [19], covering
diverse audio tasks to strengthen reasoning generalization on
challenging boundary cases.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
Model.We adopt Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct [13] as our
base model, which employs a mixture-of-experts (MoE) archi-
tecture with 30B total parameters and 3B active parameters per
token. The model lacks inherent CoT reasoning capability prior
to our Reinforcement Learning training.
Training Details.Training is conducted using the SWIFT [31]
framework integrated with Megatron-LM [32] on 8 nodes,
where one node is dedicated to asynchronous rollout genera-
tion. The global batch size is 224 with a micro batch size of 4.
We use a learning rate of 1e-6, a KL coefficientβ= 0.001, and
sampleG= 8rollout responses per GDPO optimization step.
Training Data.Stage 1 uses 39,412 samples from A VQA [16]
for initial reasoning acquisition. Stage 2 uses 29,483 samples
combining diverse open-source datasets targeting acoustically
challenging boundary cases.
Evaluation Benchmarks.We evaluate on two benchmarks:
•MMAR[33]: A challenging benchmark for deep audio rea-
soning, encompassing single-modality and mixed-modality
tasks requiring cross-modal integration. We report accuracy
and the Rubrics score from the Interspeech 2026 Audio Rea-
soning Challenge [34], which evaluates the logic and com-
pleteness of the reasoning chain at the instance level.
•MMAU[35]: A massive multi-task audio understanding
benchmark covering sound, music, and speech, with both
test-mini and full test sets. We report average accuracy across
the three domains for evaluation.
3.2. Main Results
Table 1 presents results on both benchmarks. OnMMAR,
Audio-DeepThinker achieves 74.0% average accuracy, estab-
lishing a new state-of-the-art among all open-source methods
and surpassing several closed-source models. Compared to
the base model Qwen3-Omni-Instruct (70.10%), our method
yields a +3.9% absolute improvement. The most substan-
tial gains appear in Music (+6.80%) and mixed-modality tasks
(e.g., +8.54% on Music-Speech), suggesting that explicit CoT
reasoning is especially effective for tasks requiring multi-
faceted acoustic analysis and cross-modal integration. Audio-
DeepThinker also substantially outperforms all existing RL-
based methods (Audio-Thinker: 65.30%, AudioMCQ: 67.10%,
CESAR: 62.70%), validating the joint effectiveness of the pro-
gressive training paradigm in eliciting high-quality reasoning.
OnMMAU, our method achieves 78.50% on test-mini,
ranking first among all models, and 75.44% on test, competi-
tive with AudioMCQ (75.60%). Detailed results on the MMAU
benchmark are presented in Supplementary Material Table 2.
Notably, we attain the best Speech performance on both splits
(80.78% / 77.87%), demonstrating strong reasoning on speech
understanding tasks where intermediate CoT steps help system-
atically integrate diverse speech-related evidence. On test-mini,
we surpass Omni-R1 by +1.5% and Audio-Thinker by +0.5%.
3.3. Ablation Study
We conduct ablation studies on MMAR to validate the two core
design choices: the hybrid reasoning similarity reward, and the
progressive two-stage training paradigm. In addition to accu-
racy, we report the Rubrics score from the Interspeech 2026
Audio Reasoning Challenge [34], an instance-level metric that
evaluates the logic and completeness of the reasoning chain.

Effect of Hybrid Reasoning Similarity Reward.Table 2 iso-
lates the contribution of each reward component during Stage 1.
Training with only the base reward (R acc +R fmt) yields a
marginal +0.4% accuracy gain over the vanilla baseline with
limited reasoning quality (Rubrics: 49.17%), confirming that
sparse correctness signals alone are insufficient to elicit mean-
ingful reasoning. Adding Audio-Thinker’s rewards (R con +
Rthink) [11] improves accuracy to 71.70%, but Rubrics remains
limited at 57.44%, indicating that while the model learns to gen-
erate reasoning chains, the quality remains suboptimal. Replac-
ingR think with our hybrid similarity rewardR hybrid
sim yields sub-
stantial gains in both accuracy (+1.4%, 71.70%→73.10%) and
reasoning quality (+6.89%, 57.44%→64.33%), demonstrating
that directly supervising reasoning content is far more effective
than merely encouraging longer or more structured thinking.
Effect of Progressive Two-Stage Training.Table 3 validates
the progressive curriculum across two base models. For both
models, the full progressive pipeline (Stage 1→Stage 2) con-
sistently achieves the best results on both accuracy and Rubrics,
confirming that the two stages play complementary roles. For
Qwen3-Omni-Instruct, Stage 1 alone achieves strong reasoning
quality (Rubrics: 64.33%), confirming that RL-Zero success-
fully elicits foundational CoT from scratch. Stage 2 alone yields
comparable accuracy (73.40%) but lower Rubrics (62.53%),
suggesting that training directly on boundary cases without
foundational reasoning produces superficially correct but poorly
grounded chains. The same pattern holds for Qwen3-Omni-
Thinking, where Stage 2 alone even degrades accuracy be-
low the baseline (68.70% vs. 68.10%), further confirming that
boundary-case training is ineffective without prior reasoning
foundations. Across both models, these results validate that
the model must first internalize basic reasoning patterns through
Stage 1 before Stage 2 can effectively refine its capabilities.
4. Conclusion
We present Audio-DeepThinker, a progressive two-stage RL
framework that enables high-quality CoT reasoning to emerge
from scratch in LALMs. Our hybrid reasoning similarity re-
ward directly supervises reasoning quality by combining LLM-
based logical evaluation with embedding-based semantic align-
ment, while the progressive curriculum first establishes foun-
dational reasoning patterns via RL-Zero, then refines them on
acoustically challenging boundary cases. Audio-DeepThinker
achieves state-of-the-art results on MMAR and MMAU bench-
marks, winning 1st Place in the Interspeech 2026 Audio Rea-
soning Challenge, demonstrating that progressive reasoning-
aware reinforcement learning is sufficient to unlock high-
quality chain-of-thought emergence in audio language models
without supervised fine-tuning.
