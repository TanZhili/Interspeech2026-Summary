Seed-Enh: Generative Speech Enhancement in Decoupled Semantic and
Timbre Spaces
Zengqiang Shang1, Biao Liu 1,2, Yu Zhao1,2, Pengyuan Zhang1,2
1 Laboratory of Speech and Intelligent Information Processing, Institute of Acoustics, CAS, China
2 University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing, China

Abstract
Most existing speech enhancement operate directly in the
acoustic space, where noise and speech components are in-
herently entangled, leading to artifacts such as high-frequency
attenuation and background holes. In this paper, we pro-
pose Seed-Enh, a generative speech enhancement framework
that performs enhancement in decoupled semantic and tim-
bre spaces. Our framework processes noisy speech in three
stages: (1) semantic space processing using a frozen Whisper
encoder to extract noise-robust semantic representations, (2)
timbre space processing using CAM++ embeddings and con-
text learning, and (3) space fusion via flow matching. Exper-
iments demonstrate that Seed-Enh achieves the highest over-
all quality scores compared to state-of-the-art baselines, effec-
tively suppressing noise while restoring harmonic structures and
high-frequency details. Beyond speech enhancement, Seed-Enh
also enables zero-shot voice conversion, significantly outper-
forming Seed-VC when handling noisy inputs. The demo and
pretrained model are available athttps://github.com/
shangqwe123/Seed-Enh.
Index Terms: speech enhancement, speech disentanglement,
context learning, zero-shot voice conversion
1. Introduction
Speech enhancement refers to the task of recovering clean
speech from noisy speech signals, which has wide applica-
tions in remote communication and robust speech recognition.
With the advancement of deep learning technologies, signifi-
cant progress has been made in this field. Deep learning-based
speech enhancement methods can be primarily categorized into
discriminative methods and generative methods. Discriminative
methods learn the boundary features between clean and noisy
speech by directly predicting the clean speech components from
the noisy speech [1, 2]. In contrast, generative methods focus
on modeling the underlying distribution of clean speech under
noisy conditions, demonstrating greater robustness when faced
with unseen noise scenarios. Among generative approaches,
diffusion-based models [3, 4, 5], Schr ¨odinger bridge [6], and
flow matching [7, 8] have shown particularly promising results.
However, most existing speech enhancement methods,
whether discriminative or generative, operate directly in the
acoustic space (e.g., STFT spectrograms, Mel spectrograms,
time-domain signals), where noise and speech components are
inherently entangled. Models tend to learn shortcut solutions
based on acoustic domain, rather than truly understanding the
semantic content and timbre characteristics of speech. This fun-
damental lack of understanding manifests in two critical ways.
the lack of semantic understanding makes it difficult to distin-
guish between speech and noise when they share similar acous-
tic characteristics, leading to either over-suppression of speech
components or under-suppression of noise. the lack of timbre
understanding causes artifacts such as high-frequency attenu-
ation and background holes, resulting in speaker identity loss
and timbre distortion. Early decoupling methods in denoising,
such as amplitude-phase compensation [9] and subband decom-
position [10], still operate within the acoustic space and cannot
effectively separate semantic content from timbre information.
In contrast, speech synthesis and voice conversion methods
have demonstrated the effectiveness of processing in decoupled
semantic and timbre spaces. Models like SeedTTS [11], Seed-
VC [12], and CosyV oice [13] employ a three-stage pipeline:
(1) semantic representation extraction using pre-trained models
like Whisper [14] or Hubert [15], (2) timbre-conditioned acous-
tic feature generation using diffusion models, and (3) wave-
form synthesis via vocoders. These methods operate in decou-
pled spaces, where semantic and timbre information are pro-
cessed separately, enabling better control over different speech
attributes. However, their application to speech enhancement
has been limited. AnyEnhance [16] attempts to apply decou-
pled generation to denoising, but still processes semantic infor-
mation in a way that is not fully decoupled from timbre space.
This paper proposes a novel approach that extends the de-
coupled space processing paradigm to speech enhancement.
We perform enhancement in decoupled semantic and timbre
spaces rather than the original acoustic space [17]. By leverag-
ing the noise robustness of pre-trained semantic encoders (e.g.,
Whisper) to extract clean semantic representations from noisy
speech, and separately processing timbre information in its own
space, we can achieve superior enhancement quality compared
to methods operating directly in the acoustic space. This ap-
proach enables both high-quality denoising and zero-shot voice
conversion capabilities.
2. Method
2.1. Motivation: Enhancement in Decoupled Spaces
To address the fundamental limitations of existing methods op-
erating in the acoustic space (as discussed in Section 1), we
propose performing enhancement in decoupled semantic and
timbre spaces. The key motivation for this design is twofold.
First, semantic information can be extracted in a noise-robust
manner using pre-trained models like Whisper, which have been
trained on large-scale diverse audio data and inherently learn to
preserve linguistic content even in noisy conditions. This al-
lows us to leverage the encoder’s built-in noise robustness to
obtain relatively clean semantic representations directly from
noisy speech. Second, by processing timbre information sep-
arately in its own space, we can apply specialized techniques
(e.g., context learning and speaker embeddings) to extract and

2.2. Semantic Space Processing
The semantic space processing stage leverages the noise robust-
ness of pre-trained semantic encoders to extract clean semantic
representations from noisy speech. To ensure proper decoupling
between semantic and timbre information, we split the input
audio: the second half (after applying a low-complexity timbre
perturbation module, which is employed during training) is used
for semantic extraction, while the first half is reserved for tim-
bre extraction. This audio splitting strategy prevents informa-
tion leakage by ensuring that semantic and timbre information
are extracted from different portions of the audio, while both
are derived from the same noisy input.
Given the second half of the noisy speechy, we first con-
vert it to 80-dimensional Mel spectrograms and feed them into
a frozen Whisper-Large-v2 encoder [14]. We extract features
from the last transformer layer of the Whisper encoder, result-
ing in semantic representationsc semantic∈R Ts×1024, where
Ts is the temporal length (typically 4×downsampled from the
original audio). The key advantage of processing in the seman-
tic space is that the Whisper encoder, pre-trained on large-scale
internet data containing diverse noise conditions, has implic-
itly learned to filter out noise while preserving semantic con-
tent. Therefore, the semantic representationsc semantic ex-
tracted from noisy speech are already relatively clean, effec-
tively achieving ”denoising” in the semantic space through the
encoder’s inherent noise robustness.
2.3. Timbre Space Processing
Unlike semantic information, timbre information is more sus-
ceptible to noise and cannot rely solely on pre-trained models’
robustness. Therefore, we employ specialized techniques to ex-
tract and process timbre information from noisy speech in the
timbre space. As mentioned in Section 2.2, we use the first half
of the input audioy ctx for timbre extraction to ensure proper
decoupling from semantic information.
Global Timbre Embedding:We use a pre-trained
CAM++ model [18] to extract a 192-dimensional global speaker
embeddings ctx

pact representation of speaker identity that is relatively robust
to noise due to CAM++’s training on diverse speaker verifica-
tion tasks.
Local Timbre Features via Context Learning:Since the
global embedding alone may not capture all timbre details, es-
pecially in noisy conditions, we also employ context learning to
extract local timbre features. Specifically, we concatenate the
Mel spectrogram ofy ctx and corresponding semantic embed-
dingc ctx

timbre information implicitly through the context.
The final timbre representation combines the global embed-
ding with the local Mel spectrogram features, providing both
global speaker characteristics and local timbre details.

2.6. Training and Inference
Training Procedure:We adopt a two-stage training strategy.
In the first stage, we train the DiT model using clean audio
pairs to establish the voice conversion capability. Specifically,
clean speech is provided as input, with timbre-related features
extracted from the first half and semantic pronunciation repre-
sentations extracted from the second half; the objective is to re-
construct the clean speech signal. This stage enables the model
to learn the mapping from semantic and timbre conditions to
clean speech. In the second stage, we fine-tune the model on
noisy-clean pairs to enable timbre extraction from noisy speech.
The training data consists of noisy speechy noisy (created by
mixing clean speech with noise at SNR ranging from -5 to 15
dB) as input and corresponding clean speechxas target. The
loss function is the flow matching lossL FM defined in Equa-
tion (1).
Inference Procedure:During inference, given noisy
speechynoisy, we extract semantic featurescsemantic from the
second half ofy noisy and timbre features (s ctx
global,y ctx, and
cctx
semantic) from the first half. The DiT model then generates
clean Mel spectrograms by solving the ODE using an Euler
solver with 25 steps. If clean reference audio is available, we
can use it to extract timbre features for better quality. For voice
conversion tasks, we extract semantic features from the source
speaker and timbre features from the target speaker.
3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets & Evaluation Metrics
Training setsTo comprehensively evaluate the performance
of Seed-Enh, we utilize the Emilia dataset [21] as clean au-
dio, which contains 101k hours of speech data covering mul-
tiple languages, including Chinese, English, French, German,
Japanese, and Korean. For the noise types, we employ the noise
library provided by the DNS Challenge, which contains over
60,000 samples, amounting to 181 hours. The signal-to-noise
ratio (SNR) range is configured to vary between -5 and 15 deci-
bels. The model training and inference follow the guidelines at
https://github.com/Plachtaa/seed-vc.
Test Sets and MetricsTo evaluate the denoising perfor-
mance, we employ the DNS Challenge blind test set and the
simulated LibriTTS+wham dataset. Since our proposed genera-
tive model focuses on perceptual quality rather than signal-level
fidelity, we primarily use DNSMOS [22], a neural network-
based speech quality estimator that effectively captures hu-
man auditory perception. DNSMOS provides three sub-scores:
OVRL (overall quality), SIG (signal quality), and BAK (back-
ground noise suppression), all on a scale of 1-5. We also re-
port SIM (speaker similarity) using Resemblyzer 1 to calculate
cosine similarity between speaker embeddings, and CER (char-
acter error rate) using HuBERT-Large [15] for ASR to evaluate
intelligibility. All audio is resampled to 16,000 Hz for evalua-
tion.

3.3. Experimental Results
Overall Performance Comparison:The results presented
in Table 1 highlight the performance of various speech en-
hancement methods on the DNS blindtest set and the Lib-
riTTS+wham dataset. Notably, Seed-Enh outperforms other
methods in both datasets, achieving the highest overall scores
(OVRL) of 3.095 and 3.193, respectively. This indicates that
Seed-Enh can achieve better speech quality, even though we
trained on internet data Emilia. In terms of similarity, we
achieved better timbre similarity, slightly lower than StoRM,
which may be due to the use of a higher number of iterations.
In terms of character error rate, we achieved a decent charac-
ter error rate, although it is higher than that of discriminative
models. Our model outperforms semantic-based models like
AnyEnhance and FlowMatching models FlowSE, and we used
fixed parameters for the Whisper encoder without fine-tuning it
for the denoising task, which could yield more benefits. It is
worth noting that while SB achieves slightly higher BAK, this
comes at the cost of more severe spectral holes and reduced
SIG. In contrast, our proposed model better preserves spectral
details.
Spectrogram Analysis:The comparative analysis of the
spectrograms in Fig 2 illustrates that while FullSubNet and TF-
GridNet leave noticeable residual noise during non-speech in-
tervals, generative models such as StoRM and SGMSE exhibit
limited capability in reconstructing fine details above 6 kHz,
showing evident high-frequency suppression. In contrast, SB
achieves higher background cleanliness but suffers from se-
vere over-suppression and the most pronounced attenuation of
high-frequency components. Furthermore, AnyEnhance tends
to discard critical speech segments and FlowSE introduces dis-
tinct spectral holes within harmonic regions, whereas Seed-Enh
demonstrates superior performance by effectively suppressing
noise while accurately restoring delicate harmonic structures
and semantic features, yielding a spectral distribution that most
closely aligns with the Clean reference signal.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose Seed-Enh, a generative speech en-
hancement framework that performs enhancement in decoupled
semantic and timbre spaces rather than the original acoustic
space. The key innovation lies in leveraging the noise robust-
ness of pre-trained Whisper encoders to extract clean semantic
representations, while separately processing timbre information
using CAM++ embeddings and context learning. This decou-
pled processing enables independent optimization of semantic
and timbre restoration, avoiding the mutual interference that oc-
curs in the entangled acoustic space. Experimental results on
the DNS blindtest and LibriTTS+wham datasets demonstrate
its ability to suppress noise while accurately restoring high-
frequency details. Beyond speech enhancement, Seed-Enh also
enables zero-shot voice conversion, significantly outperforming
Seed-VC when handling noisy inputs.
