Abstract
Speech anti-spoofing systems often degrade in out-of-domain
(OOD) settings due to varied unknown spoofing attacks. Do-
main generalization (DG) methods address this by improving
model robustness across diverse domains. Style augmenta-
tion is a DG approach that synthesizes new features by mod-
eling domain-shift uncertainty using feature statistics learned
during training. However, previous style augmentation meth-
ods rely on a single batch-level uncertainty by assuming a uni-
modal style distribution, which may not hold in mixed domain
batches. To address this issue, we propose Gaussian mixture
model (GMM) based aleatoric style uncertainty (ASU) to model
within-component variability. In addition, we proposed an on-
line EM-like update for the GMM in an end-to-end way. Exten-
sive experiments on anti-spoofing and spoofing-aware speaker
verification (SASV) show that ASU significantly outperforms
existing methods and surpasses state-of-the-art systems. Code
is available at GitHub.1
Index Terms: Anti-spoofing; domain generalization; out-of-
domain distributions; aleatoric uncertainty
1. Introduction
Domain generalization is essential for deploying speech anti-
spoofing systems in security critical applications. Recent stud-
ies have explored domain generalization methods for this task,
including Bayesian learning and data augmentation. For ex-
ample, Bayesian learning was used to weight the frequency
components in relaxed instance frequency-wise normalization
to address domain variation [1]. Optimized copy-paste and
mixed copy-paste augmentation methods were introduced to
align the training data with unseen domains [2]. An intra-speech
augmentation technique with meta-learning was proposed to
improve performance across multiple domains [3]. A non-
semantic audio representation was extracted to augment data
for generalizable spoofing detection [4]. A multi-stage training
framework with an ensemble of different systems was proposed
to enhance domain generalization in self-supervised learning
models [5].
Recently, style augmentation methods such as MixStyle
[6], DSU [7], and CSU [8] have gained significant attention
for improving domain generalization. These methods leverage
instance-specific feature statistics (e.g., means and standard de-
viations) that encode style attributes, enabling the generation
of diverse yet semantically consistent images that generalize
to varying domains. The feature statistics are required dur-
ing training only, and they are lightweight and do not require
extra labels. However, the feature statistics can only capture

batch-level uncertainty from limited batch samples. This is not
enough for the anti-spoofing task across multiple domains. A
training batch often spans multiple domains, including different
speakers, codecs, background noise, and spoof artifacts. Do-
main diversity induces aleatoric uncertainty characterized by
multimodal distributions, for which a single Gaussian approx-
imation is inadequate; thereby, the unimodal assumption will
limit class separability.

To address the above problems, we propose GMM-based
aleatoric style augmentation (ASU) as the within-cluster vari-
ability by modeling each sample’s style vector as a random vari-
able and using aK-component diagonal-covariance GMM in
the style space. Each GMM component is designed to capture
a distinct style mode, and we propose an EM-like online up-
date mechanism for the mixture weights, means, and diagonal
variances, which improves estimation stability with low com-
putational cost. Overall, our method accounts for two types
of variability. Batch-level variability comes from mini-batch
statistic dispersion and can approximate across-domain shifts
under domain-mixed batches, while a GMM over style vectors
models within-component variability, which we call aleatoric
style uncertainty because it reflects intrinsic within-mode vari-
ability of the observed style statistics. During training, we apply
AUS with probabilityp, and we disable it during inference so
that it adds no extra cost. Experiments on speech anti-spoofing
and SASV show that our method outperforms SOTA methods
significantly.

3. Aleatoric Style Uncertainty (ASU) with
Online GMM
DSU is limited by an implicit unimodal assumption, because it
characterizes style variation using a single batch-level variance
derived from feature statistics. However, multi-domain train-
ing data can be multi-modal in the style space, such as different
TTS and voice conversion algorithms. A single-Gaussian un-
certainty estimate is often too coarse to capture the variances
across multiple domains. We introduce ASU, which uses an
online GMM in the style space to estimate aleatoric style uncer-
tainty in a data-driven manner.

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Evaluation Tasks
Experiments were conducted on speech anti-spoofing and
SASV tasks.
4.1.1. Speech Anti-spoofing
In the open condition of ASVspoof5 Track 1 [12], we followed
the official protocol by training on the provided training and de-
velopment data and evaluating on the evaluation set (see Table
2 in [12]).
4.1.2. Spoof-aware Speaker Verification
In the ASVspoof 5 Track 2 open condition [12], SASV systems
are evaluated under a simulated telephony setting where syn-
thetic or converted speech is injected into the telephone chan-
nel, and participants can submit either a single system or a fu-
sion of automatic speaker verification (ASV) and countermea-
sure (CM) subsystems.
4.2. Implementation Details
WavLM-Base was used as the pre-trained upstream model
(Fig. 2) with about 94M parameters. The model was trained
for 10 epochs using AAM-softmax [13] (margin 0.2, scale 30)
with an initial learning rate of1×10 −4 decayed by 5% for
each epoch. MUSAN noise [14], RIR [15], RawBoost [16],
Audiomentations,3 and codecs [1] were used for data augmen-
tation. The GMM was initialized from the first mini-batch by
randomly splitting it intoKgroups and computing component
statistics using Eq. 11. We setp= 0.5,K= 7, andλ= 0.9.
For SASV , the ASV subsystem used ResNet221 following [17].
4.3. Performance Metrics
For the anti-spoofing task, we report EER and minDCF to evalu-
ate the countermeasure systems. For the SASV task, min a-DCF
was used as the primary metric, with min t-DCF and t-EER as
supplementary metrics [12].

Table 1 compares our method with SOTA systems. Score-fusion
approaches often perform better but increase complexity and in-

improves the WavLM+MHFA baseline on Dev and Eval, while

size. This suggests that modeling both batch-level variabil-
ity and aleatoric uncertainties improves domain generalization
without system fusion strategy.

6. Conclusions and Future Work
We proposed ASU for anti-spoofing and SASV , using an online
EM-like GMM to model batch variability and aleatoric uncer-
tainty. ASU significantly outperforms single-system baselines.
Future work will improve mixture stability, initialization, scal-
ing, and evaluation across datasets and attack conditions.
