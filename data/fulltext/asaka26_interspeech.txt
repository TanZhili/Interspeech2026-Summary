Two-Level Uncertainty Suppression for Robust Meeting Diarization

Naoaki Sumida2, Kazuhiro Nakadai1

Abstract
Real-meeting diarization is challenged by uncertainty propaga-
tion, where boundary ambiguity from rapid speaker turns and
overlap destabilizes downstream speaker assignment. We pro-
pose a two-level uncertainty suppression framework. For seg-
mentation, linguistically contextualized representations from
an OWSM-Encoder are integrated into WavLM via Feature-
wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) reducing boundary ambiguity
in high-activity regions. For clustering, Known-Seed Guided
Clustering (KSGC) leverages known-speaker embeddings to
stabilize global assignment under mixed known/unknown-
speaker conditions. On AMI, AliMeeting, and AISHELL-4,
FiLM reduces DER by up to 1.8 points over WavLM-large, with
largest gains in boundary-ambiguous scenarios. KSGC further
improves DER by up to 2.62 points under mixed conditions,
especially in meetings with frequent speaker turns. These re-
sults suggest that addressing uncertainty-related errors at com-
plementary levels improves robustness in meeting diarization.
Index Terms: speaker diarization, segmentation, clustering,
uncertainty modeling, mixed known/unknown speakers
1. Introduction
Speaker diarization [1] aims to determinewho spoke whenin
audio recordings and is essential for meeting analysis [2] and
speaker-attributed ASR [3, 4, 5]. Despite substantial progress,
real-meeting diarization remains challenging due to uncertainty
propagation across processing stages. In this paper, we de-
fine uncertainty as instability in speaker activity estimation at
boundaries and inconsistency in global speaker assignment re-
sulting from ambiguous segmentation. Frequent speaker turns
and overlapping speech introduce boundary-level uncertainty
in speaker activity estimation. This local ambiguity propa-
gates to clustering, resulting in global speaker assignment in-
stability. Although End-to-End Neural Diarization (EEND) [6]
and its extension EEND-VC [7, 8] improve overlap model-
ing, boundary ambiguity in real meetings still propagates to
downstream clustering. Boundary errors often produce in-
consistent speaker embeddings, leading to amplified clustering
confusion. Existing approaches enhance acoustic representa-
tions via speech separation models [9] or self-supervised mod-
els [10], but they do not explicitly model boundary-level un-
certainty. Moreover, practical meetings often involve mixed
known/unknown speaker conditions, where partial enrollment
data is available. Most frameworks assume fully unknown
or fully registered speakers (e.g., TS-V AD [11], TS-ASR-
V AD [12] and SpeakerLM [13]), leaving the use of partial
speaker priors underexplored. In this work, we propose a two-

replication-based constraints within agglomerative hierarchical

both local and global levels consistently improves DER, par-

transitions. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We introduce a segmentation strategy that suppresses
boundary-level uncertainty by injecting linguistically contex-
tualized representations into acoustically aligned intermedi-

• We propose KSGC, which suppresses global assignment un-
certainty by incorporating partially available known-speaker
embeddings as replication-based constraints.
• We demonstrate that the proposed two-level framework
yields consistent DER improvements across multiple
real-meeting corpora under frequent overlap and mixed
known/unknown speaker conditions.
2. Proposed Methods
To suppress uncertainty propagation in real meeting diariza-
tion, we address two challenges: (1) local boundary ambigu-
ity from overlapping speech and rapid speaker turns, and (2)
global speaker assignment instability. In this work, uncertainty
suppression refers to improving the stability of boundary-level
speaker activity estimation and reducing global speaker assign-
ment inconsistency. Building upon the DiariZen-based EEND-
VC framework [10, 20, 21], we introduce a two-level strategy
(Fig. 1). At the segmentation stage, an ASR-derived OWSM-
Encoder is integrated into WavLM via FiLM to reduce bound-
ary ambiguity and stabilize boundary-level predictions. At the
clustering stage, KSGC uses partially available known-speaker
embeddings as replication-based constraints to stabilize speaker
assignment and mitigate global uncertainty. Together, FiLM
and KSGC address different stages of the EEND-VC error-
propagation path, from local prediction instability to global as-
signment inconsistency.
2.1. Segmentation with Integrated Encoders
Overlapping speech and rapid speaker turns make bound-
ary detection difficult when relying solely on acoustic cues.

2.2. Known-Seed Guided Clustering
To leverage mixed known/unknown speaker scenarios in real
meetings, we propose KSGC, which incorporates prior known-
speaker information into agglomerative hierarchical cluster-
ing (AHC). From a clustering perspective, enrollment audio
can provide prior constraints that reduce assignment ambigu-
ity when embeddings are uncertain. KSGC reflects these con-
straints through a data-level weighting mechanism rather than
algorithm-level modification. Single-speaker enrollment audio
is segmented and embedded using the same embedding model
as diarization to ensure a shared feature space. For each known
speaker, embeddings are replicated using a predefined replica-
tion factor. Since AHC computes cluster centroids as averages,
replication increases the relative weight of known-speaker em-
beddings, thereby reducing variance in centroid estimation un-
der ambiguous conditions. Thus, KSGC can be interpreted as
a form of weighted constraint-based clustering. Estimated em-
beddings and replicated known-speaker embeddings are jointly
clustered by AHC. Replicated embeddings are merged at early
stages, forming weighted seeds that guide cluster formation
and stabilize global speaker assignment. By operating at the
data level, KSGC preserves the original AHC algorithm and its
stable clustering behavior. After clustering, clusters contain-
ing known-speaker embeddings are labeled by majority voting,
while the remaining clusters are treated as unknown speakers.
3. Experiments
We validate the proposed two-level framework on real meeting
corpora by analyzing (1) segmentation with OWSM integration
and (2) clustering with KSGC under mixed known/unknown
speaker conditions.

3.1. Dataset
We use three real-meeting corpora: AMI [17, 28], AliMeet-
ing [18], and AISHELL-4 [19]. AliMeeting contains frequent
speaker turns, while AISHELL-4 involves more speakers with
relatively cleaner acoustics. We follow the standard DiariZen
setup [21] for preprocessing. These original evaluation sets are
used to evaluate segmentation performance. To simulate mixed
known/unknown conditions, we construct modified test sets:
For each recording, 20 s of non-overlapping speech from three
speakers are used as enrollment data, and the remainder for
testing. Recordings not satisfying these criteria are excluded.
Statistics of the datasets are summarized in Table 1.
3.2. Model configuration and metrics
For segmentation, we use OWSM-Encoder, WavLM-base+, and
WavLM-large, integrating their hidden representations via a
learnable weighted linear combination and feeding them into a
Conformer backend following the DiariZen [21] configuration.
The OWSM-Encoder is implemented in ESPnet [29] and fine-
tuned on EEND-EDA [30] with Libri2Mix [31] (30 epochs)
and Libri3Mix (10 epochs) using AdamW (learning rate 4e−4,
batch size 20). During segmentation training, WavLM, OWSM,
and other modules are jointly optimized without freezing us-
ing AdamW with differential learning rates (2e −5/1e−5/3e−4
for WavLM/OWSM/other parts), batch size 64, for up to 100
epochs with early stopping (patience=10). The remaining
segmentation, clustering, and evaluation settings follow Di-
ariZen [21]. Model parameters are averaged over the five
epochs with the lowest validation loss. Audio is processed us-
ing 8 s chunks (3 s/0.8 s stride for training/inference), allowing
up to four speakers per chunk and frame with powerset loss.
For clustering, we apply AHC (speaker range 1–20, thresh-
old 0.7, minimum cluster size 30) using ResNet34-LM (Wes-
peaker [32]) embeddings.
Performance is evaluated with diarization error rate (DER),
decomposed into false alarm (FA), missed detection (MD), and
confusion (CN), computed with pyannote.metrics [33] with-
out collar. Additionally, we report region-specific DER, where
regions are defined based on ground-truth segmentation as
Overlap, Short (<1s), Boundary (within±0.3s of utter-
ance boundaries), Internal (excluding the former three), and
None. Boundary-region errors reflect boundary-level uncer-
tainty, while CN reflects instability in global assignment.

3.5. Hypothesis Analysis of Uncertainty Suppression
We examine whether the results are consistent with the two-
level uncertainty suppression hypothesis: (H1) boundary sta-
bilization is associated with improved assignment consistency,
and (H2) known-speaker priors are associated with more stable
clustering. For H1, Fig. 3 shows structured layer-wise align-
ment between WavLM and OWSM, providing a rationale for
correlation-guided FiLM injection. Table 3 shows that single-
layer FiLM achieves the best overall DER, suggesting that
acoustic and linguistic representations are most complementary
at intermediate layers. Multi-layer injection does not consis-
tently improve over the best single-layer setting, indicating that
targeted conditioning at a well-matched layer pair is more ef-
fective than distributed injection across multiple levels. Fig. 4
further shows reduced CN in Boundary and Internal regions,
particularly for AliMeeting. These observations are consistent
with the interpretation that improved boundary stability is as-
sociated with more consistent global speaker assignment under
rapid speaker turns. For H2, Fig. 5 shows that DER decreases as
more known speakers are incorporated, suggesting that known-
speaker embeddings act as stabilizing priors in clustering. Fig. 6
further illustrates how replicated known-speaker embeddings
anchor cluster centers, supporting improved assignment consis-

4. Conclusion
We proposed an integrated framework that suppresses both local
and global uncertainty in real meeting diarization. For segmen-
tation, we integrate the linguistically contextualized OWSM-
Encoder into WavLM via correlation-guided FiLM, reducing
boundary ambiguity under challenging meeting conditions. For
clustering under mixed known/unknown conditions, we pro-
pose KSGC, which incorporates known-speaker embeddings
as replication-based constraints and consistently improves over
standard AHC. Experimental results on AMI, AliMeeting, and
AISHELL-4 support the two-level uncertainty suppression hy-
pothesis: FiLM is associated with improved boundary stability,
while KSGC is associated with more stable speaker assignment.
Together, these findings suggest that addressing different stages
of the EEND-VC error-propagation path improves robustness
in real-meeting diarization. Future work includes incorporating
known-speaker information into the segmentation stage.
