Abstract
Self-supervised speech models learn rich acoustic represen-
tations, yet how low-level properties are internally encoded
remains opaque. We investigate this using linear probing,
Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), and Iterative Null-space Projec-
tion (INLP). While probing confirms early-layer localization of
acoustic features, raw neurons exhibit severe polysemanticity.
We apply JumpReLU SAEs to disentangle these activations, but
latent ablation reveals aHydra effect: discrete removal fails to
suppress acoustic features due to massive representational re-
dundancy. To overcome this, we employ INLP to erase entire
acoustic linear subspaces. Subspace erasure achieves targeted
suppression, selectively removing features such as pitch while
preserving other acoustic probes. Evaluated on RA VDESS,
CREMA-D, and TIMIT, our results demonstrate that acoustic
features in SSL speech models occupy separable yet distributed
subspaces, requiring subspace-level rather than neuron-level in-
tervention.

1. Introduction
Self-supervised learning (SSL) models for speech, such as
WavLM [1] and HuBERT [2], have achieved state-of-the-art
performance across diverse tasks, from automatic speech recog-
nition to emotion recognition. These models learn hierarchical
internal representations that transform raw waveforms into ab-
stract features, transitioning from low-level acoustic properties
(e.g., pitch, energy) to higher-level semantic and speaker traits
[3, 4]. However, the internal mechanisms by which these fea-
tures emerge and relate across neurons remain largely opaque.
This interpretability gap becomes more severe because of
polysemanticity[5]. While Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) suc-
cessfully extract monosemantic latents in LLMs [6, 7], their
efficacy in the continuous, highly redundant signal domain of
speech remains under-explored. In this work, we investigate
how low-level acoustic features are encoded within WavLM
representations. We first attempt targeted interventions using
SAE disentanglement, but our ablation studies reveal aHydra
effect(named after the mythological creature whose severed
heads regrow: zeroing the most informative latents fails to sup-
press features due to extreme global redundancy).
As the process of discrete ablation was ineffective, we uti-
lize Iterative Null Space Projection (INLP) [8] to effectively
erase the subspaces. Unlike neuron-level ablation, this erasure
of subspaces allows for the selective suppression of individ-
ual features of GeMAPS [9], yet leaving other acoustic prop-
erties largely decodable, showing the separability of continuous
acoustic features in subspaces of SSL models for speech.
2. Related Work
2.1. Probing Speech Models
Linear probing is the dominant method for localizing features
in speech models. Adapting NLP techniques, studies demon-
strate that lower layers capture acoustic properties (pitch, en-
ergy) while upper layers encode linguistic and phonetic abstrac-
tions [10, 3, 4]. Recent work extends this to broader structural
analyses of speech representations, including inter-model simi-
larity [11]. However, these methods provide only correlational
evidence and do not establish causal relationships or account for
polysemanticity.
2.2. Neuron-Level Analysis and Feature Disentanglement
Neuron-level analyses of speech models show that acoustic
and speaker features are not encoded in isolated dimensions.
Probing-based studies find that neurons are multivariate and
share properties, making feature separation difficult [10]. The
distributed encoding of speaker-related acoustic characteristics
motivates further research into more disentangled and inter-
pretable speech representations [12]. In order to measure this
entanglement in a more precise way, we make use of Shap-
ley Additive Explanations (SHAP) [13] to rank the contribu-
tions of individual neurons across the different features, directly
measuring the polysemanticity of the representations learned by
WavLM (Section 3.3).
We therefore turn to Sparse Autoencoders as a disentangle-
ment mechanism. Modern SOTA Sparse Autoencoders, such
as Top-k [14] and JumpReLU [15], eliminate theL 1 shrink-
age bias by decoupling sparsity from magnitude, using hard
constraints or learnable thresholds to preserve activation scale
and reconstruction fidelity (R2). Variants like BatchTopK [16],
which relaxes the per-sample sparsity constraint to the batch
level, and auxiliary reconstruction losses [14] prevent dead neu-
rons and improve global feature utilization.
While pioneered in the textual domain, recent work has be-
gun applying SAEs to audio latent spaces, identifying features
corresponding to acoustic properties such as pitch, amplitude,
and timbre [17]. We extend this to the speech SSL domain by
applying SAEs to WavLM and performing causal ablations to
test whether disentangled SAE directions truly isolate individ-
ual acoustic features, or whether redundancy persists across the
latent space.

2.3. Subspace Erasure for Acoustic Feature Suppression
In the context of Self-Supervised Learning models, while pre-
vious studies have established correlations between layer depth
and feature emergence [10, 3], probing alone cannot confirm
whether acoustic properties are causally encoded in separable
subspaces. Actively removing specific information from the
representation space is necessary to test this. While linear sub-
space erasure techniques such as Iterative Null-space Projection
(INLP) [8] offer a highly interpretable approach to concept re-
moval, adversarial training methods [18, 19] can capture non-
linear structure and are generally better at achieving stronger
erasure but at a lower level of interpretability. However, the
application of subspace erasure techniques to verify selective
acoustic feature suppression in continuous speech domains re-
mains largely unexplored.
3. Experimental Framework
3.1. Model, Datasets, and Feature Extraction
We use WavLM-Base+ [1], a 94M-parameter transformer pre-
trained on 60k hours of English speech data with masked pre-
diction and utterance mixing as our speech model. The model
architecture hasL= 12transformer layers, each with a 768-
dimensional hidden state and 12 attention heads. The pretrained
checkpoints have been obtained from HuggingFace.
Our multi-corpus dataset combines RA VDESS [20],
CREMA-D [21], and TIMIT [22], comprising approximately
6,000 vocal utterances (≈10 hours of audio, 3s average du-
ration). The selection process used here ensures a balanced
distribution of phonetic variety and prosodic diversity. We
use train/test splits, which ensure that there is strictly speaker-
independent evaluation for all the tasks.
To represent low-level acoustic properties, we extract
six descriptors from the GeMAPS using openSMILE: pitch-
related (F 0 semitone from 27.5Hzsma3nz,F 1 frequency
sma3nz), spectral (Alpha Ratiosma3, Hammarberg Indexsma3,
Harmonic-to-Noise RatioHNRdBACF), and prosodic (Loud-
nesssma3). We perform linear interpolation on the acoustic
labels because WavLM produces representations at 20 ms stride
whereas openSMILE processes audio at 10 ms intervals. This
gives us a precisely synchronized frame-wise dataset where
every WavLM activation can be matched to a corresponding
GeMAPS vector.
3.2. Feature Localization via Linear Probing
We extract intermediate representations from all 12 transformer
layers of WavLM. For each layerland target featuref, we
train a linear probe (ridge regression for continuous acoustic
features) on the layer-lactivations. We evaluate continuous fea-
tures (e.g., pitch, energy) using the coefficient of determination
(R2). To ensure that the probes capture genuine structure rather
than dataset statistics, we establish a baseline by training probes
on randomly shuffled labels; since shuffling destroys all tempo-
ral alignment between activations and targets, any probe perfor-
mance above this baseline reflects true representational struc-
ture.
To localize information down to the individual neuron
level, we rank neurons based on Shapley Additive Explanations
(SHAP) [13]. This provides an importance scoreI(n,f,l)for
each neuronn, featuref, and layerl, which provides a precise
baseline for our upcoming disentanglement experiments.
3.3. Polysemanticity Analysis and SAE Disentanglement
To evaluate whether raw WavLM representations exist in super-
position, we examined the highest-ranked neurons for each fea-
ture based on SHAP values. We observe significant polyseman-
ticity: individual neurons frequently emerge as highly important
for multiple distinct acoustic features simultaneously. This con-
firms that semantic information is heavily entangled across the
model’s standard basis, motivating the need for feature disen-
tanglement.

3.4. Causal Intervention: Latent Ablation and Subspace
Erasure
To evaluate whether the SAE provides a causally disentangled
representation of acoustic features, we perform targeted itera-
tive ablation. For a given low-level featuref low, we train a
Lasso probe on the SAE latent space and rank the latents by
their SHAP importance. We systematically ablate (zero out) the
top-Nmost critical latents and measure the relative retention of
the probe’sR 2 score. This discrete ablation tests for feature re-
dundancy and the presence of aHydra effect, where removing
highly informative directions fails to suppress the feature due to
distributed encoding.
Due to the possible resistance of continuous acoustic mani-
folds to discrete latent ablation, we then use Iterative Null-space
Projection (INLP) [8] for complete feature removal. INLP
learns linear predictors for all off low (e.g., pitch, loudness)
and then projects representations onto their null spaces to com-
pletely remove an entire linear subspace corresponding to a par-
ticular feature. By computing theR 2 retention of other acous-
tic probes on representations where a particular feature is com-
pletely removed using INLP, we can confirm that feature re-
moval is selective in that a particular feature is removed without
compromising decodability of unrelated acoustic properties.
All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX
4070 GPU with 16 GB of memory. SAE model was trained and
evaluated using multiple random seeds, and all reported results
correspond to the mean performance across seeds.

4.2. Polysemanticity Analysis
To investigate whether WavLM representations are monose-
mantic or exist in superposition, we analyze the importance
ranks of individual neurons across acoustic features. Fig. 2
illustrates the neuron importance ranks. We observe signif-
icantpolysemanticity, where individual neurons are consis-
tently ranked as important across multiple acoustically distinct
features simultaneously (e.g., neuron 44 ranks first forF 0, Jit-
ter, HNR, andF 2-amp simultaneously). This overlap confirms
the theoretical prediction of thesuperposition hypothesis[24]
within the speech domain. The existence of these highly pol-
ysemantic neurons indicates that features are not encoded in a
1-to-1 mapping with the neuron basis, making raw activations
inherently difficult to interpret. This entanglement provides the
primary motivation for employing Sparse Autoencoders to dis-
entangle the representation into a higher-dimensional, monose-
mantic feature space.

4.5. Acoustic Subspace Erasure and Feature Selectivity
To evaluate the selectivity of INLP-based subspace erasure, we
remove individual acoustic subspaces from WavLM Layer 1
representations and measure the resultingR 2 drop across all
other acoustic probes.
Figure 5 illustrates the percentage drop in probeR 2 fol-
lowing each ablation. The diagonal confirms effective self-
suppression, with target features such asF 0 semitone, Loud-
ness, and HNRdBACF dropping by 94.41%, 94.66%, and
95.35% respectively. The random projection control yields
near-zero drops across all probes, confirming that suppression
is not an artifact of generic dimensionality reduction.
Off-diagonal values reveal two distinct regimes of feature
coupling. Loudness erasure is highly selective, leaving all other
probes largely intact (all off-diagonal drops<1.5%), demon-
strating clean subspace separation. In contrast, the spectral fea-
tures (alphaRatio, hammarbergIndex, and mfcc1) exhibit strong
mutual coupling, with cross-drops exceeding 75%, suggesting
they occupy a shared or heavily overlapping subspace within
the model.F 0 semitone shows moderate spillover onto HNRd-
BACF (72.87%), indicating partial subspace overlap between
these harmonicity-related features.

5. Conclusion
We investigated the internal representations of WavLM using
linear probing, Sparse Autoencoders, and Iterative Null-space
Projection. Probing confirms that acoustic features are linearly
encoded and localized to early layers, while raw neuron acti-
vations exhibit severe polysemanticity. Although JumpReLU
SAEs yield sparse, high-fidelity latents, targeted ablation re-
veals aHydra effect: discrete removal fails to suppress acous-
tic features due to extensive representational redundancy within
the continuous acoustic manifold. To overcome this, we ap-
ply INLP-based subspace erasure, which achieves effective
and largely selective suppression of individual acoustic fea-
tures. Notably, loudness occupies a well-separated subspace,
while spectral features such as alphaRatio, hammarbergIndex,
and mfcc1 share a heavily coupled subspace. These findings
demonstrate that continuous acoustic features in SSL speech
models are distributed yet separable at the subspace level, and
that mechanistic interpretability in the speech domain requires
subspace-level rather than neuron-level intervention.
