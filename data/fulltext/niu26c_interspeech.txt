Improving Stable Speech Synthesis Post-Training with ChatScorer and
Margin-Based Preference Construction

1 College of Intelligence and Computing, Tianjin University, Tianjin, China

Abstract
Existing post-training methods for codec-based text-to-speech
(TTS) often rely on multiple automatic metrics, such as char-
acter or word error rate (CER/WER) for content consistency,
speaker similarity (SIM) for speaker preservation, and their
weighted combinations as reward signals. However, directly
fusing heterogeneous metrics into a single scalar reward can
yield weakly separated candidate scores, leading to ambigu-
ous supervision. In this paper, we propose a new post-
training framework for stable speech synthesis, which intro-
duces ChatScorer as an auxiliary reward model and combines it
with margin-based preference construction. We instantiate the
proposed framework using different post-training methods, and
experiments on codec-based TTS show that it reduces undesir-
able outputs and improves generation stability, while maintain-
ing competitive intelligibility and speaker similarity.
Index Terms: text-to-speech, reinforcement learning, prefer-
ence optimization, speech style
1. Introduction
Recent progress in large language models (LLMs) has driven
the development of neural codec language models for text-to-
speech (TTS)[1]. With strong in-context generation capabili-
ties, codec-based TTS systems [2, 3, 4] have substantially im-
proved synthesis quality and generalization.
To better align synthesized speech with human preferences,
recent TTS studies have begun to adopt preference optimiza-
tion and reinforcement learning from human or proxy feed-
back (RLHF) [5, 6]. In the DPO line, NaturalSpeech 3 [7]
incorporates RLHF to improve controllability, Emo-DPO [8]
applies direct preference optimization to emotional speech syn-
thesis, and Fine-grained Preference Optimization (FPO) [9] fur-
ther improves robustness by focusing on local segmental er-
rors. More recently, GRPO-based approaches have also been
explored for TTS. Prior work applies GRPO to LLM-based TTS
using ASR-derived rewards [10], extends it to flow-matching
TTS with WER and speaker-similarity rewards [11], and studies
multi-reward GRPO for improving prosodic stability in single-
codebook TTS LLMs [12].
Despite these advances, existing methods [13, 14, 15] still
mainly rely on automatic metrics and their weighted combina-
tions as optimization signals. Such signals are often insufficient
to reliably suppress clearly machine-like candidates, and, when
multiple rewards are merged into a single scalar, the resulting
candidate scores can be only weakly separated, leading to am-
biguous supervision during preference optimization.

To address these limitations, we focus on improving the
learnability of preference signals for TTS post-training and in-
troduce a framework that combinesChatScorer, a specially
trained auxiliary reward model, with margin-based preference
construction.ChatScoreris designed to identify clearly unde-
sirable, overly machine-like conversational outputs, while the
construction strategy converts sampled candidates into prefer-
ence subsets with clearer quality gaps for subsequent optimiza-
tion. The contributions of this work are three-fold:
• We identify a practical challenge in TTS post-training: di-
rectly aggregating different automatic metrics into a single
scalar reward may yield poorly separated candidates, thereby
introducing ambiguous supervision.
• We propose a new post-training framework that integrates a
new reward model with a carefully designed data construc-
tion strategy to get clearer supervision for better preference
optimization.
• We further introduce stability-oriented evaluation metrics, in-
cludinggroup-level failure rateandChatScore-based bad
rate, together with conventional quality measures, to system-
atically assess different post-training methods under the pro-
posed framework.

too small, the resulting supervision can be weak or even mis-

learnable.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
3.1.1. Dataset and Baseline
We adopt CosyV oice2-0.5B [21] as the base model. A super-
vised fine-tuning (SFT) model is trained with 5 hours of real
recordings for each target speaker and used only for candi-
date generation, while all preference-based post-training is per-
formed on the original base model.
To better reflect realistic conversational usage, we build a
long-form chat-style text set covering diverse everyday scenar-
ios. The prompts are generated with GPT-5 and manually fil-
tered, with relatively long utterances and language styles closer
to real human chatting. The dataset contains 2,000 training
prompts and 500 test prompts. Each prompt is used to gen-
erate 15 candidate utterances with different random seeds for
preference construction.
3.1.2. ChatScorer Training and Validation
ChatScorer is trained on conversational recordings and diverse
synthetic speech with human quality annotations, and is further
refined on manually annotated utterances. Rather than serving
as a general MOS predictor [13, 22], its purpose is to identify
candidates that are less suitable for realistic chat-style synthesis,
especially outputs that sound overly machine-like or otherwise
undesirable.
To validate ChatScorer as a reward signal, we compare it
with DNSMOS and UTMOSv2 using human A/B preference
judgments. “Human preference” indicates which utterance lis-
teners would prefer as a realistic chat-style TTS output when
comparing two candidates generated from the same prompt.
We collect ratings for 200 synthesized utterances from four
annotators and derive about 1,000 pairwise comparisons. A
comparison is considered consistent if an automatic metric and
the annotators prefer the same utterance. As shown in Fig. 3,
ChatScorer achieves markedly higher agreement than MOS-
based baselines, suggesting it better detects undesirable conver-
sational candidates.

3.1.3. Reinforcement Learning Setup
We compare three preference-based post-training strategies:
DPO, RankDPO, and GRPO. To ensure a fair comparison un-
der the same data budget, all methods are trained on the same
2,000 prompts described in Sec. 3.1.1. Two reward configura-
tions are explored throughout the experiments: (i) CER+SIM
and (ii) CER+SIM+ChatScore.
For DPO and RankDPO, the sampled candidate sets are
converted into preference data according to the construction
rules in Sec. 2.3. For GRPO, the model is optimized online
on the same training prompts using scalar rewards formed by
weighted fusion of the same automatic metrics.
Optimization is performed with AdamW [23] using a learn-
ing rate of1×10 −5. Dynamic batching is adopted, with each
batch containing 1,000 speech frames depending on sequence
length. Training is conducted on two NVIDIA A800 GPUs.

3.2.2. Main Results
Table 1 reports the main comparison results of different post-
training methods. Both DPO and Rank3DPO reduce CER rela-
tive to the SFT baseline, while SIM remains largely unchanged
across methods. This is expected, since post-training mainly
updates the autoregressive LLM rather than the flow decoder,
and thus has limited effect on speaker similarity. More impor-
tantly, the proposed framework improves generation stability.
The group-level SIM failure rate drops from 0.42 for SFT to
0.14 for DPO and further to 0.10 for Rank3DPO. In addition,
Rank3DPO also achieves the lowest BadRate, indicating that
it more effectively suppresses clearly undesirable, machine-like
outputs.
Although GRPO also reduces failure rates compared with
SFT, its overall performance remains less competitive in our
current setting, with CER still worse than DPO and Rank3DPO.
This is reasonable, since GRPO performs online updates on the
same 2,000 training prompts using fused scalar rewards over all
sampled candidates, without the explicit margin-based filtering
that selectively retains supervision with clearer quality separa-
tion.

4. Conclusion
This paper presents a new preference-based post-training
framework for codec-based TTS, in which ChatScorer is in-
troduced as an auxiliary reward to better identify undesirable
machine-like outputs, and a rank-aware data selection strategy
is designed to construct more learnable preference pairs and
ranked subsets for post-training. Experimental results show that
the proposed framework improves generation stability, reduces
undesirable outputs, and maintains competitive intelligibility
and speaker similarity. A current limitation is that the GRPO
experiments in this work are conducted on a relatively limited
data scale. Future work will extend the study to larger-scale
online multi-reward optimization with GRPO and more diverse
multilingual settings.
