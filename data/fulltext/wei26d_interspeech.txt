USV-DETR: High-Resolution and Densely Supervised Detection of Ultrasonic
V ocalizations

Abstract
Ultrasonic vocalizations (USVs) are important behavioral in-
dicators in rodent neuroscience research, yet automated detec-
tion remains challenging due to their small scale, narrow band-
width, and sparse distribution in spectrograms. Existing meth-
ods mostly rely on rule-based tools or traditional convolutional
networks, which struggle to preserve critical acoustic details
and lack effective mechanisms for modeling fine-grained and
sparse acoustic patterns. We propose USV-DETR, an end-to-
end model optimized for USV detection. Built on RT-DETR,
it introduces a high-resolution P2 feature layer to better repre-
sent narrowband, short-duration signals and adopts the DEIM
training framework to provide dense and stable supervision for
sparse small targets. Experiments across different datasets show
that USV-DETR consistently achieves the best detection per-
formance, enabling more precise time-frequency localization of
USVs and providing a useful tool for acoustic feature analysis.

1. Introduction
In behavioral and neuroscience research, acoustic signals pro-
vide an important window into social interaction, emotional
regulation, and neural function [1, 2]. Ultrasonic vocalizations
(USVs) produced by rodents serve as informative biomarkers
for assessing behavioral and neural states in this field [3]. USVs
are widely used to assess anxiety, autism spectrum disorders,
parental behavior modulation, hypoxic ischemic encephalopa-
thy, and developmental changes [4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. This broad range
of applications has created a growing need for accurate and ef-
ficient methods to detect and analyze vocalizations.
Early USV detection relied on manual annotation or rule-
based tools that applied predefined energy thresholds and
frequency ranges. These approaches were effective under
controlled recording conditions but required manual param-
eter adjustment [9]. Deep learning later enabled automated
spectrogram-based detection. DeepSqueak introduced Faster
R-CNN for USV analysis [10], and V ocalMat integrates image
processing techniques with convolutional neural networks [11].
While these methods improved detection performance, they of-
ten provide only coarse localization of vocalizations. This may
limit accurate characterization of the time-frequency structure
of individual USVs and affect downstream acoustic analysis.
Recent advances in computer vision offer promising direc-
tions for addressing this challenge. USVs share important sim-
ilarities with small targets in aerial imagery, remote sensing,
and agricultural fruit detection [12, 13, 14, 15]. Transformer-

based detectors have demonstrated strong performance on such
tasks. DETR simplifies the detection pipeline through end-
to-end training with one-to-one (O2O) matching, although its
training speed is limited [16]. RT-DETR improves conver-
gence and multi-scale modeling by combining convolutional
features with a Transformer encoder and has been success-
fully applied to various small-target scenarios [17]. Compared
with the YOLO family of models, RT-DETR offers stronger
global modeling capability and better sensitivity to fine struc-
tures, which makes it a promising candidate for precise USV
detection [18, 19, 20].
Nevertheless, the original RT-DETR is designed for natu-
ral images [17]. Its multi-scale feature hierarchy and training
mechanism do not fully reflect the structural characteristics of
acoustic spectrograms. Small USV patterns are easily weak-
ened through standard downsampling, and the O2O matching
mechanism used in DETR-style models limits training effi-
ciency, making it difficult for the model to fully adapt to the
sparse distribution of USVs [21]. Therefore, directly applying
RT-DETR yields suboptimal results for USV detection.
To address these limitations, we propose USV-DETR, an
end-to-end model optimized for USV detection. Building on
RT-DETR, we introduce systematic improvements at both the
architectural and training levels. Architecturally, we incor-
porate a high-resolution P2 feature layer to strengthen spa-
tial representation of narrowband and short duration USVs,
thereby preserving more detailed temporal and spectral infor-
mation. In terms of training, we adopt the DEIM frame-
work, which increases the density of positive samples through
Dense O2O Matching and enhances supervision stability with
a Matchability-Aware Loss that specifically adjusts low qual-
ity matches [21]. These improvements lead to faster training
convergence and significantly enhance the model’s performance
on small object detection tasks. Experimental results show that
USV-DETR performs well across different datasets and outper-
forms multiple baseline methods.
The contributions of this work are as follows:
• We present a DETR-based model for automated USV detec-
tion, leveraging global self-attention for precise localization
in complex acoustic environments.
• We introduce architectural and training adaptations for small
USV targets, including a high-resolution P2 feature layer and
the DEIM framework, which improve detection accuracy and
convergence efficiency.

2. Methods
2.1. Overall Architecture
USV-DETR is built upon the RT-DETR architecture, introduc-
ing a P2 high-resolution feature layer and DEIM training frame-
work for small-scale ultrasonic vocalization detection in spec-
trograms. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure 1.
Given a 640 × 640 input spectrogram, the HGNet backbone
extracts four-scale features (P2-P5) with downsampling strides
of 4, 8, 16, and 32 pixels and channel dimensions of [128, 512,
1024, 2048]. Unlike standard RT-DETR which starts from P3,
we introduce a high-resolution P2 layer to preserve fine-grained
acoustic details [22, 23]. USVs exhibit narrowband, short dura-
tion, and small-scale characteristics in spectrograms. The stan-
dard 8× downsampling loses critical spatial details, while the P2
layer with 4× downsampling preserves fine-grained information
necessary for detecting small targets.
The efficient hybrid encoder consists of two modules: the
AIFI module applies self-attention to the highest level fea-
ture P5 to capture global context and outputs semantically en-
hanced F5; the CCFF module fuses the semantic information of
F5 into P2-P4 in a top-down manner, generating a four-layer
feature pyramid for the decoder. The decoder employs de-
formable attention for multi-scale feature aggregation and ini-
tializes queries based on a Top-K query selection strategy.
During training, the DEIM framework is adopted as an-
other improvement. DEIM increases positive sample density
through Dense O2O Matching with Mosaic and Mixup data
augmentation, and replaces the standard Varifocal Loss with
Matchability-Aware Loss to impose stronger supervision on
low-quality matches, providing denser and more stable training
signals for sparsely distributed small USV targets. The total loss
function comprises a weighted combination of MAL, bounding
box regression loss, GIoU loss, classification loss, and distribu-
tion refinement loss.

2.3. DEIM Training Framework
Standard DETR employs an O2O matching strategy, assigning
a unique query to each ground truth target through the Hungar-
ian algorithm. This strategy suffers from positive sample spar-

sity and uneven matching quality in small object dense scenar-
ios. The DEIM framework addresses these limitations through
Dense O2O Matching and Matchability-Aware Loss.
Dense O2O Matchingincreases positive sample density
through strong data augmentation. We apply Mosaic augmen-
tation (stitching 4 images into one) and Mixup augmentation
(blending pixel values of two images) with probabilityp= 0.5.
Mosaic can increase the number of targets per sample to ap-
proximately4×the original, while Mixup generates new sam-
ples through pixel-level blending and introduces scale varia-
tions. These augmentation strategies significantly increase the
number of positive matching pairs per batch, providing denser
supervision signals for small targets.
Matchability-Aware Loss (MAL)dynamically adjusts
loss weights based on matching quality. For thek-th matched
pair, its weight is defined as:
wk =f(IoU k)·(1−p k)γ (4)
where IoUk is the intersection over union between the pre-
dicted box and ground truth box,p k is the classification confi-
dence, andγis the focusing parameter. This weighting mecha-
nism imposes stronger supervision on low-IoU matches, accel-
erating the optimization of hard samples. The DEIM framework
makes the training process more stable and converges faster
through increased positive sample density and adaptive weight
adjustment.
2.4. Loss Functions
Model training employs a multi-task loss function combining
five components: Matchability-Aware Loss (MAL) for stronger
supervision on low-quality matches, L1 bounding box regres-
sion loss and GIoU loss for optimizing coordinate accuracy and
box overlap, Focal Loss for handling class imbalance, and dis-
tribution refinement loss inherited from the D-FINE framework
to improve localization accuracy. The loss weights are set as
λbbox = 5,λ giou = 2,λ cls = 1,λ ddf = 1.5, a configura-
tion that has been validated as effective in the RT-DETR series
models.
3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics
To evaluate generalization ability, we conduct experiments
across different datasets: the publicly available SqueakOut
dataset and our newly collected USVpic dataset [24, 25].
SqueakOutcontains 12,954 mouse USV spectrogram im-
ages with a resolution of 512 × 512 pixels, including five
strains (C57Bl6/J, NZO/HlLtJ, 129S1/SvImJ, NOD/ShiLtJ, and
PWK/PhJ), with vocalizations recorded from postnatal day (P)
5 to P15 [24].
USVpiccontains 3,000 rat USV spectrogram images with
a resolution of 640 × 640 pixels. V ocalizations were collected
from 27 Wistar rats across P10–P15, and annotated using SAM
3 to prepare the training dataset [25].
The training set, validation set, and test set of the two
datasets are divided in proportions of 70%, 20%, and 10%, re-
spectively. Model performance evaluation follows the standard
metric system for object detection tasks, including Average Pre-
cision (AP), AP 50, AP75, and Average Precision for Small ob-
jects (APs) [26].

3.2. Implementation Details
All experiments are conducted on NVIDIA H100 GPUs. The
model input size is 640 × 640 pixels, trained for 50 epochs with
a batch size of 16. For baseline model comparisons, we adopt
the default hyperparameter settings from their official imple-
mentations. The related code and model checkpoints are avail-
able athttps://github.com/weiyilan9/USV-DETR.

3.4. Ablation Study
Table 2 shows the ablation results of USV-DETR on the
SqueakOut dataset, aiming to systematically evaluate the con-
tributions of the DEIM training strategy and the high-resolution
P2 feature layer to model performance. When the DEIM train-
ing strategy is removed, the model’s overall detection perfor-
mance AP drops by 0.9%. This suggests that DEIM enhances
the model’s ability to learn from sparse, small-scale USV pat-
terns by providing denser and more stable supervisory signals.
These signals help mitigate the training challenges caused by
the sparse distribution of small targets. Removing the P2 fea-
ture layer leads to a significant drop in overall detection per-
formance of 3.9%, with an even more pronounced decrease
of 5.0% in the small object detection metric AP s. This indi-
cates that high-resolution features play an important role in pre-
serving the spatial representation of narrowband, short-duration
USVs, which are often degraded during standard downsam-
pling processes. When both DEIM and the P2 feature layer
are removed, the model shows the most substantial performance
degradation, with AP decreasing by 4.2% and AP s by 5.4%.
These results further confirm that the DEIM training strategy
and the P2 feature layer contribute from two complementary
perspectives, namely supervisory signal density and feature res-
olution. Together, they enhance USV-DETR’s ability to detect
USVs.

4. Conclusion
This paper proposes USV-DETR, an end-to-end detection
framework optimized for USV detection, introducing recent ad-
vances in computer vision small object detection into animal
behavioral and neuroscience research. Addressing the small-
scale, narrowband, and sparsely distributed characteristics of
USVs in acoustic spectrograms, we systematically optimize the
RT-DETR architecture by introducing a high-resolution P2 fea-
ture layer to enhance fine-grained vocalization representation
and adopting the DEIM training framework to provide denser
and more stable supervision for sparse small targets. Experi-
mental results show that USV-DETR outperforms all baseline
models across different datasets, with ablation studies further
validating the complementary contributions of high-resolution
feature modeling and dense supervision strategies. Our work
provides a useful tool for more accurate USV detection and
downstream acoustic analysis. Future work may investigate se-
mantic segmentation-based approaches to support cross-species
vocalization detection.
