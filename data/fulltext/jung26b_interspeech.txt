Abstract
Self-conditioned (SC) end-to-end neural diarization (EEND) it-
eratively refines speaker predictions across layers. However,
conventional layer-wise permutation invariant training (PIT)
independently determines the optimal speaker permutation at
each layer. This layer-specific permutation selection causes
hierarchical permutation inconsistency (HPI), propagating per-
mutation noise into the SC path and disrupting training stabil-
ity. To resolve this, we propose two frameworks: Group-Wise
PIT (GW-PIT) and Continuous Influence PIT (CI-PIT). While
GW-PIT enforces a global permutation constraint, CI-PIT ap-
plies soft regularization by aggregating layer-wise cost matrices
using Gaussian weighting. This promotes permutation consis-
tency across the hierarchy while preserving local representation
flexibility. Experimental results on CALLHOME demonstrate
that the proposed CI-PIT suppresses permutation noise, stabi-
lizes training dynamics, and achieves DER improvements for
2- and 3-speaker scenarios.
Index Terms: speaker diarization, end-to-end neural diariza-
tion (EEND), self-conditioning, permutation invariant training
(PIT), hierarchical permutation consistency
1. Introduction
Speaker diarization, the task of identifying “who spoke when,”
is a core front-end for various speech applications [1]. To ad-
dress the error propagation and structural limitations of tra-
ditional clustering pipelines in overlapping speech [1], End-
to-End Neural Diarization (EEND) reformulated the task as a
frame-wise multi-label classification problem [2]. EEND archi-
tectures have since evolved from self-attention [3] and encoder-
decoder attractors (EDA) [4, 5].
A parallel line of research has pursued non-autoregressive
(NA) EEND models, which improve inference efficiency by ex-
tracting speaker attractors in parallel [6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. To further
refine intermediate representations, self-conditioning (SC)—
originally proposed for CTC-based ASR [11]—has been intro-
duced into EEND, where intermediate diarization predictionsˆYl
are fed back as conditioning signals to subsequent encoder lay-
ers, enabling progressive refinement of speaker activity across
the network depth [7, 12, 10].
However, a structural conflict exists between this SC mech-
anism and Permutation Invariant Training (PIT), the conven-
tional training objective for resolving speaker-label permutation
ambiguity. PIT’s hard minimum greedily resolves permutations
without constraining cross-iteration consistency, inducinglabel
switching—abrupt speaker index reassignments across training
epochs—which has been shown to destabilize convergence in
speech separation [13]. Applying PIT independently at each
layer further introduceslayer-decoupling: adjacent layers freely
adopt conflicting speaker assignments, as identified in deeper
separation models [14] and addressed through cascaded train-
ing and layer-wise optimization in speech separation [15, 16].
In EEND, auxiliary PIT losses at intermediate layers were
first introduced by [17], and further explored through layer-
specific attention supervision [18] and multi-resolution hierar-
chical fusion [19]; SC-based models inherit the same indepen-
dent per-layer optimization [7, 12, 10], where each layer re-
solves its own permutation without cross-layer coordination. In
SC-based EEND, however, this presents a more severe risk:
because ˆYl is directly passed as a conditioning signal to layer
l+1, inter-layer permutation mismatches propagate as struc-
tured noise throughout the SC path—a failure mode that layer-
wise independent optimization is inherently limited in address-
ing.
We formalize this asHierarchical Permutation Inconsis-
tency(HPI): when each intermediate layer independently min-
imizes its own PIT loss, adjacent layers may resolve permuta-
tion ambiguity in a conflicting manner, propagating permutation
noise into the SC conditioning path and disrupting progressive
feature refinement.
To address HPI, we propose two complementary frame-
works.Group-Wise PIT(GW-PIT) imposes a hard constraint
by tying all intermediate layers to a single global permuta-
tion, eliminating inter-layer mismatch.Continuous Influence
PIT(CI-PIT) applies a soft regularization scheme that ag-
gregates cost matrices across layers via Gaussian weighting,
promoting inter-layer permutation continuity while preserving
layer-specific flexibility. To analyze how each framework af-
fects permutation dynamics along the SC path, we further in-
troduce three diagnostic metrics—permutation mismatch ratio
(PMR), adjacent switching ratio (ASR), and epoch switching
ratio (ESR)—and provide a systematic evaluation under each
training objective.

We evaluated our proposed frameworks on the CALL-
HOME(NIST SRE 2000) dataset [22], a standard benchmark
for speaker diarization. We conducted experiments on both 2-
speaker and 3-speaker scenarios. The dataset was partitioned
into adaptation and test sets using the standard CALLHOME
partition 1. For training, we generated simulated conversations
(SimConv)2 [23, 24] using the LibriSpeech corpus [25] as the
source speech. To match the acoustic conditions of the CALL-
HOME dataset, the original 16 kHz audio was downsampled to
8 kHz. To simulate realistic overlap patterns and utterance dis-
tributions, we extracted statistical priors from the CALLHOME
adaptation set for the 2-speaker and 3-speaker conditions, re-
spectively. Following previous work [24], we augmented the
training data with MUSAN noise [26] without applying room
impulse responses (RIR). Detailed dataset statistics are summa-
rized in Table 1.

3.2. Model Configurations
Our baseline architecture follows the EEND-NA [7] de-
scribed in Section 2.1, re-implemented using the PyTorch-based
EEND-EDA codebase3 and trained from scratch on our Sim-
Conv datasets. The input features are 23-dimensional log Mel-
filterbanks (10 ms shift), stacked over 15 consecutive frames
into a 345-dimensional input vector every 100 ms. The Trans-
former encoder consists of 8 layers with a 256-dimensional em-
bedding size, 4 attention heads, and a 2048-dimensional FFN.
We used 500-frame audio segments with batch size 64, opti-
mized with Adam [27] and a Noam learning rate scheduler [28]
(100k warm-up steps). Pre-training on SimConv was performed
for 100 epochs; the model was subsequently fine-tuned on the
CALLHOME adaptation set for another 100 epochs at a fixed
learning rate of10 −5. In both stages, the weights of the last
10 epoch checkpoints were averaged to obtain the final model.
During inference, the full recording is processed in a single for-
ward pass. DER is evaluated with a 0.25 s forgiveness collar;
a median filter with a window size of 11 is applied as post-
processing over the network’s output, and speaker activities are
determined by thresholding the output probabilities at 0.5.

4.4. Interaction with Self-Conditioning
Table 4 isolates the contribution of SC under each PIT ob-
jective. Adding SC toL LW-PIT yields negligible improvement
(8.34%→8.30%), as permutation volatility corrupts the condi-
tioning path.L GW-PIT benefits more from SC (8.51%→8.02%),
yet remains limited by its over-constrained intermediate repre-
sentations. The combination ofL CI-PIT and SC yields the largest
reduction (8.67%→7.52%), despiteL CI-PIT alone underper-
forming the baseline. This synergy demonstrates thatL CI-PIT
is specifically designed to unlock SC’s potential: by suppress-
ing permutation noise without eliminating layer-specific flexi-
bility, it enables the progressive refinement that SC is intended
to achieve.
4.5. Effect of Gaussian Scope in CI-PIT
Table 5 shows thatσ= 1.0yields the best DER of 7.52%.
Asσincreases, the aggregation scope widens andL CI-PIT ap-
proaches the behavior ofLGW-PIT, with DER degrading to 7.97%
and 8.12% atσ= 2.0and3.0, respectively. This confirms that
localized, layer-proximate permutation alignment is optimal:σ
controls the trade-off between consistency and flexibility, and
excessive coupling eliminates the layer-specific differentiation
essential for modeling complex speaker transitions.
5. Conclusions
This paper identified that applying independent per-layer PIT—
formalized asLayer-Wise PIT(L LW-PIT)—to self-conditioned
EEND models causesHierarchical Permutation Inconsistency
(HPI), injecting structured noise into the SC path and disrupting
progressive refinement. To address this, we proposedGroup-
Wise PIT(L GW-PIT) andContinuous Influence PIT(L CI-PIT),
which softly aligns permutations across the layer hierarchy via
Gaussian-weighted cost matrix aggregation. Evaluations on
CALLHOME show thatL CI-PIT suppresses permutation noise
while preserving representational flexibility, achieving consis-
tent DER improvements across 2- and 3-speaker scenarios.
