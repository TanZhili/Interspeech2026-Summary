Abstract
To counter eavesdropping threats from recent advances in
speech technology, this paper proposes a privacy-preserving
speaker verification (SV) framework that protects both linguis-
tic content and biometric identity. We leverage FAcodec to ex-
plicitly remove content-bearing information and selectively ap-
ply multi-granularity feature obfuscation to the remaining non-
content representations, preserving speaker-discriminative cues
while discouraging faithful waveform reconstruction. Our re-
sults further indicate that ASR-based metrics such as Word Er-
ror Rate (WER) alone may not fully reflect perceptual privacy:
even without content tokens, reconstructed speech can remain
partially intelligible to listeners despite high WER. By substan-
tially reducing perceptual intelligibility, our approach achieves
an EER of 2.35% on V oxCeleb1-O, a 54.7% relative improve-
ment over prior state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating a favor-
able balance between biometric utility and linguistic privacy.

1. Introduction
Conventional Speaker Verification (SV) systems [1] face a
fundamental dilemma between authentication accuracy and
data privacy. Transmitting raw audio or high-resolution spec-
trograms to maximize performance exposes users to critical
threats: the leakage of sensitive conversations via Automatic
Speech Recognition (ASR) [2, 3] and the generation of deep-
fakes exploiting Text-to-Speech (TTS) [4] and V oice Conver-
sion (VC) [5]. Therefore, developing a privacy-preserving SV
framework [6] that robustly verifies identity while suppressing
linguistic traces is an urgent necessity.
To address this issue, attempts have been made to sepa-
rate speaker identity from linguistic content using speech dis-
entanglement techniques. For instance, SpeechTokenizer [7]
is designed to concentrate semantic information into the first
quantizer by utilizing a Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ)
[8] structure based on teacher-student learning. SafeEar [9] ap-
plies this architecture to deepfake detection by removing the se-
mantic quantizer and utilizing only the residual acoustic tokens,
further applying Global Shuffling to mask potential linguis-
tic information leakage. However, such indiscriminate scram-
bling disrupts the global temporal context essential for text-
independent SV , leading to a significant degradation in verifi-
cation performance. This highlights a limitation of existing ap-
proaches: the lack of precise control over temporal information

Furthermore, a fundamental gap exists between metric-
based and perception-based privacy evaluations. Even if content
tokens are explicitly removed through speech disentanglement,
the remaining prosody information may still contain linguistic
cues; thus, despite exhibiting high Word Error Rate (WER) and
Character Error Rate (CER) in ASR systems, the reconstructed
speech can remain intelligible to human listeners. This discrep-
ancy implies that conventional ASR-based metrics alone may
not be sufficient to assess privacy protection, suggesting the
need for an obfuscation strategy that reduces linguistic recov-
erability at both the metric and perceptual levels.
In this paper, we propose a novel SV framework that re-
solves the trade-off between privacy and verification accuracy.
To address the limitations of conventional RVQ-based architec-
tures, we utilize FAcodec [10] to explicitly and independently
decompose speech into four components: content, prosody, tim-
bre, and acoustic details. While the content component is en-
tirely discarded to prevent ASR leakage, we introduce a hierar-
chically operating Multi-Granularity Feature Obfuscation strat-
egy to eliminate residual linguistic cues within the prosody. By
systematically applying Global Temporal Aggregation (GTA)
alongside local-level operations (Local Temporal Aggregation
and Permutation; LTA, LTP), our approach substantially re-
duces perceptual intelligibility while successfully preserving
speaker-specific acoustic characteristics. The main contribu-
tions of this work are summarized as follows:
• We propose a privacy-preserving SV framework combining
explicit feature disentanglement via FAcodec with a multi-
granularity obfuscation module.
• We introduce a hierarchical obfuscation strategy based on
GTA, LTA, and LTP to overcome the limitations of existing
single-level randomization, effectively masking linguistic in-
formation across multiple temporal resolutions.
• We address the critical gap between metric-based and
perceptual-based privacy evaluations. By achieving an EER
of 2.35% and a WER of 98.83% on V oxCeleb1-O, we provide
empirical evidence that our framework improves the privacy–
utility trade-off while substantially reducing linguistic recov-
erability (supported by supplementary audio samples).

2 describes the proposed method. Section 3 presents the ex-
perimental setup. Section 4 presents experimental results and
ablation studies. Section 5 concludes the paper.

2. Proposed Method
2.1. Feature Disentanglement Framework
The proposed privacy-preserving SV framework consists of
four sequential modules: 1⃝explicit feature disentanglement,
2⃝multi-granularity feature obfuscation, 3⃝speaker embedding
extraction, and 4⃝reconstruction-based privacy evaluation. As
illustrated in Figures 1 and 2, feature disentanglement and ob-
fuscation are performed on the client side, ensuring that only
privacy-protected representations are transmitted to the server.
Explicit Feature Disentanglement via FAcodec.Given an in-
put speech signalx, FAcodec [10] decomposes it into four inde-
pendent feature matrices: content (F con), prosody (Fpro), tim-
bre (Ftim), and acoustic detail (Faco):
FAcodec(x) ={F con,F pro,F tim,F aco}(1)
where eachF∗∈R T×d denotes a time-frequency feature ma-
trix. For privacy preservation, we explicitly discardF con and
retain only the non-content features{F pro,F tim,F aco}. These
features are then processed through the multi-granularity fea-
ture obfuscation module, which masks fine-grained linguistic
traces while preserving speaker-discriminative context. The ob-
fuscated features are concatenated and fed into ECAPA-TDNN
[11] to extract robust speaker embeddings. Additionally, recon-
structed speech is generated from these obfuscated representa-
tions to evaluate both metric-based and perceptual privacy.
To ensure robust privacy without compromising speaker-
specific information, we adopt FAcodec, which utilizes Factor-
ized Vector Quantization (FVQ) to explicitly decompose speech
into four independent latent subspaces. This design offers
three advantages for privacy-preserving SV . First, the factor-
ized structure enables selective removal ofF con, a primary car-
rier of linguistic information, without degrading other speaker-
related features. Second, the framework minimizes cross-
contamination between subspaces by enforcing independence
through information bottlenecks [12] and adversarial training
[10]. Finally, by transmitting only non-semantic representa-
tions, any reconstruction from the transmitted data is necessar-
ily conditioned on semantic-removed features rather than the
original content-bearing information, supporting privacy.
However, empirical analysis reveals a critical limitation:
while removingF con yields high error rates in ASR systems,
the residual prosody feature (F pro) retains rhythmic and into-
national patterns sufficient for human listeners to perceptually
identify the spoken content. This gap between metric-based and
perceptual privacy motivates the additional obfuscation mecha-
nisms detailed in the following subsection.
2.2. Multi-Granularity Feature Obfuscation
Although FAcodec explicitly disentangles speech into indepen-
dent subspaces, the prosody feature (F pro) can retain residual
linguistic cues. Reconstructed speech from{F pro,F tim,F aco}
without content may remain partially intelligible to human lis-
teners despite high WER/CER under ASR evaluation. To bridge
this gap between metric-based and perceptual privacy, we pro-
pose a Hierarchical Transformation Strategy that aims to reduce
linguistic recoverability at both levels. The strategy proceeds
from global to local granularity: GTA substantially reduces per-
ceptual intelligibility by collapsing temporal structure, while
LTA and LTP progressively reintroduce speaker-discriminative
information without restoring linguistic intelligibility.

perceptual robustness beyond machine transcription.

4.3. Perceptual Privacy Analysis
To validate that our multi-granularity approach improves per-
ceptual privacy beyond metric-based evaluation, we pro-
vide reconstructed audio samples in the supplementary mate-
rial. We compare three conditions: (i) reconstruction from
{Fpro,F tim,F aco}without obfuscation, (ii) reconstruction with
GTA-only obfuscation, and (iii) reconstruction with our full
multi-granularity obfuscation (GTA+LTA+LTP). While condi-
tion (i) produces speech that remains perceptually intelligible
despite high WER, conditions (ii) and (iii) yield reconstruc-
tions whose linguistic content is not reliably recoverable for
human listeners, as qualitatively evidenced by the supplemen-
tary audio samples. Notably, condition (iii) achieves SV perfor-
mance nearly identical to condition (i), demonstrating that our
approach preserves speaker identity while substantially reduc-
ing perceptual linguistic information.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we presented a privacy-preserving SV frame-
work designed to simultaneously protect both linguistic con-
tent and speaker biometric information. The proposed ap-
proach combines Explicit Feature Disentanglement with a
Multi-Granularity Feature Obfuscation strategy, effectively mit-
igating the inherent trade-off between privacy preservation and
SV performance observed in prior work. Experimental results
demonstrate that the framework substantially reduces linguistic
recoverability under ASR-based and reconstruction-based eval-
uations, while preserving the speaker-discriminative character-
istics required for reliable verification performance. These find-
ings suggest that the proposed framework can serve as a practi-
cal privacy-preserving SV approach for authentication-oriented
applications, such as financial authentication systems and voice-
based access.
