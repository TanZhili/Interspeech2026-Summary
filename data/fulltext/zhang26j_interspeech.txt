vocal fold model

1 Institute of Acoustics and Speech Communication, TU Dresden, Germany

Abstract
This paper presents a geometric model of the vocal folds
for more natural-sounding articulatory speech synthesis. In
contrast to similar models, we propose a biomechanically-
motivated more gradual opening and closing of the glottis dur-
ing the phases of partial contact of the vocal folds. This was
combined with a mechanism to skew the glottal area waveform
to the left or right. The model was implemented in the articula-
tory speech synthesizer and compared against a similar model
without the improved contacting and de-contacting mechanisms
in a perception experiment with synthetic sentences for a range
of pre-phonatory model settings. In an A/B comparison, the
synthetic sentences with the new model were preferred over the
same sentences with the previous model in 58.1 % of the com-
parisons. In addition, the mean opinion scores for the quality on
a five-point scale were 3.02 and 2.68 for the new and previous
models, respectively.
Index Terms: articulatory speech synthesis, phonation
1. Introduction
Neural speech synthesizers are able to synthesize speech that is
often indistinguishable from human speech [1]. However, while
they work well for high-resource languages and allow high-
level control of speaker characteristics and emotions [2, 3], they
are less effective for low-resource languages or when precise
control of low-level articulatory and acoustic features is needed
(e.g. in basic speech research [4, 5]). Articulatory speech syn-
thesis, which is based on simulation models of the vocal tract
[6, 7], the vocal folds [8, 9], acoustic wave propagation [10, 11],
and articulatory control [12, 13], does allow the individual con-
trol of all relevant parameters of the speech production process
and is a potential candidate for TTS systems for low-resource
languages. However, speech generated with articulatory syn-
thesizers does not yet sound fully natural [14]. A particularly
important factor for their naturalness is the voice source model
[15].
This paper presents a new voice source model that was de-
signed for high-quality articulatory synthesis. It is derived from
the geometric vocal fold model by Titze [16] and is shown in
Figure 1. In Titze’s model, only the right vocal fold is mod-
eled, while the left vocal fold moves in a mirror image. The
upper and lower edges of the vocal fold are assumed to oscil-
late sinusoidally like a vibrating string (fixed at the anterior and
posterior ends) in its fundamental mode. In the coronal plane,
the glottis shape is assumed to be trapezoidal with straight lines
connecting the upper and lower vocal fold edges. For glottal
area computation, all negative values for lateral displacement,
which indicate contact between the left and right vocal folds,
are set to zero (see Sec. 2).

This basic model was later extended by a posterior chink to
model “glottal leakage” [17, 18], by introducing rounded (in-
stead of flat) medial surfaces of the vocal folds [8], and by al-
lowing the generation of skewed glottal area pulses [19], which
are commonly observed in human phonation [20, 21, 22].
A common simplification of all these model variants is that
the edges of the vocal folds perform a sinusoidal oscillation,
similar to a freely vibrating string, which is clipped at the mid-
sagittal plane. This neglects what happens during the short but
important contacting and de-contacting phases during the oscil-
lation cycle [23]. In the contacting phase, the tissue deforms
due to the (partial) vocal fold collision and leads to a restor-
ing force that decelerates their further medial movement. In
addition, when the vocal folds separate from each other during
glottal opening, the driving force has to overcome the adhesive
force between them, which should lead to an initially reduced
velocity of the lateral movement.
The vocal fold model proposed here includes these effects
during the contacting and de-contacting phases, and allows
skewed glottal area pulses to be generated. In a perception ex-
periment with synthetic sentences, this model is then compared
against a similar previous model [19], which does not include
the “soft” contacting and de-contacting, and which has a differ-
ent mechanism to generate skewed glottal area pulses. In the
following, the reference model [19] is called the “2019 model”.

3. Perceptual Evaluation
3.1. Stimulus Generation
Both the proposed vocal fold model (NEW) and the 2019
baseline model (OLD) were implemented in the articula-
tory speech synthesizer V ocalTractLab 2.4 (VTL) (www.
vocaltractlab.de, [26, 27, 28]). At the acoustic level,
the synthesizer represents the vocal tract as a branched tube
system composed of concatenated short cylindrical tube sec-
tions [29]. The glottis is modeled by two adjacent tube sec-
tions with time-varying areasA 1(t)andA 2(t)according to
Eq. (6), each having a length ofT/2, whereTdenotes the vo-
cal fold thickness (Eq. (1)). Based on these tube sections, glottal
flow is computed as in [30], including configuration-dependent
flow separation [31]. Speech stimuli were generated by copy
synthesis of nine sentences using the default male speaker
model “JD2”, preserving original phone durations andf 0 con-
tours (as shown in:https://youtu.be/J_n01Xe4aME).
Speaker-specific vocal fold reference parameters wereL 0 =
1.6cm,T 0 = 0.45cm,f nat = 120Hz andA chink = 0.02cm 2.
For modal phonation, stimuli were synthesized for all combina-
tions of the following four vocal fold model parameters:
• Lower rest displacementx rest,1∈{0.1,0.4,0.7}mm
• Upper rest displacementx rest,2∈{0.1,0.4,0.7}mm
• Phase lagϕ∈{40 ◦,70◦,100◦}
• Pulse shapes∈{−1,0,+1},s ′∈{−0.5,0,+0.5}
This resulted in 81 parameter combinations per sentence
and per model. Across nine sentences and two models, a to-
tal of 1,458 utterances were generated. Note thats(proposed
model) ands ′ (2019 model) both control pulse skewness but
have different corresponding range limits.
For the glottal configuration of consonants, the same phase
lag and pulse-shape settings were used, while abduction param-
eters were adjusted as required. The double-pulsing parameter
of the 2019 model was fixed to zero to ensure comparability,
as this feature is not available in the proposed model. Flutter
(quasi-randomf 0 fluctuation) was set to 25% for all stimuli in
both models. All stimuli are available in the supplemental mate-
rial (https://www.vocaltractlab.de/index.php?
page=birkholz-supplements).
3.2. Experimental Design and Procedure
A total of 27 listeners (8 female, 19 male; age range 18–47
years) participated in the experiment. Twenty-two were native
German speakers; the remaining participants reported high pro-
ficiency in German. All participants had normal hearing.
To prevent listener fatigue while ensuring full coverage of
the parameter space, each participant was assigned only one
subset of stimuli. For each glottis model, the complete set of
729 stimuli (9 sentences × 81 parameter combinations) was
evenly partitioned into nine subsets according to the parame-
ter combinations, with each subset containing 81 stimuli and
representing all nine sentences equally (9 stimuli per sentence).

For evaluation, the corresponding subsets of the NEW and OLD
models were combined, yielding 162 stimuli per participant.
The nine subsets together constituted one complete round. With
27 participants in total, three complete rounds were obtained.
Each participant performed two perceptual tasks: an AB
preference test comparing the OLD (2019 model) and NEW
models, and a MOS test for the evaluation of naturalness. In
the AB task, each trial presented a pair of stimuli synthesized
with identical sentence and parameter settings but generated us-
ing either the NEW or the OLD vocal fold model. Presenta-
tion order within each pair was randomized, and participants
performed a forced-choice judgment indicating which stimulus
they preferred.
In the MOS task, stimuli were presented individually in
fully randomized order. Listeners rated each sample on a five-
point scale ranging from 1 (very unnatural) to 5 (very natural).
Participants were instructed to evaluate each stimulus indepen-
dently and to avoid direct comparisons between samples. Both
tasks were conducted in a quiet environment using headphones
with calibrated playback levels.

4. Results and Conclusions
From the descriptive statistics (Figure 4), the NEW model
demonstrates an overall advantage in MOS ratings. Figure 4(b)
illustrates substantial inter-participant variability in rating be-
havior, confirming the necessity of accounting for random lis-
tener effects through the linear mixed-effects model. The LMM
revealed a significant main effect of model at the baseline con-
figuration (β1 = 0.911,p<.001), indicating higher perceived
naturalness for the NEW model. Although most interaction co-
efficients were negative, model-based predictions derived from
Eq. (15) showed that the NEW model achieved higher MOS
ratings than the OLD model in 75.3% of the 81 parameter com-
binations. Consistent with the LMM results, the binomial test
for the AB preference task indicated that the proportion of pref-
erences for the NEW model (58.1%, 95% CI [56.0%, 60.2%],
p < .001) was significantly above chance level. Additional
plots illustrating MOS ratings across individual parameter com-
binations are provided in the supplemental material.
Overall, the results indicate that the proposed vocal fold
model yields a statistically significant overall improvement in
perceived naturalness compared to the 2019 baseline model.
