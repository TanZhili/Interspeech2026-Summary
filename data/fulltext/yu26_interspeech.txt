Abstract
Large language models are increasingly applied to de-
pression assessment, yet it remains unclear whether few-shot
learning provides effective supervision for depression sever-
ity prediction. We conduct a study on PHQ-8 prediction,
evaluating five LLMs across 0–10-shot settings and introduc-
ing a contradictory-label intervention to test label dependence.
Across models, increasing shot count yields limited and non-
monotonic gains, and performance is largely insensitive to cor-
rupted demonstration labels, suggesting that few-shot examples
may serve more as structural cues than effective supervision. In
contrast, replacing full transcripts with symptom-focused ex-
cerpts consistently improves performance while reducing to-
ken usage by approximately 80% per session. Prediction aver-
aging further provides stable gains without modifying prompt
design. Our findings indicate that context relevance matters
more than demonstration quantity, offering guidance for cost-
effective clinical deployment.
Index Terms: Depression severity prediction, in-context learn-
ing, shot scaling, symptom-focused excerpt
1. Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong
performance across a range of language understanding and
reasoning tasks. Through zero-shot and few-shot prompt-
ing—implemented via In-Context Learning (ICL) without pa-
rameter updates—these models have been applied to mental
health settings, including depression screening, suicide risk de-
tection, and psychiatric symptom analysis [1,2]. Recent studies
report competitive results in depression-related benchmarks us-
ing conversational transcripts or social media text [3–6]. Mul-
tilingual evaluations further show variability across languages
and populations [7].
An open question concerns how performance changes as
the number of in-context demonstrations increases. In studies
of ICL for classification tasks such as sentiment analysis, in-
creasing demonstration count does not consistently yield im-
provements, and demonstrations may function primarily as for-
matting cues rather than as supervision signals [8, 9].
Whether similar behavior occurs in depression severity pre-
diction remains underexplored. Existing work in depression
recognition typically reports zero-shot or few-shot performance
without analyzing scaling trends [3, 7, 10]. In addition, to the
best of our knowledge, no prior work has examined whether
incorrect demonstration labels affect prediction performance in
the mental health domain, which is crucial for understanding the
role of ground-truth labels in ICL-based clinical assessment.
Beyond demonstration quantity, prompt content itself may
influence prediction performance. Interview transcripts contain
substantial dialogue unrelated to depression symptoms, poten-
tially diluting task-relevant information while increasing token
usage. Yet no prior work evaluates whether using symptom-
focused excerpts can improve prediction performance.
In this work, we conduct a controlled study of few-shot
ICL for Patient Health Questionnaire-8 (PHQ-8) [11] severity
prediction using the Extended Distress Analysis Interview Cor-
pus (E-DAIC) [12]. We evaluate five LLMs across shot settings
(0–10), introduce a contradictory-label test to examine label de-
pendence, and compare full-session transcripts with GPT-5.1-
extracted symptom-focused excerpts [13]. We further evaluate
prediction averaging across multiple independent runs to exam-
ine its effect on performance.
Our findings show that increasing in-context demonstra-
tions yields limited performance gains, whereas symptom-
focused excerpts substantially improve prediction performance,
particularly for weaker models, while reducing token usage.
These results indicate that context relevance has a greater im-
pact on PHQ-8 prediction than demonstration quantity.
2. Method and Experimental Setup
2.1. Data and Transcript Preparation
Fig. 1 summarizes the overall pipeline. In Stage 1, we introduce
the dataset and construct the inputs used in subsequent experi-
ments.
Dataset.We use the E-DAIC [12, 14] as the source dataset.
E-DAIC contains 275 semi-clinical interviews, each lasting
approximately 20 minutes, with fixed train/dev/test splits of
163/56/56 sessions. Each sample is a dialogue session between
a participant and the virtual interviewer, paired with a PHQ-8
score from 0 to 24 for depression severity prediction. The inter-
views include both Wizard-of-Oz (WoZ) and fully autonomous
AI-driven sessions, while the test set contains only AI-based
interviews. E-DAIC is a valuable and widely adopted dataset
for depression assessment; however, audio quality varies across
speakers, which may restrict generalizability.
Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR).As official tran-
scripts are generated by a prior-generation Google Cloud
Speech-to-Text system and often contain only participant re-
sponses rather than interviewer questions [12, 14], we re-
transcribe all recordings using Whisper-Large-V3 [15] to obtain
accurate and complete dialogue. The resulting transcripts serve
as the primary textual input for subsequent experiments.
Raw transcript.We define the ASR-generated full-session
transcript as the raw transcript. This text preserves the com-
plete conversational content of the interview and serves as the
baseline input for subsequent prompting experiments.

2.2. Prompt Engineering and In-Context Learning Setup
Prompting strategy.Stage 2 defines the prompt design for
PHQ-8 prediction, illustrated in Fig. 2. Each prompt consists of
(i) a task definition specifying the PHQ-8 scoring range and cat-
egories, (ii) optional in-context demonstration pairs, (iii) a test
transcript, and (iv) a fixed output format requiring a numerical
score and severity label.
In the zero-shot setting, only the task definition, test tran-
script and output format are provided, and the model is directly
instructed to estimate the PHQ-8 score. In the few-shot setting,
we insertkdemonstration examples (withk∈{2,4,6,8,10})
between the task description and the test input, with each ex-

Demonstrations are drawn from the same transcript source (raw

Each transcript is relatively long (approximately 1,110
words on average), leading to substantial prompt expansion as
the number of demonstrations increases. Under the 10-shot con-
figuration, the prompt exceeds 15,000 tokens in many cases,
substantially increasing computational cost and latency. Given
the combined considerations of computational efficiency and
diminishing empirical returns observed in preliminary experi-
ments, we restrict the maximum shot count to 10.
Contradictory-label design.To test whether models ac-
tually use the provided demonstration labels, we design a
contradictory-label condition. Specifically, we replace the gold
PHQ-8 score in each demonstration with a fixed incorrect value
based on depression status: for non-depressed cases (e.g., gold
score 3), we assign a depressed-level fake score of 15; for de-
pressed cases (e.g., gold score 18), we assign a non-depressed
fake score of 5. All other content in the demonstrations (the
transcript text and formatting) is kept unchanged. If models rely
on demonstration labels as supervision, performance should de-
grade under this intervention; otherwise, results should remain
stable.
2.3. Model Inference and Experimental Settings
We evaluate five LLMs: GPT-4o-mini, GPT-5.1, DeepSeek-
Chat, DeepSeek-Reasoner, and Llama-3.1-8B. For each model
and each experimental condition (shot count, label setting, and
transcript type), we conduct five independent trials and report
the Mean and Standard Deviation (SD) of the evaluation met-
rics.
In the few-shot setting, demonstration examples are ran-
domly sampled with seed 42 from the training split at each trial.
Specifically, for a given shot countk, we randomly selectk
training samples as demonstrations for each run. Because sam-
pling is repeated independently across the five trials, a test sam-
ple is exposed to different sets of demonstrations across runs.
For example, under the 10-shot condition with five trials, a test
sample is evaluated against up to 50 distinct demonstration in-
stances in total , reducing the impact of specific demonstration

2.4. Evaluation Metrics
We report the Mean and SD of the Concordance Correlation Co-
efficient (CCC), which is a standard metric in depression sever-
ity prediction and is used in the Audio/Visual Emotion Chal-
lenge (A VEC) 2019 [14]. To compare with existing works,
we also report Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean
Squared Error (RMSE) in Section 3.4 .
3. Results and Analysis
3.1. Shot Scaling Quickly Stops Improving Performance
This subsection examines whether adding demonstrations im-
proves CCC on the dev set across 0–10 shots (Fig. 3). Over-
all trends are largely flat: GPT-5.1 stays high (approximately
0.67–0.70) and DeepSeek-Reasoner remains steady around the
low-to-mid 0.60s, while GPT-4o-mini fluctuates narrowly near
0.46–0.49. DeepSeek-Chat shows the only clear early gain, ris-
ing from a low zero-shot CCC (around 0.38) to roughly 0.55
at 2 shots, then leveling off. Llama-3.1-8B is consistently the
lowest (roughly 0.22–0.30) with no monotonic improvement.
These patterns indicate that additional demonstrations do
not systematically improve performance. All models show a
small gain from 0 to 2 shots, after which improvements largely
plateau. For DeepSeek-Chat, benefits are concentrated in the
first few examples. Overall, increasing shot count yields limited
returns relative to added prompt length and cost.
3.2. Contradictory Labels Cause Limited Degradation for
Most Models
We evaluate label dependence by replacing gold demonstration
labels with contradictory labels while keeping all other prompt
components unchanged. Fig. 4 reports∆CCC, computed as the
difference between the mean CCC over five independent runs
under contradictory labels and the mean CCC over five inde-
pendent runs under gold labels.
The results indicate heterogeneous label utilization. GPT-
4o-mini and GPT-5.1 exhibit minimal sensitivity:∆CCC re-
mains close to zero across shot counts, indicating that incor-
rect demonstration labels have little impact on their predictions.
DeepSeek-Chat shows moderate degradation at higher shot
counts (up to 13.6% at 10 shots), while DeepSeek-Reasoner
is impacted more (up to 17.1% at 6 shots), suggesting par-
tial reliance on demonstration labels. Notably, we observe a
rough trend: performance degradation tends to increase with

3.3. Symptom-Focused Excerpts Improve Performance
with Fewer Tokens
The preceding analyses suggest that neither increasing shot
count nor ensuring label correctness consistently strengthens
supervision effects. This raises a complementary question: be-
yond demonstrations, does the quality (relevance) of the input
transcript itself constrain performance?
Full-session transcripts contain substantial conversational
content that is not directly aligned with PHQ-8 symptom cri-
teria. Such unrelated dialogue (e.g., small talk about the
weather) may dilute clinically relevant signals and increase
prompt length. To test whether contextual relevance matters, we
replace full ASR transcripts with GPT-5.1-extracted symptom-
focused excerpts and evaluate the resulting CCC. GPT-5.1 is
used for extraction because its stronger PHQ-8 prediction per-
formance in earlier experiments suggests higher-quality symp-
tom filtering.
Fig. 5 reports the CCC improvement across different shot
settings. Excerpt prompting consistently improves performance
for all models. GPT-4o-mini gains approximately +0.20 to
+0.24 CCC across shots; DeepSeek-Chat improves by +0.13
to +0.34; and Llama-3.1-8B shows the largest improvements
(+0.26 to +0.41). GPT-5.1 exhibits smaller gains (+0.02 to
+0.04), while DeepSeek-Reasoner shows modest improvements
(+0.03 to +0.10). No model benefits from the raw full transcript
over the excerpt.
These results indicate that filtering out transcript content
unrelated to symptom improve depression severity prediction.
This may be because signal density increases and distract-
ing context decreases. Notably, improvements are larger for
models with weaker performance (LLama, DeepSeek-Chat and
Deepseek-Reasoner), suggesting that using excerpts compen-
sates for weaker robustness to irrelevant conversational context.

excerpt prompting, increasing shot count still does not produce

interpretability of final LLM prediction.
3.4. Prediction A veraging Improves Performance
Each LLM is evaluated five times for every shot setting. Each
time, thekdemonstration examples are randomly sampled
from the training set, resulting in different prompts across runs.
This variability in demonstration selection introduces prediction
variance and may produce outlier predictions.
To mitigate this effect and further obtain more robust pre-
dictions, we apply prediction averaging. Specifically, for each
test sample we average the five predicted PHQ-8 scores across
runs and compute the CCC using the averaged predictions. This
procedure approximates the expected severity prediction perfor-
mance of each model under stochastic demonstration sampling.

4. Conclusion
This paper presented a controlled investigation of few-shot in-
context learning for PHQ-8 depression severity prediction on
E-DAIC. Rather than focusing solely on accuracy, we exam-
ined whether increasing demonstrations strengthens depression
severity. Across five LLMs, shot scaling produced limited and
non-monotonic improvements, and performance was largely in-
sensitive to contradictory demonstration labels. In contrast,
transcript scope had a stronger and more consistent impact:
symptom-focused excerpts improved performance while sub-
stantially reducing token usage. Prediction averaging further
yielded stable gains turning LLMs’ stochasticity to strength.
Together, these results indicate that context relevance plays a
more decisive role than demonstration quantity in depression
severity prediction from interview dialogues.
Limitations and future workOur study is limited to a single
benchmark (E-DAIC) and one clinical scale (PHQ-8), and gen-
eralization to other populations, languages, or psychiatric con-
structs requires further validation. Performance is additionally
constrained by ASR transcript quality. Future work should ex-
tend this analysis to cross-dataset settings, multilingual scenar-
ios, and multimodal systems to better understand how different
information sources contribute to clinical LLM reliability.

5. Ethical Considerations
This study uses the publicly available E-DAIC dataset, which
was collected under appropriate ethical approval and is widely
used for depression severity research.
We employed both locally deployed open-source Llama-
3.1-8B and commercial API-based models. For API-based
models, we used configurations that do not permit data reten-
tion or secondary use of submitted content, according to the
providers’ stated policies. No identifying information was in-
tentionally added to prompts beyond the dataset content.
Finally, our experiments are conducted solely for research
purposes. The presented models are not intended for clinical
diagnosis or real-world medical decision-making. Any deploy-
ment in healthcare settings would require rigorous clinical vali-
dation and regulatory compliance.
