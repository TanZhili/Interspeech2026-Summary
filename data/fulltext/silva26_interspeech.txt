Abstract
Neuro-guided speaker extraction uses brain signals captured
by EEG to extract attended speech from a multi-speaker mix-
ture. Most studies address only 2-speaker scenarios. While
real-world situations involve multiple concurrent talkers, multi-
speaker neuro-guided extraction remains largely unexplored.
We present NeuroMultiSpEx, a neuro-guided speaker extraction
system for 4-speaker scenarios using mobile ear-EEG. Our ap-
proach integrates temporal synchronization cues from an EEG-
based envelope encoder with speaker identity from an EEG-
based speaker encoder through a gated fusion mechanism that
adaptively weights “when” versus “who” guidance. Trained
end-to-end, NeuroMultiSpEx demonstrates effective speaker
extraction on an ear-EEG dataset and substantially outperforms
competing systems across all evaluation criteria. To our knowl-
edge, this is the first study on neuro-guided multi-speaker ex-
traction using wearable ear-EEG, advancing toward practical
brain-informed hearing aids.

1. Introduction
The human ability to focus on one speaker while ignoring com-
peting talkers, known as selective auditory attention, is essen-
tial for communication in complex auditory environments, the
“cocktail party problem” [1]. This selective attention is re-
flected in neural activity measured via EEG, where attended
speech induces stronger cortical responses than unattended
speech [2, 3], motivating neuro-guided speaker extraction that
leverages EEG as a reference to isolate attended speech.
Traditional speaker extraction relies on auxiliary refer-
ences, such as speaker enrollment [4] or visual and audio-visual
cues [5, 6]. However, these cues are often unavailable or unre-
liable in real-world scenarios. EEG-based approaches provide
an always-available neural reference reflecting attentional fo-
cus, making them promising for wearable hearing aids. Recent
neuro-guided methods have explored various EEG-derived ref-
erence strategies, from direct envelope reconstruction [7] and
learned speaker embeddings [8, 9] to dual-task training combin-
ing temporal synchronization with waveform extraction [10],
but all remain limited to binary (2-speaker) scenarios.
Despite recent advances, a critical gap remains: existing
methods have not been validated beyond two concurrent speak-
ers, whereas real-world cocktail parties typically involve three
or more talkers. The scalability to multi-speaker environments
remains largely unexplored due to several factors. Multi-class

tection [11], where the system has a higher chance to take
an interfering speaker as the target by mistake despite correct
AAD [12], a problem exacerbated with more competitors. Fur-
thermore, prior methods use either envelope reconstruction (im-
plicit “when” cues) or simple EEG embeddings, but have not
systematically integrated explicit speaker identity with tempo-

EEG (64+ channels) [8, 10], impractical for deployment; ear-
EEG (cEEGrid, 20 channels) offers wearability but requires
novel solutions [13].
We address these gaps with three contributions. First, we
show that neuro-guided speaker extraction can be effectively
scaled to multi-speaker (4-speaker) scenarios, a setting where
increased permutation ambiguity renders single-cue guidance
insufficient, making this the first such system beyond the binary
limitation of all prior methods. Second, we demonstrate that
jointly exploiting temporal synchronization and explicit speaker
identity from EEG, combined through learned adaptive gating,
provides richer neural guidance than either cue alone, unlike
prior dual-task methods that use envelope reconstruction solely
as an auxiliary training objective [10], our dual-encoder archi-
tecture actively conditions the extraction network on both com-
plementary cue types. Third, we validate that wearable ear-EEG
(cEEGrid, 20 channels) carries sufficient neural information for
both temporal tracking and identity decoding in 4-speaker sce-
narios, advancing beyond high-density scalp-EEG (64+ chan-
nels) [8, 9, 10] toward practical deployment.
2. Related Work
This paper is built on the success of some previous studies on
neuro-guided speaker extraction in 2-speaker scenarios and the
recent advances in neural basis auditory attention detection.
2.1. Neuro-Guided Speaker Extraction
The integration of neural signals for guided speaker extraction
has evolved through several paradigms.
BISS [7] introduced speech envelope reconstruction from
EEG as reference cues for source separation, establishing that
EEG contains sufficient information about attended speech,
though relying on subject-specific linear decoders. Neuro-
Heed [8] proposed shifting to learned speaker-specific embed-
dings via contrastive training, while NeuroSpEx [9] introduced
using cross-modal attention between speech and EEG repre-
sentations. More recently, NeuroSpEx+ [10] combined dual-
task training (waveform extraction and envelope reconstruc-
tion), achieving strong results on the KULeuven dataset [14].
However, all these methods operate exclusively on 2-speaker

2.2. Auditory Attention Detection
AAD methods have progressed from linear temporal response
functions [15] through spatiotemporal attention (STAnet [16])
and cross-hemispheric attention (XAnet [17]) to graph-based
approaches. XAGnet [11] combines graph convolutional net-
works (intra-ear dependencies) with cross-attention (inter-ear
dependencies) for 4-speaker AAD using ear-EEG, demonstrat-
ing that effective classification is achievable with minimal elec-
trodes when spatial topology is exploited [11]. These advances
in AAD open a complementary avenue for neuro-guided ex-
traction: beyond temporal envelope tracking, AAD can provide
explicit speaker identity, directly resolving the permutation am-
biguity inherent in blind source separation [18]. Temporal cues
from envelope reconstruction offer fine-grained “when” guid-
ance but degrade under high speaker overlap [19], whereas iden-
tity cues from AAD provide robust “who” disambiguation but
lack frame-level temporal resolution. However, existing neuro-
guided methods rely on either temporal references [7, 9, 10]
or cascaded attention detection [12] in isolation, and no prior
work has systematically fused both cue types for multi-speaker
extraction.
3. Methodology
NeuroMultiSpEx (Fig. 1) takes a 4-speaker audio mixture and
20-channel ear-EEG as input and outputs the attended speaker’s
waveform. The architecture comprises an EEG-Envelope En-
coder (temporal cues), an EEG-Speaker Encoder (speaker iden-
tity), a Gated Fusion module, and a Target Speaker Extraction
Network.
3.1. EEG-Envelope Encoder
The EEG-Envelope Encoder captures the fine-grained tempo-
ral dynamics of the neural response to the attended speech
stimulus. It takes as input the 20-channel ear-EEG signal,
E∈R 20×T , recorded from the bilateral cEEGrid arrays.
The encoder processes the EEG through a hierarchical ar-
chitecture. The raw signal is first passed through a Pre-Conv
layer (1×3kernel) for initial feature extraction and channel
mixing. The core consists ofm=4stacked blocks, each com-
prising two complementary sub-layers: a Self-Attention (SA)
unit (2-head) that models long-range global temporal depen-
dencies, and a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) unit
with dilated depth-wise separable convolutions (dilation factors
{1,2,4,8}) that captures local temporal variations. Together,
these sub-layers enable the encoder to build hierarchical repre-
sentations sensitive to fine-grained patterns and broader tempo-
ral context.
The resulting latent featuresH env serve a dual purpose.
First, an Envelope Decoder, comprising a series of convolu-
tional layers, reconstructs the one-dimensional speech envelope
of the attended speaker fromH env, supervised by a Pearson
Correlation Coefficient (PCC) loss that enforces temporal align-
ment with the target speech. Second,H env is forwarded to
the Gated Fusion module as the temporal synchronization refer-
ence, providing the extraction network with information about
whenthe target speaker is vocally active.
3.2. EEG-Speaker Encoder
A critical challenge in multi-speaker extraction is the risk of ex-
tracting the wrong speaker, particularly when multiple talkers
share similar acoustic properties. To mitigate this, we introduce
an EEG-Speaker Encoder that explicitly models speaker iden-
tity from the neural signal, providing the extraction network
with direct “who” disambiguation among competing speak-
ers. The encoder builds on a graph-attention architecture [11],
which has demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on 4-class
auditory attention detection using ear-EEG.
The architecture exploits the bilateral cEEGrid topology:
the 10 channels from each ear are processed by separate Graph
Convolutional Network (GCN) branches that learn intra-ear
spatial dependencies by operating on a graph defined by the
physical electrode layout, capturing functional relationships be-
tween neighboring sensors. The left and right-ear represen-
tations are then integrated by a 2-head Cross-Attention (CA)
block, enabling inter-hemispheric information exchange criti-
cal for spatial attention and sound localization [20]. This de-
sign mirrors the neural basis of binaural hearing, where bilateral
processing and interhemispheric connectivity underpin audi-
tory spatial attention [21], allowing the model to learn speaker-
discriminative features grounded in spatial cues.
The learned features are utilized in two branches. A Fully
Connected (FC) classification head produces a 4-way speaker
probability distribution supervised by Cross-Entropy loss, en-
suring discriminative identity features. While these classifica-
tion features are effective for the auxiliary AAD task, they rep-
resent a static, time-aggregated summary of speaker identity.
For the dynamic speech extraction task, these features must be
projected into a temporally resolved representation. A novel
Multi-Head Attention (MHA) Adapter fulfills this role by trans-
forming the identity representation into a time-aligned sequence
Hspk, compatible with the fusion module and matching the
temporal resolution ofH env. The MHA mechanism enables
the model to attend to different representational subspaces of
the speaker identity, capturing diverse discriminative cues [22].
3.3. Gated Fusion Mechanism
In realistic acoustic environments, the reliability of different
neural cues can vary significantly depending on the context.
For instance, envelope tracking may be robust during isolated
speech but degrades with high temporal overlap [19], where
speaker identity cues remain more reliable. To address this,
we employ a Gated Fusion module [23] that learns context-
dependent weighting of the two complementary pathways. The
module computes a soft, element-wise gate:
g=σ(W g[Henv;H spk] +b g)(1)
whereσis the sigmoid function,[·;·]denotes concatenation
along the feature dimension, andW g,b g are learnable parame-
ters. The fused reference is then obtained as:
HRef =g⊙H env + (1−g)⊙H spk (2)
where⊙denotes element-wise multiplication. This soft gating
adaptively emphasizes “when” information when the envelope
is distinct and “who” information during periods of high tem-
poral overlap, yielding a context-aware guidance signal for the
extraction network.

3.4. Target Speaker Extraction Network
The final component of NeuroMultiSpEx is the Target Speaker
Extraction Network, which isolates the clean speech waveform
of the attended speaker from the multi-talker mixture. This net-
work is based on the Conv-TasNet [24] architecture, a widely
adopted time-domain speech separation model, which we mod-
ify to accept external neural conditioning.
The process begins with a Speech Encoder [24], a 1-D con-
volutional layer that transforms the raw audio mixture wave-
form into a high-dimensional latent representationX. The core
separation logic is implemented as a stack ofn= 4Speaker
Extractor blocks. Unlike the original Conv-TasNet, which re-
lies solely on the audio input, our extractor blocks are explicitly
conditioned on the fused neural reference cueH Ref via the CA
mechanism, where both representations share the same tempo-
ral resolution.
In this configuration, the Query (Q) is derived from the neu-
ral referenceH Ref , while the Key (K) and Value (V) are de-
rived from the speech mixture encodingX. This cross-modal
attention allows the neural reference to query and selectively
attend to regions in the speech encoding that are most relevant
to the attended speaker, effectively filtering out the interfering
talkers. The output of the CA layer is subsequently refined by
a stack of TCN layers that model long-range temporal context,
followed by a1×1convolutional layer that generates maskM.
Finally, the estimated maskMis applied element-wise to
the original mixture encoding to produce the masked represen-
tation of the target speaker (X⊙M). At the end of the network,
a Speech Decoder, adopted from [24], which is the transposed
convolution counterpart of the Speech Encoder, transforms the
masked latent representation back into the time-domain, yield-
ing the reconstructed target speech waveform ˆS.
3.5. Loss Function
The entire NeuroMultiSpEx system is trained end-to-end by
minimizing a joint multi-task loss function that optimizes all
three modules simultaneously:
Ltotal =L SI-SDR +λ 1LCE +λ 2LPCC (3)
The primary objective is the Scale-Invariant Signal-to-
Distortion Ratio (L SI-SDR), which measures the quality of the
extracted speech against the ground-truth target. The Cross-
Entropy loss (L CE) supervises the speaker classification head
in the EEG-Speaker Encoder, ensuring that the learned embed-
dings are discriminative for the four competing speakers. The
PCC loss (LPCC) supervises the envelope reconstruction branch
of the EEG-Envelope Encoder, ensuring that the temporal fea-
tures are phase-locked to the attended speech. The loss weights
λ1 andλ 2 balance the auxiliary objectives and are tuned on the
validation set, following the multi-task strategy of [10].
4. Experimental Setup
We validate NeuroMultiSpEx on the PKU Ear-EEG
Dataset [25], designed for auditory attention decoding in
4-speaker environments. The dataset comprises 16 native
Mandarin-speaking participants (ages 19–27), each completing
40 one-minute trials (640 total segments). Participants attended
to one of four concurrent speakers at±30 ◦ and±90 ◦, mixed at
0 dB SNR. EEG was recorded using the cEEGrid system [13]
(20 channels, 10 per ear), sampled at 500 Hz and downsampled
to 128 Hz after bandpass filtering (1–40 Hz) and common av-
erage referencing. For evaluation, we adopt a trial-independent
split: from each subject’s 40 trials, we randomly select 4 (10%)
for testing and 4 (10%) for validation, using the remaining
32 (80%) for training. This yields 64 test trials, 64 validation
trials, and 512 training trials across all subjects. The same
four stimulus speakers appear across all trials and splits, so
the evaluation is speaker-dependent but trial-independent.
Recordings are segmented into 4-second windows with a
1-second hop, balancing AAD accuracy and latency [26, 27],
consistent with prior neuro-guided work [9, 10].
NeuroMultiSpEx is evaluated using Scale-Invariant Signal-
to-Distortion Ratio improvement (SI-SDRi) as the primary met-
ric, measuring extraction quality relative to the unprocessed
mixture, supplemented by SDRi, Perceptual Evaluation of
Speech Quality (PESQ), and Short-Time Objective Intelligibil-
ity (STOI) for signal-level and perceptual assessment. The aux-

5. Results and Discussion
Table 1 presents speaker-extraction performance comparisons
between NeuroMultiSpEx and established baselines. Results
are averaged across 16 subjects in a trial-independent man-
ner, with approximately 3,584 evaluation windows from 64 test
trials. NeuroMultiSpEx achieves 9.613 dB SI-SDRi, outper-
forming the strongest baseline NeuroSpEx+ by 0.71 dB and the
foundational BISS method by 7.44 dB. The substantial gap be-
tween BISS and neural methods confirms that end-to-end learn-
ing substantially outperforms linear envelope reconstruction in
multi-speaker scenarios. Across perceptual metrics, NeuroMul-
tiSpEx attains PESQ of 2.08 and STOI of 0.708, represent-
ing improvements of 13% and 3.7% over NeuroSpEx+, respec-
tively. For AAD, NeuroMultiSpEx achieves 82.4% accuracy on
4-way classification (chance-level: 25%), substantially exceed-
ing NeuroHeed+’s 74.6%. The envelope reconstruction PCC
of 0.023 exceeds NeuroSpEx+’s 0.018, indicating stronger tem-
poral alignment with attended speech. The relatively low PCC
values compared to scalp-EEG literature [28] reflect the reduced
spatial resolution of ear-EEG (20 channels vs. 64+ channels),
though improvements remain consistent across methods.

6. Conclusion
We presented NeuroMultiSpEx, a neuro-guided speaker extrac-
tion system designed for multi-speaker (4-speaker) scenarios
using mobile ear-EEG. By integrating temporal synchroniza-
tion cues with explicit speaker-identity information through a
gated fusion mechanism, NeuroMultiSpEx outperforms exist-
ing neuro-guided baselines on the PKU ear-EEG dataset. Abla-
tion studies confirm that speaker identity guidance is critical in
multi-speaker settings and that learned adaptive fusion outper-
forms fixed concatenation. Our results demonstrate that neuro-
guided extraction can scale beyond binary scenarios to realistic
cocktail-party environments using wearable ear-EEG, advanc-
ing the pathway toward practical brain-informed hearing assis-
tance. Future work includes cross-subject generalization, val-
idation in reverberant environments, evaluation with hearing-
impaired users, and on-device deployment.
