Semi-Supervised Joint Separation and Diarization
for Multichannel Noisy Speech Mixtures

1 National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST), Japan

Abstract
This paper presents joint separation and diarization for noisy
speech mixtures without relying on isolated speech signals.
Neural full-rank spatial covariance analysis with speaker activ-
ity (FCASA) trains a joint model using only speech mixtures
and diarization annotations by incorporating statistical blind
source separation. However, because this method largely de-
pends on spatial information, it can be degraded by diffuse noise
and result in residual noise in the separated signals. To over-
come this limitation, we propose a semi-supervised method that
uses clean speech mixtures (e.g., meeting recordings) together
with environmental noise recordings. Our method trains noise
suppression in a supervised manner while preserving unsuper-
vised separation of speech sources. Both objectives are derived
from a unified generative model of multichannel mixtures. Ex-
perimental results show that our method improves separation
performance over neural FCASA in multiple metrics.
Index Terms: neural blind source separation, speech diariza-
tion, semi-supervised learning
1. Introduction
Speech separation and speaker diarization are fundamental
front-end technologies for speech processing systems such as
distant automatic speech recognition [1]. Speech separation
aims to extract the utterances of individual speakers from a mix-
ture, whereas speaker diarization aims to determine “who spoke
when.” These tasks exhibit a chicken-and-egg dependency: bet-
ter separation facilitates diarization, and accurate diarization
can in turn guide separation. Consequently, recent work has ex-
plored joint separation-and-diarization approaches to improve
the performance of both tasks [2, 3].
Because it is difficult to obtain paired mixture and isolated
source signals in real acoustic environments, joint separation
and diarization models are often trained in a fully supervised
manner using simulated datasets [3, 4, 5]. However, models
trained on simulated data often suffer from domain mismatch
and exhibit degraded performance on real recordings [6]. To
mitigate this issue, weakly supervised learning has been pro-
posed, combining unsupervised source separation with super-
vised diarization [7, 8].
In the multichannel setting, neural full-rank spatial covari-
ance analysis with speaker activity (FCASA) [9] has recently
been introduced. Neural FCASA incorporates speaker activ-
ity into a neural generative model based on the local Gaussian
model (LGM) [10], and has demonstrated strong performance
on a real meeting corpus [11]. Despite this success, its robust-
ness in noisy conditions is intrinsically limited because it re-
lies on spatial covariance modeling. In particular, performance
can degrade in the presence of non-stationary noise, interfer-
ing noise located close to the target speaker, or multiple noise
sources, where spatial covariance statistics may become biased.
A straightforward way to alleviate this limitation is to adopt
semi-supervised learning, where we assume access to noise-free
(clean) speech mixtures and noise-only recordings from differ-
ent environments. Training examples are then constructed by
adding a clean speech mixture to a noise signal. Explicit super-
vision on the noise component encourages noise suppression,
while separation of the speech components is learned through
an unsupervised objective. Compared with collecting all con-
stituent signals within a mixture (i.e., multiple isolated speech
signals and a noise signal), it is much easier to record clean
speech mixtures and noise-only signals separately. Still, unlike
fully simulated datasets, this setting better reflects real record-
ings because the speech mixing process itself is not simulated.
Motivated by the above considerations, this paper investi-
gates a semi-supervised learning approach for joint separation
and diarization of multichannel noisy mixtures (Fig. 1). Specifi-
cally, building on neural FCASA, we introduce additional prob-
abilistic objectives defined on clean speech mixtures, in ad-
dition to the original reconstruction objective on noisy mix-
tures. These objectives are designed to improve robustness un-
der noisy conditions by encouraging the estimated clean speech
mixture to match the given clean speech mixture, which can be
interpreted as maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) be-
tween them. Furthermore, we propose probabilistic criteria that
directly maximize (i) the log-likelihood of the observed clean
speech mixtures and (ii) the log-posterior probability of the cor-
responding latent variables given the clean speech mixtures.
The main contribution of this paper is a noise-robust frame-
work for joint separation and diarization using probabilistic ob-
jective functions based on clean speech mixtures, along with a
conventional objective function. Experiments on multichannel
noisy speech mixtures show that the proposed objectives yield
substantial improvements in separation performance, while also
providing a modest gain in diarization performance.

4. Experimental Evaluation
We evaluated our method on a simulated meeting dataset, as it
allows us to evaluate reference-aware separation metrics.
4.1. Dataset
We simulated a meeting dataset with the publicly avail-
able JSALT2020 Simulate toolkit 1, using LibriSpeech utter-
ances [17] and DEMAND noise [18]. We set the number of
channels toM= 4, the number of speakers to at most 4, and
the sampling rate to 16 kHz. The microphone array positions
are(0,0,0),(0,r,0),(d,r/2,0), and(d,3r/2,0)in meters,

for train, 17.10% for dev, and 16.23% for test. We generated
a synthetic meeting dataset and split it into train, dev, and test
sets with 2086, 204, and 198 files, totaling 86.06, 4.70, and 4.74
hours, respectively. During simulation, we added white noise at
an SNR of 30 dB and defined this mixture as the clean speech
mixture. We additionally added real environmental noise from
the DEMAND database, with the SNR uniformly sampled from
2 to 6 dB with respect to the clean speech mixtures. The DE-
MAND database contains six categories, and each category in-
cludes three environmental recordings of 300 s [18]. Following
the V oiceBank-DEMAND protocol [19], we split DEMAND
into training, validation, and test sets, using the same test split
as V oiceBank-DEMAND. For the training split, we used the
noise recordings in the V oiceBank-DEMAND training set, and
we additionally included NFIELD, NRIVER, and DW ASHING
while reserving PRESTO for the validation set to balance the
categories. For validation, we used PRESTO and the remain-
ing noise recordings not used for training or testing. Before
mixing, noise recordings from DEMAND were processed with
a 50 Hz high-pass filter. Finally, we dereverberated all mixture
signals using the multichannel weighted prediction error (WPE)
method [20] with 10 filter taps and a delay of 3 frames.

4.3. Evaluation Details
For each noisy mixture in the test set, we split it into 10-second
segments and evaluated diarization and separation performance
for each segment. The diarization performance was evaluated
in terms of the diarization error rate (DER) and source count-
ing accuracy (SCA). DER consists of false alarm (FA), missed
speech (MS), and speaker confusion (SC), and is computed as
their sum. The separation performance was evaluated in terms
of SDR [25], the short-time objective intelligibility (STOI) [26],
and the perceptual evaluation of speech quality (PESQ) [27],
UTokyo-SaruLab mean opinion score (UTMOS) [28], and the
deep noise suppression mean opinion score (DNSMOS) [29].
In inference, a median filter with a filter length of 11 frames
was applied to the estimated speaker activationsunt.
As the baselines, we evaluated the performance of neural
FCASA (Section 2.2). It was trained under the same experimen-
tal configuration as the proposed method, except the maximum
number of sourcesN+1was set to 5, assuming 4 speakers and 1
noise. We also evaluated FastMNMF2, which has been shown
to achieve high performance among BSS methods based on a
generative model using JD SCMs described in Eq. (3) [10, 12].
4.4. Experimental Results
The diarization and separation performance are summarized in
Table 1. The results show that training with any of the pro-
posed objective functions (P1,P2, andP3) outperformed neu-
ral FCASA (B2) and FastMNMF2 (B1) in terms of separation
metrics. Especially, the performance metrics such as SDR and
PESQ are greatly improved by using theL (snr) objective func-
tion (P1). Although the probabilistic objective functionsL (nll)
andL (misd) (P2andP3) also contribute to separation perfor-
mance, they additionally improve the diarization performance.
The model trained with the combination ofL (snr),L (nll), and
L(misd) (P7) improves all evaluation metrics compared with
neural FCASA (B2) and FastMNMF2 (B1).

5. Conclusion
We presented a semi-supervised noise-robust framework for
joint separation and diarization using paired noisy and clean
mixtures without isolated signals. We extended neural FCASA
with a conventional reconstruction error objective and proba-
bilistic objective functions based on the likelihood or poste-
rior of the clean speech mixture. The experimental results with
real noise recordings from the DEMAND database showed that
the proposed method outperformed neural FCASA in both di-
arization and separation by additionally using the clean speech
mixtures during training. Future work includes extending the
framework to handle moving speakers and noise sources.
