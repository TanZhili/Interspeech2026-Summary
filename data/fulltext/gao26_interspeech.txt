Abstract
Current speaker verification (SV) models, including Low-Rank
Adaptation (LoRA) variants, rely on static inference-time pa-
rameters and show limited robustness to non-stationary noise.
We propose NoiseLoRA-SV, a dynamic framework that gener-
ates instance-adaptive weights on-the-fly during inference. In-
stead of full end-to-end fine-tuning, it injects noise-conditioned
LoRA modules into a lightly fine-tuned backbone with mod-
erate additional parameter overhead. A Convolutional Recur-
rent Network (CRN) extracts hierarchical noise representations:
global embeddings drive a hypernetwork to generate LoRA
matrices, while local embeddings control a frame-level time-
varying gate. Optimized via InfoNCE-based contrastive dis-
tillation, NoiseLoRA-SV aligns noisy embeddings with clean
speaker representations. Evaluated on the V oxCeleb1 corpus
with MUSAN and unseen NonSpeech100 noises, NoiseLoRA-
SV consistently yields lower equal error rates (EERs) than static
baselines.
Index Terms: speaker verification, noise-conditioned LoRA,
dynamic adaptation, contrastive distillation
1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV) systems are critical for secure authen-
tication [1, 2]. However, complex background noise severely
degrades performance [3]. Conventional methods typically cas-
cade a separately trained speech enhancement (SE) frontend
with an SV backend [4, 5]. Unfortunately, independently op-
timized SE modules lack awareness of speaker-discriminative
tasks. They may introduce artifacts during noise suppression,
degrading embeddings and verification accuracy [6, 7, 8].
To mitigate these issues, recent studies have explored joint
SE-SV optimization [9, 10], adversarial noise disentangle-
ment [11], and advanced data augmentation [12]. Further-
more, large-scale Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) models have
demonstrated improved robustness for SV [13]. Alternatively,
Knowledge Distillation (KD) [14, 15] offers a lightweight solu-
tion by aligning the embedding manifold of noisy speech with
that of clean speech using a teacher network. Despite these ad-
vances, most SV backends still operate with static network pa-
rameters at inference time. Static models learn shared weights
to generalize across acoustic conditions, but this rigidity re-
stricts their representational capacity and limits instance-level

Meanwhile, deploying large robust models imposes pro-
hibitive computational demands on resource-constrained edge
devices [17]. Driven by these constraints, Parameter-Efficient
Fine-Tuning (PEFT) paradigms, such as Low-Rank Adapta-
tion (LoRA) [18], offer efficient adaptation to unseen envi-
ronments. Yet, standard LoRA employs fixed low-rank matri-
ces post-training, lacking responsiveness to noise fluctuations.
We therefore make adaptation modules noise-conditional and
instance-adaptive. Inspired by explicit noise modeling [19, 20],
we proposeNoiseLoRA-SV, a unified and dynamic noise-
conditioned adaptation framework for noise-robust SV . We em-
ploy a Convolutional Recurrent Network (CRN) to reconstruct
noise spectrograms and extract hierarchical noise representa-
tions for stationary and transient characteristics. A hypernet-
work uses the global noise feature to generate low-rank adap-
tation matrices for ECAPA-TDNN speaker encoder [21], effec-
tively re-parameterizing key layers for the specific input, while
local temporal features drive a time-varying gate to modulate
representations frame-by-frame. Finally, a contrastive distilla-
tion objective pulls the adapted noisy embeddings closer to their
clean anchors, ensuring high speaker discriminability.
In summary, the contributions of this paper are: (1) We pro-
pose a Hierarchical Noise-Conditioned LoRA framework that
leverages global noise representations to dynamically generate
adaptation weights via a hypernetwork, addressing the limita-
tions of static models in noisy environments. (2) We introduce
a time-varying gating mechanism driven by local multi-scale
noise embeddings, enabling frame-level modulation that effec-
tively handles non-stationary noise and transient interference.
(3) We design an integrated training paradigm combining ex-
plicit noise reconstruction and contrastive distillation, which
encourages alignment of the dynamically adapted noisy em-
beddings with the clean speaker manifold. This enhances the
noise-invariance of the features, achieving robustness across
diverse acoustic scenarios. The code is available athttps:

2.2. Noise Representation and Reconstruction Network
To capture acoustic interference, we build the Noise Represen-
tation and Reconstruction Network on a CRN backbone [20].
Given a noisy log-Mel spectrogram, the CRN backbone ex-
tracts hierarchical encoder features{E 1,E 2,E 3,E 4}and re-
constructs an estimated noise spectrogram, which provides ex-
plicit supervision.
In addition, we attach the MS-NRH to the noise encoder to
extract hierarchical noise embeddings (Fig. 2(a)). For the global
noise embedding, we apply a lightweight projection head on the
deepest feature mapE 4 (3×3Conv→ReLU→1×1Conv ),
followed by global average pooling over frequency and time,
yieldingZ global∈R D, whereDdenotes the embedding channel
dimension, which summarizes the stationary background noise
context. For the local noise embedding, we align multi-scale
features{E 2,E 3,E 4}to a common resolution and concatenate
them, followed by a Multi-scale Aggregation Gating Fusion
(MAGF) module that reweights and fuses the aligned features
before a final1×1convolution producesZ local∈R D×F×T ,
whereFandTdenote the frequency and time dimensions, re-
spectively. Compared with simple fusion, MAGF emphasizes
informative noise cues and suppresses speech-dominant com-
ponents under non-stationary interference.

2.4. Loss Functions
The proposed NoiseLoRA-SV is optimized using a multi-task
learning objective, integrating speaker classification, noise re-
construction, and contrastive distillation into a framework:
Ltotal =L spk +λ distLdist +λ noiseLnoise (5)
whereλ noise is set to0.1to balance the noise reconstruction task.
For the contrastive distillation, we assign a weight ofλdist = 1.0
to ensure sufficient gradient contribution relative to the primary
task. For the primary verification task, the SV lossLspk employs
the Additive Angular Margin (AAM) Softmax loss [23] to en-
courage intra-class compactness and inter-class discrepancy.
To guide the CRN in capturing acoustic interference, the
noise extraction lossL noise is formulated as MSE between pre-
dicted and ground-truth noise spectrograms. This encourages
noise extraction while reducing speech leakage. To align the
dynamically adapted noisy embeddings with the clean speaker
manifold, we incorporate a contrastive embedding distillation
loss (Ldist). Using a frozen teacher to extract clean embeddings,
we apply a masked InfoNCE-based objective [24]:

3.2. Implementation Details
The network is trained for 200 epochs on an NVIDIA A100
GPU with a batch size of 300, using the Adam optimizer with
a weight decay of2×10 −5. The initial learning rate is0.001,
decaying by0.97per epoch. To preserve discriminative repre-
sentations acquired during the pre-training phase, the learning
rate applied to the speaker encoder is scaled down by a factor of
0.1. For the loss configurations, the AAM-Softmax employs a
margin of0.2and a scale of30, while the temperature parame-
terτfor contrastive distillation is set to0.07. The shared LoRA
scaling factor (α) is set to8for both G and HNC blocks. The
frozen pre-trained teacher model adopts the identical architec-
ture as the student’s backbone ECAPA-TDNN and is trained on
clean data to provide clean speaker-manifold guidance.
We extract 80-dimensional log-Mel spectrograms from 3-
second speech crops (25 ms window, 10 ms shift), regularized
via SpecAugment [12] prior to being processed by the network,
whose detailed architectural configurations are summarized in
Table 1, with the bottleneck rankrfixed at4. The final output
of the system is a 192-dimensional speaker embedding vector.

4. Results and Discussion
Performance on Seen Noise Conditions.Table 2 presents
the EER results on the V oxCeleb1 test set contaminated with
the MUSAN corpus (seen noise). NoiseLoRA-SV consistently
outperforms existing static robust speaker verification methods
across most SNR levels, achieving the lowest average EER
of 3.05% under noisy conditions and 1.70% on clean speech.
Compared to joint learning approaches like Diff-SV (3.90%)
and ParaNoise-SV (3.40%), our dynamic instance-level adap-
tation framework proves more effective at balancing aggressive
noise suppression and fine-grained speaker feature preservation.
Ablation Studies.The bottom section of Table 2 de-
tails our ablation studies. Removing noise reconstruction (w/o
NoiseLoss) or contrastive distillation (w/o Distill) increases the
average EER to 3.47% and 3.34%, respectively. This confirms
the importance of both noise modeling and clean-manifold
alignment. Furthermore, single-block LoRA (one block only)
or static LoRA (static) performs worse than our full hierarchical
configuration, indicating that multi-scale adaptation suppresses
both low-level artifacts and high-level semantic distortions.
Generalization to Unseen Out-of-Domain Noise.Table 3
evaluates generalization capabilities using the out-of-domain
NonSpeech100 dataset. NoiseLoRA-SV obtains the lowest av-
erage EER of 3.60%. Compared to strong baselines like Diff-
SV (4.65%) and ParaNoise-SV (3.90%), our framework ex-

construction Network and Hierarchical LoRA blocks introduces
a moderate parameter overhead, confirming NoiseLoRA-SV as
a scalable and noise-robust solution for speaker verification.
5. Conclusion
NoiseLoRA-SV is a highly effective modulation framework for
noise-robust speaker verification, integrating explicit noise re-
construction with dynamic network adaptation. Its hierarchical
noise-conditioned LoRA architecture employs a hypernetwork
for global context and a time-varying gate for local features, en-
abling fine-grained adaptation to non-stationary noises while ro-
bustly preserving speaker-discriminative features. Demonstrat-
ing lower EERs across various noise conditions, our work high-
lights the potential of noise-guided, instance-level adaptation
for robust speaker verification in real-world scenarios.
