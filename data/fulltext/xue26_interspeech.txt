Abstract
The proliferation of zero-shot voice cloning models increases
the risk of unauthorized speaker impersonation. Although ad-
versarial perturbation provides an effective defense, existing
methods face a fundamental trade-off: strong perturbations in-
troduce audible artifacts, while imperceptible ones may lack
robustness when transferred to unseen systems. We introduce
a two-stage framework exploiting human-machine perception
discrepancy. We first train a disentanglement network to sepa-
rate content and speaker information, then inject perturbations
into the speaker embedding space where machines are sensitive
but humans are not. Experiments demonstrate that our approach
achieves 87.2% defense success rate, surpassing state-of-the-
art embedding-level defenses by 7%, while maintaining a high
MOS of 4.18 and demonstrating strong transferability to black-
box voice cloning systems.
Index Terms: voiceprint protection, voice cloning defense, ad-
versarial perturbation, feature disentanglement
1. Introduction
Modern zero-shot voice cloning models [1, 2, 3] can accurately
imitate the voice of an unseen speaker given only seconds of
reference audio. However, this technology carries potential
risks of misuse, such as unauthorized voice cloning for spoof-
ing voice authentication [4] or impersonating specific individ-
uals [5], which can lead to financial fraud and public opinion
chaos.
To defend against voice cloning attacks, previous
works have explored both detection and protection methods.
Detection-based approaches [6, 7] utilize anti-spoofing models
to identify synthetic speech after generation, but cannot prevent
the creation of deepfakes. Protection-based methods add per-
turbations before potential misuse, and can be categorized by
perturbation domain. Waveform-level methods such as V oice
Guard [8] and CloneShield [9] add perturbations directly to au-
dio signals with psychoacoustic constraints. Frequency-domain
methods like V ocalCrypt [10] leverage masking effects for im-
perceptibility. However, both waveform-level and frequency-
domain approaches operate at the signal level, where pertur-
bations may be attenuated by audio preprocessing pipelines.
Embedding-space methods such as RoV o [11] perturb speaker

representations directly, achieving better robustness, but with-
out explicitly disentangling content and speaker information,
which may affect speech intelligibility. These methods face
a fundamental trade-off: strong defenses degrade perceptual
quality, while imperceptible perturbations may lack robustness
when transferred to unseen systems [12].
We propose a method to protect voices from unauthorized
cloning by exploiting the perception gap between humans and
machines. To construct such a method, we need to answer three
questions: (1) How to identify features that machines rely on
but humans ignore; (2) How to generate perturbations that dis-
rupt machine cloning while remaining imperceptible; (3) How
to maintain robustness when transferred to black-box voice
cloning systems. A straightforward approach is to directly add
perturbations to speaker embeddings, as in RoV o [11]. How-
ever, without separating content and speaker information, such
perturbations may leak into content features and introduce au-
dible artifacts. Instead, we start with the observation that voice
cloning systems universally rely on speaker encoders [13] to ex-
tract identity features from reference audio. By training a dis-
entanglement network [14] to separate content and speaker in-
formation through an information bottleneck, we can precisely
target perturbations at the dimension in the speaker embed-
ding space where machines are sensitive but humans are not, as
shown in Figure 3. After reconstruction, we apply psychoacous-
tic masking in the time-frequency domain to ensure the final
audio perturbations remain below the hearing threshold. Exper-
iments show that the proposed method achieves 87.2% defense
success rate with MOS of 4.18 and WER of 5.30%, outperform-
ing prior methods while demonstrating strong transferability to
black-box voice cloning systems. Audio samples are available
online1.

stages.Stage 1trains an AutoVC-based disentanglement-

Crucially, without Stage 1’s explicit disentanglement, perturba-
tions in the embedding space would inevitably entangle with
linguistic features, leading to audible artifacts as seen in prior
works.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
We conduct experiments on the VCTK corpus [18], which con-
tains 110 English speakers. All audio is resampled to 16kHz.
We use 80 speakers for training, 10 for validation, and 20 for
testing, with 100 utterances per test speaker. Our Stage 1 net-
work adopts the AutoVC [14] architecture for content-speaker
disentanglement. The content encoder consists of 3 convolu-
tional layers followed by a bidirectional LSTM with bottle-
neck dimension 32, while the decoder comprises LSTM lay-
ers, 3 convolutional layers, and a 5-layer postnet for refinement.
Speaker embeddings are extracted using a GE2E model 2 [13]
initialized from pretrained weights and fine-tuned end-to-end
during Stage 1 training. We train Stage 1 for 500k iterations
and Stage 2 for 200k iterations. All hyperparameters are se-
lected via grid search on the validation set, yieldingλcd = 1.0,
λdis = 0.01for Stage 1, andα= 0.10,λ per = 15.0,λ de = 5.0,
λmask = 0.1for Stage 2.
For evaluation, we measure speaker similarity via co-
sine similarity between embeddings. Sim prot quantifies iden-
tity preservation between protected and original speech, while
Simclone measures the similarity between cloned and original
speech. DSR (Defense Success Rate) reports the percentage
of samples where Sim clone < τ, withτ∈ {0.3,0.4,0.5}.
We observe consistent relative performance trends across all
thresholds. For brevity, we reportτ= 0.5in text. Audio
quality is evaluated through MOS rated by 50 listeners and
WER computed using Whisper-large [19]. We compare against
waveform-level methods V oice Guard [8] and CloneShield [9],
frequency-domain method V ocalCrypt [10], and embedding-
space methods MI-FGSM [20] and RoV o [11].
3.2. White-box Defense Performance
Table 1 presents white-box results where all methods are trained
and evaluated on AutoVC with GE2E speaker encoder [13].
V ocalCrypt [10] demonstrates that frequency-domain masking
provides moderate protection while maintaining intelligibility.
V oice Guard improves defense but suffers from audible arti-
facts. Among embedding-space methods, MI-FGSM achieves
stronger defense but with degraded perceptual quality, while
RoV o better balances protection and quality.
Our method achieves superior performance across all met-
rics. The improvement stems from our feature-space pertur-
bation strategy: by operating in the disentangled speaker em-
bedding space rather than the audio domain, perturbations can
be precisely targeted at machine-sensitive features without af-
fecting perceptually salient components. Compared to V oice
Guard, we improve both audio quality and defense effectiveness
substantially. Compared to RoV o, we achieve notably higher
defense success rate while maintaining better perceptual qual-
ity. Table 2 examines the contribution of individual loss com-
ponents. RemovingL de causes DSR to collapse, confirming
that defense loss is the primary driver of protection capabil-
ity. Without explicit guidance to disrupt cloning, the generator
produces perturbations that preserve reconstruction fidelity but
fail to degrade speaker extraction. RemovingL mel orL mask
maintains defense effectiveness but degrades perceptual quality
significantly. Notably, removingL mask causes larger quality
degradation than removingL mel, validating that psychoacous-
tic constraints are more effective than simple spectral distance
for achieving imperceptible perturbations. This aligns with
human auditory perception: perturbations below the masking
threshold are genuinely inaudible, whereas minimizing spectral
distance does not guarantee perceptual transparency.

3.3. Black-box Defense Performance
Figure 2 evaluates black-box defense performance across ar-
chitecturally diverse voice cloning systems. Although trained
solely on AutoVC, we test against YourTTS [2], V ALL-E [1],
and AdaptVC [21]. Waveform-level methods suffer severe
degradation on black-box systems. V oice Guard exhibits sub-
stantial performance loss when transferred to unseen architec-
tures. Embedding-space methods exhibit better transferabil-
ity, yet RoV o still experiences considerable protection loss on
V ALL-E. Our method achieves the highest DSR across all un-
seen systems while exhibiting the smallest white-to-black-box
degradation among all baselines. This suggests that disen-
tanglement forces perturbations into speaker subspaces shared
across encoders, rather than overfitting to specific model param-
eters, thereby effectively targeting architecture-agnostic identity
features.

4. Conclusion
This paper presents a novel two-stage framework for imper-
ceptible voiceprint protection based on human-machine percep-
tion discrepancy feature disentanglement. Our approach gener-
ates adversarial perturbations in the speaker embedding space
with psychoacoustic constraints in the time-frequency domain,
achieving effective defense against multiple voice cloning sys-
tems while maintaining high speech quality. Extensive ex-
periments demonstrate that our method outperforms existing
approaches in balancing protection effectiveness and percep-
tual transparency, offering a practical solution for voice privacy
preservation in the era of advanced generative AI. Future work
will investigate defense robustness under adaptive attack sce-
narios and extend the framework to handle real-world audio
degradations including compression and environmental noise.
