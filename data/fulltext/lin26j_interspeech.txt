Abstract
Spiking neural networks offer a promising path to energy-
efficient AI, yet their potential for low-latency inference often
remains unrealized. Existing early-exit SNNs typically rely on
extrinsic, non-spiking criteria like confidence thresholds, com-
plicating the model. We revisit this paradigm by demonstrat-
ing that SNNs possess an intrinsic early-exit capability. We
introduce the First-to-Spike framework, where inference termi-
nates immediately upon the first spike from a competitive out-
put layer. This event-driven mechanism is further accelerated
by a Winner-Take-All circuit and optimized by a novel Hybrid
Temporal Training objective. Extensive evaluations on speech
and neurophysiological datasets show that F2S sets a new state
of the art, achieving higher accuracy with significantly reduced
latency and energy consumption. Our work validates that the
spike itself can serve as a robust and efficient decision signal
for real-time applications. Source code is available online1.

1. Introduction
The advancement of intelligent edge devices has created an ur-
gent demand for machine learning models that operate under
stringent latency and power constraints. Spiking neural net-
works (SNNs) have emerged as a promising solution, mimick-
ing the brain’s event-driven and energy-efficient computation
[1, 2]. However, this potential is often squandered, as many
SNNs are trained and evaluated by processing entire input se-
quences [3], negating their inherent capacity for rapid, on-the-
fly decision-making. Speech and physiological signals inher-
ently possess significant temporal redundancy and varying de-
grees of information density [4]. A clear, distinct keyword or
strong emotional EEG response might be recognizable within
the first few hundred milliseconds, whereas a noisy, ambigu-
ous, or softly spoken utterance may require integration over the
entire sequence. Enforcing a static, maximum-latency inference
for all inputs irrespective of their complexity results in massive
computational waste.
To reduce computation and latency during inference, early-
exit mechanisms stop processing once a sufficiently confident
decision can be made. Many existing approaches, however,
equip the SNN with auxiliary non-spiking decision rules. For

In this work, we introduce an intrinsic early-exit paradigm
for SNNs that uses the spike itself as the definitive decision sig-
nal. In the proposed First-to-Spike (F2S) framework, classifi-
cation is assigned to the class whose output neuron emits the
earliest spike in the output layer, completely avoiding external
non-spiking exit logic and softmax thresholds and enabling im-
mediate, event-driven decisions. Specifically, our main contri-
butions are:
•First-to-Spike (F2S) Framework:A fully event-driven
and hardware-friendly architecture that treats the first output
spike as the classification signal, enabling native early-exit
capabilities.
•Winner-Take-All (WTA) Circuit:A biologically-inspired
lateral inhibition mechanism [7] integrated into the output
layer to enforce competition and accelerate decision conver-
gence.
•Hybrid Temporal Training (HTT) Objective:A novel loss
formulation that jointly optimizes accuracy, decision latency,
and the robustness of the firing order.
Our comprehensive experiments validate the proposed F2S
framework on the GSC V2 [8] speech command dataset. Fur-
thermore, to demonstrate the universality of our approach
for continuous, multimodal time-series data relevant to next-
generation speech and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) [9],
we extend our evaluation to the SEED/SEED-IV [10, 11] EEG
emotion recognition datasets. Across both domains, our frame-
work sets a new state-of-the-art, achieving higher accuracy at
lower latency and energy cost. This work validates that early
exit is a natural property of SNNs, providing a powerful frame-
work for building efficient, real-time intelligent systems.
2. Method
We introduce a novel SNN framework designed to perform clas-
sification with an intrinsic early-exit capability. The internal
generation of a spike itself serves as the direct signal for predic-
tion, eliminating the need for external mechanisms. This section
details the architecture and neuronal dynamics that facilitate this
event-driven, low-latency classification.

2.1. Framework Overview
The proposed framework, illustrated in Figure 1, is composed of
a multi-layer SNN backbone network and a novel First-to-Spike
Decision Layer. The input to our system is a time-series signal
(e.g., speech audio, EEG recording), which is first converted
into a sequence of feature vectors,X= (x 1,x 2,...,x T )∈
RT×D in, whereTis the number of timesteps andD in is the
input feature dimension. This sequence is then processed by
an SNN backbone network timestep-by-timestep. The final de-
cision layer, First-to-Spike, is the core of our innovation. It is
a single layer of LIF neurons, where each neuron corresponds
to a specific output class. This layer integrates the evidence
provided by the backbone network over time. The classifica-
tion decision is made—and all further processing is halted—the
moment one of these neurons generates a spike. This event-
driven mechanism ensures that a decision is rendered as soon as
sufficient evidence has been accumulated, naturally minimizing
latency and computational cost.

2.3. Winner-Take-All (WTA) Circuit
Although the F2S mechanism provides an intrinsic decision
rule, its performance can be compromised when multiple output
neurons receive strong, competing evidence, potentially leading
to ambiguous or delayed decisions. To address this, we integrate
a biologically-inspired Winner-Take-All dynamic into the F2S
Decision Layer.
This mechanism is implemented through lateral inhibitory
connections, a principle observed in biological neural circuits
where active neurons suppress the activity of their neighbors
[15, 16]. This creates a competitive environment that encour-
ages the neuron representing the most salient class to dom-
inate rapidly while suppressing others. Specifically, we in-
troduce a trainable recurrent inhibitory weight matrix,V∈

rapidly disambiguates the network’s output, forcing a single,

decision timestep and a sharpening of the network’s classifica-
tion confidence.
2.4. Hybrid Temporal Training (HTT) Objective
Standard training paradigms that only compute loss at the final
timestep, while still effective to an extent, fail to optimize the
intermediate outputs crucial for early exits. To this end, we
propose a multi-component Hybrid Temporal Training (HTT)
Objective,L total, designed to synergistically shape the spatio-
temporal dynamics of the network.
The total loss is a weighted sum of three distinct compo-
nents: a classification loss, a temporal margin loss, and an effi-
ciency loss:
Ltotal =L class +α·L margin +β·L efficiency (7)
whereαandβare hyperparameters that balance the contribu-
tion of each term. We now detail each component.
2.4.1. Classification Loss (L class): Weighted TETLoss.
The primary objective is to ensure the network can accurately
classify the input. To provide a dense learning signal across
the temporal domain, we build upon the Temporal Efficient
Training (TET) loss [17]. Standard TET applies a classification
loss at every timestep to the sub-threshold membrane potentials.
However, we argue that not all timesteps are equally informa-
tive. Early timesteps may lack sufficient context, while later
timesteps in a padded sequence may contain irrelevant noise or
silence.
To address this, we introduce a novel Weighted TETLoss,
which modulates the contribution of each timestep’s loss via a

2.4.3. Efficiency Loss (L efficiency).
The final component of our objective directly promotes early
decision and efficiency. The efficiency loss is a simple regu-
larization term that penalizes late decisions by minimizing the
firing time of the correct neuron:L efficiency =E[t correct].
This term applies a constant pressure on the network to pro-

for lower latency.
Together, these three loss components create a comprehen-
sive training objective, enabling the model to learn not just what
to classify, but how to do so rapidly and reliably.
3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
To rigorously evaluate our First-to-Spike framework, we con-
duct experiments across two distinct domains using three
benchmark datasets. For keyword spotting, we utilize the
Google Speech Commands V2 (GSC V2) dataset [8], follow-
ing the standard data split. Each one-second audio clip is pre-
processed into a sequence of 98 timesteps with 40-dimensional
log Mel-filterbank energies. To demonstrate the framework’s
generality, we also test on the SEED [10] and SEED-IV [11]
EEG emotion recognition datasets, employing a strict subject-
independent Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) cross-validation
protocol [18]. For these, we use the extracted Differential En-
tropy (DE) features directly as input sequences, maintaining the
temporal order for test sets to assess performance on contigu-
ous signals. During training, HTT hyperparameters are set to
α= 0.05,β= 0.01,M= 5.
Performance is evaluated in terms of accuracy, latency, and
energy efficiency. We measure standard classification Accuracy
(%) based on the model’s prediction at its decision time, and the
Average Decision Timestep (ADT), which quantifies the mean
latency by recording the number of timesteps processed before
a decision is made. Energy consumption is estimated per in-
ference based on 45nm CMOS technology [19, 20], where a

Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) consumes 4.6 pJ and an Accumu-

3.3. Ablation Analysis
To isolate the contributions of our proposed components, we
conducted an ablation study on GSC V2 (Table 3). A “vanilla”
F2S model, which employs only the First-to-Spike decision
rule without our Winner-Take-All circuit, and only uses stan-
dard Cross-Entropy loss for training, achieves a competitive
accuracy of 89.17% with an ADT of 69.78 (out of 98 total
timesteps). This key finding confirms that SNNs possess an
inherent early-exit capability, and the natural race-to-spike dy-
namic itself forms a robust baseline decision mechanism.
We then evaluated the individual additions of WTA and
HTT. Introducing the HTT objective effectively regularizes the
temporal dynamics, reducing the ADT to 66.32 while slightly
boosting accuracy (89.84%). Conversely, integrating the WTA
circuit via lateral inhibition yields a massive gain in accu-
racy, jumping to 92.32%, while also decreasing the ADT to
65.43. This confirms that actively suppressing competing neu-
rons rapidly clears ambiguity, allowing the network to make
sharper, earlier decisions.
Finally, the full F2S framework synergizes both compo-
nents, reaching higher accuracy and lower latency. This proves
that while the intrinsic F2S mechanism is naturally capable, our
proposed architectural and training contributions are vital for
optimizing it to achieve superior speed, precision, and energy
efficiency.

4. Discussion and Conclusion
In this work, we demonstrate that SNNs possess an intrinsic
capability for early-exit classification. Our proposed First-to-
Spike framework operationalizes this principle by treating the
first spike from a competitive output layer as the definitive clas-
sification event, entirely obviating the need for extrinsic, non-
spiking stopping criteria. This inherent dynamic is architec-
turally accelerated by a biologically-inspired Winner-Take-All
circuit and optimized for speed and robustness via a novel Hy-
brid Temporal Training objective.
Comprehensive evaluation on audio and neurophysiologi-
cal time-series data validates this approach, showing that the
F2S framework sets a new state-of-the-art by achieving higher
accuracy at lower latency and energy cost than traditional
threshold-based early exits.
While highly effective, this framework opens several av-
enues for future work. First, investigating the model’s robust-
ness to varying signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) will be critical for
deployment in unpredictable edge environments. Second, cou-
pling the F2S framework directly with asynchronous neuromor-
phic sensors, such as dynamic audio sensors (DAS) or event-
based microphones, could further eliminate redundant temporal
padding at the hardware level. Ultimately, our work confirms
that early exit is a natural property of SNNs, paving the way
for a new generation of event-driven, computationally efficient
intelligent systems for real-time edge applications.
