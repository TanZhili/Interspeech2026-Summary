Privacy and quality trade-off in real-time speaker anonymization
via editing of age and sex attributes
Waris Quamer, Ricardo Gutierrez-Osuna
Department of Computer Science & Engineering, Texas A&M University, College Station, US
{quamer.waris,rgutier}@tamu.edu
Abstract
Speaker anonymization aims to alter attributes in the speech sig-
nal to hide the identity of the speaker. While such attribute
modifications can effectively hide speaker identity, they often
degrade the quality of the synthesized speech. In this work,
we present a systematic analysis of the privacy–quality trade-
off by isolating two key attributes (age and sex) in a streaming
speech synthesis system. We use objective metrics (speaker co-
sine similarity as a proxy of identity and DNS-MOS for speech
quality) and perform linear regression to quantify how attribute
shifts impact privacy and quality, and validate these trends with
perceptual listening tests. Our results reveal that privacy de-
grades faster than quality as modifications increase, giving rise
to an optimal anonymization region where moderate attribute
changes achieve effective identity suppression with minimal
naturalness loss. These findings provide actionable guidance
for tuning any attribute-based anonymization system.
Index Terms: speech recognition, human-computer interac-
tion, computational paralinguistics
1. Introduction
V oice-based applications are becoming ubiquitous, raising new
privacy concerns. Speaker anonymization seeks to protect a
user’s identity by altering vocal characteristics while preserving
intelligibility and naturalness. A wide range of methods have
been proposed, from DSP techniques such as formant shifting
and perturbation [1–4], to deep-learning approaches including
adversarial training [5], voice conversion [6, 7], and generative
models that manipulate latent representations [8–10]. Bench-
marking efforts such as the V oicePrivacy Challenge [11] have
further advanced the field by standardizing evaluation protocols.
However, nearly all prior work treats anonymization as a
monolithic transformation without isolating the contributions
of individual speaker attributes to identity. In other words, it
remains unclear which specific attribute changes yield the best
privacy gains with the least impact on speech quality. This is a
critical gap: researchers building anonymization systems need
to knowhow muchto modifywhichattributes to reach a desired
privacy-quality operating point.
We address this gap through a focused analysis of the
privacy-quality trade-off in a real-time speaker anonymization
setting. Rather than treating anonymization as a black-box, we
isolate age and sex attributes that known to strongly affect voice
character and quantify their independent impact on identity sup-
pression (privacy) versus speech naturalness (quality). Through
systematic analysis, we identify anoptimal anonymization re-
gion: a range of moderate attribute modifications that provides
effective privacy with minimal quality degradation. Our contri-
butions are:

2. Methods
Our approach decomposes input speech into linguistic content
and speaker characteristics. As shown in Figure 1, we modify
speaker embeddings via parametric attribute editing, then re-
synthesize speech through a decoder. We describe the synthesis
model and the editing process below.
2.1. Speech synthesis model
Our streaming synthesis model follows the architecture in [12]
and comprises four components: a content encoder that predicts
HuBERT-Kmeans discrete units [13, 14] using causal CNNs; a
speaker encoder that concatenates X-vector [15] and ECAPA-
TDNN [16] representations; a speaker/variance adapter that in-
jects speaker identity, pitch, and energy via AdaIN [17] and
FiLM [18]; and a causal HiFiGAN-based decoder with adver-
sarial and multi-resolution STFT losses. For implementation
details, see [12].

2.3. Experimental setup
We trained the synthesis model and PCA decomposition on
the LibriTTS corpus [19], testing on the held-out dev and test
splits. Training followed the guidelines in [12] using two
NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs. Speaker encoders were obtained
from SpeechBrain [20]. Since LibriTTS lacks age labels, we
generated age and continuous sex (femininity probability) la-
bels using a pre-trained wav2vec2-based age/sex predictor 2,
with age normalized to[0,1].

Despite the observed quality degradation, it is important to
note that the rate of decline differs between anonymization and
quality. Specifically, the slope for the drop in speaker similarity
with attribute modifications is steeper than the slope for quality
degradation. This difference suggests there may exist a region
where speaker identity can be effectively suppressed without
causing severe quality loss–a potentialsweet spotthat balances
privacy and perceptual naturalness. In the following section,
we further explore this trade-off and investigate whether such a
region does exist.
3.3. Privacy-quality trade-off
To examine this trade-off between synthesis quality and speaker
anonymity, we generated a joint line plot of DNS-MOS (objec-
tive speech quality) and cosine similarity (the inverse of speaker
anonymity) as age and gender modifications were applied inde-
pendently –see Figure 6. Notice that cosine similarity drops
more steeply than MOS, both for age and femininity modifi-
cations, indicating that there does exist a ’sweet spot’ in the
region of+/−0.25 standard deviations in the magnitude of
modifications that significantly reduces cosine similarity while
maintaining good synthesis quality.
We also examined this trade-off when age and gender are
modified simultaneously. Results are shown in Figure 7, with
cosine distance (the reverse of cosine similarity) shown as a
heatmap and MOS as a contour along with the first eigen-
value for both bi-variate distributions. When attribute modifi-
cations are minimal, both speech quality and speaker similarity
remain high. As modifications increase whether through age
or femininity shifts, cosine distance (heatmap) declines sharply
whereas speech quality (contour) remain relatively stable. More
importantly, the two eigenvectors have different orientations, so
editing age and femininity attributes along the eigenvalue for
cosine distance decreases speaker similarity more sharply than
speech quality.

4. Discussion and conclusions
Our results show that effective real-time speaker anonymiza-
tion can be achieved by manipulating interpretable speaker at-
tributes, specifically age and sex via a lightweight (PCA-based)
decomposition of the speaker embedding space. Although
PCA-based attribute editing is conceptually straightforward, the
primary contribution of our work lies in systematically analyz-
ing how targeted attribute changes affect the balance between
identity suppression and speech quality.
Previous research on speaker anonymization has typically
emphasized complex models designed to maximize privacy or
preserve quality, but has not explicitly quantified how individual
attributes contribute to anonymization effectiveness and natu-

quality. Quality scores should not be interpreted as MOS.
ralness. In contrast, our study adopts a deliberately lightweight
and interpretable approach, partly to satisfy real-time stream-
ing constraints (latency under 350 ms), enabling us to precisely
measure the effect of specific attribute modifications. Through
this analysis, we found that sex-related voice characteristics
exert a stronger influence on identity concealment than age,
and that moderate, consistent modifications, particularly of sex,
achieve significant anonymization without substantial quality
degradation.
By explicitly examining the privacy-quality trade-off, we
identified a clearly definedoptimal anonymization region
around|λ|≈0.25std. Within this region, moderate attribute
modifications provide robust identity suppression while main-
taining high speech quality. This balance is particularly evident
when modifying age and sex simultaneously, as combined ad-
justments partially offset individual distortions. We note that
this optimal region partly stems from the intrinsic correlation
between age and sex through pitch: younger and female voices
typically exhibit higher pitch, meaning PCA inherently captures
some of their co-variability.
We chose PCA for attribute editing due to its low compu-
tational complexity and suitability for real-time applications.
Although PCA does not perfectly disentangle correlated at-
tributes, it provides robust editing by operating along direc-
tions of highest variance in the embedding space. Alterna-
tive approaches such as oriented PCA [22] could further iso-
late attribute-specific variance by identifying principal direc-
tions orthogonal to correlated variables, for instance, isolating
age-related variance independently from sex effects.
Importantly, our goal is not to propose a new state-of-the-
art anonymization system or compete with established bench-
marks. Instead, we study the fundamental interaction between
privacy and quality through systematic attribute-level analy-
sis. Our approach complements broader efforts such as the
V oicePrivacy Challenge [11] by providing insights that can help
refine attribute modification strategies and guide hyperparam-
eter tuning (e.g., adjusting voice conversion strength in other
anonymization pipelines). While our study centers on age and
sex due to their interpretability, the framework naturally ex-
tends to other attributes such as speaking rate, accent, and vocal
tract length. Future work will explore integrating this attribute-
based approach with generative models for finer-grained, multi-
attribute control.
