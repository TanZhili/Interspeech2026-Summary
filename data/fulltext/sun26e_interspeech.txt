Abstract
Automatic Depression Detection (ADD) in speech aims to es-
timate speaker’s depressive state through speech-based intel-
ligent methods, yet existing speech-based ADD works fail
to jointly address inaccurate and inexact labels for ADD in
weakly-supervised cases. To this end, we propose an ap-
proach of Label Correction enhanced Dual-stream Multiple
Instance Learning (LC-DMIL) for weakly-supervised speech-
based ADD. The proposed LC-DMIL includes a label correc-
tion module focusing on correcting inaccurate labels through
fused likelihood-ratio-based and prototype-based correction
strategies, and a depression detection module employing a dual-
stream multiple instance approach towards the inexact labels.
Experiments on weakly-supervised speech-based depression
detection data indicate that, the proposed approach performs ef-
fectively for the weakly-supervised speech-based ADD.
Index Terms: depression detection in speech, weakly-
supervised learning, multi-instance learning, label correction
1. Introduction
Social prejudice and limited medical resources on depression
diagnosis call for effective speech-basedAutomatic Depression
Detection(ADD) systems [1, 2], to provide effective support
for intelligent diagnosis [3–5], considering the availability for
spoken signals to convey affective information [6]. Hence, on
the audio part, early speech-based ADD focuses on acquir-
ing depression-related features from speech, through shallow
machine-learning techniques [7, 8], while further deep models
ofConvolutional Neural Networks(CNNs) [9–11],Recurrent
Neural Networks(RNNs) [12, 13], and Transformers [14, 15]
emerge for accurately capturing effective depression-related
paralinguistic information from speech.
In spite of the progress, several issues remain to address
towards weakly-supervised cases for ADD (e. g., inaccurate
and inexact labels). First, most works on speech-based ADD
tend to trust the judgments from annotators or subjective ques-
tionnaires in distinguishing depressed speech, ignoring poten-
tial inaccurately-supervised cases for incorrect labels. Sec-
ond, depressive traits in low-quality audio samples may be con-
fined to subtle segments within the overall recording, which

may be easily overlooked, and further, results in inexactly-
supervised cases in training. In this regard, we propose a
weakly-supervised ADD approach in speech usingLabel Cor-
rection enhanced Dual-stream Multiple Instance Learning(LC-
DMIL). For the first issue, the proposed approach involves a
label correction module to rectify possibly inaccurate training
labels. For the second issue, the approach comprises a dual-
streamMulti-Instance Learning(MIL) strategy, to effectively
capture instance-level subtle depressive traits in speech.
The proposed LC-DMIL comprises two modules of la-
bel correction and MIL-based depression detection, both em-
ploying backbone networks with a 1D-CNN layer and aBi-
directional Long Short-Term Memory(Bi-LSTM) RNN layer.
First, the label correction module aims to perform sample-
level label correction through a fusion of correction strategies.
Then, the MIL-based depression detection module performs
instance-level learning within samples using the corrected la-
bels, through setting two parallel streams (max-rule and MIL-
aggregator streams) for MIL.
We make comparison between the proposed and highly re-
lated approaches. Existing works employ CNN-based RNNs
[16] or Transformers [14, 17] as the backbones to capture ef-
fective factors for speech-based depression detection, while
the proposed approach focuses on weakly-supervised cases
via label correction enhanced dual-stream MIL. To address
weakly-supervised issues in model training [18], label correc-
tion [19, 20] techniques have proven effective in rectifying po-
tentially inaccurate labels [21]. In speech and image processing,
MIL [22–25] strategies are employed to handle inexact supervi-
sion, via treating each sample as multiple instances. Compared
with [6], this work further employs dual-stream MIL for inexact
cases in weakly-supervised speech-based depression detection.

3.3. Experimental Results
Comparisons with Existing Approaches
First, we show the comparison between the proposed LC-
DMIL and existing state-of-the-art approaches in Table 1, in
terms of the bestUnweighted Average Recall(UAR) results
and corresponding F1-score results. The compared approaches
include the DepAudioNet [3] using a convolutional RNN, the
ConvBiLSTM [28] with a convolutional BiLSTM network
(as the backbone for the proposed LC-DMIL), the Speech-
Former [29] with a Transformer framework, the support-vector-
machines model onComputational Paralinguistics challengE
(ComParE) [30] features, the audio part of a cross-modal Trans-
former network [31] (CMT-MSER (Audio)), and the label cor-
rection module of LC-DMIL (SLLC [6]). It is learnt from the
results that, the proposed LC-DMIL is superior to these state-
of-the-art approaches on the DAIC-WOZ dataset, and further,
significance is found between the proposed and compared ap-
proaches on UARs (p<0.005) using a one-tailedz-test [6].

posed approach, showing the UAR and F1-score results of the

0.7and0.3in the table for comparison, also with the improve-

Then, we further focus on using the two streams (especially

Further Analysis
On the DAIC-WOZ dataset, we briefly analyse the states of
Depressive(noted as ‘D’) andNon-Depressive(noted as ‘ND’)
for the proposed LC-DMIL through presenting confusion matri-
ces as shown in Figure 4, compared with the approaches without
both label correction and MIL (ConvBiLSTM), only with MIL
(noted as ‘DMIL’), and only with label correction (SLLC). It is
drawn from the figure that, the proposed LC-DMIL can improve
sensitivity for depression detection in speech.
To examine randomly incorrect labels, we perform exper-
iments on the A VEC 2014 dataset [37] when using ConvBiL-
STM, DMIL, SLLC, and LC-DMIL, as shown in Table 4 (also
on the DAIC-WOZ dataset). The A VEC 2014 dataset comprises
300audio files from84speakers, with100files for training as
in [6, 37] and the rest for testing. Each file is split into1.5s-
overlapping segments of3s in training (3 538samples) and non-
overlapping segments of3s in the test set (3 696samples with
1 710depression). To simulate the weak supervision, we ran-
domly select3/7samples and flip their labels in the training
set2 (leading to1 810depression samples), as in [6]. The re-
sults indicate that, the proposed LC-DMIL also performs better
in the randomly incorrect-label case, compared to its non-label-
correction and non-MIL variants.
4. Conclusion
We proposed theLabel Correction enhanced Dual-stream Mul-
tiple Instance Learning(LC-DMIL) approach for weakly-
supervised depression detection in speech, comprising a la-
bel correction module focusing on inaccurate supervision via
fused label correction strategies, and aMulti-Instance Learn-
ing(MIL)-based depression detection module employing dual-
stream MIL to address inexact supervision. Then, future
works may focus on including generated data for weakly-
supervised speech-based depression detection, and weakly-
supervised learning for other affective computing issues.
