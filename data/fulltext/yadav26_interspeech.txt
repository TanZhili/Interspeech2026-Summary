Indic-to-English Speech-to-Speech Translation

Abstract
We present ARTIST, a novel end-to-end framework for multi-
lingual Indic-to-English Speech-to-Speech Translation (S2ST)
operating in a universal articulatory space. It utilizes a Con-
former encoder with intermediate CTC supervision over source
articulatory features, explicitly aligning diverse acoustic inputs
with quantized, language-agnostic representations. To map
these features, we introduce a Convolution-Augmented Dif-
ferential Transformer decoder designed to capture both local
phonetic nuances and global context. We demonstrate excep-
tional cross-lingual transfer across 11 Indic languages, includ-
ing extreme low-resource settings (10hr/language). For in-
stance, with only 166M parameters, ARTIST outperforms the
1.2B parameter SeamlessM4T baseline on BLEU, COMET, and
chrF. ARTIST also achieves superior VRAM efficiency, estab-
lishing a high-performance paradigm for scaling direct S2ST to
resource-constrained environments.

1. Introduction
Recent progress in end-to-end Speech-to-Speech Translation
(S2ST) has shifted research from cascaded pipelines toward
direct waveform-to-waveform modeling [1]. However, con-
temporary large-scale systems such as SeamlessM4T [2], Au-
dioPaLM [3], UnitY [4], and the Translatotron [5, 6] rely
heavily on brute-force parameter scaling (often exceeding 1B
parameters) and massive quantities of supervised bilingual
data. While effective for high-resource languages, these ap-
proaches struggle in linguistically diverse, low-resource set-
tings. For many Indic languages, where parallel speech cor-
pora are severely limited, large autoregressive models tend to
overfit acoustic variance and exhibit severe sequence hallucina-
tion, particularly on long-form audio. To overcome this data
bottleneck, we pivot away from conventional acoustic or text-
derived discrete targets [7]. Instead, we ground our approach in
the physiological reality of human speech: the vocal tract. By
modeling speech within a universal articulatory space, we pro-
vide a strong structural prior that abstracts away language- and
speaker-specific variability. This enables more effective cross-
linguistic alignment and addresses challenges related to acous-
tic variability and coarticulatory effects, forcing cross-lingual
alignment naturally and enabling robust few-shot transfer even
under aggressive data constraints.
We proposeARTIST(ArticulatoryRepresentation
Transfer forIndicSpeechTranslation), a highly efficient
166M-parameter multilingual S2ST framework for Indic-to-
English translation. ARTIST maps source waveforms into
a tokenized articulatory space using a pre-trained Vector
Quantized Autoencoder (VQAE). By radically compressing
the decoder’s target complexity, this bottleneck encourages
parameter sharing across multiple languages, allowing high-
resource languages to act as phonetic scaffolds for extremely
low-resource targets. To capture both local phonetic structure
and long-range dependencies, ARTIST pairs a Conformer
encoder with a novel Convolution-Augmented Differential
Transformer decoder. To explicitly disentangle phonetic
content from acoustic noise early in the pipeline, we introduce
intermediate Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC)
supervision within the encoder. This aligns intermediate
acoustic representations directly with source articulatory units.
Furthermore, our proposed Differential Attention mechanism
mitigates noise accumulation, granting the model remarkable
temporal robustness.
To rigorously evaluate this temporal robustness, we de-
fine a controlled out-of-distribution protocol using the public
BhashaAnuvad benchmark [8]. Although ARTIST is trained
exclusively on short utterances (3-20 seconds), we evaluate
it out-of-distribution on extended long-form sequences (20-50
seconds), directly testing robustness to hallucination and atten-
tion degradation without requiring additional long-form train-
ing data1. In summary, this work makes the following contribu-
tions:
1. Shared Articulatory Latent: A quantized representation
achieving zero-shot cross-lingual transfer across languages.
2. Extreme Parameter Efficiency: A 166M-parameter archi-
tecture that outperforms 1B+ parameter baselines in low-
resource and memory-constrained settings.
3. Encoder CTC Supervision: Enforces early phonetic disentan-
glement and stabilizes cross-lingual mapping.
4. Robust Long-Form Generalization: An AR decoder that suc-
cessfully mitigates attention noise and sequence hallucina-
tion on extended audio.
2. Related Work
Traditional cascaded S2ST systems suffer from compounding
errors and latency [9]. End-to-end architectures [5, 6, 1] and
discrete acoustic unit frameworks (e.g., UnitY [4], XLS-R [10])
mitigate these issues by bridging the modality gap, but require
massive data scales to stabilize cross-lingual representations.
Other approaches employing phoneme-conditioned V AEs [11]
or direct waveform prediction [12] similarly struggle in extreme
low-resource regimes due to language-specific acoustic vari-
ability. Alternatively, physically grounded representations of-
fer innate resilience to such noise. Recent monolingual studies

3. The ARTIST Framework
In this section, we introduce ARTIST, which bypasses textual
intermediaries or purely acoustic discrete unit intermediaries
by directly mapping acoustic signals into a language-agnostic
articulatory domain. The framework consists of two primary
stages: (1) Multilingual articulatory space pretraining, where
we learn a compact, shared language-agnostic latent space via a
VQAE and (2) training a novel end-to-end S2ST model.

3.2. Speech to Articulator (S2A) Training
At the core of ARTIST is the S2A pipeline, which maps source
speech directly to the universal articulatory space utilizing a
dual-innovation architecture: an encoder with early phonetic
disentanglement and a specialized, noise-resilient decoder.
•Speech Encoder with Intermediate CTC Disentangle-
ment:The encoder processes downsampled Mel-filterbank
sequences through an initial Acoustic Conformer block [15]
(see Fig. 1b). A persistent challenge in multilingual S2ST
is the tendency of deep acoustic encoders to entangle back-
ground noise and language-specific prosody with the un-
derlying phonetic content. To isolate pure linguistic struc-
ture early in the pipeline, we inject an intermediate Con-
nectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) supervision sig-
nal. Specifically, the hidden representationsHfrom the
initial acoustic encoder are passed through a Condition-
ing Block to predict the discrete source articulatory latents,
Zs ={Z s1,...,Z sT}, which are generated by the frozen
VQAE. The auxiliary objective is defined as:L CTC =
−logP CTC (Zs|H).This regularised supervision provides
a strong structural inductive bias. By explicitly aligning
lower-layer representations with the source articulatory to-
kens, the network is forced to distill phonetic content from
acoustic variability early in the forward pass. As illustrated,
this conditioned output is then residually added back to the

•AR Decoder with Convolution-Augmented Differential
Transformer:Following the deep Conformer encoder, the
shared, language-agnostic representations are passed to a
novel Convolution-Augmented Differential Transformer de-
coder. The primary objective of this decoder is to autoregres-
sively predict the target discrete articulatory latents,Z t =
{Zt1,...,Z tT}, which are extracted from the target articu-
latory features via the frozen VQAE pathway (as depicted
in Fig. 1b). To handle noise accumulation over long au-
dio sequences and catastrophic hallucination in low-resource
settings we employ Differential Attention [16], sharpening
the model’s focus on highly salient acoustic frames. Fur-
thermore, human articulatory gestures (e.g., bilabial closures,
velar drops) are governed by physical kinematics and ex-
hibit strong local temporal continuity. We mitigate subopti-
mal global attention by interleaving the Conformer Convolu-
tional module [15] directly within each decoder block, utiliz-
ing causal padding to maintain strictly autoregressive genera-
tion. This hybrid design flawlessly marries two complemen-
tary paradigms: the convolutions explicitly model the local
temporal continuity of physical speech production, while the

Differential Attention preserves a robust, noise-free global
sequence context. To optimize this AR generation, the de-
coder is supervised using a standard cross-entropy Attention
Loss (LAttn) computed against the quantized target tokens.
The entire S2A pipeline is optimized jointly using a composite
objective formulated as:L Total =λL Attn +βL CTC.
3.3. End-to-End S2S Inference and Acoustic Synthesis
During end-to-end inference, ARTIST maps the source acoustic
waveform through the Conformer encoder and the Convolution-
Augmented Differential Transformer decoder to autoregres-
sively predict a discrete sequence of target tokens (Z t). Op-
erating exclusively on the target side, these predicted latents
are directly mapped back into sparse, continuous articula-
tory representations via the pre-trained VQAE decoder (see
Fig. 1a).To bridge the gap between these physical represen-
tations and the continuous acoustic domain, we employ a
lightweight Articulator-to-MelSpectrogram (A2Mel) Genera-
tor [17], inspired by FastSpeech-2 which is explicitly reopti-
mized to ingest sparse articulatory features, bypassing the need
for text or phoneme generation entirely. Finally, the resulting
high-fidelity Mel-spectrograms are inverted into raw waveforms
using a pre-trained HiFi-GAN-V1 vocoder [18].
4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Dataset and Evaluation
We evaluate ARTIST on 11 Indic-to-English directions using
BhashaAnuvad [8] (comprising IndicV oices-ST, MannKiBaat,
NPTEL, and WordProject). We apply strict training filters (min-
ing≥0.6, alignment≥0.8) but relax evaluation thresholds
(alignment≥0.5) to reflect realistic noise. To test our bottle-
neck, training volumes are purposefully skewed from moderate-
resource anchors down to extreme 10-hour scenarios (Table 1).
Finally, to expose autoregressive hallucination vulnerabilities,
we enforce a rigorous temporal generalization protocol: models
are trained exclusively on short clips (3–20s) but evaluated on a
curated, out-of-distribution test set featuring 1–4 hours of long-
form audio (20–50s) per language from the IndicV oicesST split
of the BhashaAnuvad corpus.

High and Mid-Resource Efficacy:Massive models like SM4T
rely heavily on aggregating primary and mined corpora, of-

bilize acoustic mappings. Conversely, ARTIST achieves supe-
rior fidelity with strictly limited primary data. In high-resource
settings like Hindi, ARTIST yields an absolute improvement
of +8.93 BLEU and +13.55 chrF using only 314 hours, com-
pared to SM4T’s 584 hours. This efficiency accelerates in mid-
resource scenarios such as Tamil, where ARTIST delivers a
+10.85 BLEU improvement utilizing roughly 141 hours against
the baseline’s 905 total hours.
Extreme Low-Resource Resilience:The inherent vulnerabil-
ities of massive autoregressive scaling are most severely ex-
posed under data scarcity. On our out-of-distribution long-
sequence test set, SM4T frequently suffers from acoustic over-
fitting and catastrophic hallucinations. For Kannada, the base-
line achieves only 6.50 BLEU with 289 total hours of data,
whereas ARTIST achieves 14.67 BLEU with merely 21 hours.
Most strikingly, in the extremely low-resource setting of Gu-
jarati, ARTIST achieves superior translation quality (16.91
BLEU, 0.6950 COMET) utilizing exactly 10 hours of training
data, sharply contrasting with the 135 hours required by SM4T.
This vast disparity in data efficiency validates our core hypoth-
esis. By enforcing a universal physiological structural prior via
the 20-token articulatory bottleneck, ARTIST effectively filters
out language-specific acoustic noise. This targeted compres-
sion enables highly efficient cross-lingual knowledge transfer,
allowing high-resource anchors to support severely constrained
languages without the need for massive dataset scaling.

discrete space does not suffer from the capacity competition
often observed in standard multilingual models. Instead, it
actively facilitates synergistic knowledge transfer; by learning
universal physiological representations across 11 diverse lan-
guages, the model refines its core phonetic grammar, ultimately
enhancing translation fidelity even for its highest-resource
anchor.
Importance of Local Context in Decoding:We ablated the
Convolutional module within our Convolution-Augmented
Differential Transformer decoder, reverting it to a standard
Differential Attention mechanism. This resulted in a noticeable
drop in translation quality (down to 19.81 BLEU). While
Differential Attention excels at capturing global context and
suppressing noise, this degradation confirms that explicit local
modeling is strictly necessary. The convolutions are required to
accurately capture the physical, temporal continuity inherent to
articulatory movements.
Efficacy of Intermediate CTC Supervision:The intermediate
CTC conditioning layer serves as a critical structural prior. We
tested two specific ablations regarding its implementation:
•Removal of CTC Conditioning:Stripping the intermediate
CTC supervision entirely resulted in catastrophic training in-
stability and acoustic collapse, with the BLEU score collaps-
ing to 1.07. This establishes that early phonetic alignment is
absolutely mandatory for filtering out background acoustics
and stabilizing the network in data-scarce environments.
•Late vs. Early Fusion:We shifted the CTC conditioning
layer from the early encoder stages to the deeper Conformer
layers. This late fusion yielded a significant penalty (drop-
ping BLEU to 19.68). This explicitly demonstrates that forc-
ing acoustic-to-articulatory alignment early in the network
using coarse acoustic features is optimal. It successfully
frees the deeper Conformer layers to dedicate their represen-
tational capacity entirely to complex cross-lingual mapping
rather than basic acoustic disentanglement.
