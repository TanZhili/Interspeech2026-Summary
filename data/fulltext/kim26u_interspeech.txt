ETC-TTS: Emotion Trajectory Learning for Controllable Emotional
Text-to-Speech
Gaeun Kim1, Jaeuk Lee1, Joon-Hyuk Chang1,∗∗
1 Department of Electronic Engineering, Hanyang University, Seoul, Republic of Korea
{gaeun8389, pier314, jchang}@hanyang.ac.kr
Abstract
Emotional text-to-speech (TTS) systems have achieved high
speech quality, yet controllable emotion intensity modeling re-
mains challenging. Existing approaches control emotion em-
beddings via scaling or interpolation at inference time, so in-
termediate emotional states are not explicitly learned. To ad-
dress this limitation, we propose Emotion Trajectory learning
for Controllable emotional TTS (ETC-TTS), a framework that
models emotion intensity as continuous trajectories in latent
style space. A residual vector quantization (RVQ)-based style
extractor provides emotion-aware representations, and a flow-
matching objective learns trajectories between neutral and tar-
get emotions. Unlike prior methods, ETC-TTS learns emo-
tion transitions during training, enabling stable intensity con-
trol. Experiments on Korean and English datasets show im-
proved controllability while preserving speech quality. Subjec-
tive and objective evaluations confirm more stable emotional
expressions than baseline methods.
Index Terms: Emotional TTS, Controllable Emotional TTS
1. Introduction
Recent advances in text-to-speech (TTS) have significantly
improved the naturalness and expressiveness of synthesized
speech. Among these developments, emotional TTS [1–8]
aims to generate speech with controllable emotional expres-
sions. Despite substantial progress in emotional style mod-
eling, controllable manipulation ofemotion intensityremains
an open challenge. In natural speech, utterances sharing the
same emotion label often exhibit diverse intensity levels, re-
flecting gradual affective variation rather than discrete cate-
gories. However, modeling such continuous intensity changes
in a stable and interpretable manner remains challenging for ex-
isting emotional TTS systems. Relative Attribute (RA)-based
methods [9–11] introduce scalar ranking values [12] to repre-
sent intensity, whereas scaling factor (SF), embedding interpo-
lation, and diffusion-based methods such as EmoMix [13–17]
modify emotion embeddings at inference time to produce in-
termediate states. Coordinate-based approaches [18, 19] further
assume predefined geometric structures within emotion spaces.
As a result, intensity control is typically realized through post-
hoc manipulation of learned representations at inference time.
In practice, such manipulation can lead to unstable intensity be-
havior, where increasing the control factor does not always yield
stronger perceived emotion, and may degrade speech natural-
ness when samples fall outside the training distribution [15].
Fundamentally, existing methods treat emotion intensity as
a scalar control variable applied to static emotion representa-

tions, creating a training–inference mismatch: models are opti-
mized on discrete emotional endpoints while continuous transi-
tions are only imposed at inference time. This implicit assump-
tion—that simple latent manipulation guarantees perceptually
consistent intensity variation—is not enforced by the training
objective and often results in unstable intermediate behavior.
These observations suggest that emotion intensity should be
modeled as a trajectory between emotional states rather than
as isolated scalar modifications. Thus, we reformulate emotion
intensity control as trajectory dynamics in latent style space and
propose Emotion Trajectory Learning for Controllable Emo-
tional TTS (ETC-TTS). ETC-TTS learns continuous neutral-to-
emotion transitions during training, enabling stable and inter-
pretable intensity control without heuristic inference-time ma-
nipulation. The main contributions of this work are summarized
as follows:
1.Emotion Trajectory Reformulation:We reformulate emo-
tion intensity control as trajectory learning in latent style
space, moving beyond inference-time embedding control.
2.Neutral-Centered Trajectory Learning:We introduce a
flow-matching objective [20] that explicitly learns continuous
transitions from neutral to emotional states, enabling consis-
tent intermediate intensity generation.
3.Emotion Prototype Representation:We design emotion
prototypes based on residual vector quantization (RVQ) [21],
yielding emotion anchors in latent style space.
Experimental results show improved monotonic controllability
while maintaining competitive speech quality over baselines.
These results suggest that emotion intensity should be learned
as a trajectory during training rather than imposed through
heuristic embedding manipulation at inference.

2.3. Prototype-Anchored Emotion Trajectory Learning
2.3.1. Emotion Trajectory Formulation

to a prototype-defined trajectory, reducing non-monotonic be-
havior where increasing the control factor does not necessarily
yield stronger emotion. Rather than applying heuristic embed-
ding scaling or interpolation as in [13, 15, 17], we formulate
emotion control as learning a continuous velocity field over in-
termediate states between neutral and emotional prototypes.

2.5. Training Procedure
Training is conducted in three sequential stages.
1.Prototype initialization:After one epoch of initial training,
RVQ codebooks are initialized using k-means clustering over
extracted style representations.
2.Extractor-focused training:The style extractor (and the
TTS backbone) is trained while the flow predictor is frozen
to stabilize emotion prototypes.
3.Joint training:All components are jointly optimized after
stabilization.
3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
We conducted experiments on two emotional speech datasets:
AIHub Korean emotional speech1 and ESD (Emotional Speech
Dataset)2. For the AIHub dataset, we used approximately
80 hours of emotional speech containing seven emotion cate-
gories (Angry, Anxiety, Embarrassment, Happy, Hurt, Neutral,
and Sad) spoken by four speakers. The data were split into
38,334/1,500/500 training/validation/test samples. Audio was
converted into 22.05-kHz, 80-bin mel-spectrograms, with pitch,
energy, and phoneme durations extracted using PyWorld 3 and
MFA [30]. For English experiments, we used the ESD dataset,
which contains 350 parallel sentences recorded by 10 speakers
under five emotions (neutral, happy, angry, sad, and surprise),
resulting in 17,500 utterances. We used 50/100 utterances for
validation/test and the remaining 17,350 for training. Experi-
ments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU.

3.2. Implementation Details
All models were built on the same FastSpeech2 backbone, and
only the style modeling component (i.e., emotion control mod-
ule) was replaced while all other architectural and training set-
tings remained identical. The resulting ETC-TTS model con-
tains approximately 34.1M trainable parameters. All models
were trained for 500k steps with a batch size of 32 using the
Adam optimizer [32]. For ETC-TTS, training was conducted in
two stages: the style extractor was first trained for 300k steps
with the style predictor frozen, followed by joint optimization
of all modules for the remaining steps.
3.3. Model Performance
Table 1 reports objective and subjective results on ESD and AI-
Hub. We compared ETC-TTS with three FastSpeech2-based
baselines: FastSpeech2 conditioned on emotion labels, Fast-
Speech2 with relative attribute (RA) control [9], and Fast-
Speech2 with scaling factor (SF) control [13]. Ground-truth
and vocoder reconstruction results are also provided for refer-
ence. We focused on comparisons under a unified FastSpeech2
backbone to ensure that differences in acoustic modeling do
not confound the evaluation of style modeling performance.
Evaluation metrics include mean opinion score (MOS) for nat-
uralness (N-MOS) and emotion expression (E-MOS) for sub-
jective assessment, and character error rate (CER) [33], F0
RMSE (pitch accuracy), UTMOS [34], and emotion accuracy
(EmoAcc) [35] for objective evaluation. N-MOS and E-MOS
evaluations were conducted with 30 listeners, while the AB test
involved 25 listeners. The proposed ETC-TTS achieves consis-
tent improvements in emotion-related metrics, particularly E-
MOS and EmoAcc, while maintaining competitive speech nat-
uralness compared to baseline methods.
3.4. Design Analysis of Style Modeling
To assess key design choices, we compared three variants:
(1) the full model with RVQ-based prototyping and neutral-
centered flow, (2) a SER-based continuous embedding variant
with flow, and (3) a Gaussian-prior variant replacing the neutral-
centered source. Results show that replacing RVQ prototypes
with SER-derived embeddings significantly degrades emotion
recognition accuracy (EmoAcc), while using a Gaussian prior
instead of the neutral-centered source reduces speech quality
(lower N-MOS and UTMOS).

3.5. Intensity Controllability
Listeners performed a pairwise AB test by comparing two utter-
ances with different intensity levels and selecting the one per-
ceived as stronger. Selecting the lower-intensity sample was
considered an error (monotonicity violation), while selecting
“same”was not counted as an error since it does not contra-
dict the expected intensity ordering. Table 2 reports the AB
intensity error rate(%,↓), which measures violations of mono-
tonic intensity ordering. Compared to baseline methods, the
proposed model shows generally lower error rates across inten-
sity pairs, indicating improved monotonic controllability. Fig. 2
shows SER [35] probability trends across intensity levels. Here,
Mix denotes an interpolation method using the same model as
SF [13], where the emotion vector is interpolated with a neu-
tral vector at inference time [16]. Specifically, while SF applies
simple scaling (semoα), Mix performs neutral–emotion inter-
polation,ˆ sneu(1−α) +ˆ semoα, whereαis the scaling factor
controlling emotion intensity. The proposed method exhibited
more consistent intensity-dependent changes with 0.1-step con-
trol, while baseline methods showed less stable and partially
non-monotonic trajectories. RA generally follows an increas-
ing trend but shows fluctuations at higher intensity levels, while
SF and Mix exhibit limited SER probability changes at lower
intensities and sharper increases only at higher ranges (around
0.8–1.0), indicating less gradual intensity control.

3.7. Emotion-aware Style Representation
Fig. 3 visualizes latent style distributions using t-SNE for the
original RVQ and the proposed prototyped RVQ. Compared to
the original RVQ, the proposed method produced more compact
and clearly separated emotion clusters, indicating improved
emotion-aware structuring in latent style space. This result sug-
gests that prototyped RVQ encourages more consistent emotion
representation and reduces inter-emotion overlap.
4. Conclusion
We proposed ETC-TTS, a trajectory-based emotional TTS
framework that models emotion intensity as transitions in latent
style space. By learning neutral-to-emotion trajectories with
RVQ prototypes and flow matching, the model enables stable
intermediate intensity control without heuristic inference-time
manipulation. Experiments on AIHub and ESD demonstrate
improved monotonic controllability while maintaining compet-
itive speech quality compared to baselines.
