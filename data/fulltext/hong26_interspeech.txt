Abstract
Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) is a strong alternative to
relative positional encoding in modern automatic speech recog-
nition (ASR) encoder models. However, its discrete, uniformly-
spaced chronological index is misaligned with the continuous,
semantic nature of speech features. We propose Convolutional
Dynamic RoPE (CD-RoPE), a dynamic positional encoding
complementing Branchformer’s attention path by warping the
rotary temporal index conditioned on local acoustic context via
a lightweight depthwise-separable convolution. Additive posi-
tion modulation preserves RoPE’s harmonic structure, enabling
continuous input-dependent shifts. CD-RoPE achieves consis-
tent WER improvements over RelPos on Branchformer across
all LibriSpeech test sets with 2.2M fewer parameters. Targeted
robustness evaluation on Speech Robust Bench (SRB) confirms
that CD-RoPE’s advantage is strongest under temporal pertur-
bations, validating the temporal warping mechanism.

1. Introduction
Positional encoding mechanisms for transformer-based ASR
encoders, such as RelPos [1] and RoPE [2], assume discrete,
uniformly-spaced integer indices, which is an assumption mis-
aligned with the continuous and semantically elastic nature of
speech features. RoPE encodes position through rotation in a
complex plane, providing an effective relative inductive bias.
However, its rigid chronological index treats every frame as
equidistant, ignoring the variable information density across
speech segments. Furthermore, recent work [3] has shown that
RoPE can diverge during ASR training, highlighting the need
for more robust positional encoding. RelPos partially alleviates
this rigidity through learnable relative biases, but relies on lin-
ear projections that are less expressive for capturing temporal
dynamics in continuous-domain features.
Self-supervised models (wav2vec 2.0 [4], HuBERT [5],
WavLM [6]) use convolutional features as input-dependent po-
sitional encodings. However, these embeddings are highly cor-
related with the input, cannot preserve the magnitude of the
original feature, and are only applied at the first layer. CD-RoPE
addresses all three limitations through multiplicative integration
via RoPE’s rotation mechanism, applied independently at every
layer.
We propose Convolutional Dynamic RoPE (CD-RoPE),
a dynamic positional encoding that warps RoPE’s tem-
poral index conditioned on local acoustic context via a
lightweight depthwise-separable convolution. A bounded,
input-conditioned shift∆tis added to the base integer index
before multiplication by the inverse frequencies, preserving
RoPE’s harmonic structure, since the inverse frequencies re-
main pure outer multipliers of the shifted index, leaving the fre-
quency spectrum itself unperturbed while enabling continuous
and input-dependent temporal repositioning.
CD-RoPE is particularly well-suited to Branchformer [7],
whose attention path processes global context without inter-
mediate convolutional layers. Unlike Conformer [8] and E-
Branchformer [9], which incorporate convolution modules that
implicitly provide local temporal context within or adjacent
to the attention computation, Branchformer’s attention branch
operates without such convolutional context. The depthwise-
separable convolution in CD-RoPE fills this gap, providing
complementary local temporal modeling directly within the po-
sitional encoding.
We empirically demonstrate that CD-RoPE achieves con-
sistent WER improvements over RelPos on Branchformer
across all LibriSpeech [10] evaluation sets while using 2.2M
fewer parameters. Targeted evaluation on Speech Robust Bench
[11] further validates the temporal warping mechanism, with
the largest improvements observed under temporal perturba-
tions.
2. Related Works
2.1. Static Positional Encoding
The original Transformer architecture [12] is inherently
permutation-equivariant and relies on absolute sinusoidal po-
sitional encodings added directly to the input embeddings to
retain sequence order. Subsequent models, such as BERT
[13], demonstrated that absolute positional embeddings could
be fully parameterized and learned during training. However,
absolute encodings fail to generalize to sequence lengths un-
seen during training and struggle to represent the distance be-
tween tokens explicitly.
Relative Positional Encodings [14] were introduced to re-
solve this, modeling the pairwise distances between tokens, a
concept heavily refined in Transformer-XL (RelPos) [1]. More
recently, Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) [2] bridged the
gap between absolute and relative formulations by multiplying
the query and key representations with an orthogonal rotation
matrix in a complex plane. Although RoPE provides a highly
effective relative inductive bias, these static methods share a
fundamental limitation: they rely strictly on a rigid, discrete
chronological integer index (t∈Z), completely agnostic to the
underlying semantic or acoustic elasticity of the input signal.
ALiBi [15] replaces positional parameters with a static, linearly
decaying attention bias; while effective for length extrapolation,
this fixed approach cannot model input-dependent temporal dy-
namics.

2.2. Input-dynamic Positional Encoding
To move beyond static grid assumptions, recent literature in
Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV)
has explored input-dynamic positional encodings. In CV , Con-
ditional Positional Encodings for Vision Transformers (CPVT)
[16] introduced a Positional Encoding Generator (PEG) to dy-
namically generate encodings conditioned on the local visual
neighborhood. In NLP, Positional Encoding via Token-Aware
Phase Attention [17] proposed a mechanism to learn position
phase conditioned on token representations for language mod-
eling.
More recently, dynamic adaptations of RoPE have emerged.
Context-aware Rotary Position Embedding (CARoPE) [18] uti-
lizes a linear projection to modulate the frequency of the ro-
tation based on input dependencies. Similarly, Selective Ro-
tary Position Embedding [19] introduces an input-conditioned
RoPE mechanism designed specifically for gated linear atten-
tion models. Furthermore, ComRoPE [20] parameterizes the
rotational mechanism with trainable commuting angle matrices
to improve positional robustness and length extrapolation.
Despite these advancements in text and vision, applying
input-conditioned positional encodings remains heavily under-
explored in speech and audio modalities. Furthermore, exist-
ing dynamic RoPE methods in NLP typically modulate the fre-
quency or phase angle directly. In contrast, our proposed CD-
RoPE modulates the temporal index itself, physically warping
the time domain while preserving the crucial harmonic consis-
tency between low and high rotational frequencies necessary for
acoustic modeling.
2.3. Convolutional Positional Encoding
In the audio domain, convolutions have long been utilized to
inject local temporal awareness into otherwise permutation-
invariant architectures. Wav2vec 2.0 [4] successfully replaced
explicit absolute positional embeddings with a convolutional
feature encoder, using grouped convolutions to implicitly model
relative positional information directly from the raw audio
waveform. Similarly, the Conformer architecture [8] interca-
lates convolution modules between self-attention layers to cap-
ture local, fine-grained acoustic patterns, effectively acting as
an implicit relative positional encoding. E-Branchformer [9]
achieves strong ASR via enhanced branch merging, but its
convolutions extract general features rather than providing ex-
plicit positional modulation. Alternatively, Zipformer [21] by-
passes positional encoding entirely, modeling temporal dynam-
ics through variable-ratio resolution reduction.
While CPVT [16] demonstrated the efficacy of convo-
lutional positional encoding generators in vision, CD-RoPE
uniquely adapts this concept for the speech modality. By uti-
lizing a depthwise-separable 1D convolution as an acoustic and
semantic edge detector, CD-RoPE merges the local feature-
extraction strengths of convolutional models with the rigor-
ous multiplicative phase-shifting properties of RoPE. This al-
lows the model to explicitly learn continuous temporal shifts
(∆t) rather than simply adding a learned vector to the resid-
ual stream. This is particularly relevant for architectures like
Branchformer, where the attention path processes global con-
text without intermediate convolutional layers.
3. Methodology
3.1. Preliminaries
Transformer architecture relies on self-attention mechanisms
that are permutation-equivariant, inherently lacking the ability
to process sequential order. RoPE addresses this by encoding
absolute positional information through rotation matrices in a
complex plane. For a given token representation at chronolog-
ical time stept∈Z ≥0, the features are divided into pairs, and
each pairi∈[0,d/2)is rotated by an angleΘ t,i strictly defined
as:
Θt,i =t·θ i =t·b −2i/d (1)
wheredis the hidden dimension andbis a pre-defined base
frequency (typically 10,000). Although RoPE effectively in-
jects relative positional inductive bias, it relies on a discrete,
static integer indext, assuming that every sequential token rep-
resents an identical and inflexible duration within the semantic
space.
3.2. Convolutional Dynamic Rotary Positional Embedding
Instead of relying on a static, chronological, and discrete integer
indext, our proposed CD-RoPE (Figure 1) dynamically com-
putes a continuous positional shift∆tconditioned on the input
feature that contains rich acoustic context. We add the learned
time-shift∆tto the absolute position indextbefore multiplying
by the inverse frequenciesθ:
ˆΘt,i = (t+ ∆t t,i)·θ i (2)
The design choice of additive modulation on the time do-
main is intentional, in order to preserve the harmonic consis-
tency, thus creating a more robust dynamic positional shifting.
Because the inverse frequencyθi operates as an outer multiplier,
the physical properties of the rotation are preserved. This mech-
anism dynamically warps the underlying temporal grid while
preserving the phase relationships required for preserving ro-
bust acoustic information. We observed that modulating∆t
is more stable than directly modulating∆θ, where the latter
caused numerical instability and divergence during early train-
ing epochs. We use harmonic consistency to direct that the
inverse-frequency spectrumθ i enters only as an outer multi-
plier of the shifted index (2), so the geometric properties of ro-
tational frequencies across bands are left unperturbed and high-
frequency bands continue to rotate proportionally faster than
low-frequency ones. Because|∆t t,i|≤1, the additive index
shift contributes a per-band angular perturbation bounded by
θi, whereas modulating the frequency directly replacesθ i with
θi + ∆θi and injects a position-growing termt·∆θ i.
To extract the positional shift∆t∈R B×T×d/2 from
the acoustic context, we utilize a depthwise-separable 1D con-
volution over the time dimension of the input featureX∈
RB×T×d model. Here, the dimensionality of∆tis set tod/2
since RoPE operates on pairs of dimensions, requiringd/2shift
values. To ensure that the position shift does not overlap with
base time stepstand to prevent potential temporal collapse
(where positions merge to a single scalar), the predicted shift
is bounded as∆t∈[−1,1]via a hyperbolic tangent (tanh) ac-
tivation. For training stability, the output is scaled by a ReZero-
style [22] learnable parameterα:
∆t=α·tanh(Conv1D(X))(3)

In standard Transformer implementations, the hidden di-
mensiondof query and key representations are split intoH
attention heads prior to applying RoPE, forcing every feature
across all heads at steptto share an identical temporal coordi-
nate. An alternative design would compute∆tdirectly per-head
in the lower-dimensional head space (d head =d/H). CD-
RoPE instead computes the positional shift across the full d-
dimensional embedding before head splitting, reducing tod/2
shift values via the depthwise-separable convolution. This de-
sign choice is motivated by the fact that the full-dimensional
convolution operates over a richer feature space, capturing
cross-feature temporal correlations that would be inaccessible
within the narrower per-head view (d head = 64in our config-
uration vs.d= 512). As a result, each of thed/2positional
shifts can be informed by the complete local acoustic context
rather than a single head’s subspace. After head splitting, differ-
ent heads receive different subsets of these shifts, naturally en-
abling per-head temporal diversity without requiring indepen-
dent per-head computation.
4. Experiments
4.1. LibriSpeech ASR
We train and evaluate transformer-based ASR models on the
LibriSpeech dataset [10], which consists of 960 hours of la-
beled speech. We train the models on the dataset corpus of con-
catenating ‘train-clean-100’, ‘train-clean-360’, and ‘train-other-
500’. We evaluate the models on ‘dev-clean’, ‘test-clean’, and
‘test-other’ datasets. We utilize the SpeechBrain toolkit [23] to
incorporate the proposed method into existing ASR transformer
models and launch experiments.
We apply CD-RoPE to Branchformer [7], a transformer-
based ASR encoder whose parallel MLP-attention architecture
processes local and global context through separate branches.
We compare the proposed positional encoding method with the
well-established RelPos [1] as a baseline. We do not include
vanilla RoPE as a baseline because prior work [3] reports train-
ing divergence of RoPE on Conformer-based ASR, and we ob-
served similar instability in our preliminary experiments. Since
vanilla RoPE fails to converge to a usable model under these
training conditions, direct comparison is uninformative. All
models are trained from scratch with identical architectures and
hyperparameters, varying only the positional encoding type.
The detailed configurations are as follows. We compare Rel-
Pos (109.8M parameters) and CD-RoPE (107.6M parameters),
consisting of 18 encoder layers and 6 decoder layers, following
the standard SpeechBrain Branchformer recipe. Both models
use encoder modules of model dimension 512, 8 heads, FFN
expansion factor of 4, and GELU activation in the FFN. Also,
all models share the same decoder and language model (LM) ar-
chitectures. We also matched to use the same hyperparameters
for training across the models, including the gradient accumu-
lation steps of 8, allowing the global batch size to exceed 128.
We followed the hyperparameter settings as initialized in the
SpeechBrain toolkit, and the exhaustive list of hyperparameters
can be found in the published recipes [23].
For CD-RoPE module, we select depthwise-separable con-
volution with kernel size 9, tanh bounding, and ReZero-style
scaling [22] throughout the experiment. We select kernel size
9 based on preliminary sweeps conducted on the smaller train-
clean-100 subset (100h), which showed minimal WER variation
amongk∈{7,9,11}(Table 1), suggesting the method is ro-
bust to this hyperparameter. The main experiments (Table 2) use
the full 960h training set. We use tanh bounding and ReZero-
style learnable gating parameter, consistent with the stability
analysis in Section 3.2.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we introduce CD-RoPE, a positional encoding
method that extracts continuous semantic information from
the incoming hidden representations of the transformer, trans-
lates into a continuous positional time shift∆t, and seam-
lessly integrates continuous dynamic positional offsets to the
transformer by rotation. We empirically demonstrate that
CD-RoPE achieves consistent WER improvements on Branch-
former across all LibriSpeech evaluation sets while using 2.2M
fewer parameters than RelPos. Targeted robustness evaluation
on Speech Robust Bench further validates the temporal warping
mechanism, with CD-RoPE showing its largest advantages un-
der temporal perturbations where adaptive repositioning is most
beneficial.
Our evaluation is intentionally confined to Branchformer
to isolate the temporal-warping mechanism from confound-
ing intermediate convolutions, ensuring observed gains are at-
tributable to the positional encoding rather than added con-
volutional capacity. Establishing whether the gains trans-
fer to architectures with built-in convolution (Conformer, E-
Branchformer), to large self-supervised encoders, and to cor-
pora with higher acoustic variability and out-of-domain conver-
sational speech, is left to future work. In addition, a formal
ablation of individual components is deferred to an extended
version of this work. We also acknowledge the following lim-
itations: (a) evaluation on a single dataset with a single seed,
and (b) the lack of a direct mechanistic interpretability analysis
on the learned∆t, although the consistency of improvements
across all three evaluation splits and temporal perturbation on
SRB suggests systematic architectural effect and its temporal
warping nature.
