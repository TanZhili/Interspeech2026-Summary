Abstract
The fixed-filter methods are widely employed in active noise
control (ANC) systems due to their computational efficiency.
They are typically designed to minimize the mean squared
sound pressure at the error microphone, thereby attenuating
disturbance components across the entire frequency spectrum.
Due to the limited low-frequency reproduction capability of mi-
cro loudspeakers, this approach is prone to causing mechanical
over-excursion. One solution is to cascade a high-pass filter
before or after the control filter to limit the power of the out-
put signal. But the group delay introduced by this filter in-
creases the electronic latency. This paper proposes an ANC
method with frequency-response gain constraints, where gain
constraints are imposed on both the low-frequency range and
the target noise reduction (NR) frequency range of the control
filter’s frequency response. Simulation and experimental results
confirm the effectiveness of our proposed method in terms of
NR performance.
Index Terms: Active Noise Control, fixed-filter, mechanical
over-excursion, frequency-response gain constraint
1. Introduction
Active noise control (ANC) exploits the principle of signal su-
perposition by generating an anti-noise signal from a secondary
source that has the same amplitude but opposite phase as the un-
wanted noise [1, 2, 3], thereby attenuating it. ANC is widely ap-
plied in devices such as ventilation ducts [4], headphones [5, 6],
and automotive cabins [7, 8].
With the growing demand for portability, smart devices
such as smartphones and smart glasses have limited the allow-
able size of micro loudspeakers. The limited low-frequency
response of micro loudspeakers makes compact ANC devices
prone to mechanical over-excursion and nonlinear distortion
when the output exceeds physical limits [9]. Although nonlin-
ear adaptive algorithms have been proposed to mitigate such
distortions, once the control signal amplitude substantially ex-
ceeds the threshold, neither linear nor nonlinear ANC algo-
rithms can deliver sufficient power or mechanical momentum to
cancel the disturbance [10]. An unconstrained filter inherently
provides excessively high gain at low frequencies, which com-
presses the available dynamic range within the effective can-
cellation band and tends to cause mechanical over-excursion
[11]. A frequency-domain adaptive ANC method with gain
constraints has been proposed to reduce the control filter re-
sponse in frequency regions where the gain constraint is vio-
lated [12]. However, conventional full-band output constraints
are also unsuitable, since micro-speakers exhibit particularly

restricted low-frequency capability [13, 14]. Moreover, given
the divergence risk and high computational cost of adaptive al-
gorithms, fixed-filter methods are more practical and widely
adopted in commercial products [15, 16]. A simple clipping
approach may produce many high-frequency components [17].
Cascading a band-stop filter before or after the fixed filter can
suppress low-frequency output power, but the added group de-
lay increases the electronic latency of the ANC system, leading
to a lower NR upperbound.
In this paper, we focus on micro loudspeakers in ANC
systems, which are prone to low-frequency mechanical over-
excursion. To address this issue, a low-frequency gain con-
straint is introduced for training the fixed filter, referred to as
the ANC method with low-frequency frequency-response con-
straint (ANC-LF-FRC). This method ensures that the output
signal remains within the low-frequency gain limit without in-
creasing the group delay. However, experimental results show
that applying the constraint solely in the low-frequency range
induces the Gibbs phenomenon at discontinuities in the desired
frequency response, thereby degrading NR performance. To
mitigate this issue, an additional constraint is imposed on other
frequencies within the target NR band. This method is here-
after referred to as the ANC method with frequency-response
constraint (ANC-FRC). This work represents a new idea for
pre-training fixed filters considering gain constraints, which can
guarantee that the dynamic range is utilized where the micro-
loudspeaker is physically capable of cancelling noise.
The remaining parts of this paper are organized as follows:
Section 2 presents the ANC methods, including the uncon-
strained Wiener-Hopf method and the ANC method consider-
ing the frequency response constraint. Evaluation metrics and
experimental results are shown in Section 3. Finally, Section 4
concludes this paper.
2. ANC With a Frequency-response Gain
Constraint for Micro-Loudspeakers
We first review the application of the Wiener–Hopf equation in
ANC systems, which forms the basis of the proposed method.
Second, the ANC-LF-FRC method is used to prevent low-
frequency output saturation. Third, to alleviate the Gibbs phe-
nomenon caused by the low-frequency constraint, the ANC-
FRC method is further introduced.

2.2. The ANC-LF-FRC Method
In practical ANC systems, the NR performance is closely de-
pendent on the reproduction capability of loudspeakers. A key
challenge arises from the limited capability of micro loudspeak-
ers to reproduce low-frequency components, especially in com-
pact devices. Excessive input in this range may cause hardware
overload or signal distortion. Consequently, the constraints are
imposed on the low-frequency range of the control filter to avoid
excessive amplification at specific frequencies. The noise sup-
pression problem under output constraints can be formulated as
an optimization problem:

performance [22, 23], as demonstrated in Section 3.2.

3.3. The experimental performance of the ANC-FRC
method
Experiments are conducted with different real-world noises to
evaluate the noise attenuation performance of the proposed
ANC method. To protect the loudspeaker’s lifespan, the con-
trol signal must be processed before being fed into it. Conven-
tional methods such as cascading a highpass filter after the con-
trol filter are used due to its convenience. This highpass filter
is implemented as a first-order Butterworth filter with a cutoff
frequency of 400 Hz.
The NR curves for two noise types are presented in Fig-
ure 5. Among the methods, the proposed ANC-FRC method
demonstrates superior NR performance. Figure 6 shows the fre-
quency response of the control filter trained by different ANC
methods. It can be observed that the frequency response of the
control filter trained by the ANC-FRC method is more similar
to that of the ANC-Wiener method within the 400–2000 Hz fre-
quency band, due to theℓ 2 norm regularization term of (9).

To better illustrate the NR performance of ANC methods,
the NR levels in different frequency bands are obtained by av-
eraging the results over 10 independent Monte Carlo experi-
ments. A comparative analysis of the average NR performance
across three methods is summarized in Table 1. Among them,
ANC-Wiener is an unconstrained method that provides suffi-
cient gain at low frequencies to achieve anti-noise cancellation.
However, due to the limited low-frequency output capability of
micro loudspeakers in practice, this method leads to mechan-
ical overdrive, thereby degrading the service life of the loud-
speakers. It is found that the ANC-Highpass method degrades
NR performance outside the constrained frequency band, which
is attributed to the group delay. The additional group delay at
low-frequencies results in phase distortion, which accounts for
the limited ANC performance achieved by the ANC-Highpass
method. Therefore, to avoid the adverse impact of group de-
lay on noise reduction, constraining the low-frequency response
during filter design offers a valid solution. Moreover, the ANC-
FRC method achieves the best overall performance across a
wide frequency range. The results remain consistent across both
train and white noise types, confirming the robustness and effi-
cacy of the ANC-FRC method.
4. Conclusion
Considering the practical limitations of micro loudspeakers in
applications, the proposed gain constraint is designed to pre-
vent low-frequency overload while maintaining satisfactory NR
performance. However, experimental results show that apply-
ing the gain constraint only in the low-frequency range may
cause Gibbs phenomenon, thereby degrading NR performance.
In contrast, the proposed ANC-FRC method achieves superior
NR performance while avoiding mechanical over-excursion at
low frequencies. Both simulation and experimental results fur-
ther confirm its effectiveness.
