OPERA-Net: Octave-aware Phase-sensitive Enhanced Recognition
Architecture for Singing V oice Deepfake Detection
Fengwei Ye1, Kun Zeng1,∗
1 Sun Yat-sen University, China
yefw@mail2.sysu.edu.cn, zengkun2@mail.sysu.edu.cn
Abstract
The proliferation of high-fidelity singing voice synthesis re-
quires robust Singing V oice Deepfake Detection (SVDD) coun-
termeasures. However, state-of-the-art speech spoofing detec-
tors degrade significantly when applied to singing. This vul-
nerability arises from dense background music (BGM) interfer-
ence and reliance on magnitude spectra, which discard critical
phase discontinuities. To address these, we propose OPERA-
Net, a novel dual-stream architecture for SVDD. We introduce
a Phase-Consistent CQT (PC-CQT) representation that models
instantaneous frequency derivatives to capture phase anoma-
lies. A Semantic-Guided Gating mechanism leverages pre-
trained WavLM semantic priors to filter the PC-CQT stream,
isolating vocal forgery traces from BGM. Cross-corpus evalua-
tions on the CtrSVDD and SingFake datasets show OPERA-Net
achieves state-of-the-art performance, with superior generaliza-
tion over existing baselines.
Index Terms: singing voice deepfake detection, anti-spoofing,
phase representation
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of singing voice synthesis (SVS) has
been supported by variational models [1], generative adversar-
ial networks [2, 3], and diffusion-based methods [4], opening
up a new stage for digital creativity. Meanwhile, the generation
of high-fidelity singing voices and highly realistic vocal cloning
technology has also brought about serious security risks, such
as copyright infringement, voice impersonation, and the spread
of misinformation. For this reason, research on audio anti-
spoofing defense has gradually attracted widespread attention,
especially under the promotion of the ASVspoof [5] and ADD
[6] projects.
In the field of speech anti-spoofing, early methods mainly
focused on detecting abnormal subband spectral distributions
using handcrafted features, including Constant-Q Cepstral Co-
efficients (CQCC) [7] and Linear Frequency Cepstral Coeffi-
cients (LFCC) [8]. After that, the research paradigm gradually
moved toward data-driven structures, ranging from Light CNNs
(LCNN) [9] to end-to-end models such as RawNet2 [10] and
AASIST [11], as well as large-scale self-supervised learning
(SSL) models like Wav2Vec 2.0 [12] and WavLM [13]. Al-
though these methods have achieved considerable success in
speech detection, state-of-the-art detectors designed for speech
usually suffer from obvious performance degradation when di-
rectly used for Singing V oice Deepfake Detection (SVDD) [14].
This limitation mainly comes from two unique acoustic
challenges in singing. First, compared with speech, singing

has a wider dynamic range, obvious vibrato, and a far more
complex harmonic structure. Although some early datasets in-
cluding SingFake [14] and CtrSVDD [15] have been built, spe-
cific detection methods for singing are still relatively scarce.
Most existing baseline systems directly adopt models originally
designed for speech, and often use Mel-spectrograms as in-
put, which lack sufficient high-frequency resolution to describe
subtle spectral smearing artifacts produced by advanced neu-
ral vocoders. In addition, although some recent studies have
explored noise-robust representations from speech foundation
models like Whisper [16] for SVDD, it is still difficult to effec-
tively capture fine-grained forgery traces. Dense instrumental
accompaniment in real audio acts as a strong acoustic mask that
hides typical fake characteristics, and few models take the ini-
tiative to isolate vocal components to suppress such polyphonic
interference.
More importantly, traditional audio forensics methods rely
heavily on magnitude-based representations, such as STFT
magnitude spectra and Mel-spectrograms, while ignoring cru-
cial structural information contained in the phase domain [17].
Recent studies have shown that even advanced high-fidelity
neural vocoders [18] struggle to maintain consistent phase evo-
lution between frames, often leaving distinct phase discon-
tinuity artifacts that sound perceptually metallic [19]. The
Constant-Q Transform (CQT) is widely used in music informa-
tion retrieval because its logarithmic frequency resolution aligns
perfectly with musical octaves [20, 21]. However, conven-
tional CQT pipelines only extract magnitude spectra and discard
phase-related anomalies. Although instantaneous frequency
(IF) features have demonstrated potential in audio anti-spoofing
[22], reliably extracting stable and discriminative phase cues
from real singing mixtures remains extremely challenging due
to the masking effect of background accompaniments [23].
To solve these problems, we propose OPERA-Net (Octave-
aware Phase-sensitive Enhanced Recognition Architecture),
a novel dual-stream framework specifically built for robust
SVDD. To capture vocoder artifacts at the signal level, we for-
mulate a Phase-Consistent CQT (PC-CQT) representation. Un-
like conventional methods that focus solely on spectral ampli-
tudes, PC-CQT explicitly models the instantaneous frequency
(i.e., the temporal derivative of phase), enabling the network to
accurately detect microscopic phase discontinuities in synthe-
sized singing. Meanwhile, to alleviate the interference caused
by background music, we design a Semantic-Guided Gating
module. This module leverages the rich linguistic and acoustic
context information from the pre-trained WavLM [13] branch to
adaptively filter PC-CQT features. By dynamically generating
a soft attention mask, the module amplifies the perception of
forgery artifacts in vocal segments while effectively dampening
non-vocal polyphonic noise.

2. Proposed Method
In this work, we propose OPERA-Net (Octave-aware Phase-
sensitive Enhanced Recognition Architecture), a unified frame-
work designed to detect singing voice deepfakes by synergizing
physical signal traces with semantic inconsistencies. As illus-
trated in Figure 1, the architecture consists of three key com-
ponents: a Phase-Consistent CQT (PC-CQT) stream that cap-
tures vocoder-induced phase discontinuities; a Semantic Stream
leveraging a pre-trained WavLM model to encode high-level
prosodic features; and a Semantic-Guided Gating mechanism
that adaptively fuses these multi-view representations.
2.1. Phase-Consistent CQT Stream
Unlike speech, singing voice is characterized by significant
pitch variations and high-frequency harmonics. While the stan-
dard Constant-Q Transform (CQT) provides excellent pitch-
aligned frequency resolution, it typically discards phase infor-
mation, relying solely on magnitude spectra. However, neural
vocoders often fail to reconstruct continuous phase patterns in
high-frequency bands, leading to perceptible artifacts.
To capture these anomalies, we propose the Phase-
Consistent CQT (PC-CQT). Given a raw waveformx, we first
compute its complex CQT spectrumX(k,n)∈C K×N , where
kandndenote the frequency bin and time frame index, respec-
tively. The complex spectrum can be expressed as:
X(k,n) =M(k,n)·e jϕ(k,n) (1)
whereM(k,n)is the magnitude andϕ(k,n)is the phase.
Standard approaches only utilize the log-magnitude spec-
trumS mag = log(1 +M(k,n)). In OPERA-Net, we explic-
itly model the temporal evolution of the phase by calculating the
Instantaneous Frequency (IF) derivative. The unwrapped phase
difference is computed as:
Sphase(k,n) =wrap(ϕ(k,n)−ϕ(k,n−1))(2)
where wrap(·)maps values to the range(−π,π]. This deriva-
tive feature highlights the unnatural phase jumps often present
in synthetic singing.
Finally, we stack the magnitude and phase features channel-
wise to form the PC-CQT inputX pc∈R 2×K×N . This dual-
channel tensor is processed by a lightweight ResNet-18 encoder
to extract the signal-level embeddingFsig∈R C×T .
2.2. Semantic Stream via WavLM
To capture long-range dependencies and semantic inconsisten-
cies, we employ WavLM Base+ [13], a state-of-the-art self-
supervised model pre-trained on 94k hours of audio. WavLM
is particularly effective at modeling the naturalness of human
vocal production.
The raw waveform is fed into the WavLM encoder. To adapt
the model to the SVDD task while preventing overfitting, we
freeze the bottom 6 transformer layers and fine-tune the top 6
layers. The output of the final hidden state is projected to the
feature dimensionCvia a linear layer, yielding the semantic
embeddingFsem∈R C×T . Note that temporal downsampling
is applied to align the time resolution ofFsem withFsig.
2.3. Semantic-Guided Gating Fusion
A major challenge in SVDD is the interference from back-
ground music, which dominates the CQT spectrum in non-vocal
regions. Naive concatenation ofF sig andF sem would intro-
duce significant noise.
To address this, we propose a Semantic-Guided Gating
mechanism. The intuition is to leverage the semantic knowl-
edge from WavLM (which can distinguish vocals from accom-
paniment) to “gate” the signal features. Specifically, we gener-
ate a soft attention maskGfrom the semantic features:
G=σ(MLP(F sem))(3)
whereσ(·)is the Sigmoid function, andG∈[0,1] C×T .

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets and Metrics
To rigorously evaluate the proposed OPERA-Net, we leverage
two distinct benchmarks: the controlled CtrSVDD dataset and
the ”in-the-wild” SingFake dataset.
CtrSVDD [15]:A dataset specifically designed for con-
trolled singing voice deepfake detection. The training and de-
velopment sets comprise 59 and 55 singers, generated using var-
ious SVS and SVC algorithms (labeled A01-A08). The evalua-
tion set contains 48 singers with unseen spoofing attacks (A09-
A13). Following the SVDD 2024 Challenge official protocol
[24], the primary ranking metric is the pooled Equal Error Rate
(EER) across attacks A09 to A13.
SingFake [14]:A challenging dataset consisting of ”in-
the-wild” audio tracks collected from social media, featuring
heavy background music and diverse recording environments.
We evaluate our model across three distinct subsets with in-
creasing difficulty: T01 (seen singers), T02 (unseen singers),
and T03 (unseen singers processed through diverse communi-
cation codecs).
We adopted the Equal Error Rate (EER, %) as the pri-
mary metric to quantify system performance. For CtrSVDD,
pooled EER is adopted to strictly penalize score calibration
shifts across different unseen attacks.
3.2. Implementation Details
All acoustic inputs were resampled to 16 kHz and segmented
into fixed-length 4-second windows (via cropping or zero-
padding). The Phase-Consistent CQT (PC-CQT) front-end was
implemented with the nnAudio toolkit [25], using a minimum
frequency off min = 32.7Hz, 84 total frequency bins (12
bins per octave), and a hop length of 320 samples to ensure
strict temporal alignment with the WavLM [13] embeddings.
OPERA-Net was trained using the AdamW optimizer [26] with
a layer-wise learning rate decay strategy to prevent catastrophic
forgetting of the pre-trained WavLM [13] semantic backbone.

The baseline comprising solely the fine-tuned WavLM
stream yields a 2.85% EER on CtrSVDD and 6.15% on

CQT branch provides moderate performance gains. However,
upgrading to our Phase-Consistent CQT (PC-CQT) configura-

derivatives are indispensable for exposing neural vocoder arti-

The full OPERA-Net architecture further enhances per-
formance by replacing naive feature concatenation with the
Semantic-Guided Gating mechanism. Notably, the proposed
gating mechanism yields consistent improvements on both
datasets, reducing the EER from 1.82% to 1.54% on CtrSVDD
and from 4.95% to 4.72% on SingFake. The gain on the
more challenging in-the-wild SingFake benchmark suggests
that semantic-guided attention helps suppress non-vocal inter-
ference and improves robustness under complex polyphonic
conditions.
4. Conclusion
In this study, we introduced OPERA-Net, a dual-stream ar-
chitecture specifically tailored to overcome the unique chal-
lenges of Singing V oice Deepfake Detection (SVDD). To ad-
dress the blind spots of conventional magnitude-centric speech
countermeasures, OPERA-Net explicitly targets the acoustic
hallmarks of synthesized singing. By formulating the Phase-
Consistent CQT (PC-CQT) representation, the network effec-
tively exposes the microscopic phase discontinuities charac-
teristically left by neural vocoders. Complementarily, our
Semantic-Guided Gating mechanism dynamically isolates these
vocal anomalies from dense background accompaniment by
leveraging top-down semantic priors.
Cross-corpus evaluations on the CtrSVDD and SingFake
benchmarks establish that OPERA-Net achieves state-of-the-art
detection performance, exhibiting exceptional generalization in
complex, ”in-the-wild” acoustic environments. Comprehensive
ablation studies further validated the indispensable roles of both
instantaneous frequency derivatives and semantic acoustic fil-
tering.
