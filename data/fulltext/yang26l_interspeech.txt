Abstract
While zero-shot text-to-speech models perform well in global
voice cloning, they struggle with fine-grained prosodic con-
trol, as strict word-level intensity and tempo manipulation can
disrupt acoustic priors and introduce artifacts. We propose
CraftTTS1, a three-stage framework enabling stable word-level
control without sacrificing fluency. First, a compute-driven
zero-shot pipeline automatically constructs large-scale prefer-
ence pairs without manual annotation. Second, joint supervised
fine-tuning and direct preference optimization improve sensitiv-
ity to local prosodic tags. Third, group relative policy optimiza-
tion with a multi-dimensional prosodic reward balances local
controllability and global naturalness by regulating intelligibil-
ity, intensity contrast, and rhythm. Experiments demonstrate
that CraftTTS achieves state-of-the-art fine-grained expressive-
ness while preserving zero-shot generation capability.
Index Terms: text-to-speech, fine-grained prosody control,
preference optimization, reinforcement learning
1. Introduction
Humans convey semantic nuance through prosody by modu-
lating intensity and speaking rate, forming temporally local-
ized linguistic structures known as speech cadence. Modeling
these fine-grained variations is essential for natural, expressive,
and context-aware speech synthesis. While early research fo-
cused on phoneme- or word-level acoustic modeling [1, 2, 3],
the advent of large language models (LLMs) [4] has propelled
modern Text-to-Speech (TTS) models into unified generative
frameworks capable of high-fidelity synthesis and zero-shot
voice cloning with strong contextual understanding. To reg-
ulate speaking style, contemporary methods primarily rely on
reference-based transfer [5, 6], structured prosody tags [7, 8, 9],
natural language instructions [10, 11, 12], or explicit word-level
acoustic planning [13, 14, 15].
Despite their impressive global generation capabilities, cur-
rent systems struggle with stable, fine-grained prosodic control.
Enforcing strict word-level instructions often introduces acous-
tic artifacts, unnatural pauses, or global emotional leakage, dis-
rupting overall fluency. This limitation stems from two core
challenges: first, a severe scarcity of precisely aligned expres-
sive speech data without human annotation; second, an inher-
ent conflict within autoregressive LLMs, where aggressive lo-
calized acoustic conditioning disrupts the global acoustic prior.
These unresolved challenges motivate a critical question:Can
we systematically align modern LLM-based TTS systems to

To answer this, motivated by the potential of structured
prosody tags, we proposeCraftTTS, a data-efficient three-
stage alignment framework for stable word-level prosody con-
trol, resolving the conflict between strict local instructions and
global naturalness. In Stage 1, we adopt a compute-driven zero-
shot synthesis strategy to construct high-quality preference data
without manual annotation. An external teacher model (In-
dextts2 [5]) is guided via multi-round reasoning to produce tag-
consistent speech. Multiple candidates are generated and fil-
tered using a “best-of-N” strategy to select the optimal sample,
improving tag adherence and acoustic quality while avoiding
self-sampling bias. These optimal samples serve as positive an-
chors for constructing contrastive preference pairs. In Stage 2,
we adapt the target backbone (CosyV oice 2 [7]) via a joint Su-
pervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Direct Preference Optimiza-
tion (DPO) [16, 17] strategy, anchoring the model’s sensitivity
to local intensity and tempo tags. Finally, in Stage 3, to resolve
the fundamental trade-off between strict local control and global
naturalness, we introduce Group Relative Policy Optimization
(GRPO) [18] governed by a multi-dimensional prosodic reward
system. Rather than rigidly forcing acoustic parameters, our
decoupled rewards are grounded in universal speech dynamics:
preserving general intelligibility, anchoring global emotion to
amplify local intensity contrasts (strong/weak), and contin-
uously regularizing rhythm to prevent tempo over-optimization.
This design prevents extreme acoustic distortion and ensures
stable alignment.
Experimental results demonstrate that our approach funda-
mentally breaks the performance bottleneck of localized con-
trol. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
•Zero-shot Data Pipeline:We propose a scalable, compute-
driven synthesis pipeline that leverages LLMs and a separate
robust TTS teacher to automatically construct large-scale,
high-quality prosody preference pairs. This completely elim-
inates the need for human annotation while preventing self-
generation degradation.
•Unified Audio Alignment Paradigm:We introduce a sys-
tematic adaptation of the LLM alignment pipeline (SFT→
DPO→GRPO) specifically designed for discrete audio to-
kens, establishing precise local controllability without de-
stroying the pre-trained autoregressive prior.
•Multi-Dimensional Prosody Rewards:We design a uni-
versally applicable, decoupled reward mechanism targeting
intelligibility, intensity, and tempo. Extensive experiments
show that CraftTTS achieves state-of-the-art perceptual nat-
uralness and fine-grained expressiveness while preserving the
robust zero-shot voice cloning capabilities of the backbone.

2. Methodology
2.1. Overview
As shown in Fig. 1, we propose a three-stage framework for
fine-grained word-level prosody control in zero-shot TTS. We
first construct preference data via multi-round sampling, then
establish basic tag controllability through SFT and DPO. Fi-
nally, we apply GRPO with multi-dimensional prosodic rewards
to improve robustness and suppress acoustic artifacts.
2.2. Stage 1: Fine-Grained Preference Data Construction
Modern speech synthesis increasingly adopts LLM-based archi-
tectures that model text and acoustic tokens in a unified discrete
space, where prosodic control tags are essential for eliciting
latent acoustic behaviors. Shifting from heuristic acoustic ex-
traction to semantics-driven alignment, we leverage the contex-
tual reasoning ability of LLMs to infer semantic emphasis and
rhythm from linguistic context, enabling the assignment of fine-
grained span-level prosodic tags. Combined with autoregressive
multi-round inference and tag-conditioned multi-sampling from
pre-trained TTS backbones, this pipeline automatically builds a
high-quality preference dataset, alleviating the scarcity of word-
level prosody supervision without human annotation.
1) LLM-driven Word-level Prosody Annotation.We first
leverage the natural language understanding and contextual
reasoning capabilities of DeepSeek-V3 [19] to analyze large-
scale plain text corpora. The model is prompted to perform
prosodic analysis from the perspective of a language expert, in-
serting context-aware prosodic annotations. Specifically, four
tags are introduced at the word or phrase level:strongand
weakfor local intensity, andfastandslowfor speak-
ing rate control. For example, annotated segments appear as
<strong>words</strong>. These tags serve as explicit
fine-grained control instructions for downstream synthesis.
2) Multi-round Autoregressive Inference.We adopt a multi-
round inference paradigm for fine-grained prosodic control.
Exploiting the reference sensitivity of Indextts2 [5], tagged
segments (strong,weak) are guided by randomly sampled
target-specific style prompts. Rather than concatenating inde-
pendently stylized chunks, each segment is anchored to its pre-
ceding acoustic context by prepending the terminal acoustic to-
kens of the previous segment as an autoregressive prefix. This
token-level overlapping continuation forms an acoustic bridge
that propagates pitch and energy across control boundaries,
eliminating discontinuities while preserving global speech con-
tinuity under localized expressive constraints.
3) Tag-conditioned Multi-sampling Selection.We introduce a
dual-purpose multi-sampling mechanism that stabilizes speaker
identity and enables zero-shot speaking rate control under tag
conditioning.
•Timbre Preservation:To prevent speaker drift caused by al-
ternatingstrongandweakprompts, multiple candidates
are generated and the one with the highest cosine similarity
to the reference speaker embedding is selected, preserving
vocal identity under localized emphasis shifts.
•Speaking rate Control:Exploiting the stochasticity of autore-
gressive decoding, acoustic token length is used as a proxy
for speaking rate. The shortest candidate is selected forfast
tags and the longest forslowtags, achieving word-level
speed control without external duration modeling.
4) Preference Pair Construction.For DPO training, we
construct contrastive pairs(y w,yl), where the multi-round
prosody-controlled audio serves as the preferred sample and the
corresponding baseline synthesis without prosodic tags serves
as the rejected sample.

2.3. Stage 2: Fine-Grained Prosody Alignment
Since standard SFT does not penalize control failures, we for-
mulate word-level prosody control as a preference-optimization
problem and train CosyV oice 2 with joint SFT+DPO. This con-
trastive approach natively penalizes prosodic entanglement and
tag-ignoring behaviors, enforcing precise boundary and seman-
tic alignment without external reward models.

conditioned winning samples (yw) constructed in Stage 1 to es-

2.4. Stage 3: Synthesis Stability and Prosody Enhancement
Although DPO improves local tag adherence, overly aggressive
fine-grained conditioning may disturb the global acoustic prior
of the autoregressive LLM, resulting in prosodic exaggeration
and temporal instability in long-form generation. To reconcile
strict local controllability with utterance-level naturalness, we
introduce GRPO for discrete audio generation.
1) Policy Formulation.The SFT+DPO model is treated as the
active policyπθ. The state consists of the input text and previ-
ously generated acoustic tokens, and the action corresponds to
the next-token distribution.
2) Multi-Dimensional Prosody Reward Design.Instead of
binding rewards directly to specific tags, we ground optimiza-
tion in three universal dimensions of human prosody: intelli-
gibility, intensity, and tempo. The resulting composite reward
decouples these factors to ensure balanced optimization.

during RL exploration, we incorporate an ASR-based reward.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
Data.All datasets used in this work are Chinese. Texts for data
construction are drawn from subsets of AISHELL-3 [21] and
THCHS-30 [22]. To guide prosodic realization in Indextts2, we
build a style prompt pool from StoryTTS [23], selecting utter-
ances with emphatic (strong) and attenuated (weak) prosody.
Speaker identity prompts are sourced from an internal speech
dataset. Following the Stage 1 pipeline, we generate 9,330 ut-
terances for Stage 2 training, and sample an additional 3,224
text prompts for Stage 3. For evaluation, we use the Chinese
subset of InstructTTSEval [24], where texts are annotated with
the same LLM-based tagging pipeline as in training. Speaker
references are drawn from seed-tts-eval [25].
Training Details.All experiments were conducted on a single
node with 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. In Stage 2, joint SFT-DPO
fine-tuning was trained for 13 epochs using Adam with a learn-
ing rate of1×10−5. The DPO scaling factor was set toβ= 0.1,
and gradient clipping (max norm 5.0) was applied. In Stage 3,
we optimize the policy using GRPO with a clipped objective.

3.2. Evaluation Metrics
Objective Metrics.Linguistic fidelity is evaluated by CER
using the whisper-large-v3 model [20], and speaker similar-
ity (Sim.) with a pre-trained speaker verification model [26].
Prosody controllability is assessed via acoustic statistics, in-
cluding pitch standard deviation, pitch range, mean intensity,
intensity range, and characters per second (CPS). Pitch and in-
tensity reflect F0 and loudness variability, while CPS measures
speaking rate as the number of characters divided by the non-
silent duration after removing long silences via energy-based
voice activity detection.
Subjective Metrics.We conduct four 5-point MOS evalua-
tions: SMOS (speaker similarity), NMOS (prosodic natural-
ness), STMOS (stress matching forstrong/weak), and SP-
MOS (speed matching forfast/slow). Mean scores with
95% confidence intervals are reported.
3.3. Experimental Design
Stage-wise Performance Evaluation.We progressively eval-
uate the original CosyV oice 2 backbone, +SFT, +SFT+DPO,
and the full CraftTTS model on the same multi-speaker test
set. Objective metrics are computed on all 200 utterances. For
subjective evaluation, 5 samples are randomly selected for each
prosodic control tag.
Controlled Tag Sensitivity Analysis.To evaluate the expres-
siveness and stability of control tags, we construct 120 se-
mantically tagged short utterances (30 samples per control tag,
3 speakers). Short texts help reduce accumulated inference
variability. Each text is synthesized with and without tags,
and acoustic deviations are computed to quantify tag-induced
changes in intensity and speaking rate. This paired comparison
isolates the causal effect of tags, ensuring that measured acous-
tic shifts arise from explicit instructions rather than phonetic
variation or global prosodic drift.

4. Conclusion
We propose a three-stage training framework for stable word-
level prosody control in large generative TTS models, inte-
grating automatic preference data construction, preference op-
timization, and reinforcement learning. The method enhances
prosodic naturalness, transition smoothness, and tempo control-
lability while preserving intelligibility and speaker consistency.
Experimental and acoustic analyses demonstrate improved syn-
thesis stability and fine-grained cadence control. Future work
will investigate richer prosodic attributes and more scalable
training strategies for controllable speech generation.
