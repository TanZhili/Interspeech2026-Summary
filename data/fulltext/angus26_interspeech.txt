Abstract
We introduce Argmax Pro, a unified real-time inference system
that orchestrates three state-of-the-art billion-scale transformer
models for speech-to-text with speaker diarization and custom
vocabulary. The system is optimized to run efficiently on a wide
range of mobile devices across iOS and Android without re-
source contention with other apps and with minimal impact to
battery life and thermals. The goal of this system is to offer a
peer alternative to cloud-based systems for feature-rich speech
recognition with frontier-level accuracy. To achieve this, we
built Argmax Pro on top of concurrent research in context bias-
ing and streaming speaker diarization.

1. Introduction
Speech recognition has recently crossed critical mass of adop-
tion in high-stakes industries such as healthcare, legal and fi-
nance. In the consumer market, use cases such as dictation,
video captioning and meeting notes are becoming ubiquitous.
The initial wave of adoption was primarily driven by the avail-
ability of cloud-based feature-rich speech recognition platforms
([1] [2]). However, the limitations of cloud-based platforms
such as reliability of latency, intermittent service unavailabil-
ity, per-minute-of-audio pricing and data privacy concerns have
led to the development of on-device inference systems.
The first generation of on-device speech recognition sys-
tems were either missing key features or made an accuracy com-
promise when used in real-time mode ([3]). Hence, they were
not a viable alternative to cloud-based platforms. To deliver
on the promise of on-device speech recognition, Argmax Pro
solves the following challenges:
• Accuracy parity across pre-recorded and real-time streaming
audio input for Parakeet v2 ([4]), a state-of-the-art speech-to-

2. System
2.1. Streaming Inference for Non-Streaming Models
Parakeet v2 ([4]) is a state-of-the-art speech-to-text model that
natively supports pre-recorded audio input. For inference on
streaming audio input, the original Nvidia NeMo ([7]) imple-
mentation provides a chunk-based algorithm which leads to an
accuracy-latency trade-off.
In order to achieve accuracy parity across pre-recorded and
real-time streaming audio input for Parakeet v2 ([4]), we lever-
aged the streaming inference algorithm from [8] that allows the
model to correct its own errors before finalizing each emitted
transcript word, a capability offered by cloud-based platforms
where output text streams are classified asinterim(mutable)
andfinal(immutable).
2.2. Custom V ocabulary
Frontier accuracy for speech-to-text systems is not achievable
with a single general-purpose model. Every use case has
domain-specific jargon. Every user has personal context. We
harnessed the Canary v2 CTC [5] model using the CTC-WS
algorithm [9] to give users and developers the ability to create
their own frontier-level accuracy for their own use cases.
CTC-WS enabled Argmax Pro to efficiently scale the cus-
tom vocabulary size to 3000 words without impacting runtime
latency on mobile devices. In contrast, most cloud-based plat-
forms limit the custom vocabulary size to a less than 500 words
([1] [2]).
[10] analyzes the keyword recognition accuracy of Argmax
Pro with an emphasis on names (people, companies and prod-
ucts) in the wild and shows that Argmax Pro attains frontier-
level accuracy.
2.3. Real-world Robustness for Streaming Speaker Di-
arization
Streaming Sortformer v2 ([6]) is a state-of-the-art streaming
speaker diarization model as established on academic bench-
marks. After extensive use in production, we have noticed
that the model’s accuracy is sensitive to real-world variations
in acoustic conditions, speaker loudness and background noise.
To address this, we fine-tuned the model using synthetic data to
model these real-world variations using FastMSS ([11]).
2.4. On-device Inference for Mobile Devices
The most challenging part of deploying multiple billion-scale
transformer models on mobile devices and running them in real-
time for extended periods of time is to maintain all-day battery
life, stable thermal behavior and minimal resource contention
with other apps. In order to achieve this multitude of objectives,
we harnessed the Apple Neural Engine (ANE) ([12]) to run in-
ference on iPhone, iPad and Mac devices. On Android, we op-
timized the same models for Qualcomm & MediaTek NPUs as
well as Google Tensor TPUs.
