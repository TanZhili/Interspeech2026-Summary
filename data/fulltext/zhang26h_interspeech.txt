Abstract
Checked tones in Chinese languages often undergo smooth-
ing, yet their phonetic mechanisms and sociophonetic condi-
tioning remain understudied. This study examines the checked
tone (T6) in Kaihui Xiang and tests whether gender differences
indicate ongoing de-checking. UsingF 0, duration, and voice
quality, we compare checked and unchecked tones across gen-
ders. Results show complete coda loss, but T6 remains dis-
tinct through a stableF 0 contour and shorter duration. Phona-
tion cues are also reorganized, with laryngealization shifting
toward the syllable onset. Male speakers retain a conserva-
tive pattern with stronger durational truncation, while female
speakers show weaker temporal cues and innovative phonatory
dynamics. These patterns suggest Kaihui Xiang is in a late
de-checking stage: tonal contrast is phonologized but phonetic
grounding continues to shift, with gender asymmetry reflecting
a gradual, socially conditioned redistribution of acoustic cues.
Index Terms: Checked tone, Kaihui Xiang, gender difference
1. Introduction
Checked syllables are typically defined as those ending with an
oral or glottal stop coda. In Chinese languages, they are inex-
tricably linked to a historical tonal category known asrusheng
(“checked tone”). Because acoustic measurements of checked
syllables effectively characterize the tone category itself, and to
align with previous literature [1], we collectively refer to this
phenomenon as the “checked tone” hereafter.
Phonetically, checked tones are characterized by multiple
acoustic correlates beyond the presence of a final stop. They
typically exhibit significantly shorter duration, constrainedF 0
contours, and increased laryngealization, which is often re-
flected in flatter spectral tilt (e.g., lower H1 *-H2*) and reduced
harmonic-to-noise ratios [2, 3, 4]. Thus, checkedness consti-
tutes a multidimensional phonetic complex involvingF 0, dura-
tion and phonation.
Across Chinese languages, there is a well-documented di-
achronic tendency for checked tones to lose these features and
become “unchecked”, a process known asrusheng shuhua(“de-
checking” or smoothing of checked tones; e.g., [1, 5]). The
outcomes of this process vary along a continuum. At one end-
point, Mandarin represents the most advanced stage, in which
historical checked tones have fully merged with unchecked cat-
egories. At the conservative end, varieties such as Cantonese [2]
and certain Min varieties [6] retain robust oral stop codas. In-
termediate stages are observed in many Wu and Xiang varieties,
where the coda may weaken or disappear while other phonetic
correlates – such as shortened duration or marked phonation –
remain [4, 7]. In Urban Changsha Xiang, for example, only a
distinct tonal category persists without other phonetic features
of checkedness [7, 8].
Despite extensive acoustic analysis of checked tones, the
sociophonetic dimensions driving this attrition remain underex-
plored. Specifically, the role of gender in shaping this trajec-
tory is largely unknown. Since women frequently lead sound
changes in progress (e.g., [9, 10, 11]), gender-based asymme-
tries may provide crucial evidence regarding the active direc-
tion and stage of de-checking. To address this research gap, we
examine an understudied language variety hypothesized to be
undergoing an intermediate stage of this process: Kaihui Xi-
ang, a suburban subvariety of Changsha Xiang [12]. Unlike
Urban Changsha Xiang, which has already reached a late stage
of smoothing [7], the status of the checked tone in Kaihui Xiang
remains unclear.

If phonetic cues to checkedness are weakening but not en-
tirely lost, Kaihui Xiang offers a unique opportunity to examine
the late stages of coda attrition and subsequent tonal restructur-
ing. Crucially, if de-checking is an active sound change here, fe-
male speakers may exhibit more advanced smoothing – such as
longer vowel duration or reduced laryngealization – compared
to their male counterparts.
Against this background, the present study addresses the
following two research questions:
RQ1: What are the phonetic characteristics of the checked
tone (T6) in Kaihui Xiang across vowel duration,F 0 contour,
and voice quality?

2. Methods
To address these questions, we conducted a controlled produc-
tion experiment investigating the acoustic features and gender
differences of the checked tone in Kaihui Xiang.
2.1. Participants
The study recruited sixteen native speakers of Kaihui Xiang (9
female, 7 male; age range: 42–80, Mean=60.19, SD=12.86).
This older demographic was intentionally selected to reflect the
local sociolinguistic landscape, as younger generations increas-
ingly favor Mandarin, meaning that this variety is primarily spo-
ken by older speakers. All participants could also speak Man-
darin. They reported no speech or hearing impairments at the
time of participation. Each participant provided informed con-
sent and received compensation for their time.
2.2. Materials
The stimuli used in our study consisted of 104 monosyllabic tar-
get words in Kaihui Xiang. These were constructed as minimal
pairs to contrast checked and unchecked tones across a wide
range of vowel (e.g., /i/, /e/, /a/, /o/, /u/) and consonant (as-
pirated and unaspirated stops and affricates, unaspirated frica-
tives) combinations (e.g.,八(eight) /pa T6/ vs.爬(climb)
/pa T2/). All target syllables conformed to a CV or V structure
and included all six tones of the language.
2.3. Procedure
In terms of the procedure, after providing informed consent,
participants were instructed to embed each target syllable into
the carrier sentence “/No21fei55kaN24TARGET/ (I know how
to say “TARGET”) and to read the resulting sentence aloud as
clearly and naturally as possible. The recordings were con-
ducted using a Zoom H4n Pro recorder and were digitized at
a sampling rate of 44.1 kHz and 24-bit depth. The record-
ings yielded an initial corpus of 3,328 tokens. After manual
screening, 536 tokens were discarded due to background noise,
recording artifacts, or disfluent renditions. The remaining 2,792
tokens formed the basis for subsequent analysis. The relatively
high exclusion rate is likely attributable to two interrelated fac-
tors: occasional interference from Mandarin during the elicita-
tion session, and the low frequency of certain target syllables
in everyday use—a familiar challenge in fieldwork with endan-
gered or recessive varieties (cf. [5]).
2.4. Acoustic and statistical analysis
Four primary acoustic dimensions were examined: coda pres-
ence,F 0, vowel duration, and phonation. Coda presence was
determined via visual inspection of spectrograms, focusing on
characteristic patterns of glottalization. Continuous acoustic pa-
rameters –F 0, duration, and two voice quality measures (H1 *-
H2* and HNR05) – were extracted using V oiceSauce [15]. Fol-
lowing [5], H1 *-H2* (the formant-corrected amplitude differ-
ence between the first two harmonics) was used as an indicator
of spectral tilt. HNR05 was used to reflect the relative strength
of harmonic versus noise components in the lower frequency
range (0-500 Hz). To account for inter-speaker variation, all
extracted measures were normalized for each speaker. V owel
duration, H1*-H2*, and HNR05 were converted to z-scores.F 0
was first log-transformed and then z-scored per speaker, follow-
ing the procedure in [16].
Statistical analyses were conducted in R 4.5.1 [17]. To ana-
lyze the temporal trajectories ofF 0,H1

3. Results
This section presents the acoustic findings for the checked
tone in Kaihui Xiang. To isolate the phonetic hallmarks of
the checked tone and examine gender-specific variations, all
unchecked tones were aggregated into a single reference cat-
egory for contrastive analysis.
Our acoustic inspection reveals that oral stop codas are in-
consistently realized or entirely absent. As a result, we posit
that the checked tone in Kaihui Xiang has largely lost its con-
sonantal coda, though it may retain non-modal voice quality in
the syllable rime. The following sections will focus on theF 0,
vowel duration, and voice quality correlates of the checked tone.
3.1.F 0
Figure 1 presents the normalizedF 0 contours of the six tones
across nine time points based on the raw data. The checked
tone (T6) displays a characteristic falling contour:F 0 remains
relatively stable in the initial portion of the syllable before de-
clining progressively, reaching its lowest point at the final time
step. This falling trajectory is consistent with the phonetic re-
alization of high checked tones in related Chinese languages,
such as Xiapu Min [5].

4. Discussion and conclusion
This study set out to address two research questions concerning
the phonetic realization of the checked tone (T6) in Kaihui Xi-
ang and the role of gender in driving the de-checking process.
The results provide evidence that T6 occupies a late transitional
stage along the continuum ofrusheng shuhua(de-checking),
characterized by robust tonal stability alongside phonetic re-
structuring.
Regarding RQ1, the checked tone retains a distinct tonal
identity while exhibiting substantial reorganization across its
acoustic dimensions. In terms ofF 0, T6 maintains a stable
falling contour that clearly differentiates it from unchecked
tones. This contour is robust and invariant across speakers, in-
dicating that tonal category membership has been phonologized
independently of segmental closure. De-checking in Kaihui Xi-
ang therefore cannot be reduced to simple coda loss; rather, it
reflects a gradual decoupling of tonal contrast from its historical
segmental basis.
At the same time, duration continues to function as an
important cue to checkedness. The checked tone is signifi-
cantly shorter than unchecked tones, demonstrating that tem-
poral truncation still remains a systematic, contrastive prop-
erty. In the absence of an overt coda realization, duration ap-
pears to carry an increased functional load in maintaining the
checked–unchecked distinction, a finding consistent with pre-
vious studies [5, 4]. V oice quality measures, however, reveal a
more complex, non-linear trajectory of change. The H1 *-H2*
results do not display the expected final-phase lowering asso-
ciated with canonical laryngealization or creakiness. Instead,
lower values occur early in the syllable, suggesting a leftward
migration of non-modal phonation, where the laryngeal feature
of the historical coda has been re-anchored to the syllable on-
set. Furthermore, HNR05 exhibits a rising–falling trajectory,
indicating that the syllable edges remain noisier than the modal
nucleus. Taken together, these patterns indicate a spatial redis-
tribution of phonetic cues. Checkedness in Kaihui Xiang is no
longer anchored in final segmental closure; instead the case of
Kaihui Xiang implies a specific timeline for this “de-checking”
process: once the coda is lost andF 0 is phonologized, phona-
tory cues undergo complex spatial reorganization (e.g., leftward
shifting), leaving duration as the most resilient phonetic cue to
be lost.
Regarding RQ2, the results further demonstrate that gender
systematically conditions the phonetic realization of T6. Al-
though the durational effect is relatively subtle, this is expected
given the short absolute duration of checked tones and does not
undermine the overall interpretation, especially when duration
is considered together withF 0 and phonation measures. Cru-
cially, no gender difference is observed inF 0, indicating that
variation is concentrated in phonetic implementation rather than
tonal category membership. Male speakers maintain a more
conservative checked profile, characterized by stronger dura-
tional truncation and a sharper modal peak in the syllable nu-
cleus (as evidenced by HNR05). In contrast, female speakers
exhibit attenuated durational reduction alongside a more pro-
nounced leftward shift of laryngealization (i.e., significantly
lower initial H1*-H2*).
This asymmetry suggests that female speakers represent
a more advanced stage along the de-checking trajectory. If
rusheng shuhuainvolves the gradual reduction of marked
checked-syllable properties and increasing structural smooth-
ness, the weakened durational contrast and reorganized phona-
tory profiles among female speakers are consistent with a pos-
sible ongoing change. This pattern aligns neatly with the so-
ciolinguistic generalization that women frequently lead sound
changes in progress [9].
Two limitations must be acknowledged. First, examining
only one prosodic configuration precludes evaluating cue sta-
bility related to checkedness across different structural envi-
ronments, since the realization of checked tones is sensitive to
prosodic positions [4]. Future research incorporating multiple
prosodic contexts would allow for a more precise evaluation of
cue stability across structural environments. Second, the lack
of younger speakers prevents a direct apparent-time compari-
son; thus interpreting these patterns as an ongoing sound change
rather than stable sociophonetic variation remains provisional
pending inter-generational evidence.
In sum, the checked tone in Kaihui Xiang remains tonally
distinct while undergoing profound phonetic reorganization.
While duration serves as a resilient cue to checkedness, phona-
tion features display partial restructuring – notably a leftward
shift of laryngealization – indicating a late stage ofrusheng
shuhua. Gender differences suggest that this process is ongoing,
with female speakers leading the attenuation of temporal cues
and the innovation of phonatory dynamics. Ultimately, the loss
of a checked category is not a simple deletion of features, but a
gradual, socially conditioned redistribution of acoustic cues.
