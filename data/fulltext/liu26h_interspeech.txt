Abstract
Low-resource multilingual ASR commonly relies on joint
training, but excessive parameter sharing can cause negative
transfer. Meanwhile, consistency learning as a remedy for lim-
ited labels may amplify cross-view instability and confirmation
bias when predictions are unreliable. To tackle this, we pro-
pose a Whisper-based parameter-efficient framework. Archi-
tecturally, it adopts a shared-private Fusion-LoRA design (SPF-
LoRA): a shared branch learns cross-lingual commonality, a
private branch captures language-specific traits, and a learnable
fusion weight adaptively balances both to mitigate multilingual
interference. In training, we introduce a Mean-Teacher consis-
tency regularization strategy (MT-CR), where an EMA teacher
provides more stable consistency targets and reliability filtering
suppresses noisy consistency signals. On Kathbath, we reduce
macro WER by 35.4% over Whisper-small+LoRA, outperform
the reported Whisper-medium+LoRA baseline, and improve all
five languages.

Whisper, EMA teacher, SPF-LoRA
1. Introduction
In recent years, end-to-end speech recognition has achieved
remarkable progress driven by deep learning and large-scale
data [1, 2]. Sequence-to-sequence large models, which unify
acoustic modeling and language generation, have demonstrated
strong performance in complex speech scenarios [3]. However,
their success heavily relies on massive, high-quality transcribed
data, and thus recognition for low-resource languages remains
fundamentally constrained by data scarcity [4, 5]. To mitigate
the lack of annotations, low-resource automatic speech recogni-
tion (ASR) commonly leverages cross-lingual transfer, multilin-
gual joint training, and regularization strategies [6, 7, 8]. Large-
scale multilingual pretrained models such as Whisper make it
feasible to adapt models efficiently while keeping the backbone
frozen [9], and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods
like low-rank adaptation (LoRA) have increasingly become a
mainstream choice in low-resource settings [10, 11, 12].
Existing studies improve low-resource ASR via multilin-
gual joint training and parameter sharing [13], but in parameter-
efficient fine-tuning methods such as LoRA this can lead to
competition for adaptation capacity [14, 15]. After train-
able parameters are compressed into low-rank updates, high-
resource languages are more likely to dominate the optimization

of shared parameters [16], forcing low-resource languages to
adapt to mismatched acoustic and textual distributions [6, 17].
This results in gradient conflicts and negative transfer, and
the degradation becomes more pronounced when annotations
are scarcer [18]. Beyond such cross-lingual interference, low-
resource ASR also suffers from weak supervision due to limited
transcribed data. To better exploit scarce annotations and im-
prove robustness under perturbations, consistency learning and
self-training are often introduced as auxiliary training strate-
gies. However, these strategies are also more fragile in low-
resource settings: the output distribution is inherently unstable,
and strong augmentations further amplify uncertainty [7, 19].
If the model’s own predictions are directly used as supervision,
it is easy to incur noisy alignment and confirmation bias, con-
tinuously reinforcing errors [20, 21]. Therefore, the key is to
construct reliable and trustworthy teacher signals [22, 23].
To address these challenges, we propose a parameter-
efficient learning framework for Whisper that improves low-
resource multilingual training from both architectural and train-
ing perspectives. Our method combines structural decoupling
with temporal ensembling based on an exponential moving av-
erage (EMA), following prior teacher–student and uncertainty-
aware self-training studies [24, 25], to mitigate negative transfer
and improve supervision reliability.
On the architectural side, we introduce a shared–private de-
coupled Fusion-LoRA design (SPF-LoRA): a cross-lingually
shared branch learns universal representations, while a
language-specific private branch models language characteris-
tics [26]. An adaptive fusion mechanism balances sharing and
specialization, thereby reducing multilingual interference. On
the training side, we incorporate Mean-Teacher consistency reg-
ularization (MT-CR), where an EMA teacher provides smoother
and more reliable targets, enabling stable output constraints un-
der augmented views and improving consistency learning in
low-resource settings [24, 27].
Our contributions are twofold: (1) we propose SPF-
LoRA, which explicitly decomposes adaptation into shared and
language-private branches to alleviate negative transfer in low-
resource multilingual settings; (2) we integrate MT-CR into a
PEFT framework, using an EMA teacher to provide smoother
targets and thereby improve the stability of the consistency sig-
nal.

2. Proposed methodology
2.1. Method overview
We propose a parameter-efficient framework for Whisper, as
shown in Figure 1. With the backbone frozen, our method
improves both adaptation structure and training supervision:
architecturally, we adopt SPF-LoRA to balance cross-lingual
transfer and language-specific specialization; in training, we
use confidence-gated MT-CR, where an EMA teacher provides
more stable consistency targets, improving training stability in
low-resource settings.

3. Experimental settings
3.1. Datasets
Experiments are conducted on the Kathbath dataset [29]. Kath-
bath provides read speech–text pairs for multiple Indian lan-
guages, with transcripts sourced from public text resources such
as Wikipedia and news articles, yielding diverse topics and
vocabulary; it is also a key component of the IndicSUPERB
benchmark. We focus on the ASR task and strictly follow the
official train/validation/test splits to ensure comparability and
reproducibility. We select five Indo-Aryan languages as our tar-
gets: Gujarati (gu), Hindi (hi), Marathi (mr), Punjabi (pa), and
Urdu (ur). These languages are representative in terms of writ-
ing systems, phonetic characteristics, and morphological vari-
ation, and they exhibit different data scales and difficulty lev-
els under low-resource conditions, allowing us to capture both
shared challenges and cross-lingual differences in low-resource
multilingual ASR.
3.2. Training details
The base model is Whisper-small. All experiments were con-
ducted on a Linux server equipped with a single NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 4090 GPU using NVIDIA driver version
570.169. Models are trained for 5 epochs with a per-GPU batch
size of 8 and 2 gradient accumulation steps, resulting in an ef-
fective batch size of 16. The learning rate is set to5×10−4 with
2,000 warm-up steps. Logs are recorded every 50 steps, while
validation and checkpointing are performed every 2,000 steps.
SPF-LoRA is applied to theqkvofcmodules in the last 6 en-
coder and decoder blocks, withr= 16,α= 32, and a dropout
rate of 0.05. Here,q,k,v, andorefer to the attention projec-
tionsW q,W k,W v, andW o, whilefcdenotes the two feed-
forward network (FFN) linear layers. Mean-Teacher CR uses
an adapter-only EMA teacher with momentumm= 0.9995.
The consistency weightλis increased to 0.5 after a 1,000-step
warm-up and a 5,000-step ramp, while the confidence threshold
τrises from 0.6 to 0.8 over 5,000 steps. For two-view augmen-
tation, additive noise is applied with probability 0.2 at 10–50 dB
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), speed perturbation with probability
0.5 at rates of 0.9–1.1, and volume perturbation with probability
0.5 using gains from−15to+15dB.
4. Results and discussion
4.1. Main results: overall performance and cross-lingual
consistent gains
Table 1 summarizes the main results on the five Kathbath lan-
guages. Zero-shot Whisper-small yields a macro-average word
error rate (WER) of 84.70%, showing that direct transfer from
pretraining is insufficient for low-resource multilingual ASR.
LoRA and MAS-LoRA reduce the macro WER to 30.73%
and 29.47%, respectively, but multilingual interference remains.
SPF-LoRA further lowers the macro WER to 23.93%, indicat-
ing that the shared–private adaptation structure can better dis-
tribute model capacity across languages and mitigate negative
transfer. With MT-CR, the macro WER decreases to 19.85%,
while Gujarati WER drops from 38.86% to 25.04%, suggesting
that EMA-teacher consistency provides more stable supervision
for the most challenging low-resource language. Despite using
the smaller Whisper-small backbone, our method outperforms
all reported small-model baselines and the Whisper-medium
LoRA baseline in macro WER, with additional gains on Gu-
jarati and Urdu.

5. Conclusion
We propose a parameter-efficient Whisper framework for low-
resource multilingual ASR. With the backbone frozen, it uses
SPF-LoRA with per-language gating and MT-CR with an
adapter-only EMA teacher plus token-level confidence gat-
ing. Our method outperforms supervised fine-tuning and same-
model two-view consistency, and our Whisper-small model sur-
passes the Whisper-medium+LoRA baseline in macro WER.
