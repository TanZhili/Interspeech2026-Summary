Abstract
This paper establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for
Singing V oice Synthesis (SVS) of Chinese Yue Opera in the Wu
dialect, addressing a critical gap in this unexplored field. Our
benchmark integrates three key components: (1) the Yue Opera
Audio-Text (YOAT) dataset, a high-quality corpus of 565
studio recordings with phoneme-level alignments for duration
and pitch; (2) targeted data augmentation methods from the per-
spectives of pronunciation and pitch; and (3) YueOpera-Singer,
a strong baseline model built on conditional flow-matching.
Comprehensive objective and subjective evaluations verify
the reliability of the YOAT dataset and the effectiveness of
the proposed data augmentation methods. YueOpera-Singer
delivers competitive synthesis quality with a subjective MOS
of 3.92, acting as a robust baseline for the benchmark. The
dataset, code, and demo samples are publicly available on the
https://anonymous.4open.science/api/repo/
YueOpera_Benchmark-6914/file/index.html.

1. Introduction
The number of recognized traditional Chinese opera 1 genres
has dropped sharply from over 350 in 1957 to 162 national in-
tangible cultural heritage items in the 21st century, with many
on the brink of extinction [1]. As a popular national intangible
cultural heritage, Yue Opera2 is in urgent need of digital preser-
vation and innovative dissemination, and singing voice synthe-
sis (SVS) has emerged as a key technical approach for this goal
[2, 3, 4, 5, 6].
SVS generates human-like dry vocal audio from lyrics, mu-
sical scores and singer information [4], breaking the human
resource constraints of opera inheritance while preserving its
artistic characteristics. Unlike text-to-speech (TTS) datasets
with only audio and transcribed text, SVS datasets require
precise musical score annotations (phoneme boundaries, dura-
tion and pitch), leading to more complex construction. Exist-
ing SVS research focuses mainly on pop song (Chinese, En-
glish, Japanese, etc.), with high-quality aligned datasets such
as M4Singer [7], SingStyle111 [8] and GTSinger [9] laying a
solid data foundation, while Chinese opera SVS research re-
mains limited.

Existing Chinese opera SVS research has only covered four
genres, namely Peking Opera [10], Gezi Opera [5], Cantonese
Opera [6], and Huangmei Opera [11]. Notably, Yue Opera
(Wu dialect) has remained completely unexplored in prior work,
with no dedicated datasets available before our study. Further-
more, Chinese opera SVS research is inherently a low-resource
task [12]. The core causes of Chinese opera SVS’s low-resource
predicament are: (1) scarce professional Chinese opera per-
formers, leading to higher audio recording costs than pop song
and speech data collection and stricter professional require-
ments for recording staff; (2) scarce standard electronic scores
for Chinese opera, and performers’ actual vocals are not en-
tirely consistent with existing scores; (3) small data scale is in-
sufficient to support the training of automatic alignment models
(e.g., Montreal Forced Aligner [13]), requiring labor-intensive
manual annotation; (4) existing source separation models per-
form poorly on Chinese opera, failing to separate vocals from
accompaniment in Chinese opera audio. These factors collec-
tively hinder large-scale acquisition of high-quality opera data.
To fill the Yue Opera SVS research gap, we build the
first comprehensive benchmark for Yue Opera SVS. We in-
troduce YOAT, a high-quality single-singer Yue Opera audio-
text dataset with precise audio-text alignment, which is the first
dedicated dataset for Yue Opera SVS. Compared with other
Chinese opera datasets, it has the longest single-singer dura-
tion. Aiming at its low-resource characteristic, we propose
three targeted data augmentation strategies from pronunciation
and pitch perspectives. Finally, we develop YueOpera-Singer,
a baseline model for Yue Opera SVS based on conditional flow
matching [14], which provides standardized benchmark perfor-
mance for subsequent research.
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
• We design an efficient pipeline for Yue Opera SVS dataset
construction, and build the first high-quality aligned dataset,
filling the Yue Opera SVS gap and laying a foundation for
Wu dialect-based opera SVS research.
• We propose three data augmentation strategies from the per-
spectives of pronunciation and pitch, and empirically verify
their effectiveness through extensive experiments.
• We develop YueOpera-Singer, a conditional flow matching-
based baseline model, which serves as a robust baseline for
the Yue Opera SVS benchmark.
• Experimental results confirm the YOAT dataset’s reliabil-
ity. YueOpera-Singer achieves competitive synthesis perfor-
mance, establishing the first benchmark for Yue Opera SVS
and providing a standardized reference for future research.

2. The YOAT Dataset
To construct the Yue Opera SVS dataset, we design a complete
data processing pipeline, as illustrated in Figure 1. This pipeline
is capable of transforming raw data into standard SVS data with
sentence-level audio-text boundary alignment.
2.1. Data collection
Data collection involved the capture of singing audio and mu-
sical score images, as standardized electronic musical score for
Yue Opera is scarcely available. To ensure data quality, audio
was collected in a professional recording studio. A professional
Yue Opera actress specializing in the Xiaosheng (young male)
role was invited to record a total of 1.35 hours of singing data,
covering three representative arias from her repertoire. Specifi-
cally, Yue Opera actress wears headphones to listen the accom-
paniment, with printed sheet music placed in front to ensure ac-
curate delivery. Each audio file contains clean, isolated vocals
only, with no background music. All raw audio was recorded in
48kHz, 16-bit, mono-channel wav format.
The collected raw data were at the paragraph level. The au-
dio was segmented into sentence-level clips based on complete
lyrical sentences prior to annotation.
2.2. Data annotation
Data annotation primarily involved phoneme labeling, duration
labeling, and pitch labeling. A total of 565 audio clips were ob-
tained through segmentation, with durations ranging from 2.03
to 25.18 seconds and an average duration of 8.61 seconds.
(1) For phoneme labeling, we use a public Yue Opera pro-
nunciation application named “Yueyinyitong”. It serves as a
standardized and comprehensive pronunciation dictionary for
Yue Opera. Chinese lyrics are processed by searching the dic-
tionary to obtain and annotate their corresponding phonemes.
For characters with multiple pronunciations, annotators verify
the actual pronunciation in the recording and choose the cor-
rect phoneme. If the audio segment is silent, it is labeled with
a special symbolSP. If the audio segment corresponds to an
inhalation sound, it is labeled with a special tokenAP. (2) For
duration labeling, we use Praat 3 to manually label the phoneme
boundaries. Given the small dataset size, which is insufficient
to train a high-performance automatic alignment model, man-
ual annotation is a more direct and precise approach. (3) For
pitch labeling of phonemes, we use the accurate automatic pitch
extraction library Parselmouth 4 for pitch annotation. Opera
singing exhibits rich pitch variations in finals, consequently re-

2.3. Data augmentation
In Yue Opera vocal performance, the principle of “precise ar-
ticulation and rounded tone” is a fundamental artistic criterion
[15]. To achieve this aesthetic ideal, accurate pronunciation and
precise pitch control are both essential. We develop targeted
data augmentation methods for these two aspects.
2.3.1. Pronunciation augmentation
Chinese opera is divided into four disciplines: singing, recita-
tion, acting, and combat. “Recitation” refers to the delivery of
lines in the form of spoken dialogue. First, we collected high-
quality, noise-free Yue Opera recitations by a professional per-
former from online sources, totaling approximately 1 hour. We
refer to this data augmentation method as DA1. Second, we
extracted individual word pronunciations from the “Yueyinyi-
tong” application to build a phonetic pronunciation dictionary.
Using authentic scripts from 31 Yue Opera plays, we then built
sentence-level recitation data by matching and concatenating
the corresponding audio clips of individual words. This ap-

proach guarantees the accuracy of single-word pronunciations,
generating a relevant dataset subset of 8 hours in total. This data
augmentation method is named as DA2.
2.3.2. Pitch augmentation
To enhance the pitch accuracy of the Yue Opera SVS model, we
leverage aligned data from other opera genres for model train-
ing, specifically adopting 4.5 hours of aligned Gezi Opera data
[5] for this purpose. Gezi Opera and Yue Opera share a consis-
tent pitch and duration annotation system, and no coding con-
flicts exist in the Gezi Opera dataset after phoneme adjustment,
which renders the mixed training of the two datasets rational.
This data augmentation method is denoted as DA3.
3. YueOpera-Singer
To achieve high-quality Yue Opera singing voice synthesis, we
construct a baseline model named YueOpera-Singer based on
conditional flow matching. YueOpera-Singer is composed of
an acoustic model and a vocoder. The acoustic model con-
sists of an encoder, a length regulator, and a decoder, as illus-
trated in Figure 3, with its input including four components:
the phoneme sequence of the lyrics, the phoneme-level duration
and pitch sequences derived from the musical scores, and the
singer identity. The acoustic model outputs the predicted mel-
spectrogram, which is further fed into the vocoder that converts
the mel-spectrogram into the target audio waveform.

3.1. Encoder and length regulator
The encoder employs a Transformer [16] architecture that pro-
cesses lyric phonemes integrated with singer identity embed-
dings. This combined representation is passed to a length regu-
lator, which expands it to the frame level using pre-aligned du-
rations from the musical score rather than predicted durations.
The expanded sequence is fused with pitch embeddings and po-
sitional encodings to form the input fusion representationµ.
3.2. Decoder
We choose optimal transfer conditional flow matching (OT-
CFM) [14] as the generative paradigm. The decoder is a neu-
ral network with a U-Net [17] architecture consisting of three
main components. The first component comprises a convolu-
tional residual block and a Transformer block, followed by a
downsampling operation. The second component consists of
a convolutional residual block with a subsequent Transformer
block. The third component includes a convolutional residual
block and a Transformer block, with an upsampling operation
implemented afterward. The input of the decoder includes in-

4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets
We conducted experiments on our constructed YOAT dataset.
The YOAT dataset comprises 543 samples for training and 22
for testing. For the data augmentation experiments, we used
about 1 hour of collected authentic Yue Opera recitation audio,
8 hours of artificially constructed recitation data, and a 4.5-hour
Gezi Opera dataset 5.
4.2. Comparative models
FFT-Singer[18] is a non-autoregressive acoustic model
adapted from FastSpeech2 for SVS.DiffSinger[19] is the
first diffusion-based [20] acoustic model with shallow diffu-
sion mechanism to accelerate training and inference, achieving
SOTA performance in pop song SVS.FT-GAN[5] specializes
in Chinese opera SVS with sophisticated discriminator archi-
tecture that enable fine-grained tune modeling, representing the
current opera SVS SOTA model. To ensure a fair comparison
of acoustic models, all comparative models utilize the same pre-
trained HiFi-GAN singing model 6 as the vocoder.
4.3. Model configuration and training details
The encoder and decoder employ a 4-layer Transformer block
with 4 attention heads, a head dimension of 256, an embedding
dimension of 256, and an FFN dimension of 1024. The decoder
consists of 2-layer modules for downsampling, mid-processing,
and upsampling, respectively. In other words, N in Figure 3 is
set to 4, while M1, M2 and M3 are set to 2. In terms of data
processing, the audio sampling rate is set to 24kHz. Since we
extract real mel-spectrograms from real audio, the fast fourier
transform window length is set to512, and the hop length is set
to128. The number of mel bins is set to80. During training,
σmin is set to1×e −4. We use the AdamW [21] optimizer with a
learning rate of1×10 −4,β 1 = 0.9, andβ 2 = 0.98. YueOpera-
Singer employs the Euler method as the ODE solver [22]. Our
maximum training step is100,000. We train the model on a
single NVIDIA A40 GPU. For all comparative models, we re-
train them using the official open-source implementations pro-
vided in their original papers. Specifically, comparative models
are trained with the optimal hyperparameter settings reported in
their respective works. Each model is trained using the same
dataset splits. We test the performance of all models during in-
ference on the same machine with an NVIDIA 3090.

4.4. Evaluation metrics
We evaluate the quality of the synthesized audio using both ob-
jective and subjective metrics. For the objective evaluation, we
use the Logarithmic Rooted Mean Square Error of the Funda-
mental frequency (F0 RMSE) to evaluate the accuracy of the
fundamental frequency. For the subjective evaluation, we con-
duct a Mean Opinion Score (MOS) assessment. MOS-P denotes
the MOS for word pronunciation while MOS-N represents the
overall naturalness and quality [23]. The scores range from 1 to
5. We invite ten Yue Opera professionals to participate in this
evaluation, ensuring they are unaware of the audio source to
maintain impartiality. We also compared the model’s param-
eter size (Size, in Million), GPU memory footprint (GM, in
Megabytes) during inference, and inference speed, quantified
by the Real-Time Factor (RTF, in Second).

far more significant improvement in synthesis performance than
DA1 and DA2. In contrast, the spoken recitation data for DA1
and DA2 has limited pitch ranges and exhibits low matching
with the rhythmic characteristics of Yue Opera singing, leading
to weaker pitch and naturalness augmentation effects than DA3.
6. Conclusion
We establish the first benchmark for Yue Opera singing voice
synthesis, integrating the high-quality YOAT dataset, targeted
data augmentation methods and the YueOpera-Singer baseline
model. Comprehensive experiments validate the reliability of
the YOAT dataset and the effectiveness of our proposed aug-
mentation methods. YueOpera-Singer achieves a MOS of 3.92
on this benchmark, providing a robust baseline for Yue Opera
SVS research. This work fills key gaps in data, methodology
and evaluation for the field, laying a solid technical foundation
for the innovative development of Chinese Yue Opera.
We also observe that WenetSpeech-Wu [24], an 8000-hour
large-scale Chinese Wu dialect speech dataset, has been re-
cently completed. Upon its public release, we will further inves-
tigate effective methods for leveraging this large-scale dataset to
improve Yue Opera singing voice and recitation synthesis
