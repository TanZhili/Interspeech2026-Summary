Abstract
Electromyography (EMG)-to-speech (ETS) synthesis generates
speech from articulatory muscle activity without acoustic in-
put. We propose TAP-ETS, a time-aligned phoneme guid-
ing framework that conditions mel-spectrogram generation on
frame-wise phoneme sequences through cross-attention. Un-
like prior work that treats phonemes as auxiliary supervision,
our model injects aligned phoneme embeddings directly into
the decoder. We also introduce refinement strategies that redis-
tribute merged semantic guidance over frame-wise EMG sig-
nals, enabling the seamless integration of arbitrary phoneme-
or text-level correction methods without modifying the synthe-
sis model. On the Gaddy silent EMG benchmark, TAP-ETS
reduces WER from 25.12% to 19.77%, achieving state-of-the-
art performance and demonstrating the importance of accurate
frame-level alignment.
Index Terms: emg, silent speech, speech synthesis
1. Introduction
EMG-to-speech (ETS) synthesis aims to generate intelligible
speech from articulatory muscle activity, enabling silent speech
interfaces that support communication without vocalization.
Unlike conventional speech synthesis, ETS must recover acous-
tic information solely from neuromuscular signals that capture
articulatory movements, making the EMG-to-acoustic mapping
inherently challenging [1].
Recent ETS approaches have improved performance by in-
troducing auxiliary phoneme classification loss or by replacing
acoustic targets with discrete speech units [2, 3]. While these
strategies provide useful supervision, phoneme information is
typically treated as a secondary objective rather than an explicit
conditioning signal for generation. As a result, linguistic guid-
ance is implicitly learned and tightly coupled to the training ob-
jective, limiting controllability and making it difficult to incor-
porate external correction models in a unified manner.
To address these limitations, we propose TAP-ETS, a time-
aligned phoneme (TAP) guided ETS framework that explicitly
conditions mel-spectrogram generation on frame-wise phoneme
sequences. Instead of using phoneme information only as aux-
iliary supervision, our model injects TAP embeddings directly
into the decoder through cross-attention. In addition, we in-
troduce TAP refinement strategies that aligns corrected merged
phoneme sequences with EMG frames, so that phoneme- or
text-level corrections can be expanded into frame-wise guid-
ance while preserving the original temporal structure. Because
this alignment operates only on phoneme sequences and EMG
frame durations, diverse correction modules can be integrated
into TAP-ETS without modifying the synthesis model. Au-
dio samples, demo materials, and source code are available on,
https://github.com/ongdyub/TAP-ETS.
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
• We propose a time-aligned phoneme (TAP)-conditioned
ETS architecture that generates mel-spectrograms guided by
frame-wise, time-aligned phoneme embeddings.
• We introduce TAP refinement strategies that redistribute cor-
rected phoneme sequences over EMG frames while preserv-
ing the temporal structure.
• We demonstrate that this architecture enables the seamless
integration of diverse phoneme- and text-level corrections
within a single framework.
2. Related Works
2.1. EMG-to-Speech Synthesis
EMG has long served as a primary modality for silent speech
interfaces (SSI) [4]. Early studies focused on discriminative
tasks such as phoneme or word-level classification using surface
EMG signals, typically relying on signal processing pipelines
combined with HMM-based modeling [5, 6]. With the advent of
deep learning, especially Transformer architectures with long-
range receptive fields, silent speech recognition (SSR) perfor-
mance has improved substantially [7, 8, 9].
Beyond recognition, EMG-to-speech (ETS) synthesis aims
to reconstruct intelligible speech directly from articulatory mus-
cle activity. Earlier studies [10, 11] attempted EMG-to-speech
conversion using voiced EMG recordings but were limited by
small training sets and produced speech with low naturalness.
Gaddyet al.[12] pioneered generative ETS by releasing a
benchmark dataset of paired vocalized and silent EMG record-
ings and introducing a Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)-based
alignment strategy between EMG and acoustic features. Subse-
quent works have extended this paradigm using diffusion-based
generative models [13, 14] and disentangled voice conversion
frameworks based on pretrained self-supervised learning (SSL)
speech representations [3, 15].
To better capture linguistic information, Gaddy and Klein
[2] introduced a surrogate optimization framework combining
acoustic reconstruction with a phoneme classification loss. This
strategy significantly reduced WER and demonstrated the im-
portance of phoneme-level supervision in ETS. However, in
such approaches, phoneme prediction serves only as an aux-
iliary training objective and does not directly condition the de-
coder during acoustic generation. Consequently, phoneme rep-
resentations are not explicitly injected as controllable genera-
tion signals, and inference-time linguistic correction cannot be
seamlessly integrated without retraining the synthesis model.

2.2. Semantic Correction and Controllability
Recent advances in language model-based correction [16, 17,
18, 9] have shown that high-level semantic priors can effec-
tively improve recognition accuracy. Nevertheless, these cor-
rection strategies operate at the text level and are detached from
acoustic synthesis. They lack a mechanism to project the cor-
rected linguistic information back onto the frame-level temporal
structure required for speech generation.
In contrast, our proposed TAP-ETS framework treats
phoneme sequences not merely as auxiliary supervision,
but as explicit, frame-aligned conditioning signals for mel-
spectrogram generation. By decoupling phoneme estimation
from acoustic synthesis and enabling time-aligned projection
of corrected phoneme sequences, our method allows diverse
phoneme- or text-level correction modules to be integrated into
ETS without modifying the synthesis backbone.
3. TAP-ETS Framework
In this section, we present TAP-ETS, a framework designed for
ETS synthesis leveraging time-aligned phoneme (TAP) guid-
ance. The TAP-ETS architecture consists of two primary stages:
(1) ETS training with TAP conditioning (Figure 1) and (2) TAP
refinement for semantic guidance during inference (Figure 2).

3.2. TAP Refinement for Semantic Guidance
A key advantage of our framework is the use of explicit
phoneme features as conditioning signals. By decoupling
phoneme estimation from acoustic synthesis, it enables seam-
less integration of advanced semantic refinement strategies dur-
ing inference without retraining. However, external semantic
guidance (e.g., from a language model) provides phoneme se-
quences without explicit frame-level timing information. Since
our synthesis backbone operates on frame-wise representations,
an additional refinement step is required to transform merged
phoneme sequences into frame-wise conditioning signals while
preserving the original EMG-derived duration structure.
To this end, as illustrated in Figure 3, we introduce two
complementary phoneme refinement strategies: (1) a Leven-
shtein distance–based method that rectifies alignment errors via
phonetic distance, and (2) an independently trained refinement
model that leverages contextual priors such as typical speech

4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets
We use the publicly available EMG dataset released by Gaddy et
al. [19], a widely adopted benchmark in silent speech research.
The dataset contains bothvoicedandsilentEMG recordings.
For voiced data, time-aligned speech audio is available, and
phoneme sequences are obtained using the Montreal Forced
Aligner (MFA) [20]. For silent data, each utterance is paired
with its corresponding voiced recording of the same sentence;
following prior work [3, 2], we use this pairing with DTW to
align the sequences and transfer speech targets to the silent con-
dition. To ensure comparability with prior ETS work, we adopt
the test dataset of [2]. The model with the highest frame-wise
phoneme accuracy on the 198-sample validation set is selected,
and results are reported on the 98-sample silent EMG test set.
We train the masking-based refinement model (Sec. 3.2.2)
on the LibriSpeech [21]train-clean-100split, which pro-
vides diverse phoneme sequences with natural prosodic timing.
The phoneme sequences are also extracted via MFA.
4.2. Experimental Settings
We compare our model against Gaddy [2] and Scheck [3],
which represent the current state-of-the-art in ETS intelligibil-
ity. For comprehensive evaluation, we report Phoneme, Charac-
ter, and Word Error Rates (PER, CER, and WER), using ASR
transcriptions generated by the Whisper-medium [22] model.
To measure frame-wise phoneme accuracy, frame-level align-
ments were extracted using MFA. All systems utilize a consis-
tent HiFi-GAN vocoder [23] to ensure a fair comparison.
In this experiment, we apply text-level guidance by lever-
aging MONA-LISA [9], which performs EMG recognition
coupled with GPT-3.5-based text-level correction (achieving a
recognition WER of 12.70%). The predicted words are con-
verted into phoneme sequences via G2P and subsequently redis-
tributed using our proposed sequential time-aligned phoneme
refinement strategies (Sec. 3.2.1 followed by Sec. 3.2.2).
5. Results
5.1. Intelligibility Evaluation of EMG-to-Speech
As summarized in Table 1, TAP-ETS significantly outper-
forms existing baselines across all intelligibility metrics, includ-
ing Accuracy, PER, CER, and WER. Notably, our framework
achieves a new state-of-the-art WER of 19.77%, with statisti-
cal significance confirmed by paired t-tests (p <0.05). These
results highlight the combined benefit of TAP conditioning dur-
ing synthesis and semantic guiding via TAP refinement at in-
ference. This synergy enables effective synchronization be-
tween high-level semantic guidance and frame-wise acoustic
features, allowing TAP-ETS to preserve linguistic intelligibil-
ity and prosodic consistency, thereby bridging the gap between
silent EMG signals and natural speech synthesis.

5.2. Ablation Studies
5.2.1. TAP Refinement Strategies
We evaluate the individual and collective impact of our pro-
posed refinement strategies. Lev denotes Levenshtein distance-
based refinement, NN refers to the masking-based refinement
model, and Lev+NN represents their sequential application.
As shown in Table 2(a), both refinement strategies improve
intelligibility over the baseline (25.12%). Levenshtein-based
method primarily corrects token-level inconsistencies while
preserving duration structure. The masking-based model fur-
ther mitigates frame-level noise and duration allocation errors.
Their sequential application yields the best performance, sug-
gesting that deterministic temporal projection and learned con-
textual refinement address complementary error sources.
While our framework supports both phoneme- and text-
level correction, we focus on text-level corrected output
sequence for refinement, as it consistently outperformed
phoneme-level alternatives. We hypothesize that modern lan-
guage models capture high-level linguistic context more effec-
tively in the text domain than in the phoneme domain, reinforc-
ing the utility of TAP refinement in bridging these domains.
5.2.2. Efficacy of Time-Aligned Phoneme Conditioning
In this experiment, we compared various conditioning for-
mats to justify the use of TAP. First, we compared frame-wise
phoneme guidance against frame-wise text guidance. For a fair
comparison, we utilized ground-truth labels for both formats to
ensure precise time alignment, which explains the inherently
lower WER observed in these configurations.
As shown in Table 2(b), phoneme guidance yielded supe-
rior performance compared to text guidance, and frame-wise
phoneme guidance further outperformed the merged-sequence
approach. This suggests that, unlike standard TTS systems that
predict alignment internally from token-level sequences, ETS
models benefit significantly from explicit frame-level guidance.
We posit that the inherent ambiguity within EMG sig-
nals makes it difficult for models to learn high-level linguistic
or time-misaligned representations. Bridging biosignals with
phonemes—which are acoustically more representative than
characters—simplifies the learning task by providing a more di-
rect mapping to the target speech.

misalignment settings: (1) Padding, where initial frames are
zero-padded while the remaining alignment is kept intact, and
(2) Time Shift, which globally offsets the entire sequence.

severely harms synthesis quality. Moreover, the Random Dura-

5.2.4. Complementarity of EMG and Semantic Guidance
We conducted mismatch experiments to evaluate the reliance
on raw EMG versus TAP-based guidance. We tested EMG-
mismatch and Phoneme-mismatch settings by shifting one
modality to the next sample while keeping the other matched.
As shown in Table 2(d), both settings lead to performance
degradation, confirming that both sources are essential for ac-
curate prediction. Notably, the degradation is more severe in
the EMG-mismatch setting. This suggests that our TAP-based
refinement functions not as a rigid TTS-like constraint but as an
auxiliary guide that complements the EMG signal, enhancing
intelligibility through effective frame-wise conditioning.
6. Conclusion
We presented TAP-ETS, a time-aligned phoneme-guided EMG-
to-speech framework that conditions the decoder on frame-wise
phoneme sequences. By casting semantic guidance as a tem-
poral projection problem, it refines merged phonemes or text
into frame-aligned signals while preserving the original EMG-
derived timing structure, enabling flexible integration of exter-
nal corrections without retraining the backbone. On the Gaddy
silent EMG benchmark, TAP-ETS achieves state-of-the-art in-
telligibility. Ablations show that (1) frame-wise phoneme con-
ditioning outperforms merged token conditioning, (2) temporal
misalignment or duration perturbation degrades performance,
and (3) both EMG and aligned semantic guidance are required
for accurate mel-spectrogram generation. These results under-
score the importance of precise temporal conditioning in bridg-
ing biosignals and natural speech synthesis.
We note a limitation that our framework relies on language
models to generate corrections for alignment and refinement,
making performance dependent on the quality of these models.
Nevertheless, the framework is flexible and can incorporate var-
ious correction modules that provide phoneme or text guidance.
As these models improve, future work will explore approaches
that better reflect the characteristics of EMG data.
