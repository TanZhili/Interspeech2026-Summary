Abstract
Real-time voice conversion systems struggle to achieve high
speaker similarity in zero-shot scenarios, especially under
lightweight computational constraints. This challenge stems
from a lack of model expressivity and the difficulty of capturing
comprehensive speaker-relevant information within the strict la-
tency requirements. We propose a real-time voice conversion
framework that addresses this limitation by adding lost prosody
explicitly through an excitation signal and by fusing comple-
mentary information into the conventional global speaker em-
beddings. To ensure robust feature disentanglement, we fur-
thermore employ an encoder-specific information perturbation
strategy during training. Our approach maintains causal infer-
ence requirements and preserves prosodic and linguistic con-
tent while operating under low-latency constraints. Experimen-
tal evaluations demonstrate improvements in speaker-matching
metrics over state-of-the-art real-time baselines.
Index Terms: voice conversion, speech representation learn-
ing, real-time, speech generation
1. Introduction
V oice conversion (VC) systems aim to modify speaker iden-
tity while preserving linguistic content, requiring the disentan-
glement of speaker-specific traits from phonetic and prosodic
information. For low-latency VC models, such factorization
is particularly advantageous: by explicitly separating speech
components, architectural bottlenecks and low-capacity sub-
networks can be constrained to encode only their designated
factors. This reduces representational redundancy and enables
resynthesis under real-time and memory constraints [1].
However, advances in large language models (LLMs) and
self-supervised learning (SSL) have enabled representations
that unify acoustic and linguistic information [2, 3, 4]. Con-
sequently, SSL-based features have largely replaced traditional
VC encoders and disentanglement methods built on automatic
speech recognition (ASR) and automatic speaker verification
(ASV). Particularly, many modern VC pipelines utilize content
and speaker embeddings derived from intermediate SSL repre-
sentations or their discretized variants [5, 6, 7, 8]. Although
SSL-based models offer performance advantages in VC, their
non-causal nature and computational complexity pose chal-
lenges for real-time deployment. StreamVC [9] addresses this
through knowledge distillation, using a causal encoder to ap-
proximate HuBERT units. In contrast, RT-VC [10] employs
articulatory synthesis with a differentiable digital signal pro-
cessing (DDSP)-based synthesizer and a frozen WavLM CNN

feature extractor as its speaker encoder. While both systems
enable real-time VC, they exhibit lower speaker similarity than
non-real-time approaches. We identify two contributing factors:
(1) Lightweight architectures lack the capacity to encode com-
prehensive speaker-specific characteristics beyond static global
embeddings. (2) Content representations based on discrete units
or articulatory synthesis discard prosodic information, creat-
ing a representational gap where time-varying speaker features
must be recovered elsewhere in the pipeline.
This paper addresses the inherent trade-off between latency,
expressivity, and speaker similarity in real-time VC. First, to
compensate for the degraded prosodic information in the con-
tent embeddings, we explicitly inject an excitation signal, in-
creasing the effective model capacity without adding significant
architectural overhead. Second, inspired by the pipelines in [11]
and [12], we model speaker identity as conditioned on both con-
tent and prosody. Concretely, we introduce a lightweight com-
plementary speaker encoder alongside a global speaker embed-
ding to capture residual speaker characteristics, which are re-
trieved by fusing speaker, content, and prosody information us-
ing causal multi-head attention (MHA). Finally, we encourage
disentanglement between speaker and content representations
by applying factor-specific perturbations during training. The
paper is organized as follows: 2 describes the proposed pipeline,
3 presents training and evaluation details, and 4 presents and
discusses the results. The main contributions are:
• A VC framework with improved zero-shot performance at
low operational latency.
• An excitation signal injection to restore prosody information
lost in parallel embeddings.
• A complementary speaker encoder capturing time-varying
and time-invariant characteristics beyond static global em-
beddings under real-time constraints.
• An encoder-specific training perturbation strategy enhancing
feature disentanglement via targeted input manipulation.

1D convolutions (CNNs) used for compression or up-sampling.

We create a speaker encoderS Enc pretrained on the V ox-

pooling (ASP) is finally used to combine the remaining time

2.4. Complementary Speaker Encoder
As noted in [11], speaker timbre is not strictly static but varies
with time-dependent factors such as linguistic context and pitch.
Under the causal and low-complexity constraints required for
real-time VC, encoding such cues into a single global speaker
embedding is challenging. At the same time, auxiliary represen-
tations may lack sufficient capacity to compensate for it. For
example, if the content embedding is limited to linguistic in-
formation, prosodic features like speaking style and pitch range
must be inferred solely from the speaker embedding, which may
be missing for low capacity models.
To address this, we propose a synergistic combination that
fuses global speaker embeddings with dynamic, time-varying
features. More specifically, we introduce a complementary
speaker encoder,S 2Enc , which extracts time-varying acous-
tic features that the model may inadequately represent. We
employ a similar architecture toP Enc and use MHA to tok-
enize the output, forming speaker tokensS T oken. These to-
kens are then fused with time-varying features andS Global
via a causal cross-attention mechanism, yielding a task-specific
speaker representationS Emb. Specifically, we useS T oken as
valuesV, the concatenation[Z;F 0 ;A p ;L]as the queryQ, and
50 trainable latent vectors as keysK. Although time-varying,
attention-based embeddings have been explored in prior work
[11, 20, 12, 21, 22], such works rely on non-causal or higher-
latency architectures. Instead, we demonstrate that effective
speaker–content–prosody fusion can be achieved with only two
MHA blocks, explicitly designed for strict real-time operation
via causal masking. Despite operating under streaming con-
straints, this minimal encoder and attention stack proves suf-
ficient to model extra identity cues. It adds only≈1.7M pa-
rameters, preserving the lightweight architecture required for
real-time deployment.
2.5. Information Perturbation
Rather than relying solely on knowledge distillation, pretrain-
ing, or bottlenecks to achieve disentanglement, we directly ma-
nipulate the input data to force each encoder to learn its in-
tended representation. We propose two strategies. First, we
address the fact that content representations may retain speaker
or prosodic information, stemming from inaccurate encoder ap-
proximations (due to reduced capacity) or from speaker traits
that may still be embedded in the discrete teacher labels, as
noted in [23]. To mitigate this, we disrupt speaker cues in
the content encoder input through timbre perturbation, applying
pitch-shifting (ps), noise addition (na), and parametric equaliza-

3. Experiment
We adopt similar datasets and evaluation procedures as the
state-of-the-art (SOTA) baselinesStreamVC[9] andRT-VC
[10]. Thus, the model is trained on alltrainsubsets of the Lib-
riTTS dataset [25], which contain 555 hours of speech from
2311 speakers. For the zero-shot conversion evaluation, we re-
trieve the same 377 unseen source utterances in the LibriTTS
test-cleansubset. Similarly, we select the same 6 unseen target
speakers (3 male and 3 female) from the VCTK dataset. Lastly,
we perform an ablation analysis to highlight the improvements
of the proposed methods. The ablations consist of the proposed
model without perturbation (w.o perturbation), as well as the
model conditioned only on the static global embedding.
3.1. Training Details
The model is trained using a combination of losses from
[13, 14]; a multi-resolution, mel-spectrogram lossL mel, an ad-
versarial (GAN) lossL adv and a feature lossL f eat. We addi-
tionally add the cross-entropy-based content lossL C:
L=αL mel +L adv +βL f eat +L C,(4)
whereαandβare loss weights of12.0and2.0respectively. We
train the model at 16kHz for 800k steps, with a batch size of 32,
using the AdamW optimizer and a learning rate of 2e-4 for both
the model and the discriminators. For the MHA blocks, we use
8 heads with a hidden dimension of 128. During training, we
apply the perturbations listed in Table 1.
3.2. Metrics
The conversions are evaluated across intelligibility, speaker
similarity, andF0consistency. Intelligibility is assessed via
WER and CER, using the HuBERT-Large ASR model2 against
normalized ground-truth transcriptions. Speaker similarity is
measured using cosine similarity (SECS) between processed

4.1. Objective Evaluation
As illustrated in Table 2, the models demonstrate compara-
ble levels of intelligibility. With respect to the WER metric,
StreamVC achieves the best performance at6.22%, whereas the
proposed model attains superior CER at2.03%. Notably, both
ablation variants that exclude perturbation yield improved intel-
ligibility, which we attribute to residual integration of content
and acoustic information within the content latent, conversely
diminishing speaker similarity through information leakage.

4.2. Subjective Evaluation
As shown in Table 2, the speaker-similarity patterns observed
in the objective metrics are supported by human listeners. Our
proposed model achieves the highest similarity ratings (mean =
3.25), followed by StreamVC (3.16) and RT-VC (2.96). How-
ever, no statistical significance can be proven. In terms of
Q-MOS, StreamVC (3.66) is slightly better than the proposed
model (3.60). Statistical analysis shows a significant differ-
enceH(3) = 82.64,p= 0.005with a small effect size
(ϵ2 = 0.021). Dunn’s post hoc test reveals that StreamVC
and the proposed model are rated significantly higher than the
source (p = 0.01 and p = 0.037, respectively), with no other sig-
nificant differences observed. StreamVC achieved the highest
N-MOS among all models, with Dunn’s tests showing signif-
icant differences between the source (best) and all converted
models. Moreover, StreamVC was rated significantly more nat-
ural than RT-VC (p <0.001). We attribute StreamVC’s ad-
vantage in naturalness to its more elaborate training procedure.
Furthermore, its frame-level prosody conditioning may bene-
fit naturalness through implicitly learned representations; how-
ever, this appears to come at the cost of SECS and F0 PCC. We
note that all models adopt the ”quality” of the target sample, as
evidenced by lower Q-MOS scores for the source speech.
4.3. Inference Latency
We use a cached configuration for real-time usage [26], avoid-
ing multi-frame lookahead. With a 256-sample frame (16 ms at
16 kHz) and an average processing time of 48.9 ms per chunk
on an Intel® Xeon® CPU, the total latency is 64.9 ms. We addi-
tionally calculate the real-time factor for a buffer of 2048 sam-
ples, yielding 1/RTF = 2.7 on the CPU and 11.05 on a TITAN
X GPU. A demonstration is provided on the webpage.

5. Discussion
While our results demonstrate significant improvements in
speaker similarity, the enhanced performance does not prove the
extraction of ”dynamic” timbre information as characterized in
related works [5, 12]. This is evident in Fig. 3, which shows that
even thoughS Emb clearly contains different information than
SGlobal, no exact relationship to speech events can be detected.
Rather, our findings suggest that the complementary speaker
encoder captures speech features that the model fails to repre-
sent, thereby increasing expressivity and creating a model better
matched to the VC task. This interpretation aligns with the ob-
servation that discrete unit representations, while beneficial for
disentanglement, inherently discard prosodic information, such
as F0 and energy variations, which must be represented else-
where. Thus, by explicitly incorporating prosodic features into
the model, both as an excitation signal and a fused speaker em-
bedding, our approach addresses the representational gap intro-
duced by real-time architectural constraints.
6. Conclusion
This paper introduces a lightweight voice conversion model ca-
pable of real-time operation. Compared to baseline systems,
our approach maintains intelligibility and audio quality while
improving speaker matching in zero-shot scenarios. In particu-
lar, the combination of a complementary embedding, an excita-
tion signal, and perturbation strategies yields measurable gains
in speaker similarity metrics and pitch contour preservation. We
attribute the improved performance to increased expressivity.

7. Ethical Considerations
We fully acknowledge the ethical concerns around real-time VC
and do not condone any exploitation of this technology. To mit-
igate misuse, such as impersonation and privacy risks, we will
only release code to reproduce the paper’s results, provide a
TorchScript export with fixed speaker targets, and a demo. For
the review process, we have added source code to reproduce
metrics and try the model in the supplementary material.
