Abstract
Generative speech enhancement methods have shown im-
pressive performance by directly modeling clean speech
distribution, yet their effectiveness critically depends on
the reliability of conditional information. Mainstream
conditioning strategy face two primary challenges: the
shallow features extracted from noisy speech often fails
to capture structured acoustic features such as har-
monic structures, and extracting accurate and reliable
semantic information from noisy signals poses a chal-
lenge comparable to the enhancement task itself. To ad-
dress these limitations, we propose HFMSE, a Harmonic-
guided Flow Matching method for Speech Enhance-
ment. Specifically, we design an eﬀicient harmonic en-
coder that extracts harmonic structural features from
noisy speech through a two-step process of fundamen-
tal frequency localization and harmonic mask genera-
tion. These features serve as strong conditioning guid-
ance and are integrated into the flow matching genera-
tion process to improve the structural integrity and per-
ceptual naturalness of the output speech. Extensive ex-
periments on DNS Challenge 2020 show that HFMSE
achieves state-of-the-art performance, demonstrating its
superior capability and robustness. Code is available at
https://github.com/xxnhq/HFSE/.

1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) aims to extract clean target
speech signals from complex acoustic environments to
improve speech clarity and intelligibility [ 1]. It plays a
critical role in fields such as hearing aids [ 2], automatic
speech recognition (ASR) [3], and audio broadcasting [4].
Discriminative speech enhancement methods have
been demonstrated effective in noise suppression [ 5, 6],
yet their generalization capability remains limited in
complex scenarios. With growing demand for high-
quality and high-fidelity speech, generative speech en-
hancement models have attracted extensive research at-
tention due to their potential to surpass the upper
bound of speech quality constraints and exhibit stronger
generalization. Generative speech enhancement meth-
ods can be broadly divided into discrete token-based
approaches [ 7, 8, 9] and continuous diffusion or flow-
matching-based models [ 10, 11, 12]. The former employ
neural encoders to compress speech into discrete tokens,

differences, both paradigms fall under conditional gener-
ative modeling and rely on the quality of the condition.
Existing conditioning strategies can be broadly di-
vided into two types. The first relies on shallow acous-
tic features (e.g., noisy Mel-spectrograms or waveforms)
as conditions. These shallow features are inherently cor-
rupted by noise, often resulting in incomplete or distorted
speech structural information (e.g., harmonics and for-
mants). This can lead to artificial spectral components,
compromising naturalness and intelligibility. The sec-
ond strategy attempts to extract high-level semantic fea-
tures from noisy speech using pre-trained deep models
(e.g., speech codecs, ASR models [ 13], or speech lan-
guage models [ 14]) as conditions. Ideally, this approach
transforms the enhancement task into conditional speech
synthesis, but it rests on a strong assumption: the pre-
trained model can extract complete and accurate seman-
tic information from noisy inputs. This fundamentally
conflicts with the premise of speech enhancement, which
is needed precisely because noise severely interferes with
or even masks semantic content. Although some studies
have attempted to employ dedicated noise-robust feature
extractors such as WavLM [15], this essentially pre-solves
a sub-problem as challenging as the enhancement task it-
self, resulting in a circular dependency.
To overcome the limitations of unreliable conditional
information in generative SE, we propose HFMSE, a
novel flow-matching-based framework guided by explicit
harmonic structural priors. Instead of relying on noisy
shallow features or high-level semantic representations
extracted by pre-trained models, HFMSE directly esti-
mates and exploits harmonic regularities that are intrin-
sic to speech production and more robust under noise cor-
ruption. Specifically, we design a lightweight harmonic
encoder that performs soft fundamental frequency local-
ization and harmonic mask estimation using a Mel-scale
Pitch-Harmonic Conversion Matrix (MPCM). Different
from conventional pitch-aware enhancement or harmonic
masking methods that operate as post-processing or aux-
iliary supervision, the proposed harmonic features are
injected as persistent conditioning into the flow match-
ing model, directly shaping the learning of the velocity
field and the generative trajectory from noise to clean
speech. This allows the model to explicitly reinforce har-
monic structures while suppressing inter-harmonic noise

2.2. Conditioning Strategy
As shown in Formula (1), this formulation treats speech
enhancement as a masked prediction task: the model
learns to reconstruct the masked region using contextual
information from the unmasked parts of My and sup-
plementary cues from Mx. Different from standard con-
ditional flow matching where all conditions are treated
equally and randomly dropped, the harmonic prior serves
as a persistent structural constraint, anchoring the gen-
erative dynamics throughout the entire ODE trajec-
tory. Unlike reference-based inference methods (e.g.,
SenSE [18]), which can concatenate a clean reference ut-
terance at inference time, our method sets the masked
region to the full sequence length during inference, re-
quiring no external reference and thus accommodating
general enhancement scenarios.
2.3. Two-step Process of Harmonic Encoder
To extract structured harmonic priors from degraded
speech, we design a lightweight two-step module with an
attention-like gating mechanism. This encoder employs
a Mel-scale Pitch-Harmonic Conversion Matrix (MPCM)
to perform pitch localization and harmonic mask estima-
tion on spectral features, thereby enhancing harmonic
information while suppressing inter-harmonic noise in-
terference.
2.3.1. Mel-scale Pitch-Harmonic Conversion Matrix
MPCM ∈ RNc×F is built by first defining a linear-
frequency matrix where each row corresponds to a candi-
date fundamental frequency, where Nc denote the length
of candidate pitch. For each fundamental frequency, the
positions of its harmonic overtones across the spectrum
are calculated and assigned an amplitude that decays
proportionally to 1/√p, where p is the harmonic order.
Gaps between consecutive harmonic peaks are smoothly
interpolated using a cosine-based function to simulate a
natural energy decay. Finally, this linear-frequency ma-
trix is projected onto the Mel scale using a Mel filter
bank. The resulting matrix effectively encodes an ide-
alized harmonic comb pattern for each candidate pitch,
enabling the transformation of an input spectrum into a
pitch-dependent harmonic representation that facilitates
robust fundamental frequency localization and harmonic
mask estimation. Unlike explicit pitch estimators that
output a single F0 trajectory, MPCM provides a soft,
frame-wise probabilistic harmonic representation, which
is more compatible with the uncertainty modeling re-
quired in generative flow matching.

2.3.3. Harmonic Mask Generation
The probability distribution of fundamental frequency
attention weight is subsequently multiplied with the
MPCM in a second matrix operation. The MPCM es-
sentially encodes a physical ”pitch-to-harmonics” prior.
Thus, this multiplication physically infers and aggregates
the expected energy distribution of all possible harmon-
ics across the time-frequency plane based on the esti-
mated pitch probability from the first step, thereby gen-
erating a harmonic mask. In this mask, bright regions
indicate harmonic structures (including the fundamental
frequency and its overtones) that should be enhanced ac-
cording to the estimated pitch, whereas areas with low
pitch probability interact with corresponding low values
in the MPCM and appear dark in the mask. For unvoiced
or noise-dominated frames, the estimated pitch distri-
bution becomes diffuse, resulting in a near-uniform har-
monic mask, thereby avoiding over-suppression of non-
harmonic speech components.
2.3.4. Gating Mechanism
Finally, the modulated value tensor V is multiplied by
this harmonic mask and then compressed and aggregated
by a lightweight gated mechanism. The gating mecha-
nism employs adaptive average pooling to capture global
feature information, and utilizes convolutional modules
for channel compression and expansion, thereby enabling
feature weight aggregation across channel dimensions.
The resulting representation is normalized via a Sigmoid
function to determine gating intensities, which are then
applied to the original features through a gating opera-
tion. Based on acoustic physical principles, this mech-
anism recalibrates spectral information via a harmonic
mask and provides structured prior knowledge to the gen-
erative model, which then guides spectral reconstruction.
3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
To construct a comprehensive and challenging train-
ing environment, this work integrates multiple public
speech and noise datasets. Clean speech is drawn from
VCTK [ 19], LibriTTS [ 20], Common Voice 11.0 [ 21],
and the clean subset of DNS5 [ 22], totaling nearly 1200
hours. Noise samples are taken from WHAM! [ 23] and
DNS5 [22] noise libraries, approximately 250 hours. To
simulate realistic indoor acoustic conditions, room im-
pulse responses (RIRs) [ 24] from the OpenSLR26 and
OpenSLR28 datasets are used. Speech and noise are
mixed at SNRs from –5 dB to 20 dB, with 40% probabil-
ity of reverberation, yielding a 2000 h training set. For
vocoder training, only clean speech from all the afore-
mentioned corpora is used. All audio clips are resam-
pled to 24 kHz for training, and outputs are resampled
to 16 kHz for fair evaluation.
For evaluation, we selected the DNS Challenge 2020
test set for an open comparison. All test utterances are
resampled to 16 kHz mono-channel prior to processing to
ensure consistent evaluation conditions, and all reported
metrics are taken from published results.
3.2. Architecture and Training Details
STFT is performed with a Hanning window of length
1024 samples (42.67 ms) and a hop size of 256 samples
(10.67 ms). The resulting spectrograms are converted to
100‑band Mel‑scale spectrograms via a Mel filterbank.
The DiT‑based flow matching model uses 1024 fea-
ture channels, a depth of 22 layers, and 16 attention
heads. The feature dimension of the harmonic encoder
is set to 64. Training is conducted for 50 epochs on a
2000-hour dataset using large-scale multi-GPU training.

4.1. Model Comparison
Table 1 compares the performance of various models on
the DNS Challenge 2020 test dataset. Experimental re-
sults demonstrate that the proposed HFMSE matches
or surpasses the performance of current mainstream
speech enhancement models across multiple key percep-
tual metrics. It outperforms the discriminative model
TF-GridNet on all metrics. This advantage stems from
the generative approach’s ability to directly model the
clean speech distribution without relying on noise-specific
priors, offering greater expressive freedom and a higher
quality ceiling than deterministic mapping methods.
Furthermore, HFMSE shows clear gains over recent
discrete token-based generative methods. Token-based
approaches typically depend on pre-trained speech codecs
or language models to extract high-level semantic tokens,
a process that is inherently fragile under heavy noise
and can create a cyclic dependency with the denoising
task. HFMSE avoids this issue by leveraging low-level,
noise-robust harmonic features as conditioning, thereby
preventing degradation in speaker similarity and timbre
naturalness caused by corrupted semantics.
Compared to the flow matching method FlowSE,
HFMSE achieves better results on most metrics. No-
tably, FlowSE’s best performance requires additional
text guidance; its results degrade without it. We observe
that the harmonic guidance yields particularly larger
gains in low-SNR and reverberant conditions, where se-
mantic or shallow features become unreliable. In con-
trast, HFMSE’s harmonic prior, derived from speech pro-
duction physics, offers greater noise robustness and gen-
erality without needing any external linguistic model.
4.2. Ablation Study
To evaluate the impact of each proposed module and in-
novative operation on model performance, we conduct a
detailed ablation study on the without reverb test set of
the DNS Challenge 2020. In table 2, “w/o” denotes the
removal of a specific component, while “-” indicates a
sub‑ablation experiment.
Notably, removing the harmonic encoder causes a
larger performance drop than removing the noisy speech
condition, indicating that the extracted harmonic prior
remains reliable even under severe noise. Furthermore,
ablating the gating mechanism by replacing it with sim-
ple point‑wise multiplication also degrades performance.
This result suggests that an explicit compression and
aggregation module is necessary to selectively combine
channel-wise representations and distill truly effective
harmonic information. The final ablation experiment
shows that training using the filling method can lead to
more stable training results.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we presented HFMSE, a flow-matching-
based speech enhancement framework guided by explicit
harmonic priors. By designing a two-step harmonic
encoder to extract structured harmonic information as
robust conditioning, our method addresses the limita-
tions of relying on noisy shallow features or unreliable
high-level semantics. Comprehensive experiments on
the DNS Challenge 2020 benchmark demonstrate that
HFMSE achieves state-of-the-art performance, outper-
forming both advanced discriminative models and recent
generative counterparts across multiple perceptual met-
rics. The ablation studies confirm the critical role of
the proposed harmonic-guided conditioning and the gat-
ing mechanism. Our work validates that injecting struc-
tured, physically-motivated acoustic priors is an effec-
tive strategy for guiding generative speech enhancement,
paving the way for more reliable and high-fidelity speech
restoration in complex acoustic environments.
