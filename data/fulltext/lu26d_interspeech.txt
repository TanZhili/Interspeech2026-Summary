Abstract
Audio grounding, or speech-driven open-set object detection,
aims to localize objects directly from speech, enabling gener-
alization beyond predefined categories. However, the scarcity
of paired audio-image data challenges models trained from
scratch, while previous text-mediated pipelines suffer from er-
ror propagation. To address these bottlenecks, we introduce
Speech2See, an end-to-end framework harnessing semantic pri-
ors via a progressive pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm.
Specifically, we design a Query-Guided Semantic Aggregation
module to resolve temporal speech redundancy by extracting
compact, semantics-rich representations. During fine-tuning, a
parameter-efficient Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts architecture en-
ables nuanced cross-modal alignment while maintaining ro-
bust generalization. Experiments demonstrate thatSpeech2See
achieves direct “listening-to-localization” without intermedi-
ate text, yielding state-of-the-art performance across multiple
benchmarks.

1. Introduction
Open-set object detection extends traditional closed-set de-
tection by recognizing not only known but also novel cate-
gories [1]. Motivated by how humans rely on language for
semantic interpretation, recent research has explored multi-
modal learning to construct shared visual-language representa-
tions from large-scale image–text pairs [2, 3]. This paradigm
enables broader category coverage and zero-shot capabilities.
Representative approaches, such as GLIP [4], YOLO-World [5],
and Grounding DINO [1], rely on text to provide semantic guid-
ance for object localization.
However, most existing studies remain confined to im-
age–text modalities [6, 7, 8]. In real-world applications like
human–robot interaction and voice-guided navigation, textual
input is often impractical or unavailable. Speech serves not
only as a natural and immediate alternative but also offers a
richer communication channel. Unlike static text, speech sig-
nals encapsulate inherent paralinguistic cues [9], such as into-
nation and emphatic stress, which can resolve referential am-
biguities by highlighting specific targets. Despite these advan-
tages, leveraging speech directly for open-set detection remains
a promising yet underexplored frontier.
A pioneering attempt is YOSS [10], which adopts a two-
stage design combining pre-trained audio and vision models. It
first aligns an audio encoder to visual features via textual media-
tion through CLIP [3], and then integrates the aligned audio en-

to map speech to image regions. However, this pipeline suffers
from two limitations. First, the decoupled two-stage training
hinders efficient end-to-end optimization, leading to subopti-
mal convergence [12]. Second, its reliance on text mediation
limits direct cross-modal fusion, underutilizing the rich acous-
tic cues and properties of speech [13]. Furthermore, progress in
this field is fundamentally constrained by the scarcity of paired
audio–image data, which makes training robust, generalizable
models from scratch extremely challenging.
To address these challenges, we proposeSpeech2See, a
novel end-to-end framework that effectively transfers seman-
tic knowledge from large-scale pre-trained models to the audio
grounding task. By leveraging the rich text-image alignment
from Grounding DINO [1] and robust acoustic representations
from HuBERT [14], our approach mitigates the reliance on mas-
sive paired datasets. It is noteworthy that a modality gap exists
between these two types of knowledge, necessitating modality
alignment. To bridge this gap,Speech2Seeemploys a progres-
sive pre-training and fine-tuning paradigm, enabling the effec-
tive transfer of prior knowledge.
During pre-training, we introduce a lightweight Query-
Guided Semantic Aggregation (QSA) module. Acting as a se-
mantic adapter, QSA distills raw speech features into a compact
representation that can be directly fused with visual features.
During fine-tuning, although the transferred decoder parameters
retain semantic knowledge from text–image alignment, they re-
main suboptimal for speech-guided decoding. To deepen the
alignment, we enhance the decoder with a Mixture-of-LoRA-
Experts (MoLE) architecture, inspired by LLaV A-MoLE [15].
Extensive experiments demonstrate thatSpeech2Seeachieves
state-of-the-art performance in audio grounding, effectively
overcoming the challenges of data scarcity and the limitations
of prior methods.

3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Implementation Details
Datasets.Following the standard open-set object detection pro-
tocol, we conduct experiments on four widely used multimodal
benchmarks. Since these datasets lack native speech annota-
tions, we construct a multi-speaker audio corpus by synthesiz-
ing thetextual annotations(i.e., category names or captions)
usingedge-TTS 1. To ensure acoustic diversity and model ro-
bustness, we employ 10 distinct speakers with varying timbres
and prosody. The overall dataset statistics are summarized in
Table 1.
Configuration.Our method is built upon Grounding DINO [1],
employing Swin-T [16] and HuBERT-Base [14] as visual and
speech backbones, respectively. The detector is configured with
900 object queries, and the speech input is normalized to a fixed
length of 256 tokens. Following the standard protocol [1], the
detection loss comprises a contrastive loss, an L1 bounding box
loss, and a GIoU loss [18]. For the Hungarian matching step,
the cost coefficients are set toλ cls = 2.0,λ L1 = 5.0,λ giou =
2.0, while the final loss weights for optimization areλ cls =
1.0,λ L1 = 5.0,λ giou = 2.0. For the MoLE fine-tuning, the
load-balancing loss weight is set toλ lb = 1×10 −2.
Training Strategy.In thepre-training stage, the Swin-T and
HuBERT backbones, along with the detection decoder, are
frozen. Only the QSA module and projection layers are op-
timized using AdamW with a learning rate of1×10 −4 and
a batch size of 64 for 10 epochs, following a linear warm-up
schedule. In thefine-tuning stage, we insert MoLE modules
with rankr= 64andK= 2experts into the FFN layers. Only
the MoLE parameters are updated for 2 epochs, while all other
parameters remain frozen. All experiments are conducted on
8×NVIDIA A800 GPUs.

3.2. Experimental Results
We comprehensively evaluate the performance ofSpeech2See
under both closed-set and zero-shot detection settings, and fur-
ther examine the contribution of the Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts
(MoLE) fine-tuning strategy.
Closed-set Audio-Visual Detection.We conduct a closed-
set evaluation on the COCO2017-val benchmark, as shown in
Table 2. Compared with the two-stage approach of YOSS, our
approach achieves substantial improvements, outperforming the
strongest YOSS variant by a significant margin of +17.0 AP
(56.2 vs. 39.2). This remarkable gain validates the superior-
ity of our end-to-end design, which establishes a direct speech-
vision alignment that effectively avoids the information bottle-
necks and error propagation inherent in previous multi-stage
systems.
Zero-shot Detection on COCO.We evaluate the zero-shot
capability on the COCO benchmark, where the model is not
exposed to any COCO images or annotations during training.
Although the baseline YOSS is fully supervised on COCO (and
thus ineligible for zero-shot comparison), we include it as a su-
pervised reference. As shown in Table 2,Speech2Seeachieves
competitive results. Remarkably, our zero-shot performance
(42.7 AP) even surpasses theclosed-setperformance of the su-
pervised YOSS-large (39.2 AP). This significant result affirms
the vital role of transferring text-image prior knowledge and
our progressive alignment strategy in achieving robust speech-
driven detection. While a performance gap remains compared
to the text-driven upper bound, this reflects the inherent am-
biguity and complexity of acoustic signals, such as noise and
speaker variance, compared to explicit text prompts.
Zero-shot Detection on LVIS.We further evaluate
Speech2Seeon the long-tailed LVIS benchmark, which con-
tains 1,203 categories. As shown in Table 3, this task remains
highly challenging for speech-based models. Notably, our ap-
proach achieves a consistent performance improvement over
the two-stage YOSS model across all metrics, with a gain of
+3.6 AP(19.9 vs. 16.3). Despite these improvements, a gap
still exists compared with text-driven detectors. This is primar-
ily attributed to the fundamental difference in representation:
while text prompts consist ofdiscrete symbols with explicit se-
mantics, acoustic features arecontinuous and inherently en-
tangled with paralinguistic variations(e.g., prosody, speaker
identity), making precise semantic alignment more challenging.
Effect of Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts Fine-tuning.The
results in Table 2 and Table 3 demonstrate that incorporating
the MoLE architecture consistently improves performance in
both closed-set and zero-shot settings. This validates our hy-
pothesis that a static adaptation mechanism is insufficient for
the complexities of real-world speech. By adaptively routing
inputs to specialized experts, MoLE refines the initial align-
ment, preventing the transferred knowledge from being diluted
by speech’s inherent diversity and paralinguistic cues. This al-
lows the model to learn more nuanced cross-modal mappings,
leading to more robust and accurate detection.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposeSpeech2See, an end-to-end frame-
work that enables robust open-set object detection directly from
speech. By introducing a progressive training paradigm, we
successfully bridge the gap between continuous acoustic sig-
nals and discrete semantic concepts, effectively mitigating the
data scarcity issue. Our core designs, the Query-Guided Se-
mantic Aggregation and Mixture-of-LoRA-Experts, work syn-
ergistically to extract sparse semantic cues and adaptively han-
dle speech diversity. Empirical results confirm thatSpeech2See
not only establishes new state-of-the-art performance in zero-
shot scenarios but also outperforms cascaded systems in both
accuracy and inference efficiency. Since relying on synthesized
data remains the current norm, our contribution as a pioneering
study is to propose the novel end-to-end framework and validate
its fundamental effectiveness. Future work will benchmark on
real-world datasets and explore more robust alignment strate-
gies for noisy environments and long-tailed categories.
