Abstract
Modern video understanding requires integrating multimodal
signals, yet current Multimodal Large Language Models
(MLLMs) often process audio and visual streams separately,
causing inconsistencies in temporal and semantic alignment.
We propose a novel framework for coherent audio-visual un-
derstanding using: 1) rotary position encoding with frequency
injection for better synchronization; 2) cross-frame patch differ-
ential attention to track visual changes linked to audio events;
and 3) temporally aligned cross-modal attention with adaptive
weighting to enhance information exchange while preserving
modality-specific features. Experiments on the A VSD bench-
mark yield state-of-the-art accuracy of 50.30%, an 11.59% im-
provement over baseline. Out-of-domain tests confirm strong
generalization, especially in audio-driven hallucination detec-
tion and long-form video understanding. Our results highlight
that successful audio-visual understanding depends not only on
multimodal processing, but on precise temporal and semantic
alignment.

1. Introduction
In the real world, people naturally interpret events by integrating
both what they see and what they hear. Human understanding
is inherently multimodal: we do not process visual scenes and
sounds in isolation, but instead combine these signals to make
sense of complex situations. Inspired by this, multimodal large
language models (MLLMs) have rapidly evolved to integrate di-
verse data streams. However, achieving consistent and coherent
audio-video understanding remains a particularly challenging
frontier, as it demands precise temporal alignment and seman-
tic correspondence between visual and auditory information [1].
While large language models have made remarkable strides in
text comprehension and generation—from GPT-2 [2] to GPT-
4 [3]—and open-source alternatives like Llama3 [4] continue to
expand the boundaries of language understanding, developing
models that can truly mirror human-like, integrated audio-visual
perception is still an open challenge.
Vision models have advanced from foundational ap-
proaches like DETR [5] and ViLBERT [6] to more sophisti-
cated vision-language models such as CLIP [7], BLIP-2 [8],

speech recognition capabilities, while parameter-efficient ap-
proaches such as Qwen2-Audio [11] have shown promise in in-
tegrating audio features with language processing. Recent work
such as UniA VLM [12] also explores unified audio-visual lan-
guage modeling. However, a critical gap persists in developing
systems that ensure temporal coherence and semantic consis-
tency across audio and video streams.
Current multimodal systems often process audio and video
in separate pathways, failing to capture the intricate temporal
correspondences and semantic alignments between what is seen
and heard. Even advanced frameworks like MiniCPM-V [13]
and Janus Pro [14], while excelling at vision-language tasks,
lack mechanisms to ensure consistency in audio-visual under-
standing. Similarly, recent audio-visual models like MiniCPM-
o [13] and Qwen-Omni [15], despite their multimodal capabil-
ities, still rely on separate encoders with limited cross-modal
interaction, preventing true temporal alignment between audio
cues and visual events. This separation creates challenges in ap-
plications requiring fine-grained temporal synchronization and
semantic coherence across modalities, such as multimedia con-
tent analysis, audio-visual event localization, and video moment
retrieval.
To address these challenges, we present a novel framework
for Consistent and Coherent Audio-Video Understanding that
systematically integrates temporal alignment and semantic cor-
respondence between audio and visual streams. Our approach
introduces:
• A cross-modal temporal alignment mechanism ensuring
frame-level correspondence between streams,
• A bidirectional attention framework with frequency injection
that captures semantic coherence between modalities, and
• A differential attention module that identifies and tracks
changes across video frames corresponding to audio events.
Our experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance
across multiple metrics, particularly in scenarios demanding
precise cross-modal understanding.

As shown in Table 1, C 2A VLM achieves state-of-the-art per-

Despite Qwen-Omni processing all available frames while

MiniCPM v2.6 and its audio-enabled variant o2.6 (merely
0.04% improvement) demonstrates that audio capability with-
out proper integration mechanisms offers minimal benefit.
These results confirm that effective audio-video under-
standing requires not just processing multiple modalities, but
establishing proper temporal alignment and semantic corre-
spondence between streams, as implemented in our approach.
3.3. Ablation study
We conduct ablation studies examining audio modality contri-
bution, temporal encoding mechanisms, and cross-modal in-
teraction components on the A VSD dataset (Table 2). Results
show each component’s critical contribution: excluding audio
during evaluation significantly decreases accuracy; removing
temporal encoding mechanisms (RoPE and Frequency-Aware
Feature Projection) substantially impairs performance; while
both CFPDA and cross-modal attention independently improve
base performance, with their combination yielding optimal re-
sults—demonstrating complementary roles in modality fusion
and dynamic audio-visual correspondence detection.

4. Conclusion
In this work, we introduced a novel framework for consis-
tent and coherent audio-visual understanding that effectively
captures temporal alignment and semantic correspondence be-
tween modalities through temporal encoding, cross-frame patch
differential attention, and temporal aligned cross-modal atten-
tion with adaptive weighting. Our model achieves state-of-the-
art performance on the A VSD benchmark while demonstrating
impressive generalization capabilities in out-of-domain evalua-
tions—particularly excelling in audio-driven hallucination de-
tection on A VHBench and maintaining consistent performance
on long-form video understanding in Video-MME. These re-
sults confirm that careful temporal alignment and deep cross-
modal interaction are essential for robust audio-visual reason-
ing, highlighting the potential of principled multimodal integra-
tion for building systems that more closely mirror human per-
ception and understanding.
