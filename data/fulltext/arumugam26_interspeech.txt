A Human-in-the-Loop Multi-Agent Companion for Real-Time Entity
Extraction and SLU-Driven ASR Error Correction
Shiva Shankar Arumugam, Ameyaditya Achar J, Aashraya Sachdeva
Observe.AI, India
{shiva.shankar,ameyaditya.achar,aashraya.sachdeva}@observe.ai
Abstract
Contact-center agents handle thousands of entity-rich voice in-
teractions per day, where a misrecognised customer name, ac-
count identifier, or domain term cascades into wrong CRM en-
tries and additional manual rework. We demonstrate MACE
(Multi-Agent Companion Environment), a closed-loop archi-
tecture in which a real-timecompanion– an AI overlay that runs
alongside the live agent and surfaces extracted named entities as
proactive suggestions for in-place confirmation – routes every
UI correction back into the ASR stack as either a contextual-
biasing entryBor, once recurrent, a post-ASR substitution rule
R, with no acoustic retraining and no a-priori entity list. On the
English ContextASR-Bench benchmark with Whisper-large-v3,
the loop reduces NE-WER by17.1%and EditRate by18.6%
over a no-biasing baseline, closing23.6%of the gap to an ora-
cle that is given the entity list in advance.
Index Terms: contextual biasing, human-in-the-loop, multi-
agent systems, named entity recognition, ASR error correction,
demonstration
1. Introduction
Contact-center traffic is dense in named entities – customer
identifiers, product SKUs, drug names, policy numbers, proper
nouns – that must reach a CRM accurately and in real time.
Streaming ASR persistently misrecognises these tail-frequency
terms [1, 2], and the downstream extraction stage propagates the
error to the interface the human agent is monitoring during the
live call. That interface is what we term acompanion: a real-
time AI overlay that runs alongside the agent, extracts named
entities from the conversation, and surfaces them as proactive
suggestions for in-place confirmation or correction [3]. Each
correction is the cleanest possible supervision signal – a human
verifying a specific machine prediction against the audio they
just heard – yet it is seldom recycled back into the ASR or ex-
traction stack. We close that loop: every companion-UI correc-
tion becomes either a contextual biasing entryBconsumed by
the next ASR call or, once recurrent, a post-ASR substitution
ruleR[4, 5]. No acoustic retraining and no a-priori vocabu-
lary; the system bootstraps its own domain dictionary from the
agent’s edits.
The contribution is twofold. First, a multi-agent architec-
ture in which a dedicated reflection sub-agent autonomously
converts companion-UI corrections into biasing updates and
post-ASR substitution rules, dispatched off the audio path so
live transcription latency is unaffected. Second, an empirical
demonstration on ContextASR-Bench [2] with Whisper-large-
v3 in which the loop reduces NE-WER by17.1%and Edi-
tRate by18.6%over the no-biasing baseline, closing23.6%
and22.0%of the gap to oracle biasing.

3. Workflow and integration
The loop deploys into a real-time voice pipeline serving live
customer calls with three additive properties: the reflection up-
date is dispatched off the audio path so live transcription latency
is unaffected;BandRare persisted in a key-value store con-
sulted at the start of every new call and scoped per companion
agent (no cross-tenant leakage); and a reflection consumer ob-
serves edit events to bootstrapBdynamically while committed
substitution rules can additionally be injected into the running
word-correction stage for the same call’s next transcript frame.
4. Experiment methodology and results
4.1. Dataset
We evaluate on the English ContextASR-Dialogue split of
ContextASR-Bench [2]: 5,273 multi-speaker dialogues with
∼63,000named-entity mentions across10+domains,9to
21entities per dialogue (mean12), TTS-synthesised under a
phoneme-error-rate quality gate, dominated by person names,
work titles, and technical terms (categories where streaming
ASR commonly fails).
4.2. Methodology
A single sweep over the same audio produces three conditions
on a shared denominator:No Biasing(B=R=∅),Oracle Bi-
asing(B=E ∗
i , an upper bound that knows the entity list in ad-
vance), andMACE Adaptive(BandRgrowing per batch under
the reflection rule). Dialogues are shuffled with seed 42 and
partitioned intoK=100sequential batches; updates committed
during batchkbecome visible only to batchk+1, mirroring the
next-call semantic of the deployed loop. A deterministic sur-
rogate extractor (substring match plus SequenceMatcher fuzzy
fallback at similarity≥0.7) proxies the LLM extraction sub-
agent so that thousands of dialogues can be replayed without
per-call LLM cost; an oracle editor against the ground-truth en-
tity list emits the∆ i deltas, and the reflection routine [5] up-
datesBandRvia Eq. 1 with recurrence thresholdτ=2, the
minimum cross-dialogue repeat count required forNewRules
to promote a substitution from candidate to committed rule (gat-
ing against one-off fuzzy-alignment artefacts).
