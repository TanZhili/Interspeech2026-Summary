Abstract
Parametric array loudspeakers (PALs) provide directional sound
for private speech applications but exhibit strong nonlinear be-
havior, making accurate modeling challenging. Recent deep
learning methods typically adopt an audio-to-audio (A2A)
framework that maps baseband audio input directly to audi-
ble outputs, which implicitly compounds ultrasonic modula-
tion with airborne self-demodulation, demanding high com-
plexity to resolve the integrated nonlinearity. Motivated by
underlying physics, we propose U2A-Net, a multi-rate neural
ultrasound-to-audio (U2A) framework that maps modulated ul-
trasonic signals to audible outputs. By decoupling the modula-
tion process, U2A-Net focuses entirely on the intrinsic nonlin-
ear self-demodulation. Experiments on a representative PAL
show that U2A-Net consistently outperforms A2A networks
and V olterra filters, achieving average nonlinear distortion mod-
eling error below 1.62%, which highlight the advantage of
physics-motivated reformulation for PAL modeling.

1. Introduction
Parametric array loudspeakers (PALs) generate directional au-
dible sound by exploiting the nonlinear acoustic interactions
of ultrasonic waves in air [1, 2]. This unique property makes
PALs attractive for various speech and audio applications, such
as private speech communication systems [3–5], spatial audio
reproduction [6–8], and active noise control [9, 10]. However,
the inherent nonlinearity responsible for self-demodulation also
introduces substantial distortion in the reproduced audio, no-
tably harmonic and intermodulation products, which signifi-
cantly limits their broader adoption in high-fidelity audio engi-
neering [11,12]. As a result, nonlinear distortion-oriented iden-
tification and compensation of PAL nonlinearities has long been
recognized as a challenging problem, where modeling fidelity is
assessed by how accurately a model reproduces harmonic and
intermodulation artifacts quantified by total harmonic distortion
(THD) and inter-modulation distortion (IMD).
Early analytical studies established the Berktay’s far-field
solution, which relates the audible pressure to the squared en-
velope of the ultrasonic signal [13]. While this model provides
important physical insight, it relies on restrictive assumptions—
such as weak nonlinearity, collimated plane-wave propagation,
and far-field observation—and thus fails to accurately capture
the complex nonlinear behavior of PALs in real-world sce-
narios. Subsequent approaches formulated PALs as nonlinear

Recently, deep learning-based approaches have been ex-
plored within an audio-to-audio (A2A) modeling framework,
where both the input and output are audio-band signals [18–20].
These methods provide a flexible and general solution that
achieves high identification accuracy when sufficiently large
model capacity is employed. However, the A2A framework
implicitly entangles ultrasonic modulation, transducer’s fre-
quency response, and airborne self-demodulation into a sin-
gle black-box mapping. This forces the model to simultane-
ously learn the modulation mechanism and the nonlinearity of
demodulation—two complex nonlinear processes—often lead-
ing to excessive model complexity and training difficulty.
Physically, the audible sound in PALs originates from the
nonlinear interaction of modulated ultrasonic waves, which
is described by Westervelt equation [21–23]. This mecha-
nism suggests that PAL identification is more naturally for-
mulated using the modulated ultrasonic signal as the system
input. Based on this underlying physics, previous ultrasonic-
driven studies have employed VF-based identification under an
ultrasonic-to-ultrasonic modeling framework (U2U-VF) [24],
attempting to map ultrasonic excitation to the full-band radiated
sound. However, the full-band signal is dominated by primary
ultrasonic waves with energy orders of magnitude higher than
the self-demodulated audio. This imbalance in spectral energy
forces the model to focus on reconstructing the dominant ultra-
sonic carrier, often at the expense of accurately modeling subtle
audio-band nonlinear distortions that we truly care about.
To address this mismatch between the modeling target and
the actual application objective, we propose U2A-Net, a neural
Ultrasonic-to-Audio (U2A) framework for PAL system identi-
fication that directly maps modulated ultrasonic driving signal
to the purified audible output. By explicitly removing the need
to model the modulation process and the ultrasonic component
in output, the U2A-Net formulation substantially simplifies the
nonlinear learning task, allowing the model to concentrate its
full expressive power on the intrinsic ultrasonic-to-audio non-
linear behavior that are of primary interest. To accommodate
the cross-domain resolution disparity, we adopt a multi-rate

2. Nonlinear system identification of PALs
To systematically investigate the impact of task formulation on
PAL modeling, we categorize the problem into three distinct
mapping paradigms based on their input-output signal domains,
as illustrated in Fig. 1. Given a desired baseband audio signal
sa(t), it is first modulated onto an ultrasonic carrier, producing
a ultrasound-band driving signals u(t). This modulated signal
is then amplified and radiated into the air by ultrasonic transduc-
ers. During propagation, nonlinear acoustic interactions within
the medium induce self-demodulation, generating a secondary
audible componenty a(t)alongside the ultrasonic component
yu(t). The total radiated acoustic pressure can therefore be ex-
pressed asy(t) =y u(t) +y a(t). In practical audio applica-
tions, the primary quantity of interest is the audible component
ya(t), particularly its nonlinear distortion characteristics which
dictate the sound quality. Thus, the objective of PAL nonlin-
ear system identification is to accurately predict or emulate this
audible acoustic response under arbitrary excitation.
2.1. Audio-to-Audio (A2A) formulation
Conventional PAL identification typically adopts an A2A
framework (Fig. 1), with the baseband audio signals a(t)as the
system input and the demodulated audible soundy a(t)as the
output:
ya(t) =F A2A(sa(t)).(1)
Under this formulation, the model must implicitly capture a
composite of cascaded effects, including ultrasonic modulation,
transducer frequency response, and nonlinear airborne propa-
gation within a single nonlinear mapping. From an analytical
perspective, Berktay’s far-field solution [13] represents a clas-
sical instance of this A2A formulation, as it approximates the
audible pressure solely in terms of the squared envelope of the
modulated signal. However, such black-box mapping forces the
model to learn disparate physical processes simultaneously, of-
ten leading to excessive complexity.

By aligning the model input with the actual physical excitation

two distinct advantages:
•Decoupling:It removes the burden of learning the modula-
tion mechanism inherent in A2A.
•Focus:It eliminates the need to reconstruct the dominant ul-
trasonic carrier, a common pitfall in U2U.
Consequently, this framework provides a principled and task-
oriented foundation for accurate nonlinear PAL modeling.

Unlike conventional A2A identification, which operates en-
tirely in the audio band at a single sampling rate (e.g., 48 kHz),
U2A-Net involves signals in different frequency ranges. The
modulated ultrasonic input is centered around the carrier fre-
quency (e.g. 40 kHz) and therefore requires a higher sampling
rate for accurate waveform representation and anti-aliasing.
Consequently, we sample the ultrasonic signals u(t)at 192 kHz
in this work. While the audible outputy a(t)can also be sam-
pled at 192 kHz, its effective bandwidth lies within the audio
range. Using a uniformly high sampling rate for both input and
output would introduce redundancy and increase computational
cost. Therefore, we adopt a multi-rate setting, where the ul-
trasonic input is sampled at 192 kHz and the audible output is
represented at 48 kHz. This introduces an inherent4×temporal
resolution reduction that the neural architecture must bridge.

4. Experiments
4.1. Evaluation metrics
Unlike conventional audio modeling where waveform similar-
ity may dominate evaluation, PAL identification is particularly
sensitive to low-amplitude nonlinear artifacts that are perceptu-
ally salient yet may contribute little to waveform-domain MSE
due to the dominance of the fundamental component. We there-
fore adopt THD and IMD, two key metrics for nonlinear dis-
tortion, as primary metrics and report the absolute deviation be-
tween the distortion curves computed from measured outputs
and those computed from model predictions, which directly tar-
gets the nonlinear artifacts of interest. THD is computed as the
square root of the ratio of the power sum of the harmonic com-
ponents to the total power (fundamental plus harmonics) [27].
IMD is computed following [27] by aggregating the specified
intermodulation products relative to the two primary tones. In
addition to distortion metrics, the linear frequency response of
the PAL is also evaluated to assess the modeling accuracy of the
linear component.
A step sine is used for the linear frequency response and
THD evaluation with center frequencies spanning 1/12-octave
bands from 500 Hz to 7550 Hz. For IMD, another pure tone at
1.7 kHzis added in the step sine, and the amplitude of the pure
tone is 4 times as that of the step sine as specificed in [25]. The
absolute deviation between predicted and measured THD/IMD
values is reported as the distortion modeling error.
4.2. PAL prototypes and dataset acquisition
The laboratory-fabricated PAL prototype used in experiment
consists of 576 circular ultrasonic emitters (Murata MA40S4S,
Kyoto, Japan) which has a resonance frequency of40 kHzand
a radius of5 mm. The ultrasonic emitters are arranged in a
compact square array with side lengths of around24 cm. The
modulation method used in this PAL system is USBAM, which
suppresses one modulation sideband and significantly reduces
harmonic distortion in the demodulated audible sound under
ideal conditions. However, due to the inherent nonlinear acous-
tic interactions during propagation in air, the system still ex-
hibits significant IMD. Consequently, the distortion pattern of
the USBAM-based PAL is characterized by relatively low THD
but comparatively higher IMD. This indicates that the nonlinear
behavior is not dominated by simple harmonic generation, but
rather by more complex cross-frequency nonlinear interactions.
Such characteristics impose additional challenges for nonlinear
system identification and provide a meaningful benchmark for
evaluating different modeling paradigms.
To construct the identification dataset, a diverse set of base-
band audio signals—including speech, music, and environmen-
tal sounds—is used as excitation. The corresponding modu-
lated ultrasonic driving signalss u(t)and the radiated audible
responsesy a(t)are recorded simultaneously. All measurements
are conducted in an anechoic chamber at a fixed on-axis dis-
tance of 1.8 m from the PAL. In total, approximately 3 hours of
synchronized input–output data are collected. The recordings
are segmented into fixed-length clips of 1.37 s for batch train-
ing of neural networks. The dataset is randomly divided into
training, validation, and test subsets in a 7:2:1 ratio.
4.3. Compared methods
In experiments, we compare three identification approaches: (a)
U2A-Net realized by a modified multi-rate WaveNet, (b) A2A-
Neural modeling baseline (denoted as A2A-Net) by a single-
rate (48 kHz) WaveNet [18], and (c) a U2A-V olterra baseline
(denoted as U2A-VF), where a conventional second-order VF
is trained under the proposed U2A input–output formulation.
The U2A-VF baseline is included as a controlled reference to
disentangle the impact of the U2A task formulation from the
choice of model class (V olterra vs. neural networks).
The multi-rate WaveNet used for U2A-Net has 16 resid-
ual blocks, where the dilated convolution layers have a dilation
pattern ofd k ={1,2,4,...,2 15}with a common kernel size
M= 6, and share a common channel numberC= 16with
the pointwise convolution layers. For fair comparison, the A2A
baseline uses the same WaveNet backbone and hyperparame-
ters; the downsampling modules are disabled (stride set to 1)
to keep a single-rate 48 kHz input–output mapping. Addition-
ally, the total duration of dataset used for A2A-Net is also the
same as U2A-Net. The U2A-VF adopts a typical second-order
realization with memory lengths ofN 1 = 480andN 2 = 240.

4.4. Results and analysis
Figure 3 presents the measured and synthesized frequency re-
sponse, THD, and IMD curves across 500 Hz–7550 Hz under
four different levels of input audio amplitude, while Table 1
reports the corresponding averaged absolute THD and IMD de-
viations from the measured ground truth. These THD/IMD pat-
terns directly reflect audible-band nonlinear artifacts (harmonic
and intermodulation products) that are critical to fidelity in di-
rectional speech and audio reproduction. The close agreement
in the linear responses (top row of Fig. 3) suggests that the dif-
ferences observed in THD/IMD primarily originate from how
each method models the nonlinear self-demodulation behavior.
In terms of nonlinear distortion, the proposed U2A-Net
consistently yields the lowest average error and closely follows
the measured THD and IMD profiles across most excitation lev-
els. In contrast, A2A-Net exhibits significant performance fluc-
tuations; specifically, its THD predictions suffer from large er-
rors in the low-frequency region and consistently exhibit partic-
ularly substantial deviations around the 3 kHz mark. Further-
more, A2A-Net tends to noticeably overestimate the IMD prod-
ucts in the mid-frequency range. It is noted that at the lowest
input level (0.3), where the excitation is weak and the system
exhibits relatively mild nonlinearity, the advantage of the U2A-
Net is less pronounced, with U2A-Net even showing slightly
larger errors at specific frequency points compared to higher
levels. However, as the excitation increases to the highest input
level (1.0) and nonlinearities become dominant, the U2A-Net
demonstrates superior robustness and tracking fidelity, which is
quantitatively confirmed by the minimal average absolute devi-
ations recorded in Table 1.
Although U2A-VF adopts the same input–output formula-
tion as U2A-Net, its distortion modeling accuracy remains sub-
stantially worse, with the predicted THD and IMD staying close
to zero across most frequencies as shown in Fig. 3. This lim-
itation stems from the restricted expressiveness of the second-
order V olterra series. Under the U2A formulation, a second-
order VF can capture only limited quadratic interactions from
the ultrasonic driving signal to the audible output, which are in-
sufficient to characterize the cascaded, higher-order nonlinear
mechanisms governing distortion generation in practical PAL
systems. In principle, extending VF to higher orders could im-
prove its modeling capability; however, the number of V olterra
coefficients grows rapidly with the kernel order and memory
length, making higher-order realizations increasingly difficult
to estimate and deploy in practice. These results suggest that,
while the proposed U2A formulation is beneficial, accurate PAL
distortion modeling also requires a sufficiently expressive yet
tractable nonlinear model, as provided by U2A-Net.
5. Conclusions
In this paper, we propose U2A-Net, a neural Ultrasound-to-
Audio framework for PAL system modeling. Motivated by
underlying physics, U2A-Net explicitly removes the need to
model the modulation process and the ultrasonic component in
output, thus substantially simplifies the nonlinear learning task,
allowing the model to focus its expressive power on the nonlin-
ear self-demodulation behavior that is of primary interest. Ex-
periments conducted on a USBAM-based PAL system demon-
strate that the proposed U2A-Net consistently outperforms ex-
isting VF method and the A2A neural approach, establishing it
as a practical and effective solution for PAL system modeling.
