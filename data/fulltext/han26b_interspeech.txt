Abstract
Acoustic scene classification (ASC) requires lightweight mod-
els that effectively capture diverse temporal and spectral cues
for mobile devices. Multi-branch convolutional architectures
improve receptive field diversity, but their outputs are typically
aggregated without explicitly modeling complementary rela-
tionships or compensating for branch-specific limitations. We
propose a branch-wise complementary attention (BCA) mod-
ule that allocates channel, time, frequency, and joint time-
frequency attention maps across branches according to their re-
ceptive field characteristics. By explicitly addressing the inher-
ent limitations of each branch, BCA enhances multi-scale fea-
ture integration beyond simple aggregation. Integrated into the
Rep-Mobile backbone, BCA consistently outperforms existing
attention mechanisms on the TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2020
and 2022 Mobile benchmarks.
Index Terms: Acoustic scene classification, multi-branch con-
volution, branch-wise attention, channel-time-frequency atten-

1. Introduction
Acoustic scene classification (ASC) aims to categorize prede-
fined acoustic environments from audio recordings [1, 2, 3, 4].
This capability is crucial for mobile sensing, smart devices, and
embedded systems. These applications demand models that
achieve both high accuracy and low computational complexity
on resource-limited hardware. Convolutional neural networks
(CNNs) have shown strong performance on ASC by effectively
extracting discriminative time-frequency patterns [5, 6, 7, 8, 9].
However, CNNs with fixed kernel structures struggle to cap-
ture acoustic events occurring at diverse temporal and spec-
tral scales, limiting their generalization to complex environ-
ments [10]. To address this issue, multi-branch CNNs em-
ploy kernels of different sizes in parallel to increase receptive
field diversity [11, 12]. Despite this, their outputs are typi-
cally aggregated through simple concatenation or summation.
Although simple aggregation improves representation capacity,
it does not explicitly capture complementary relationships be-
tween branches. As a result, the strengths and limitations of
individual kernels are not adaptively leveraged.
Attention mechanisms provide a natural solution for fea-
ture recalibration. Originally designed for image recognition,
modules such as squeeze-and-excitation (SE) [13] and efficient
channel attention (ECA) [14] have been widely adopted to re-
fine channel-wise representations. In audio applications, atten-
tion mechanisms have extended to the frequency and time di-
mensions to better capture spectro-temporal structures [15, 16,

In this work, we propose a branch-wise complementary at-
tention (BCA) module that explicitly leverages branch diver-
sity in multi-branch CNNs for ASC. BCA learns channel, time,
frequency, and joint time-frequency attention maps and allo-
cates them across convolutional branches in a complementary
manner based on their receptive field characteristics: time at-
tention is applied to spectral-oriented branches, and frequency
attention to temporal-oriented branches. This cross-branch al-
location compensates for branch-specific limitations and en-
ables more effective multi-scale feature integration beyond sim-
ple aggregation. We instantiate BCA on the Rep-Mobile back-
bone [20] and evaluate it on the TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes
2020 (TAU 2020) and TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2022 Mo-
bile (TAU 2022) datasets [21]. The results demonstrate consis-
tent performance gains over strong baselines and existing atten-
tion modules while maintaining computational efficiency suit-
able for resource-constrained environments.

2.2. Branch-wise Complementary Attention Module
We propose a BCA module, which assigns attention weights
to each convolutional branch to account for its receptive field
characteristics, as illustrated in Fig. 2. BCA computes chan-
nel, time, frequency, and joint time-frequency attention maps
using global descriptors similar to channel-time-frequency at-
tention (CTFA) [17]. These attention maps are then allocated to
the four branches in a complementary manner: channel atten-
tion to the3×3branch, time attention to the spectral-oriented
3×1branch, frequency attention to the temporal-oriented1×3
branch, and a joint time-frequency attention to the1×1branch.
This allocation ensures that each branch benefits from contex-
tual information not directly captured by its own receptive field.

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets
We evaluated our method on two benchmark datasets: TAU
2020 and TAU 2022 [21]. Both datasets contained single-
channel recordings from 10 urban acoustic scenes, captured
with multiple real and simulated mobile devices to incorporate
diverse recording devices. TAU 2020 and TAU 2022 consisted
of 10-second and 1-second audio segments, respectively; all
recordings were sampled at 44.1 kHz.
3.2. Data Augmentation
To improve robustness and generalization, we employed three
data augmentation strategies: (1)Time shifting: Input wave-
forms were circularly shifted by a random offset within±125
ms. (2)SpecAugment: Frequency masking was applied with
a maximum width of 48 Mel frequency bins [28]. (3)Freq-
MixStyle: A frequency-domain extension of MixStyle [29] was
adopted to mix feature statistics across samples along the fre-
quency axis withα= 0.3andp= 0.7[30].
3.3. Training Setup
All recordings were downsampled to 32 kHz. Log-Mel spectro-
grams were computed using an STFT with a window length of
3,072 samples, a hop size of 512, and a 4,096-point FFT, fol-
lowed by a Mel filterbank with 256 filters. The models were
trained for 150 epochs using the Adam optimizer [31] with a
batch size of 128. The learning rate was linearly increased from
0 to 0.01 during the first 3,000 steps and then decayed to zero
using a cosine annealing schedule.

4. Results and Analysis
4.1. Comparison to Other Methods
Table 1 shows the comparison of CP-Mobile [23] and Rep-
Mobile [20] with various attention mechanisms on TAU
2020 and TAU 2022 [21]. Rep-Mobile improved over CP-
Mobile, confirming the effectiveness of the multi-branch de-
sign. Conventional attention modules (SE [13], ECA [14], and
CTFA [17]) were adapted to be applied branch-wise. However,
they provided only limited improvements over the Rep-Mobile
baseline. In contrast, the proposed BCA achieved 72.03% and
63.24% accuracy on TAU 2020 and TAU 2022, outperforming
Rep-Mobile by 3.17% and 1.72%, respectively. Notably, BCA
consistently surpassed SE, ECA, and CTFA on both datasets,
demonstrating the effectiveness of branch-wise complemen-
tary allocation. These improvements were obtained with only
marginal increases in parameters and MACs.
We further evaluated BCA-Lite, a lightweight channel-split
variant designed for low-complexity scenarios. Compared to
Rep-Mobile, BCA-Lite reduced parameters (126K→125K)
and MACs (286.5M→274.0M on TAU 2020, 29.1M→27.8M
on TAU 2022) while maintaining competitive accuracy (71.26%
/ 63.20%). Although slightly below full BCA, BCA-Lite pre-
served most of the performance gains with even lower compu-
tational cost than the reparameterized Rep-Mobile backbone.

Among these variants, the proposed allocation achieved the
best performance on TAU 2020 and TAU 2022. In particular,

tary to each branch improved feature integration. This result
further supported the design principle of BCA, where comple-
mentary allocation was used to compensate for branch-specific
receptive field limitations.
4.3. Contribution Analysis of Attention Types
Table 3 presents an ablation study on BCA by removing chan-
nel attention (CA), time attention (TA), and frequency attention
(FA). The proposed BCA achieved the highest accuracy on both
TAU 2020 and TAU 2022. Removing any single component
consistently degraded performance, confirming the importance
of each attention type.
In particular, removing CA resulted in drops on both
datasets, indicating its consistent contribution to feature recali-
bration. Removing TA caused the largest degradation on TAU
2020, suggesting that temporal modeling was especially impor-
tant for 10-second recordings. Conversely, removing FA led to
the largest drop on TAU 2022, where shorter clips provided lim-
ited temporal context and relied more on spectral information.
These results highlighted the complementary roles of time and
frequency attentions across different clip durations.

4.4. Visualization of Effective Receptive Fields
To analyze how BCA affected the receptive field of the net-
work, we visualized the effective receptive fields (ERFs) [32]
using a gradient-based visualization [33]. Gradients were back-
propagated from the central time-frequency location of the fi-
nal convolutional layer to the input log-Mel spectrogram, and
only positive gradients were retained using a ReLU function.
To quantify the ERF distribution, we measured the area ratio of
the minimum rectangular region containingτ% of the total gra-
dient energy forτ= 20%, 30%, and 50%. As shown in Table 4,
BCA consistently enlarged ERFs across all thresholds on both
TAU 2020 and TAU 2022. In particular, on TAU 2020 withτ
= 50%, the ERF area ratio increased from 13.58% to 28.47%,
indicating a substantial expansion of coverage. Similar trends
were observed on TAU 2022.
Figure 3 provides a visual comparison of the ERFs. The
baseline Rep-Mobile showed concentrated gradient responses
around the central region, whereas BCA exhibited broader ac-
tivation patterns extending along both time and frequency axes.
These results suggest that BCA allowed the network to incor-
porate information from a wider time-frequency context.
5. Conclusion
We proposed BCA for multi-branch CNNs in ASC. By allo-
cating channel, time, frequency, and joint time-frequency atten-
tions based on branch-specific receptive fields, BCA improves
multi-scale feature integration with minimal overhead. Experi-
ments on TAU 2020 and 2022 showed consistent improvements
over baselines and existing attention mechanisms, demonstrat-
ing the effectiveness of complementary allocation.
