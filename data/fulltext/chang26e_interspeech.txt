Personalized Electrolaryngeal Voice Conversion with a Single Pre-operative
Utterance
Devin Chang1, Hsin-Te Hwang1, Ming-Chi Yen1, Shu-Wei Tsai2, Yu Tsao1, Hsin-Min Wang1
1 Academia Sinica, Taipei, Taiwan
2 National Cheng Kung University Hospital, Tainan, Taiwan

Abstract
Electrolaryngeal voice conversion (ELVC) enhances the me-
chanical speech of laryngectomees to improve naturalness and
intelligibility. While personalized ELVC typically requires ex-
tensive pre-operative recordings for supervised training, this pa-
per investigates a one-shot scenario where only one natural ut-
terance is available for timbre preservation. We evaluate two
strategies using zero-shot VC models: (1) a cascaded approach,
performing ELVC followed by secondary conversion to the tar-
get timbre; and (2) a pseudo-target strategy, using a zero-shot
model to generate synthetic training data for a supervised ELVC
model. Results indicate that the cascaded approach outperforms
the pseudo-target strategy, remarkably improving speaker sim-
ilarity and quality with minimal impact on intelligibility. We
also demonstrate that direct application of zero-shot models to
ELVC fails due to domain mismatch, confirming the neces-
sity of our proposed framework for one-shot personalized voice
restoration.
Index Terms: Electrolaryngeal Speech, voice conversion, tim-
bre preservation
1. Introduction
Patients with advanced laryngeal cancer often require total la-
ryngectomy (TL), a surgical procedure that removes the larynx,
resulting in the loss of natural voice and speech [1]. To restore
patients’ ability to communicate after TL, electrolarynxes have
been developed, which generate mechanical speech by inducing
vibrations in the neck skin [1]. However, the robotic quality and
low naturalness of electrolaryngeal (EL) speech often lead to
poor intelligibility and listener discomfort, creating communi-
cation barriers that can significantly affect patients’ social lives.
[2].
To improve communication for these patients, voice con-
version (VC) techniques have been extensively studied to im-
prove the intelligibility of EL speech. Traditional statistical
methods such as Gaussian-Mixture Model [3, 4] and neural net-
work–based approaches [3, 5, 6, 7, 8] have achieved success
in improving EL speech intelligibility. Although these meth-
ods significantly improve intelligibility, the converted speech
typically resembles the voice of a pre-defined target speaker
rather than the patient’s original voice. Consequently, most ex-
isting ELVC systems focus on converting EL speech to another
speaker instead of restoring the patient’s own voice.
When pre-operative recordings of a patient’s natural (NL)
voice are available, personalized ELVC that restores both intel-
ligibility and speaker identity becomes highly desirable. Previ-
ous studies have explored converting EL speech to a patient’s
original voice using VC systems trained with parallel pre- and
post-operative recordings [9]. However, such approaches typi-
cally require a substantial amount of pre-operative speech data.
In real-world clinical scenarios, collecting large amounts of pre-
operative recordings is often impractical. Patients are rarely
recorded extensively before TL, and in many cases, only a very
limited amount of NL speech is available. Recent progress in
zero-shot and one-shot VC suggests that speaker identity can be
inferred from a single reference utterance [10, 11, 12, 13, 14]. In
related biomedical tasks such as dysarthric speech reconstruc-
tion (DSR), two-stage frameworks that separate intelligibility
restoration and speaker identity transfer have shown promising
results [15, 16]. These observations motivate the exploration of
similar strategies for personalized ELVC under extremely lim-
ited data conditions.
In this study, we investigatedone-shot personalized ELVC,
where only a single NL utterance from the source speaker is
available as the timbre reference for voice restoration. To our
knowledge, this work presents the first systematic benchmark
for one-shot personalized ELVC with only a single simulated
pre-operative utterance. Motivated by the failure of direct zero-
shot VC on EL speech, we explored two strategies. The first is
acascade approachinspired by two-stage DSR [16], where
EL speech is first converted to intelligible NL speech using
the locally linear embedding (LLE)-based ELVC (LLE-ELVC)
[17, 18] and then passed to a pretrained zero/one-shot VC model
to restore speaker identity. The second is apseudo-target
strategythat uses pretrained VC models to synthesize pseudo
training targets for personalized ELVC. We evaluated three pre-
trained zero/one-shot VC backbones: FreeVC [10], Vevo [11],
and Seed-VC [12]. Experimental results show that directly
applying zero/one-shot VC models to EL speech fails due to
severe domain mismatch. In contrast, the cascaded frame-
work significantly improves speaker similarity while maintain-
ing comparable intelligibility. By feeding intermediate LLE-
ELVC output features directly into the second-stage VC mod-
els, the cascade framework further reduces syllable error rate
(SER). With supervised refinement, the best cascaded system
slightly surpasses a supervised ELVC baseline that assumes ac-
cess to 240 simulated pre-operative NL utterances in restoring
intelligibility, while demonstrating improved speech quality and
substantially higher speaker similarity.
The main contributions of this work are summarized as fol-
lows:
1. We formulated and benchmarked the problem ofone-shot
personalized ELVC, where only a single NL utterance from
the target speaker is available for timbre preservation.
2. We investigated two strategies for one-shot personalized
ELVC: a pseudo-target strategy and a cascaded framework
that separates timbre transfer from intelligibility restoration.
3. We demonstrated that feature-level cascading and super-

2. Proposed Methods
2.1. The Pseudo-target Strategy
In this work, our setup used only 240 EL-NL parallel speech
utterances for ELVC training. As shown in Fig. 1a, with a pre-
trained zero-/one-shot VC model, we first converted the tim-
bre of the 240 recordings of an NL speaker to match that of
the single simulated pre-operative recording of the EL speaker,
thereby generating 240 pseudo target utterances. LLE-ELVC
was then trained to convert EL speech to pseudo targets, im-
proving both intelligibility and speaker similarity.
2.1.1. Zero- and One-shot VC Methods
We evaluated three VC models that support single-reference
timbre conversion.
•FreeVC[10]: A VITS-based one-shot VC model that per-
forms timbre conversion from a single reference utterance. It
uses WavLM [19] features for content representation and an
information bottleneck to filter speaker characteristics.
•Vevo[11]: A zero-shot VC framework that decouples style,
timbre, and linguistic content. Since our task only required
timbre conversion, we used the flow-matching transformer
component, which uses HuBERT [20] for continuous hidden
features.
•Seed-VC[12]: A zero-shot VC model that incorporates an
external timbre shifter to reduce timbre leakage and mis-
matches between training and inference. It utilizes Whisper-
small [21] as the semantic encoder and a diffusion trans-
former to produce a more robust timbre representation from
reference speech.
2.1.2. LLE-VC and LLE-ELVC
Exemplar-based methods such as LLE-VC [22, 23] have been
used in ELVC [17, 18] since they require relatively small paral-
lel training data. In this work, we adopted an LLE-VC frame-
work [18] consisting of four stages: neighbor search, weight es-
timation, feature reconstruction, and waveform synthesis with a
HiFi-GAN vocoder [24].
Following previous ELVC studies, Chinese-HuBERT fea-
tures [25] were used for robust content representation during
neighbor search, while WavLM-6th layer features [19] were
used for weight estimation and reconstruction. The HiFi-GAN
vocoder [24] was trained on LibriSpeech [26] and COSPRO
Mandarin [27] corpora.
2.2. The Cascade Approach
In addition to the pseudo-target strategy, we evaluated an al-
ternative framework that cascades LLE-ELVC with a zero/one-
shot VC method (Fig. 1b). In this framework, EL speech was
first converted to intelligible NL speech through LLE-ELVC.
The converted waveforms were then passed to the pretrained
FreeVC, Vevo, or Seed-VC models with default settings. Using
the single simulated pre-operative NL utterance as reference,
the zero- or one-shot VC model enhanced speaker similarity
while preserving intelligibility gained from LLE-ELVC.
2.2.1. Cascading with LLE-ELVC Output Features
We further evaluated a feature-level cascade that directly feeds
LLE-ELVC output features into zero- or one-shot VC mod-
els, bypassing intermediate waveform synthesis. LLE-ELVC
was configured to output the same semantic features used by
FreeVC, Vevo, or Seed-VC, reducing artifacts from waveform
synthesis and re-encoding.
2.2.2. Supervised Fine-tuning of Zero-shot VC Methods
To further refine the cascade approach, we performed speaker-
dependent supervised fine-tuning on the zero- or one-shot VC
models (Fig. 2), adapting them to LLE-ELVC-generated wave-
forms and the target speaker’s voice characteristics. LLE-ELVC
outputs were generated using leave-one-utterance-out (LOUO)
on the 240 EL-NL training utterance pairs. The resulting 240
utterances (features) served as source speech, while the cor-
responding Seed-VC generated pseudo-targets (Sec. 2.1) were
used for supervision. Dynamic time warping (DTW) [28] was
applied instead of the default truncation to align source and tar-

3. Experimental Setup and Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
3.1.1. Datasets
Our dataset consists of three male (EL01–NL08–NL01,
EL08–NL01–NL08, BYEL02–NL08–BNL02) and one female
(EL06–NL05–NL06) speaker pairs. Since real pre-operative
recordings from laryngectomy patients are rarely available, we
simulated the one-shot personalized ELVC scenario using non-
laryngectomized speakers. EL speech (EL01, EL08, BYEL02,
and EL06) was produced by these speakers using an electro-
larynx, and the corresponding NL recordings (NL01, NL08,
BNL02, and NL06) were treated as pre-operative speech. For
each speaker pair, we additionally assumed the presence of an-
other NL speaker with more recordings available. For all speak-
ers, we obtained 320 recordings of 10-character Mandarin sen-
tences from the Taiwanese Mandarin Hearing in Noise Test
(TMHINT) dataset [29], with all audio resampled to 16 kHz.
The recordings were split into 240/40/40 utterances for training,
development, and evaluation, respectively. Under the one-shot
assumption, only the first utterance from the development set of
the target speaker was used as the identity reference.
3.1.2. Compared Systems
We evaluated the following systems:
•Supervised ELVC Baseline: LLE-ELVC trained using fully
paired EL–NL data, assuming access to 240 pre-operative NL
utterances.
•Direct Zero-shot VC: applying Seed-VC, Vevo, or FreeVC
directly to EL speech.
•Pseudo-target Strategy(Sec. 2.1): pseudo-target speech
generated by zero-/one-shot VC models was used as super-
vision for LLE-ELVC.
•Cascade (Waveform)(Sec. 2.2): LLE-ELVC followed by
zero-/one-shot VC on reconstructed waveforms.
•Cascade (Feature)(Sec. 2.2.1): intermediate acoustic fea-
tures were directly passed to the second-stage VC model.
•Refined Cascade(Sec. 2.2.2): further improving the feature-
level cascade through speaker-dependent supervised refine-
ment.
3.1.3. Evaluation Metrics
We report four objective metrics: SpeechBERTScore (semantic
fidelity) [30], SER for intelligibility, speaker similarity based
on embedding cosine similarity, and UTMOS [31] for predicted
perceptual quality. The embedding cosine similarity was calcu-
lated with a resemblyzer provided by the Amphion toolkit [32].
To compute the SER, we trained a syllable recognition system
using the 196-hour MATBN corpus [33]. In addition, subjective
listening tests were conducted to evaluate perceived similarity
and intelligibility.
3.2. Results and Analysis
3.2.1. Direct Zero-Shot Failure and Structural Comparison
Table 1 summarizes the average performance across four
speaker pairs. The unprocessed EL input exhibits degraded
intelligibility (SER: 79.48%) and low perceptual quality (UT-
MOS: 1.31), confirming the need for enhancement. Directly
applying pretrained zero- or one-shot VC models to EL speech
leads to severe degradation. For example, FreeVC produces
SER exceeding 100%, while Seed-VC and Vevo yield SER
above 85%, which are worse than the unprocessed EL speech.
These results indicate a strong distribution mismatch between
EL speech and the NL speech assumed by pretrained VC mod-
els, motivating the need for an EL restoration stage before tim-
bre transfer.
We then compare the two strategies for one-shot personal-
ization. Using Seed-VC as the backbone, the cascaded frame-
work (Waveform + Seed-VC) achieves substantially higher
speaker similarity (0.82) and UTMOS (1.91) than the pseudo-
target strategy (0.74 similarity and 1.64 UTMOS), although
the pseudo-target approach yields slightly lower SER (65.70%
vs. 69.65%). Similar trends are observed with Vevo and
FreeVC. Overall, these results suggest that separating intelligi-
bility restoration and timbre modeling is a more effective design
under the one-shot constraint.
3.2.2. Improving the Cascade Approach
In the waveform-level cascade method, the second-stage VC
model operates on reconstructed waveforms from LLE-ELVC,
which may introduce vocoder artifacts. To mitigate this issue,
we adopt feature-level cascading where intermediate acoustic
representations are directly fed into the VC backbone.
For Seed-VC, this modification significantly improves in-
telligibility, reducing SER from 69.65% to 60.08% while main-
taining high speaker similarity (0.82→0.81) and improving
perceptual quality (UTMOS: 1.91→2.08). These results indi-
cate that bypassing intermediate waveform reconstruction better
preserves the acoustic structure required by the pretrained VC
model. Building upon this design, we further introduce super-

3.2.3. Backbone-Specific Analysis
We also analyze the behavior of different VC backbones.
FreeVC achieves the highest perceptual quality in the wave-
form cascade (UTMOS: 2.64) but suffers from poor intelligi-
bility (SER: 79.70%), indicating sensitivity to residual EL ar-
tifacts. Vevo shows moderate performance across metrics but
does not outperform Seed-VC. In contrast, Seed-VC consis-
tently provides the most balanced trade-off between intelligi-
bility, speaker similarity, and perceptual quality. One possible
explanation is that these VC models are pretrained on different
corpora and objectives, resulting in varying robustness to distri-
bution shifts introduced by EL restoration.

4. Conclusion
This paper establishes a benchmark for personalized ELVC in
an extreme one-shot setting where only a single NL utterance is
available to preserve speaker identity. We compared a pseudo-
target strategy and a cascaded framework that separates intel-
ligibility restoration from timbre transfer. Experimental results
show that direct zero-shot VC fails due to severe domain mis-
match. In contrast, the cascaded framework effectively restores
intelligibility prior to timbre transfer. With feature-level cas-
cading and supervised refinement, our system surpasses a su-
pervised ELVC baseline in intelligibility while significantly im-
proving speaker similarity and perceptual quality. Future work
will investigate the latency and robustness of the framework on
larger speaker populations, alternative evaluation methods, and
more ELVC front-end models.
