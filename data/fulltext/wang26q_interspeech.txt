Abstract
With the development of speech large language models (speech
LLMs), users can now interact directly with assistants via
speech. However, most existing models only convert response
content into speech without fully capturing the rich emotional
cues in user queries, where the same sentence may convey dif-
ferent meanings depending on the expression. Emotional under-
standing is thus essential for improving human–machine inter-
action. Most empathetic speech LLMs rely on massive datasets,
demanding high computational cost. A key challenge is to
build models that generate empathetic responses with limited
data and without large-scale training. To this end, we propose
Emotion Omni, a model that understands emotional content in
user speech and generates empathetic responses. We further de-
veloped a data pipeline to construct a 200k emotional dialogue
dataset supporting empathetic speech assistants. Experiments
show that Empathy Omni achieves comparable instruction-
following ability without large-scale pretraining, while surpass-
ing existing models in speech quality and empathy. These
results confirm its improvements in both speech fidelity and
emotional expressiveness. Demos are available athttps://
anonymous.4open.science/w/omni_demo-4876/.

1. Introduction
With the emergence of systems such as GPT-4o [1, 2], inte-
grating speech capabilities with large language models (LLMs)
has substantially improved user experience and broadened ap-
plication scenarios, including customer service, education, per-
sonal assistance, and therapeutic support. Recent end-to-end
multimodal voice interaction models, such as Mini-Omni [3],
LLaMA-Omni [4], and V ocalNet [5] move beyond conventional
cascades (ASR→LLM→TTS) by generating text and speech
responses directly from spoken queries, which can reduce la-
tency and improve interaction naturalness.
Beyond semantic question answering, effective speech in-
teraction requires robust modeling of paralinguistic informa-
tion [6, 7, 8]. Paralinguistic cues—including emotion, atti-
tude, and intention—are central to communication and can fun-
damentally change the meaning of identical words when de-
livered with different affect. Without accurately interpreting
the user’s emotional intent and responding empathetically, a
speech system may produce inappropriate outputs and degrade
trust, especially in emotionally sensitive settings such as men-
tal health support, customer service, and educational tutor-
ing. While prosodic and emotional dialogue systems have ad-
vanced [9, 10, 11] and recent speech LLMs begin to address
empathetic response generation [12, 13, 14], many approaches
rely on implicitly learning emotion consistency from data with-
out explicit affective planning, which increases dependence on
high-quality emotional dialogues and large-scale training. In
practice, emotional dialogue data is scarce and expensive to an-
notate, motivating data-efficient and controllable approaches.
To address these challenges, we propose Empathy Omni, an
end-to-end model that explicitly leverages emotional features in
user speech to recognize affect and to control empathetic spoken
responses. The model conditions on both semantic and emo-
tional representations extracted from the input speech. It gen-
erates (i) textual responses and (ii) a token-synchronous emo-
tion trajectory that serves as an explicit affective plan for speech
synthesis. This separation between semantic generation and af-
fect control mitigates feature entanglement and enables finer-
grained prosody control, including emotion transitions within a
single utterance. Complementing the model, we develop a cost-
effective and scalable emotional dialogue construction pipeline
using controllable TTS and automated quality filters, and fur-
ther incorporate real-world emotional recordings to improve
acoustic diversity and generalization.
Our contributions are threefold: (1) We propose Empa-
thy Omni, an end-to-end speech LLM that explicitly predicts
a token-synchronous emotion trajectory and uses it to con-
trol speech generation. (2) We introduce a DTW-based align-
ment scheme to supervise token-level emotion planning with-
out forced alignment, which is robust to non-verbal vocaliza-
tions. (3) We build EmotionalQA-200k through a scalable
synthesis-and-filtering pipeline and combine it with real emo-
tional recordings to improve diversity and generalization.
2. Methods
We adopt a two-tower end-to-end architecture similar to omni-
directional speech LLMs [4]. A LLM performs speech under-
standing and response text generation, while a causal speech
decoder generates acoustic tokens conditioned on linguistic and
paralinguistic cues. To explicitly model paralinguistic informa-
tion, we extract complementary semantic and emotional fea-
tures with two frozen pretrained speech encoders. After tem-
poral downsampling, these features are fused and projected into
the LLM hidden space. The LLM outputs response tokens and
a token-synchronous emotion trajectory. The speech decoder
conditions on token embeddings, corresponding LLM hidden
states, and the emotion trajectory to generate acoustic tokens
autoregressively. Training proceeds in two stages (Fig. 1).

2.1. Speech Encoder
Given a speech query, we run two pretrained encoders in par-
allel to extract complementary features: Specifically, we uti-
lize the Whisper large-v3[15] encoder as the semantic branch
to capture robust linguistic and phonetic content, while the
emotion2vec[16] large model serves as the emotion branch to
extract rich paralinguistic and affective cues. Because the na-
tive frame rates of these speech foundation models are relatively
high (e.g., typically 50 Hz), directly feeding their outputs into
a Large Language Model (LLM) would result in overly long
sequences and high computational overhead.
To address this, we introduce lightweight downsampling
projectors to reduce the frame rate to 10 Hz, thereby im-
proving efficiency while retaining salient prosodic dynamics.
Adapted from the SLAM[17], our downsampling module uti-
lizes a frame-stacking multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architec-
ture rather than traditional convolutional layers. For a given
sequence, the projector first truncates residual frames to ensure
the sequence length is perfectly divisible by the downsampling
rate k (e.g., k=5). It then reshapes the sequence by concate-
nating k consecutive frames along the feature dimension and
passes the stacked vectors through a two-layer linear projection
with a ReLU activation, mapping the intermediate features to
the LLM’s hidden size. To preserve the robust representations
of the pretrained models and stabilize the optimization process,
both the Whisper and emotion2vec encoders are kept strictly
frozen. We train only the downsampling adapters and the sub-
sequent projection parameters. At each frame, the downsam-
pled semantic and emotion vectors are concatenated and pro-
jected to yield a synchronized sequence of speech embeddings.
These joint embeddings holistically represent both what is spo-
ken (semantics) and how it is expressed (emotion), effectively
conditioning the LLM during multimodal understanding and re-
sponse generation.

Acoustic tokenization and waveform synthesis.We
adopt the discrete acoustic tokenizer and the spectrogram-

3. EXPERIMENTS
3.1. Dataset Curation
We train and evaluate Empathy Omni using V oiceAssistant-
400k [5] and an emotional dialogue corpus EmotionalQA-200k.
EmotionalQA-200k.We construct EmotionalQA-200k to
cover 20 domains and six basic emotion categories (happiness,
anger, fear, sadness, disgust, surprise). The dataset combines
synthetic generation and real-world recordings through three
strategies:
Strategy 1: End-to-End Synthesis Pipeline.GPT-4o gen-
erates dialogue text pairs across 20 domains and six emotion
categories. Emotion labels are expanded into descriptive in-
struction texts to guide CosyV oice2 controllable synthesis. All
samples are filtered using (i) ASR-WER intelligibility filtering
and (ii) SER emotion consistency filtering. This strategy con-
tributes∼135k samples (67.5%).
Strategy 2: ESD-Based Text Rewriting and Speech Syn-
thesis.We use 17.5k read-style sentences from ESD as seeds.
GPT-4o rewrites each sentence into a natural spoken-style user
query and generates an empathetic response. Both sides are
synthesized with CosyV oice2 under emotion instructions; refer-
ence speaker embeddings are randomly sampled from a speaker
pool to enhance diversity. After ASR-WER and SER filtering,
we retain∼15k samples (7.5%).
Strategy 3: Multi-Stage Extraction from Real-World
Recordings.We collect real-world recordings from publicly
available emotional speech corpora and cinematic dialogue
datasets. Each recording undergoes V AD-based segmentation,
voice separation and noise reduction, ASR transcription, and
dual-side emotion annotation and consistency filtering on both
speech and text modalities. This yields∼50k samples (25%)
with higher acoustic naturalness and richer emotional dynam-
ics.
3.2. Configurations
We adopt a two-stage training strategy with AdamW opti-
mization. In Stage 1, we fine-tune the LLM backbone with
LoRA [26] while training the downsampling adapters and
the emotion prediction head. The backbone is Qwen2.5-7B-
Instruct [27] (28 decoder blocks, hidden size 3584), and LoRA
rank is set to 8. The emotion head is a lightweight Transformer
encoder plus MLP mapping hidden states to a 768-dim emotion
vector. We use learning rates of5×10−5 for LoRA parameters
and2×10 −4 (weight decay 0.01) for adapters and the emotion
head.
In Stage 2, we train the speech decoder with six Trans-
former decoder blocks and a hidden size of 3584. We use a
learning rate of2×10 −4 and weight decay of 0.01 for the de-
coder. All training was conducted on 8 NVIDIA H20 GPUs.
4. Evaluation and Results
4.1. General QA and Empathetic Generation
General spoken instruction following.We evaluate gen-
eral speech understanding and instruction following on
V oiceBench [28], using the AlpacaEval, CommonEval, Wild-
V oice, IFEval, and SD-QA subsets. Speech naturalness is as-
sessed using UTMOS [29]. Table 1 summarizes the results.
Overall, Empathy Omni achieves competitive performance
across all V oiceBench subsets, indicating robust speech under-
standing and instruction execution. In particular, our model at-
tains the best scores on CommonEval (3.47) and IFEval (27.89),
suggesting strong instruction-following robustness and con-
straint satisfaction. While GLM-4-V oice achieves the highest
Alpaca score and WildV oice score, Empathy Omni remains
close (3.84 vs. 3.97 on Alpaca; 3.13 vs. 3.18 on WildV oice),
demonstrating that introducing explicit affect modeling does not
substantially trade off general QA capability.
Speech quality.Empathy Omni obtains the highest UT-
MOS score (4.41), outperforming LLaMA-Omni (3.98) and
other baselines. We attribute this improvement to the proposed
speech decoding design: (i) gated fusion of token embeddings
and LLM hidden states stabilizes pronunciation and long-range
coherence, and (ii) explicit token-synchronous emotion trajec-
tories injected via AdaLN enable prosody control without dis-
rupting linguistic content, reducing unnatural pitch/rhythm arti-
facts.
Empathetic response generation.To assess empathy be-
yond general QA, we construct a test set of 1,000 emotional
queries spanning diverse scenarios and six emotion categories.
At inference, the model produces both text and speech re-

Empathy alignment (Emotion GPT Score).We employ
GPT-4o as a blind judge to rate empathy on a 1–5 scale (Emo-
tion GPT Score). The judge is provided with the user query
and its target emotion label, the ASR transcript of the generated
speech, and the emotion predicted by an external speech emo-
tion recognizer (SER), but not the model identity. The judge
evaluates whether the response (i) acknowledges the user’s
emotional state, (ii) conveys an appropriate empathetic stance,
and (iii) remains semantically and emotionally consistent. We
use a fixed evaluation prompt and deterministic decoding (tem-
perature 0) for consistency across all samples.
Human listening test (Speech Emotion MOS).We further
conduct a subjective listening test with 40 native speakers. All
samples from different systems are presented in randomized or-
der and evaluated in a blind setting. Raters assign a 1–5 MOS
score considering overall naturalness and emotional expressive-
ness (Speech Emotion MOS). We report mean MOS with 95%
confidence intervals.
As shown in Table 2, Empathy Omni achieves the highest
Emotion GPT Score (3.97) and Speech Emotion MOS (4.23),
together with the lowest ASR-WER (5.61%). Compared with
OpenS2S and LLaMA-Omni, these gains suggest that explicitly
predicting a token-synchronous emotion trajectory improves
emotional consistency and expressiveness in speech, while the
decoder conditioning design helps preserve intelligibility.
Qualitative observations.We find that Empathy Omni is
especially effective for emotions requiring sustained prosody
shaping (e.g., sadness and fear), where the explicit trajectory
helps maintain a coherent tone across long responses. Remain-
ing failure cases mainly arise for subtle or mixed emotions,
where the model may generate semantically appropriate yet
emotionally generic speech, motivating finer-grained affect rep-
resentations and intensity control in future work.
4.2. Ablation Study
We ablate the proposed fusion module that integrates token-
level text embeddings, LLM hidden states, and emotion trajec-
tories via AdaLN. Table 3 reports Emotion GPT Score, Speech
Emotion MOS, and ASR-WER.
Effect of removing the fusion module.Without the fu-
sion module, the decoder cannot effectively combine lexical
precision from token embeddings with contextual semantics
from LLM hidden states, nor can it be reliably modulated by
the emotion trajectory through AdaLN. As a result, emotional
alignment and naturalness degrade while pronunciation stabil-
ity weakens. Quantitatively, the Emotion GPT Score drops from
3.97 to 3.15, Speech Emotion MOS decreases from 4.23 to 3.85,
and ASR-WER increases from 5.61 to 6.42.
Listening-based diagnosis.Subjective inspection indi-
cates that removing token embeddings tends to introduce pro-
nunciation and rhythm issues, while removing emotion trajec-
tory conditioning leads to emotionally flatter speech even when
the textual content appears empathetic. These findings confirm
that the fusion design is critical for producing speech that is si-
multaneously intelligible and emotionally expressive.

5. CONCLUSIONS
In this study, we present Empathy Omni, a speech LLM that rec-
ognizes speech input and generates empathetic responses by ef-
fectively integrating semantic and emotional features. Our cost-
effective emotional dialogue dataset generation pipeline reduces
reliance on large-scale labeled data, making empathetic speech
system development more accessible. The ability to generate
high-quality empathetic speech with limited resources demon-
strates the scalability and real-world potential of our approach.
While results are promising, challenges remain in handling sub-
tle emotions, as the model sometimes prioritizes semantic con-
tent over emotional cues. Future work will focus on improving
sensitivity to nuanced emotions. This research provides a foun-
dation for building more efficient, accessible, and emotionally
intelligent speech generation systems.
