Abstract
We propose a few-shot, open-set framework for joint deep-
fake detection and generative source attribution. Addressing
the rapid evolution of synthetic speech, we introduce a tempo-
ral evaluation strategy using the MLAAD v9 dataset, partition-
ing models by launch year and architectural family. Our model
utilizes a WavLM-Large model with targeted LoRA adapters,
optimized via-a hierarchical metric learning objective: Condi-
tional AAM Softmax + Center Loss. We simulate real-world
forensic deployment by performing prototype-based inference
on unseen 2025+ model families, establishing new class cen-
troids with only 12 samples. Evaluated on the ASVspoof 5.0
benchmark, our framework achieves SOTA performance with
0.49% EER and 0.09 minDCF, while attaining 99% accuracy
in tracing generated speech to its source. These results demon-
strate that hierarchical representation learning and few-shot pro-
totype adaptation provide a robust, scalable defense against the
shifting landscape of audio synthesis.

1. Introduction
Driven by advances in neural codecs and deep generative mod-
eling, speech synthesis has transitioned to autoregressive, dif-
fusion, and flow-matching architectures [1, 2] capable of gen-
erating perceptually indistinguishable speech. By directly opti-
mizing latent manifolds under perceptual loss objectives, these
systems systematically suppress the spectro-temporal artifacts,
phase discontinuities, spectral smearing, unnatural pitch con-
tours that legacy discriminators historically exploited. This es-
calates voice cloning and zero-shot [3] identity spoofing into
severe operational threats against ASV , while the architectural
diversity of modern synthesis systems, codec-based autoregres-
sive models [4], diffusion pipelines, and end-to-end variational
architectures imposes structurally distinct inductive biases on
the generated signal, producing a heterogeneous threat surface
that no single detection strategy can uniformly cover. The field
has consequently shifted toward self-supervised speech repre-
sentations [5, 6, 7], which acquire rich hierarchical encodings
spanning low-level spectro-temporal texture through to abstract
phonetic and speaker discriminative structure from large unla-
beled corpora. Models trained under joint masked prediction
and denoising objectives [7] are particularly consequential in
this context, where, by reconstructing clean speech from cor-
rupted inputs, representations are encouraged to preserve fine-
grained generative characteristics that survive acoustic pertur-
bation. Critically, projecting diverse acoustic signals into a uni-
fied high-dimensional latent space disentangles natural intra-
speaker variance from residual generative fingerprints, provid-
ing a theoretically principled foundation for downstream attri-
bution that purely feature-level approaches cannot offer.
Detection alone, however, is no longer a sufficient forensic
objective. The more consequential and technically demanding
problem is finegrained attribution, determining not merely that
a recording is synthetic, but identifying which model produced
it, including systems entirely absent from the training set. This
constitutes the open world recognition [8] problem, where con-
ventional classifiers fail categorically by assigning high confi-
dence predictions to novel, unseen generators rather than with-
holding judgment. The temporal dimension of this problem is
equally important synthesis systems are released continuously,
meaning that any attribution framework must accommodate an
expanding class inventory without periodic retraining a require-
ment that static architectures are structurally incapable of satis-
fying. Metric learning [9] addresses this by training networks to
produce embeddings in a space where geometric proximity re-
flects semantic similarity, enabling inference via nearest neigh-
bor search over an open and extensible class inventory. Proto-
type networks [10] formalize this principle further, representing
each class through a mean embedding centroid so that newly
emerged synthesis systems can be registered from a small num-
ber of samples without any modification to model parameters a
property indispensable for realistic deployment. The hierarchi-
cal organization of synthesis systems[11] by architectural fam-
ily further motivates a structured metric space, where coarse
family level boundaries and finegrained model level clusters
jointly govern the embedding geometry. The principal contri-
butions of this work are as follows:
• Dual-Tier Hierarchical Metric Learning: Joint optimiza-
tion of dynamically conditioned AAM-Softmax margins and
EMA-anchored Center Loss to resolve both broad synthesis
families and fine grained syntesizer fingerprints.
• Dynamic Prototype Anchoring: A few-shot prototype mech-
anism for inference time attribution of emergent, zero-day
generative architectures without parameter updates.
• Empirical State-of-the-Art: Establishing a new benchmark
on the open-set ASVspoof Track 1 evaluation with a 0.49%
EER and 0.09 minDCF.

Beyond binary detection, source attribution has emerged as a
distinct forensic task. [15, 16] proposed open-set extensions
via threshold based rejection over closed set classifiers, while
the Patchout Spectrogram Attribution Transformer (PSAT) [11]
achieved over 95% closed set accuracy on ASVspoof 2019 and
DARPA SemaFor datasets. Self-supervised transformer based
systems [17] have further demonstrated 99.8% closed-set ac-
curacy with open-set generalization exceeding 90%. The het-
erogeneous threat surface introduced by ASVspoof 5 under-
scores the need for attribution frameworks that generalize be-
yond closed-set assumptions.
The adoption of SSL speech models wav2vec 2.0 [5], Hu-
BERT [6], and WavLM [7] has fundamentally expanded the
feature landscape for forensic tasks, yielding representations
that encode hierarchical phonetic and speaker-level structure
without label supervision. Deeper transformer layers cap-
ture abstract, task-agnostic structure while earlier layers re-
tain finegrained spectro-temporal detail, a stratification partic-
ularly valuable for attribution. On the metric learning side,
Prototype Networks [10] formalized few-shot generalization
through embedding centroids, while ArcFace [18] demonstrated
the efficacy of additive angular margin losses for hyperspheri-
cal discrimination, subsequently confirmed in speaker verifica-
tion [19] to outperform softmax-based objectives. Parameter-
efficient fine-tuning via LoRA [20] enables targeted adaptation
of large SSL backbones to specialized forensic tasks while pre-
serving pretrained representations, a property critical in low-
data settings where full fine-tuning risks catastrophic forgetting
of the generic acoustic structure essential for generalization to
unseen synthesis systems.
3. Temporal Source Tracing via Few-Shot
Prototype Adaptation
We formulate synthetic speech attribution as a hierarchical
open-world recognition problem. The system must not only dis-
criminate among known generative paradigms but also extend
attribution to zero-day architectures at inference time, with-
out retraining. As illustrated in Figure 1, the pipeline oper-
ates across three phases, (Phase 1) Training, (Phase 2) Regis-
tration, and (Phase 3) Inference, unified by three core novelties:
(1) Dual-Tier hierarchical metric learning that jointly resolves
architectural families and finegrained synthesizer fingerprints,
(2) Dynamic Prototype anchoring that registers emergent gen-
erative models at inference time via few-shot centroid compu-
tation, and (3) A LoRA-adapted SSL backbone that preserves
pretrained acoustic generalizability while specializing the latent
manifold for open-set deepfake attribution.
1. Phase 1: Hierarchical Metric Learning (Training)
• The backbone is WavLM-Large [7], selected for its denois-
ing pre-training objective that preserves finegrained gener-
ative fingerprints under acoustic perturbation. Rather than
full finetuning which risks catastrophic forgetting of the
pretrained representations essential for open-set general-
ization, LoRA is applied exclusively to the feature pro-
jection and attention output projection layers, constraining
adaptation to a low-rank parameter subspace while retain-
ing the backbone’s broad acoustic generalizability.
• The embedding space topology is governed by a joint geo-
metric objective, our first core novelty that simultaneously
optimizes inter-class separability and intra-class compact-
ness via Conditional AAM-Softmax [18] with dynamically
trainable margins (my) and via Center Loss with EMA sta-
bilized centroid updates, mitigating high variance gradient
noise from class imbalanced mini-batches respectively.
• Training follows a two-stage hierarchical curriculum moti-
vated by the manifold hypothesis. Stage 1 forces the LoRA
adapters to discriminate broad architectural families like
LLM-Codec, Diffusion, Flow-Matching, FastSpeech, and
VITS establishing a coarse, globally discriminative mani-
fold. Stage 2 refines this topology by resolving finegrained
intra-family synthesizer identities, building upon the estab-
lished family-level geometry rather than optimizing from a
flat class structure. Upon completion, the mean embedding
of each model class and architectural family is cached as
a prototype vector, initializing the attribution registry (Fig-
ure 1, Phase 1).
2. Phase 2: Dynamic Prototype Anchoring (Registration)
To counter the continuous emergence of zero-day generative
architectures our second core novelty is novel synthesis mod-
els from Set 2 that are registered into the prototype store
without any parameter update or retraining (Figure 1, Phase
2). For each new model class c,K support samples are pro-
jected through the frozen backbone to compute a new proto-
type centroid. Empirically we confirmK≈ 36 as the critical
threshold for centroid stabilization within a bounded cosine
radius across diverse synthesis families. The prototype reg-
istry thereby scales monotonically with the threat landscape,
absorbing zero-day models as they emerge, without disrupt-
ing the established hyperspherical geometry. Bonafide proto-
types are registered analogously from V oxCeleb 2 [21] using

gender conditioned centroids independent male, female, and
mixed prototypes to robustly model the multimodal distribu-
tion of natural intra-speaker variance.
3. Phase 3: Nearest-Prototype Retrieval (Inference)
During deployment, a test utterance is projected into the la-
tent space through the frozen backbone, and attribution is
executed via nearest-neighbor retrieval against the full pro-
totype registry (Figure 1, Phase 3). Cosine similarity is
strictly employed over Euclidean distance to maintain met-
ric consistency with the hyperspherical topology induced by
the AAM-Softmax training objective. This parameter free
retrieval mechanism eliminates class-specific threshold cali-
bration entirely, yielding simultaneous architectural family-
level and individual synthesizer-level attribution decisions at
negligible computational overhead establishing a new state-
of-the-art on the ASVspoof 5 Track 1 open-condition evalu-
ation.
4. Experimental Setup
Dataset & Partitioning Details: Three strictly disjoint
datasets are employed across training, registration, and
evaluation. MLAAD v9 [22] serves as the primary corpus,
partitioned temporally into Set 1 (pre-2025 models) for
backbone fine-tuning and initial prototype computation, and
Set 2 (post-2025 models) both sets are subdivided into family
stratified subsets to simulate the incremental emergence of
zero-day synthesis architectures. VoxCeleb 2 [21] provides
genuine speech samples for bonafide prototype construction,
sourced externally to eliminate acoustic bias toward evaluation
conditions and prevent data leakage. ASVspoof 5 Track 1
(open condition) is reserved exclusively for evaluation no
portion of this corpus is exposed during training, finetuning, or
registration with performance reported via EER and minDCF
consistent with standard ASVspoof evaluation protocols [23].
WavLM-Large [7] is selected as the SSL backbone owing to
its masked speech prediction and denoising pretraining objec-
tive, which instills representations sensitive to both speaker
identity and finegrained acoustic artifacts. Properties directly
relevant to synthesizer fingerprint discrimination, and empiri-
cally demonstrated to outperform HuBERT and wav2vec 2.0
on speaker and spoofing related downstream tasks [7]. The
backbone is adapted via Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [20]
with rank r = 8 and scaling factor α = 16 .0, applied se-
lectively to the feature projection.projection and
attention.attention.out proj layers a configura-
tion determined through systematic ablation over alternative
layer combinations and rank settings. The complete codebase
is implemented in PyTorch and all experiments are conducted
on a multi-GPU server equipped with three NVIDIA RTX 3090
(24 GB VRAM each) GPUs and 512 GB system RAM.

The near-zero EER is particularly meaningful in open-set
detection theory, it implies widely separable score distributions
between genuine and spoofed speech. A property difficult to
achieve when the test distribution contains synthesis systems
unseen during training, as in the ASVspoof 5 open-condition
evaluation. The low minDCF of 0.09 corroborates this, confirm-
ing that the optimal decision threshold incurs minimal weighted
cost across both error types. However, the elevated actDCF of
0.97 reveals a score calibration gap consistent with metric learn-
ing systems, where cosine similarity scores cluster near the unit
hypersphere boundary rather than spanning the full range. This
is addressable through post-hoc normalization such as adaptive
s-norm or Platt scaling, which we identify as future work. No-
tably, this calibration gap does not reflect a failure of embed-
ding geometry but rather an inherent property of hyperspher-
ical metric spaces that is decoupled from discriminative per-
formance. The performance gap between SSL-based ( ▲) and
non-SSL systems (△) further validates optimality of WavLM-
Large as a backbone. Its denoising pretraining disentangles nat-
ural speech variation from synthesis artifacts. A discriminative
foundation non-SSL architectures cannot recover through archi-
tectural complexity alone, as evidenced by T47’s comparatively
poor minDCF of 0.26 despite being an ensemble system.

types, which resolves as coverage increases. minDCF stabi-

tion separability saturates once sufficient prototype coverage
is achieved.
• Efficacy of Multiple Real Prototypes: The most significant
performance jump arises solely from replacing the unified
real prototype with 3-way gender conditioned prototypes.
This confirms that genuine speech occupies a bimodal, non-
isotropic region of the embedding space, where a single mean
prototype introduces systematic misassignment for speak-
ers deviating from the population centroid. At inference,
the real-class similarity score for an incoming query embed-
ding is computed as the maximum cosine similarity across
all three gender prototypes ensuring that the query is eval-
uated against its nearest geometrically homogeneous subre-
gion rather than a blended centroid. Gender conditioned pro-
totypes thereby partition the real speech manifold into geo-
metrically homogeneous subregions, directly sharpening the
real-versus-synthetic decision boundary and driving the final
EER to 0.49% and accuracy to 99.0%/97.0%.

6. Conclusion
This work addressed finegrained synthetic speech attribution in
an open-world setting, where binary detection and closed-set
classification frameworks are fundamentally inadequate. We
proposed a hierarchical, continually extensible attribution sys-
tem built on a LoRA adapted WavLM-Large backbone, trained
under a joint Conditional AAM-Softmax and EMA anchored
Center Loss that simultaneously optimizes inter-class angu-
lar separability and intra-class compactness. A two-stage cur-
riculum organizes the learned manifold at architectural fam-
ily and model granularity. While few-shot prototype registra-
tion accommodates unseen synthesis systems without retrain-
ing, the core novelty enabling true open-world generalization.
On MLAAD v9 and ASVspoof 5 Track 1, the system achieves
99.0% family-level and 97.0% model-level attribution accuracy
with an EER of 0.49% and minDCF of 0.09, surpassing all
single-system and ensemble submissions. Progressive evalua-
tion further revealed that the genuine speech manifold is non-
isotropic, necessitating gender conditioned prototyping at infer-
ence time. Future work will target post-hoc score calibration,
continual prototype refinement over deployment, and robust-
ness against adversarially perturbed synthetic speech.
