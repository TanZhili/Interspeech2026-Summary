Abstract
Existing text-to-speech systems predominantly focus on single-
sentence synthesis and lack adequate contextual modeling and
fine-grained control for coherent multicast audiobooks. To ad-
dress this, we propose a context-aware, emotion-controllable
speech synthesis framework with three innovations: a context
mechanism for consistency, a disentanglement paradigm to de-
couple style from prompts, and self-distillation to boost ex-
pressiveness. Experiments show consistent gains: chapter-level
generation achieves 4.25 M-MOS(14% relative gain over the
strongest baseline), dialog reaches 4.11 S-MOS, and emotion
control improves by 31 percentage points in high-intensity dis-
crimination. Ablation studies validate our methods. Demo:
https://semisemi-ux.github.io/.

1. Introduction
Long-form audiobooks, a widely adopted content format, in-
tegrate information delivery and auditory engagement. How-
ever, traditional production methods, whether fully manual or
human-involved, face high costs and long production cycles, es-
pecially for multicast albums with multiple characters.
In response, researchers have recently developed automated
solutions for high-quality audiobook generation[1, 2, 3, 4, 5].
AudioStory[2] employs an LLM to process instruction inputs,
decomposes long audio into structured subtasks, and gener-
ates short clips sequentially. MultiActor-Audiobook[1] uti-
lizes a Transformer-based multimodal model to capture char-
acter traits, uses LLMs for emotional guidance, and synthe-
sizes speech at the sentence level. Dopamine Audiobook[3]
and MM-StoryAgent[4] adopt multi-agent pipelines for story-
based generation, but integrate existing TTS systems such as
cosyvoice2[6] rather than proposing new synthesis methods.
Similarly, Shaja et al.[5] proposed a system that enhances im-
mersion via spatial audio, which also relies on established TTS
backbones. These approaches achieve better automation but are
limited by insufficient explicit inter-sentence modeling, conse-
quently yielding inconsistent contextual outputs.
Several industry approaches have incorporated context
modeling for long-form speech. MoonCast[7] targets podcast
generation with long-context modeling and colloquial scripts;
MOSS-TTSD[8] achieves state-of-the-art long-segment qual-
ity through efficient codecs and data pipelines; CoV oMix[9]
and koel-TTS[10] focus on conversational expressiveness. De-
spite these advances, such systems remain largely tailored to
podcasts, modeling long context with limited controllability—a

critical requirement for audiobooks, which demand precise nar-
rative flow and expressive multi-character portrayal.
Some prior efforts partially address context or controlla-
bility but exhibit inherent shortcomings. The prosody anal-

TTS[12] improves long-sequence continuity but lacks emo-
tional control; the memory module in[13] over-relies on ex-

improve controllability, yet it is still short of scene adaptation
for audiobooks.
To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel speech
synthesis framework tailored for long-form multicast audio-
books, named Audiobook-CC. Our main contributions include
the following.
• We introduce a context modeling mechanism for long-form
audiobook generation that significantly improves semantic
consistency across context.
• A disentanglement training paradigm is designed to decouple
the style of generated speech from speech prompt, which fa-
cilitates the tone of generated speech following the semantic
information of the given text more closely.
• A self-distillation method is proposed to greatly enhance the
emotional expressiveness and controllability of the instruc-
tion of the generated speech.

We describe in more detail the training and inference strategies

2.1. Contextual Consistency via Disentangled Training
Although pre-contextC pre and post-contextC post intuitively en-
hance contextual consistency, training solely on textual context
without explicit speaker modeling still requires prompt speech
for voice specification during inference. Consequently, syn-
thesized speech prosody remains constrained by the prompt
speech, limiting semantic-prosodic alignment and narrative co-
herence in long-form synthesis.
semantic consistency:To improve the alignment between
synthesized audio and text semantics while mitigating exces-
sive prosodic interference from prompt audio, we adopt a de-
coupled training strategy. Rather than the traditional coupled
“prompt-target” training model, we employ independent mod-
eling that focuses on the adaptive relationship between target
audio prosody and text semantics. During training, for exam-
ple, we use the audio1 of Speaker A (with unrelated content) as
the source of V to synthesize the audio2 of Speaker A, guiding
the model to preserve shared speaker traits—such as timbre and
persona—while allowing the prosody and expressiveness of au-
dio2 to emerge purely from its corresponding text and context.
A key practical challenge in realizing this approach lies in ac-
quiring large-scale, high-expressiveness datasets where individ-
ual speakers are represented with sufficient data (each speaker
covers no less than 5 minutes of data) spanning diverse narra-
tive styles, emotions, and speaking rates. This approach reduces
inter-audio prosodic interference at the data level and enhances
consistency between synthesized audio and current text seman-
tics.
persona consistency:To avoid acoustic timbre shifts
caused by insufficient prompt matching while decoupling, we
propose a multi-constraint prompt selection mechanism with
three refinements: 1) prompts are selected within the same
chapter with voiceprint similarity constraints [14] to reduce
cross-chapter variance; 2) an optimal similarity threshold is ex-
perimentally determined to balance voice stability and prosodic
diversity; 3) high-quality labeled multi-emotional speech data is
incorporated to alleviate sample sparsity and ensure consistent
persona expression across emotions.
2.2. Controllability Enhancement
This section introduces our controllability enhancement strat-
egy, focusing on training and inference mechanisms.
controllability:During training, instructions derived from
fiction or persona are decomposed into discrete attribute labels.
Based on datasets with high prosodic consistency, we use LLM
83
to parse instructions or contextual text, extracting nine emotions
(admire/alert/anger/disgust/fear/joy/neutral/sadness/surprise)
along with their intensity levels, as well as categorical
values for volume (high/medium/low) and speaking rate
(fast/medium/slow). The LLM output is then normalized
by manually defined rules into fixed tokens, where emotion
intensity is quantized into four standardized levels: extremely,
considerable, some, and slight. This enables the generation of
rich and compositional control signals such as “very joyful with
slight surprise”. For example, “shouting angrily” decomposes
into “very angry, low volume, slow speed” for an ill elderly
person and “very angry, high volume, fast speed” for a healthy
person, facilitating explicit attribute–acoustic feature mapping
learning. During inference, a third module converts user
instructions into attribute combinations. This discretization
mitigates linguistic ambiguity and improves the precision of
control.
emotional intensity:To alleviate the scarcity of high-
intensity emotional samples, we first trained an initial model
using data collected internally from character-voice. Subse-
quently, the synthetic data generated by this initial model—with
qualified samples(the phoneme error rate(PER)[15]<2%,
speaker similarity (SS)[14]>0.7 and pitch fluctuation consis-
tent with emotional characteristics) and highly emotional data
prioritized as targeted data—is used to iteratively refine the sta-
bility of the model. This process employs a self-distillation
strategy consisting of three key steps: First, samples with vary-
ing intensity levels are synthesized using a pre-trained emo-
tional TTS model; Second, the generated samples are filtered
through the phoneme error rate (PER)[15], speaker similarity
(SS)[14] and pitch to guarantee quality; Third, the intensity dis-
tribution is balanced through targeted data augmentation.
3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets and Training:There were three stages in our
training. First, 1,000,000 hours of data was used—including
audiobooks, podcasts, TV dramas, etc.—to fine-tune the
model initialized from cosyvoice2[6]. Second, a 150,000-hour
context-aware audiobook dataset and a 100-hour dataset of
high-quality recordings labeled with instructions were used to
further fine-tune the model. In particular, each sentence in the
context-aware data set was annotated with timestamps, speaker
IDs, and positional information within the corresponding chap-
ter. Third, to enhance the expressiveness and controllability
of instruction-following, a 100-hour data augmentation dataset
based on the model constructed on the model after the first two
training stages.
During training, the model was optimized using the
AdamW optimizer[16], with learning rates of 1e-4, 1e-5, and
1e-6 for the first, second and third stages, respectively. The
model was trained on 64 NVIDIA A800 GPUs with a batch
size of 384 for 720k, 300k, and 10k steps in the three stages,
respectively.
To evaluate performance in audiobook scenarios, we con-
structed three test sets: a narrative test set (Test-NAR) con-
sisting of 100 paragraphs (each containing more than 240 sen-
tences), a dialog test set (Test-DIA) comprising 570 dialog sen-
tences, and a chapter-level test set (Test-CHAP) built from 15
chapters (each ranging from 2,000 to 4,000 sentences) that in-
clude both narration and dialog.
Model Evaluation:For a comprehensive evaluation of our
system, we employed two subjective evaluation methods[17]:
the single-sentence mean opinion score (S-MOS) for dialog
samples and the multi-sentence mean opinion score (M-MOS)
for narration and long-chapter samples. We randomly selected
20 paragraphs fromTest-NAR, 60 sentences fromTest-DIA, and
10 chapters fromTest-CHAPfor subjective rating. Fifty native
Chinese speakers were recruited to evaluate both S-MOS and
M-MOS.
Furthermore, we conducted ABX listening tests to com-
pare our system against two baselines: single-sentence ABX
(S-ABX) for dialog samples and multi-sentence ABX (M-
ABX) for narration and chapter-level samples. The baselines—
cosyvoice2[6] and MOSS-TTSD[8]—were selected to cover
distinct synthesis regimes: cosyvoice2[6] demonstrates strong
stability and expressiveness in short-form synthesis, whereas
MOSS-TTSD[8] has shown effectiveness in long-form speech
generation. Each baseline is evaluated using its native recom-
mended configuration.
In addition to subjective evaluations, we employed objec-
tive metrics—phoneme error rate (PER)[15] and speaker sim-
ilarity (SS)[14]—to evaluate phonetic precision and speaker
consistency of our model. Our proposed model was evalu-
ated and compared under the following three inference con-
figurations:Infer-ctx: inference using only the additional con-
text sequence;Infer-inst: inference using only the additional
instruction sequence;Infer-ctx&inst: inference incorporating
both context and instruction sequences.

4. Conclusions
The paper proposes a controllable, context-aware TTS frame-
work for multicast audiobooks, with three innovations: 1) a
context mechanism for contextual consistency, 2) a disentangle-
ment paradigm to decouple style control from speech prompts
for semantic consistency, and 3) self-distillation to boost emo-
tional expressiveness and controllability. Experiments show
that the framework outperforms baselines in narration, dialog,
and chapter generation, with ablation studies validating its key
components. In the future, we can expand the chapter data or
select specific data to mitigate data sparsity, explore reinforce-
ment learning for performance improvement, and further inves-
tigate “thinking”-style architectures for speech models, where
contextual understanding and model control are directly trained
within the large speech generative model itself, rather than be-
ing delegated to external or modular components.
