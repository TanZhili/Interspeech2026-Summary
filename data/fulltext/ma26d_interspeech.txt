Abstract
Large-scale pretrained automatic speech recognition (ASR)
models such as Whisper achieve strong generalization but suf-
fer catastrophic forgetting under sequential domain adaptation.
We propose a strict rehearsal-free continual learning method
that uses distillation from the previous model to define a re-
tention direction. We refine it with entropy-guided token-level
distillation, up-weighting confident teacher tokens and down-
weighting uncertain ones. When supervised and distillation
gradients conflict, we apply retention-preserving gradient pro-
jection to attenuate the opposing supervised component with
a tunable projection coefficient. Based on Fisher information,
we freeze the encoder and adapt only the decoder. Compared
with Learning without Forgetting (LwF), our method achieves
a 7.2% relative reduction in mean word error rate (WER) after
sequential adaptation to LibriSpeech, AMI, TED-LIUM, and
SPGISpeech. It reduces the average multilingual degradation
on Common V oice by 51.5%.

1. Introduction
Large-scale pretrained automatic speech recognition (ASR)
models [1, 2, 3] have substantially improved recognition across
diverse languages and recording conditions. However, practical
deployments still require domain adaptation to handle domain-
specific terminology and named entities. As new terms emerge
continuously, models must be updated over time while pre-
serving both previously achieved domain performance and the
general capabilities acquired during pretraining. We study this
problem from a continual learning perspective [4] under a strict
rehearsal-free constraint. This constraint reflects practical set-
tings where neither past data nor parameter-importance esti-
mates are available for large-scale pretrained models.
Accordingly, we assume that only the previous model pa-
rameters are available from past domains when adapting to a
new domain. A widely adopted baseline for rehearsal-free con-
tinual learning is Learning without Forgetting (LwF) [5]. LwF
uses the previous model as a frozen teacher and distills the
teacher predictions on current-domain inputs to preserve prior
knowledge. Distillation is therefore central to retention because
it anchors parameter updates to match the teacher outputs [6].
Since retention is driven by the distillation gradient, its quality
becomes crucial.
In sequential adaptation, standard LwF suffers from two is-
sues when distillation is uniformly weighted and directly com-

bined with the supervised loss. First, weighting all tokens
equally in distillation allows high-uncertainty teacher outputs to
act as noisy retention targets. Second, supervised and distilla-
tion gradients can conflict, and combining them without explicit
handling may compromise retention.
Our key idea is to define the distillation gradient as a re-
tention direction and to modify the supervised update only
when it conflicts with this direction. We refine this retention
direction via entropy-guided token-level distillation, using en-
tropy as a proxy for teacher uncertainty. We then perform
retention-preserving gradient projection, introducing a tunable
projection coefficient η for explicit control over the retention–
adaptation trade-off. Based on a Fisher information analysis [7],
we freeze the encoder and adapt only the decoder, enabling ef-
ficient rehearsal-free continual adaptation for encoder–decoder
ASR models such as Whisper [3]. Experiments on four English
domains show that the proposed method reduces average word
error rate (WER) and improves retention compared with LwF.
It also mitigates degradation of pretrained multilingual capabil-
ities.
2. Related work
2.1. Continual learning
Continual learning methods are commonly divided into
rehearsal-based and rehearsal-free approaches, depending on
whether past data is stored for replay [4]. Rehearsal-free meth-
ods include parameter regularization, such as EWC [7] and SI
[8], and knowledge distillation, such as LwF [5]. For large-
scale pretrained models such as Whisper, the original pretrain-
ing data and parameter-importance estimates from prior tasks
are typically unavailable, making importance-based regulariza-
tion impractical. Distillation-based approaches therefore be-
come the natural baseline. LwF uses a fixed weighting between
the supervised and distillation losses, treating all teacher predic-
tions equally regardless of their uncertainty. Adaptive weight-
ing based on teacher uncertainty [9, 10, 11] and token-level dis-
tillation [12] have been explored in knowledge distillation, but
have received limited attention in rehearsal-free continual learn-
ing for ASR.
2.2. Gradient projection for continual learning
Gradient projection methods address task interference at the op-
timization level. GEM [13] and A-GEM [14] project gradients
using reference gradients from episodic memory to prevent in-
creasing loss on past tasks. OGD [15] stores principal gradient
directions from past tasks and projects new gradients toward or-
thogonal directions. These methods require rehearsal buffers or

2.3. Continual learning for pretrained ASR
Recent work has applied continual learning to large-scale pre-
trained ASR models. Task-specific LoRA adapters with orthog-
onal constraints have been explored for multilingual ASR, but
the additional LoRA parameters grow with the number of tasks
[17]. Gradient surgery has also been applied to the Whisper
decoder for multilingual adaptation, but it relies on replay sam-
ples from previous tasks [18]. In contrast, our method remains
strictly rehearsal-free while refining the distillation signal and
resolving gradient conflicts via projection.
3. Proposed method
We present a rehearsal-free continual learning framework for
ASR. In this framework, we retain only the previous model
parameters and use the frozen previous model as the teacher.
To mitigate forgetting, we project the supervised gradient only
when it conflicts with the distillation gradient. The projection
coefficient η controls the retention–adaptation trade-off. We
compute the distillation gradient via entropy-guided token-level
distillation to down-weight uncertain teacher predictions. Fig-
ure 1 provides an overview of these two components. We de-
scribe entropy-guided token-level distillation in Section 3.2 and
retention-preserving gradient projection in Section 3.3.
We measure the adaptation sensitivity of Whisper Large-v3
using a diagonal Fisher approximation. Decoder parameters are
consistently more sensitive than encoder parameters, by factors
of 1.73 on LibriSpeech [19] and 1.79 on AMI [20]. Accord-
ingly, we fine-tune only the decoder and freeze the encoder to
reduce computation and mitigate forgetting.
3.1. Baseline: Learning without Forgetting
Given a pretrained ASR model θ0 and a sequence of target
domainsD1,D2,..., DK, we sequentially adapt the model to
each domain. At adaptation stage k, only the current domain
dataDk and the previous model parametersθk−1 are available.
Following the LwF framework, we useθk−1 as a frozen teacher
to preserve prior knowledge through distillation on current do-
main inputs.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental setup
We evaluate our method on sequential English domain adapta-
tion using Whisper Large-v3 [3] as the base model. The model
is adapted across diverse domains in the order LIB→ AMI→
TED→ SPG. Table 1 summarizes the datasets used for sequen-
tial adaptation and evaluation. For LibriSpeech, we use only
the train-other-500, dev-other, and test-other splits. For SPGIS-

Unless otherwise stated, we freeze the encoder and fine-
tune only the decoder. For our method, we use temperature

tion coefficientη = 0.75. Training employs AdamW [24] with
learning rate 1× 10−5, batch size 64, and 5 epochs per domain.

Table 2 presents WER (%) on all domains after each sequen-

Our rehearsal-free method attains the lowest final average

Table 3 compares our method with rehearsal-based ap-

Table 4 presents a component analysis on LIB→AMI from the

Adding gradient projection yields a clear improvement in
retention, reducing WER on LIB from 4.27% to 4.11%, and it
also improves performance on the unseen-domain and multilin-
gual evaluations. These results suggest that explicitly handling
interference between the supervised update and the distillation-
based retention direction is critical under a strict rehearsal-
free constraint. However, projection restricts updates toward
the new domain and increases AMI WER from 10.89% to
13.20%. When entropy-guided weighting is combined with
projection, AMI adaptation partially recovers from 13.20% to
12.96% while the retention and generalization benefits of pro-
jection are largely preserved.

5. Conclusion
We proposed a rehearsal-free continual adaptation method for
Whisper-based ASR that combines entropy-guided token-level
distillation with retention-preserving gradient projection, and
freezes the encoder based on a Fisher information analysis.
Compared with LwF, our method reduces the final average
WER from 8.75% to 8.12%, a 7.2% relative reduction across
LibriSpeech, AMI, TED-LIUM, and SPGISpeech. BWT im-
proves from−4.63 to−2.82 compared with LwF. Component
analysis shows that gradient projection drives the primary gains
in zero-shot generalization and retention. Entropy-guided token
weighting modestly improves adaptation under projection by
down-weighting uncertain teacher predictions while maintain-
ing retention. The projection coefficientη provides control over
this retention–adaptation trade-off. Multilingual evaluation on
Common V oice results in an average degradation of 0.95%, a
51.5% relative reduction compared to 1.96% for LwF.
