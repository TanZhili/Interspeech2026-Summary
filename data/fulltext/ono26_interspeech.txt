Abstract
This paper addresses DOA-informed multichannel speech sep-
aration under overdetermined conditions, where the number of
speakers does not exceed the number of microphones. We focus
on FastMNMF, a BSS method robust to unknown acoustic envi-
ronments. Since FastMNMF represents real-source spatial co-
variance matrices in a jointly diagonalized domain, its diagonal
components correspond to virtual spatial components rather than
the real sources themselves. We propose directivity-regularized
FastMNMF, which encourages each pseudo-separation filter to
pass signals from the target speaker direction and suppress those
from other speaker directions while tolerating DOA errors. The
separation matrix is updated using a closed-form VCD-based
rule. Experiments with simulated noisy simultaneous speech
mixtures show that the proposed regularization improves separa-
tion performance.
Index Terms: multichannel speech separation, blind source
separation, direction of arrival, beam pattern regularization
1. Introduction
Multichannel source separation is essential as a front-end pro-
cessing step for distant speech enhancement, speech recognition,
and speaker identification, requiring stable operation in diverse
acoustic environments. In particular, for real-time applications
using smart glasses and wearable devices, the ability to selec-
tively enhance the speech of a specific person to whom the user
is paying attention is crucial [1–3].
Blind source separation (BSS) has the advantage of operat-
ing relatively robustly even in unknown environments, as it per-
forms unsupervised learning solely from observed data without
prior information. Multichannel nonnegative matrix factoriza-
tion (MNMF) [4] has often been used as a versatile BSS method
applicable even to underdetermined conditions. It is based on
the maximum likelihood (ML) estimation of a unified probabilis-
tic model consisting of an acoustic model and a spatial model.
However, the full-rank spatial covariance matrix (SCM) repre-
senting the inter-channel correlation structure of each source has
a high degree of freedom, making iterative optimization prone
to falling into poor local optima. To address this issue, FastM-
NMF [5, 6], which imposes a joint diagonalization constraint on
the SCMs, has been proposed, achieving both highly accurate
and computationally efficient source separation.
We consider applying FastMNMF to speech separation us-
ing smart glasses. With recent advances in object detection tech-
nologies, it is becoming possible to accurately localize multi-

In this paper, we propose FastMNMF withdirectivityregu-

known. While FastMNMF is applicable to overdetermined con-
ditions, the matrix used for jointly diagonalizing the SCMs of all
sources corresponds to the separation matrix in determined BSS,
separating the signals into the same number ofvirtualsources
as microphones. It is thus effective to regularize each pseudo-
separation filter so that it passes signals around the assigned tar-
get DOA and suppresses signals around the other source DOAs.
Specifically, we add a regularization term such that the inner
product with the steering vector, which is geometrically calcu-
lated from the microphone array configuration, approaches 1
around the target DOA and 0 around the DOAs to be suppressed.
Considering the DOA estimation error of the target speaker,
the user’s head movement, and the directivity of the speech, we
also propose a robust weighting strategy for the regularization
term. Specifically, we use the probability density function (PDF)
of the von Mises distribution centered on the DOA to be passed
or suppressed as a weight. All the parameters of the proposed
method can be iteratively optimized in a closed form. Specifi-
cally, we use the multiplicative update algorithm for the source
model, and vector coordinate descent (VCD) [9] for the spatial
model. We show that this strategy achieves significant improve-
ment in performance and stability.
2. Background
This section introduces speech and source separation techniques,
often with spatial regularization.
2.1. Blind source separation
Conventional BSS methods, such as frequency-domain inde-
pendent component analysis (FD-ICA) [10], independent vec-
tor analysis (IV A) [11, 12], and independent low-rank matrix
analysis (ILRMA) [13], rely on a rank-1 spatial model. This re-
stricts their application to determined conditions and limits their
performance in highly reverberant environments. In contrast,
MNMF [4] and its variant FastMNMF [5, 6] assume a full-rank
spatial model, providing high flexibility even under underdeter-
mined conditions.

4.3. Experimental Results
Table 1 shows the separation performance under varying num-
bers of sources N. The proposed DR-FastMNMF with von
Mises weighting achieved the best performance forN= 1 and
N= 2 , clearly outperforming vanilla FastMNMF and the point-
weight variant. These results indicate that combining full-rank
spatial modeling with probabilistic directivity regularization ef-
fectively guides the separation matrix Qf toward acoustically
valid solutions, particularly under realistic conversational situa-
tions when the number of competing sources (speakers) is small.
A critical observation is the superiority of von Mises weight-
ing over point-wise constraints. For N= 1 , the point-weight
variant DR-FastMNMF achieved only 13.3 dB SDR, nearly
5 dB below the proposed method, while also exhibiting substan-
tially higher variance (±3.4 vs.±2.4 dB). This gap highlights
that soft, uncertainty-aware regularization is more robust than
hard directional constraints, which are susceptible to steering
mismatch and local optima.
ForN= 3 andN= 4 , SR-FastMNMF with point weight-
ing achieved the highest SDR, suggesting that the distortionless-
response constraints become harder to satisfy in spatially con-
gested conditions. Nevertheless, the proposed method remained
competitive with vanilla FastMNMF in perceptual metrics, in-
dicating that the directivity regularization can still be beneficial
even when SDR improvement is limited.

5. Conclusion
This paper proposed directivity-regularized FastMNMF for
DOA-informed multichannel speech separation. By reinterpret-
ing the joint diagonalization matrix as a pseudo-separation ma-
trix and distributing regularization weights over a continuous an-
gular domain via von Mises probability density functions, the
proposed method achieves soft directional control that accom-
modates DOA estimation errors and reverberant spatial spread,
while admitting closed-form iterative updates within the VCD
framework. Experiments confirmed consistent performance im-
provements over unregularized FastMNMF and rank-1 counter-
parts, with the probabilistic weighting reducing variance com-
pared to point-wise constraints.
These results suggest that integrating probabilistic spatial
priors with full-rank source modeling offers a promising path
toward robust speech separation in real-world acoustic environ-
ments. In practical systems, DOAs can be obtained from exter-
nal localization modules, such as microphone-array-based local-
ization or visual tracking, and evaluation with estimated DOAs
is an important direction for future work.
