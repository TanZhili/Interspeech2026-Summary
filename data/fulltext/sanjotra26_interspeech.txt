Investigating the Relationship between Objective AI-Driven Metrics and
Subjective MOS for In-the-Wild Speech
Jasmer Sanjotra ID 1, Nagendra Kumar ID 1, Shekhar Nayak ID 2
1 Indian Institute of Technology Indore, India

Abstract
Automated Mean Opinion Score (MOS) predictors are widely
used for TTS evaluation but are trained on studio or denois-
ing corpora. Compared to human MOS ratings, O-MOS corre-
lations collapse on in-the-wild (ITW) discrete-token TTS. We
conduct a controlled ablation across four systems—continuous
clean TTS, discrete ITW TTS (MQTTS), an additive noise con-
trol, and ground-truth recordings—and collect 768 human nat-
uralness ratings under a headphone-screened protocol. UT-
MOSv2 correlation collapses fromr=0.51(p<0.01) on the
continuous clean system tor=−0.01(n.s.) on the discrete
ITW system; other neural predictors also collapse. DNSMOS
P.835 penalises an additive noise condition 0.42 points more
severely than a generative-artefact condition despite listeners
rating both equivalently, directly contradicting human percep-
tion. These findings challenge the validity of current objective
metrics as standalone proxies for naturalness evaluation of in-
the-wild generative TTS.

1. Introduction
The last five years have witnessed a fundamental paradigm shift
in speech synthesis. Models trained on curated studio data
achieve near-human naturalness on reading-style speech [1, 2].
However, the current frontier has moved decisively toward zero-
shot generation from arbitrary in-the-wild (ITW) audio sources
- YouTube recordings, podcasts, and spontaneous speech with
inherent acoustic noise, channel variation, and prosodic irregu-
larity [3, 4]. This capability is practically transformative; yet it
introduces a critical evaluation blind spot.
Current practice widely employs automated objective MOS
(O-MOS) predictors - UTMOSv2 [5], DNSMOS P.835 [6],
PLC-MOS [7] - alongside DSP proxies such as PESQ [8] and
SI-SDR [9]. All share a provenance mismatch: trained on de-
noised or clean continuous speech, they have no exposure to
generative semantic artifacts. When a discrete token model
hallucinates a phoneme or inverts prosody, the waveform may
exhibit smooth spectral texture yet be judged unintelligible by
listeners. We term thisacoustic camouflage:a generative er-
ror perceptually salient to listeners but acoustically invisible to
surface-quality metrics. The inverse also holds - adding babble
noise preserves semantic fidelity while incurring large metric
penalties—theadditive-generative paradox:metrics penalise
semantically intact additive noise more severely than seman-
tically corrupted generative artefacts.
We design a paired ablation study with four systems dif-
fering only in architecture and noise origin, and evaluate four
neural MOS metric families against human naturalness ratings.
We report three findings:
1. A statistically significantcorrelation collapse: UTMOSv2
achieves Pearsonr= 0.51(p <0.01) on the continuous
TTS system but falls tor=−0.01(p= 0.95, n.s.) on the
discrete ITW system for this MQTTS-based model, a drop
confirmed by Steiger’s test [10] for dependent correlations
(t(29) = 2.24,p= 0.033). All evaluated neural MOS pre-
dictors similarly collapse to non-significance.
2. Anadditive-generative paradox: neural MOS predictors pe-
nalise an additive noise condition by up to 0.42 points relative
to the discrete ITW system (DNSMOS P.835 OVRL:3.06vs.
2.64), despite humans assigning statistically equivalent natu-
ralness ratings to both (∆MOS= 0.10,p= 0.14).
3. Anacoustic camouflageeffect: scatter analysis (Figure 1) re-
veals systematic non-monotonicity, with UTMOSv2 scores
exceeding 3.8 on 7 discrete ITW files rated below 2.5 by hu-
man listeners.
Collectively, these results argue that evaluating generative
ITW discrete-token TTS using neural MOS predictors trained
on denoised or clean-speech corpora, without supplementary
methods such as automatic speech recognition, risks invalid sys-
tem comparisons.
2. Related Work
2.1. Subjective speech quality evaluation
Mean opinion score (MOS), defined under ITU-T Rec.
P.800 [11], is the standard for perceptual TTS evaluation.
Crowdsourced administration is now common [12, 13, 14]. Al-
ternatives such as Best–Worst Scaling (BWS) and AB pref-
erence tests can mitigate material-set sensitivity in principle;
Wells et al. [15] find MOS, AB, and BWS broadly equally valid
for synthesis evaluation. The SOMOS dataset [16] collected
MOS ratings for 200 neural TTS systems; all clean, continu-
ously parameterised; no ITW discrete synthesis was included.
We collectN= 768naturalness ratings under headphone-
screened conditions following the Absolute Category Rating
(ACR) convention [16].
2.2. Automated MOS prediction for TTS
The V oiceMOS Challenge 2022 [17] showed that fine-tuning
self-supervised speech models is effective for in-domain MOS
prediction; the winning UTMOS [18] achieves strong corre-
lation on Blizzard and VCC data but loses it sharply out-of-
domain. TorchAudio-Squim [19] packages reference-less esti-
mators of PESQ, STOI, SI-SDR, and MOS (via NORESQA-
MOS with a non-matching reference). Cooper [20] surveys

2.3. Non-intrusive speech quality metrics
DNSMOS P.835 [6] and NISQA [21] both define quality as the
degree to which a clean signal is preserved underadditivecor-
ruption. DSP proxies (PESQ [8], STOI [22], SI-SDR [9]) ap-
ply this framework via explicit reference comparison. Com-
plementary paradigms assess other dimensions: speaker sim-
ilarity is commonly measured as cosine similarity between
speaker-verification embeddings [23], and distributional met-
rics such as TTSDS [24] score system-level distributional close-
ness across prosody, speaker identity, and intelligibility factors.
All frame-level and distributional approaches above are struc-
turally blind to discrete-token semantic failures - token hallu-
cinations and prosodic inversions that preserve smooth spectral
texture - which produce no additive noise signal.
2.4. In-the-wild and discrete-token TTS
Early neural TTS (Tacotron 2 [2], FastSpeech 2 [25]) used
clean studio data. Zero-shot models (V ALL-E [4], MQTTS [3])
extend to arbitrary speakers from short reference prompts.
MQTTS targets ITW data via multi-codebook VQ and mono-
tonic alignment. The TITW database [26] formalises this
paradigm with a V oxCeleb1-derived corpus; even its easy split
retains DNSMOS scores well below clean TTS corpora, con-
firming the distributional gap current O-MOS metrics were not
designed to bridge. Discrete token synthesis introduces a new
failure class absent from all metric training corpora: sequences
of individually valid tokens that are collectively semantically
incoherent.
3. Methodology
3.1. Experimental systems
Four systems were constructed fromN= 32diverse text
transcripts and reference speaker prompts drawn from TITW-
Easy [26]. Speaker identity and text content are held constant
across all four systems to isolate architectural and degradation-
type effects. No active control of the human MOS distribution
was applied; ratings span the full 1–5 ACR scale as determined
by the four-system design. Listener eligibility, block randomi-
sation, and internal consistency checks (§3.2) are the controls
applied.
SYS-A (Continuous clean anchor).StyleTTS 2 [1], pre-
trained on clean studio data, conditioned on adenoisedITW
reference for speaker identity. Output is clean and high-fidelity
- the system on which O-MOS metrics are validated.
SYS-B (Discrete ITW target).MQTTS [3] is extended
with a semantic token encoder, trained and operated onraw,
unprocessed ITW reference audio. MQTTS uses a vector-
quantised multi-codebook approach with monotonic alignment,
specifically architected for real-world spontaneous speech. The
semantic encoder enriches content and speaker style represen-
tation. This places the system in an ITW generative regime
that is largely absent from the training distributions of UT-
MOSv2, DNSMOS, and related metrics. It exhibits charac-
teristic discrete-token generative artefacts - token repetitions,
prosodic inversions, and occasional phoneme-level hallucina-
tions - withnoadditive background noise.
SYS-C (Additive noise control).SYS-A outputs with
MUSAN [27] babble noise added at a calibrated level. Because
no clean reference exists for SYS-B, noise level was set non-
intrusively: NISQA [21] scores were matched between SYS-
C and SYS-B, ensuring equivalent perceived degradation with-
out a clean-signal reference. SYS-C introducesadditivedistor-
tion while preserving full semantic fidelity - the distortion class
acoustic metrics were designed to detect.
SYS-D (Ground truth).Original real-world recordings of
the same utterances, serving as the perceptual upper bound and
scale anchor.
3.2. Subjective evaluation
Platform and procedure.The study was hosted on LimeSur-
vey GmbH [28]. Each participant accessed a single, randomly
assigned rating block of 16 audio samples (4 per system), with
playback order fully randomised. One sample was silently re-
peated within each block as an internal consistency check.
Pre-screening.All participants were required to pass a
headphone listening test prior to entering any ratings, enforc-
ing minimum transducer quality and controlling the listening
environment.
Elicitation.Participants rated a single dimension:“Rate
the naturalness of the audio”on a 5-point ACR Likert scale: 1
(Bad) – 2 (Poor) – 3 (Fair) – 4 (Good) – 5 (Excellent).
Participant pool.Internationally diverse (L1: Russian,
Mandarin, Hindi, Dutch, Spanish, among others); all confirmed
sufficient English familiarity for naturalness judgements. No
significant L1-group fixed effect was found in the mixed-effects
model, confirming diversity mitigates rather than introduces
systematic bias.
Quality control.An entire participant response vector was
discarded if the repeated-sample score deviated by more than
±1point from the original rating. Participants who completed
the survey in under three minutes or who assigned identical rat-
ings to all items were also excluded.
Corpus and analysis.The study comprised 48 valid par-
ticipants, each assigned to one of 8 randomised rating blocks (6
participants per block). Each block contained 16 audio samples
(4 per system), yieldingN= 768valid ratings in total, with
192 independent observations per system and 6 ratings per au-
dio file. System-level effects are estimated with a linear mixed-
effects model—MOS∼System+ (1|Listener) + (1|Text)—
implemented withlme4[29]. Bootstrapped 95% confidence
intervals are reported for system means.
3.3. Objective metrics evaluated
Four neural O-MOS families are evaluated; each maps a monau-
ral waveform to a MOS-like score on a 1–5 scale without tran-
script or a matching clean reference.UTMOSv2[5] fuses SSL
speech representations with spectrogram features from a pre-
trained EfficientNetV2 encoder, trained on V oiceMOS Chal-
lenge naturalness labels [17], and regresses perceived quality
of synthetic utterances from the waveform.DNSMOS P.835
OVRL[6] is a CNN on log-power spectrograms trained on
ITU-T P.835 crowdsourced ratings of noise-suppressed speech;
we report the overall (OVRL) dimension.DNSMOS Pro[30]
(BVCC and VCC2018 checkpoints) extends the DNSMOS de-
sign as a compact end-to-end model that predicts a Gaussian
MOS posterior from log-magnitude spectrograms, with sepa-
rate checkpoints trained on those neural-TTS MOS corpora.

PLCMOS[7] is a non-intrusive neural estimator of human
MOS for audio processed by packet-loss concealment. The set
spans TTS-oriented MOS regression, noise-suppression MOS,
TTS-domain specialisation, and telecommunications degrada-
tion; PLCMOS is included to test whether correlation collapse

4. Results and Differential Analysis
4.1. System-level MOS and objective baselines
Table 1 presents system-level human MOS means with boot-
strapped 95% confidence intervals, alongside key objective met-
ric means. Human ratings rank the systems SYS-D>SYS-A
>SYS-B>SYS-C, consistent with the experimental design.
Critically, the SYS-B and SYS-C gap is narrow and statisti-
cally non-significant (∆MOS= 0.10,p= 0.14, mixed-effects
contrast), confirming that the NISQA-calibrated noise addition
achieved the intended perceptual parity between additive and
generative degradation conditions.
The objective metrics fail to reproduce this human order-
ing. UTMOSv2 assigns its highest mean to SYS-A (µ= 4.22)
but reverses SYS-B/SYS-C (3.40vs.3.49). DNSMOS P.835
OVRL and DNS-Pro BVCC penalise the additive condition
more than the generative one (2.64vs.3.06;2.35vs.2.77),
despite human equivalence. Ground-truth SYS-D receives the
lowestUTMOSv2 score (µ= 3.35)—an expected domain-
boundary effect, as real recordings lack the clean-synthesis
spectral signature the metric was trained to reward, not evidence
of anomalous miscalibration.
4.2. Finding 1: The correlation collapse
Table 2 reports file-level Pearsonrand Spearmanρbetween
each objective metric and human MOS, computed separately
for SYS-A, SYS-B, and SYS-C (n= 32files per system; SYS-
D is excluded as a non-synthesised reference class).
For SYS-A (continuous clean TTS), UTMOSv2 achieves
statistically significant moderate correlation (r= 0.51,ρ=
0.47,p <0.01), consistent with its training on comparable
continuous synthesis data. DNSMOS P.835 OVRL, DNS-Pro
BVCC, and DNS-Pro VCC2018 exhibit weaker but significant
correlations (r= 0.48,0.41, and0.37, respectively). For
SYS-C (additive noise), these correlations are maintained, as
expected given that additive noise is the distortion class these
metrics were trained to detect.
For SYS-B (discrete ITW), every metric without excep-
tion falls to statistical non-significance. UTMOSv2 drops to
r=−0.01(bootstrapped 95% CI:[−0.36,+0.34];p= 0.95),
DNSMOS OVRL tor= 0.13(p= 0.44), and DNS-Pro BVCC
and VCC2018 tor= 0.10andr= 0.08respectively (both
p >0.65). The confidence interval for UTMOSv2 on SYS-
B is centred essentially at zero, confirming that the correlation
is indistinguishable from chance. This represents a total rank-
order failure: no evaluated metric can reliably distinguish high-
naturalness from low-naturalness ITW discrete-token outputs.
The magnitude of the collapse is large; Steiger’s test for depen-
dent correlations—the appropriate test when two correlations
share the same sample—confirms the SYS-A to SYS-B drop
(fromr= 0.51, 95% CI[0.20,0.73], tor=−0.01) is sta-
tistically significant (t(29) = 2.24,p= 0.033). The visual
evidence is presented in Figure 1.
4.3. Finding 2: The additive-generative paradox
A direct comparison of SYS-B and SYS-C exposes a systematic
inversion between metric preferences and human perception.
Humans rate both conditions statistically equivalently (SYS-B:
MOS= 3.32; SYS-C: MOS= 3.22;p= 0.14). Yet the objec-
tive metrics diverge sharply.
DNSMOS P.835 OVRL penalises the additive condition by
∆ = 0.42(SYS-B:3.06; SYS-C:2.64), and DNS-Pro BVCC
by∆ = 0.42(SYS-B:2.77; SYS-C:2.35). These are penal-
ties for a deterministic additive noise process that preserves ev-
ery phoneme and prosodic contour intact. Meanwhile, SYS-
B’s generative artefacts (which permanently corrupt semantic
fidelity) are invisible to these same metrics. UTMOSv2 further
reflects this paradox by assigning higher scores to SYS-C than
SYS-B (3.49vs.3.40), reversing the human ordering entirely.
These metrics evaluate the absence of surface-level noise
and have no mechanism to detect incorrect phonemes, inverted
prosody, or semantically void repetitions. For ITW discrete
synthesis they penalise the wrong distortion class. PLC-MOS,
trained on packet-loss rather than additive noise, assigns iden-
tical scores to SYS-B and SYS-D (4.06each), thus indifferent
to both generative artefacts and real-speech characteristics and
showing the failure extends beyond noise-trained metrics to any
predictor lacking ITW generative training signal.
4.4. Finding 3: The acoustic camouflage effect
The correlation collapse for SYS-B is not merely a mean-level
effect; it reflects systematic non-monotonic behaviour at the
sample level, as visualised in Figure 1. In 7 of 32 SYS-B files
where human naturalness ratings fell below 2.5, UTMOSv2 as-
signed scores exceeding 3.8. In 3 of those cases, the metric
exceeded 4.0 while the human mean fell below 2.2. A further
group of files with comparably low human MOS received ap-
propriately low UTMOSv2 scores (correctly-penalised cases),
demonstrating that the camouflage is specific to the subset of
generative artefacts that preserve smooth spectral texture. No
analogous divergence of this magnitude was observed in SYS-
A or SYS-C, confirming it is not a general property of low-
naturalness utterances.
The mechanism is the same as for acoustic camouflage: UT-
MOSv2 evaluates frame-level spectral texture, thehowof syn-
thesis - and cannot detect token-level semantic errors that de-
termine human ratings. A metric assigning scores above 4.0 to
samples rated below 2.2 by humans is not merely inaccurate but
actively misleading: system rankings derived from it risk being
the inverse of ground truth.
5. Conclusion

ing three distinct failure modes of standard O-MOS metrics
for zero-shot in-the-wild discrete-token TTS. First, thecor-
relation collapse: Pearsonrfor UTMOSv2 drops from0.51
(p<0.01) on continuous clean TTS to a non-significant−0.01
(p= 0.95) on the MQTTS-based ITW system (Figure 1);
all other evaluated neural MOS predictors similarly collapse

severely than generative-artefact conditions (DNSMOS OVRL:
3.06vs.2.64; DNS-Pro BVCC:2.77vs.2.35), while humans
perceive both conditions as statistically equivalent—metrics pe-
nalise the wrong distortion class. Third,acoustic camouflage:

The root cause is provenance mismatch: metrics trained on
denoising or clean synthesis evaluate spectral surface quality;
human naturalness perception evaluates communicative and se-
mantic integrity. No current metric bridges this gap for ITW
discrete synthesis.
A limitation of the present study is that the correlation col-
lapse is demonstrated for a single discrete ITW architecture
(MQTTS with semantic encoder). Whether the same magnitude
of failure occurs with other discrete systems (e.g. V ALL-E [4])
depends on their specific artefact profiles and cannot be directly
inferred from these results. We therefore frame the findings as
evidence thatanydiscrete-token ITW system with generative
artefact characteristics similar to those documented here will
expose the same metric failure modes, rather than as a claim
about all discrete synthesis universally.
The practical implication is direct: neural MOS predictors
should not be used as standalone proxies for ITW discrete-token
TTS evaluation. The failure modes are semantic and prosodic,
i.e. dimensions that ASR-based word error rate (WER) is de-
signed to capture. A composite protocol pairing a neural MOS
predictor (acoustic surface quality) with WER (semantic in-
tegrity) would be sensitive to both; we leave its empirical val-
idation to future work. Additional directions include multi-
dimensional perceptual evaluation [16] and purpose-built met-
rics trained on ITW discrete synthesis data.
