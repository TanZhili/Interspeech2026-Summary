UGPCB: Uncertainty-Gated Phonetic Contextual Biasing for Improving
Hotword Recognition in Large Speech Models

Abstract
Automatic speech recognition systems exhibit recognition bias
when processing hot words. Context bias techniques effectively
tackle this issue by integrating user-defined vocabularies, thus
enhancing keyword transcription accuracy. Although speech
matching technology has advanced context bias techniques by
resolving subword fragmentation, existing methods still face
challenges with over-bias and homophone misclassification. To
address this, this study proposes a training-free decode-time
framework named Uncertainty-Gated Phonetic Contextual Bias
(UGPCB). This framework employs an entropy-driven gating
mechanism and bimodal contrastive penalties, and it is eval-
uated on the Dolphin base model. The experimental results
demonstrate a 16.04% recall improvement (achieving an F1
score of 90.81%) while restricting precision reduction to 0.78%.
Even when 1,000 distractors are introduced, the recall still in-
creases by 14.26%, fully demonstrating the method’s potential
for ASR applications.
Index Terms: automatic speech recognition, hotword recogni-
tion, contextual biasing, entropy-driven, zero-shot decoding
1. Introduction
Although end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition
(ASR) architectures [1] have achieved strong performance in
conversational transcription, their reliance on training data dis-
tributions often causes severe degradation in hotword recogni-
tion, including proper nouns, domain-specific terms, and user-
defined keywords [2]. Contextual biasing mitigates this is-
sue by injecting target vocabularies during decoding to encour-
age correct keyword transcription. Common approaches range
from shallow fusion [3] to unsupervised biasing without explicit
metadata [4]. However, integrating external knowledge with-
out disrupting the model’s general language capability remains
challenging [5, 6].
A key limitation of exact-matching biasing is its depen-
dence on subword tokenization (e.g., BPE), the basic unit in
many E2E ASR systems. Hotwords are often fragmented into
irregular subword sequences that are difficult to predict, mak-
ing text-only matching brittle. To reduce sensitivity to subword
boundaries, pronunciation-aware matching has been explored
[7], where fuzzy matching in the phonetic or pinyin space im-
proves recall. However, phonetic biasing introduces two vul-
nerabilities: over-biasing and homophone ambiguity. Apply-
ing global bias scores regardless of acoustic confidence can dis-
tort posterior distributions and degrade recognition quality [6].

Moreover, phonetic similarity can trigger false positives, espe-
cially when the biasing lexicon contains many distractors [8],
leading to a trade-off between recall and hallucinations.
Large-scale speech foundation models, such as Whisper
[9], improve general robustness through scaling, but resolving
homophone ambiguity often incurs substantial overhead. Re-
cent methods increasingly rely on parameter-intensive designs,
including contrastive retrieval for scalable biasing (e.g., BR-
ASR) [10] and learnable context adapters for selective gating
[11]. LLM-based rescoring has also been used to filter hal-
lucinations via semantic context [12]. While effective, these
approaches typically require training, architectural changes, or
prohibitive inference latency.
To address these limitations, we propose Uncertainty-Gated
Phonetic Contextual Biasing (UGPCB), a training-free decod-
ing framework. UGPCB estimates model uncertainty on the
fly using normalized Shannon entropy from acoustic posteri-
ors via a lightweight logit bridge. It activates biasing only un-
der high uncertainty, reducing over-biasing in confident regions.
To suppress homophone hallucinations, a Grapheme-Phoneme
Contrastive Penalty cross-validates graphemic and phonetic ev-
idence and penalizes phonetic-only matches. Evaluated on Dol-
phin with up to 1,000 distractors, UGPCB improves hotword re-
call with small background degradation, providing a lightweight
inference-only solution for contextual ASR.
2. Methodology
This paper presents an uncertainty-gated phonetic contextual bi-
asing framework. Unlike existing deep biasing approaches [13,
14], the proposed framework operates on top of a pretrained
acoustic foundation model and builds on shallow fusion and
N-best rescoring, with uncertainty-aware gating as the main
control mechanism and grapheme-phoneme matching as the
Mandarin-specific matching layer. It requires neither additional
learnable parameters nor fine-tuning of the foundation ASR
model. The core idea is to leverage the model’s own predictive
uncertainty during decoding to adaptively modulate the injec-
tion strength of external biasing signals, while exploiting Man-
darin homophone characteristics and prior homophone-aware
contextual biasing [15] to construct a multi-granularity match-
ing mechanism. This design targets a better trade-off between
hotword recall and background false alarm rate. For clarity, bi-
modal refers to two matching channels: graphemic (BPE trie)
and phonetic (pinyin trie). The contrastive penalty enforces
cross-channel consistency at decoding time, without any con-
trastive learning.

2.1. Architecture and Logit Bridge
As illustrated in Figure1, given an acoustic feature sequence
Xand a target hotword setH={h 1,...,h M}, contextual
biasing aims to steer beam search decoding toward hypothesis
spaces containing entities fromH. Conventional shallow fusion
strategies [3, 16] treat hotwords as static lexical patterns and ap-
ply fixed score weighting. This paradigm has two limitations:
it overlooks the homophone variants prevalent in spoken Man-
darin, and the static bias scores, applied regardless of acous-
tic confidence, can distort the model’s posterior distributions in
high-confidence regions.
Training-Free Logit Bridge.To couple biasing signals
with acoustic uncertainty without modifying model parameters,
we introduce a logit-bridging mechanism between the decoder
and the external biasing module. At each autoregressive time
stept, this mechanism intercepts the decoder’s forward pass and
caches the raw unnormalized logitslt into a shared tensor space,
without modifying model parameters or decoder computations.
The biasing module then retrieves these logits to perform en-
tropy computation and dynamic gating. This parameter-free de-
sign enables token-level posterior estimation at each step while
maintaining full decoupling between the biasing algorithm and
the backbone parameters.
2.2. Grapheme-Phoneme Dual-Track Alignment
To capture both orthographic and phonetic features of tar-
get hotwords, we construct parallelgrapheme-phoneme dual-
track tries. In the orthographic space, target hotwords are de-
composed into BPE [17] subword sequences to build a BPE trie.
In the phonetic space, hotwords are mapped into both tone-
aware and tone-agnostic pinyin sequences, which are used to
build two parallel phonetic tries for strict matching and fuzzy
matching that tolerates tonal variation, respectively.
A key challenge is that BPE tokens span variable num-
bers of characters, causing conventional prefix-based phonetic
matching to suffer from traversal interruptions—a problem we
termphonetic truncation. To address this, our system pre-
compiles a global phonetic mapping tablePcovering the entire
vocabulary. The pinyin sequences are obtained using a stan-
dard Mandarin pinyin toolkit with canonical pronunciation (i.e.,
without enumerating heteronyms). At each decoding stept,
the historical token sequence is projected into a phonetic sym-
bol sequence:Φ(Y <t) = [phon(y 1),...,phon(y t−1)]. For
multi-character BPE tokens, only thefirst phonemeis indexed
into the reverse lookup table to prevent misaligned insertions
from internal syllable offsets. The phonetic tries then perform
sliding-window queries over this phonetic sequence. Com-
bined withexplicit whitespace boundariesinjected at both
ends of the pattern strings, this design eliminates matching fail-
ures caused by subword tokenization. The whitespace bound-
aries further prevent false matches from cross-syllable recom-
bination.

2.4. Contrastive Homophone Penalty

Penalty (CHP) duringN-best rescoring.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
3.1.1. Dataset and Evaluation Protocol
The proposed UGPCB framework is evaluated on AISHELL-
1 [19], a publicly available Mandarin speech corpus, following
the evaluation protocol of the SeACo benchmark [20]. The test
set consists of 808 utterances specified by the official SeACo ut-
terance list, with the target hotword list adopted from the SeACo
repository without modification. Hyperparameters are tuned on
an independent development subset of 550 utterances randomly
sampled from the SeACo development set (1,334 utterances),
with no overlap with the test set. For the distractor experiments,
distractors are synthetically generated (random seed 2026) by
sampling from a curated word pool and random character com-
positions, followed by filtering to ensure that no distractor ap-
pears as a contiguous substring in any reference transcript.
3.1.2. Baseline and Contrastive Systems
Dolphin [21], a multilingual speech foundation model (small
variant, 372M parameters, BPE tokenization, beam size 5),
serves as the base ASR system. Four contrastive configurations
are evaluated:
•B1 (Baseline): Unmodified Dolphin without contextual bias-
ing.
•B2 (Text Shallow Fusion): Exact BPE-matching trie prefix
boosting [22] withN-best rescoring; phonetic matching dis-
abled.
•B3 (Decode-time UGPCB): Entropy gating with dual-track
pinyin matching applied during decoding;N-best rescoring
disabled to isolate decode-time contributions.
•B4 (Full UGPCB): The complete framework combining
decode-time entropy gating, dual-track pinyin matching,
andN-best rescoring with grapheme-phoneme contrastive
penalty.

tance.
•B-CER (Background CER): Error rate over non-hotword

acters andHthe set of character positions belonging to hot-
word spans, identified through Levenshtein alignment. Let
S′,D′, andI′ denote substitution, deletion, and insertion er-
rors at non-hotword positions, respectively:

•Precision, Recall, and F1: Entity-level metrics computed

longest-match-first counting to prevent substring nesting.
•FA (count): Total number of false positives at the hotword-

3.1.4. Hyperparameter Selection
A grid search on the development set covers the entropy
thresholdτ∈ {0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20,0.25}and hotword
weightλ hw∈{1.5,2.0,2.5,3.0}. The configurationτ=0.10,
λhw=2.0achieves the highest F1 and is adopted for all
subsequent experiments. The remaining parameters (fuzzy
threshold= 0.35, homophone boost= 0.8) are fixed based on
preliminary experiments.

4. Conclusion and Future Work
The ablation confirms that dual-track pinyin matching and en-
tropy gating are complementary: pinyin matching improves
recall by mitigating the phonological blind spot of BPE-level
graphemic matching, while entropy gating preserves precision
by suppressing over-biasing at confident steps. Unlike end-to-
end biasing approaches such as SeACo [20] and TCPGen [23],
UGPCB operates purely at decode time with frozen parameters,
yet achieves competitive recall (84.19%) and F1 (90.81%). Un-
der larger distractor catalogs, UGPCB maintains a recall advan-
tage. Future work will reduce false alarms via adaptive gating
and candidate pre-filtering, and evaluate UGPCB on multilin-
gual datasets and additional speech foundation models.
