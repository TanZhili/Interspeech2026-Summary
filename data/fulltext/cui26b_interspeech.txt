Abstract
This paper explores novel attention mechanisms for speech en-
hancement tasks. Existing Harmonic Attention (HAtt) enhances
the reconstructions of the voiced speech spectrum and overall
speech quality by constructing a harmonic dictionary. How-
ever, HAtt often underperforms on unvoiced components, as
its design is constrained by a heavy reliance on prior knowl-
edge for modeling harmonic structures. To address this limita-
tion, we propose a Dictionary-Free Discrete Attention (DFDA)
module, which draws inspiration from finite scalar quantization
to discretize both keys and values in the attention module in a
data-driven manner, thereby learning latent dictionary represen-
tations without hand-crafted designs. The HAtt and proposed
DFDA are adapted to the TFGridNet block as a new speech
enhancement model. Results show this network significantly
improves speech quality and intelligibility by effectively sup-
pressing noise and better recovering both voiced and unvoiced
spectral structures.
Index Terms: Speech enhancement, Dictionary-free discrete
attention, Attention mechanism, Finite scalar quantization
1. Introduction
Speech Enhancement (SE) [1] aims at improving speech qual-
ity and intelligibility, particularly degradation that is caused by
various acoustic environments. SE systems are widely used in
applications such as telephones, hearing aids, and data prepro-
cessing, and often serve as a front-end for speech recognition
and speaker verification systems.
Classical SE methods [2, 3] often struggle to handle non-
stationary noise effectively. In contrast, supervised speech
enhancement based on Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) has
demonstrated substantial improvements, recently progressing
along two major paradigms: discriminative [4, 5, 6, 7] and gen-
erative models [8, 9]. A notable trend is that both approaches
are increasingly converging on the transformer architecture as
their fundamental block. State-of-the-art generative models are
now almost exclusively transformer-based, while discrimina-
tive models, such as TFGridNet [6] and MambAttention [10],
achieve significant performance gains by incorporating atten-
tion blocks. Therefore, innovating on the attention mechanism
itself presents a pathway to advance speech enhancement.
In the speech enhancement task, Harmonic Attention
(HAtt) [5] effectively models harmonic structures by incorpo-
rating hand-crafted priors, thus improving voiced speech re-
construction. The success of HAtt suggests that establishing

a structured codebook for the key and value is a direction for at-
tention mechanisms. This leads to a key hypothesis: if unvoiced
speech components could be structured by a data-driven code-
book, the performance of attention-based speech enhancement
could be further improved. From this perspective, the reliance
on a hand-crafted harmonic dictionary is a primary limitation
for designing a more comprehensive representation for atten-
tion’s keys and values.
Inspired by neural codec paradigms, we note that modern
neural codecs [11, 12] overcome similar limitations of hand-
crafted priors. For example, they employ a neural autoen-
coder with learnable quantization modules to replace the tradi-
tional source-filter model used in speech codecs such as code-
excited linear prediction [13], which relies on excitation code-
books and a linear predictive model of the vocal tract. These
codecs use a quantization core [12, 14], with or without a code-
book, to compress signals in an autoencoding fashion. Simi-
lar to Transformer-VQ [15], which uses vector quantization to
reduce the computational complexity of attention, a codebook-
based attention mechanism can be developed for keys and val-
ues, serving as a data-driven alternative to HAtt.
This mechanism aligns with a broader ”search-and-
reconstruct” structure in speech enhancement, which clusters
signal components to guide reconstruction. For example, Non-
negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) [16, 17] learns separa-
tion bases, fuzzy classifiers cluster spectral patterns [18] for
masking, and HAtt [5] uses a hand-crafted dictionary to recover
voiced structures.
Inspired by these approaches, we introduce quantization
into the attention mechanism, specifically on the key and value
representations, to construct an information bottleneck within
attention. This bottleneck imposes a finite capacity constraint
on the attention representations, encouraging the model to
preserve dominant speech-related patterns while suppressing
noise-induced stochastic variations during reconstruction.
To bridge this idea with SE, we integrate Finite Scalar
Quantization (FSQ) [14] into the attention mechanism via a
lightweight encoder–quantizer–decoder structure that processes
keys and values. By discretizing the key–value representations,
the attention weight computation is directly affected, encourag-
ing queries to match against a set of stable speech-related pat-
terns. This reduces stochastic fluctuations in the attention out-
puts and improves the robustness of the enhancement process.
We term this approach Dictionary-Free Discrete Attention
(DFDA). In contrast to fixed dictionaries, DFDA learns a latent
dictionary representations codebook in an end-to-end manner,
allowing keys to cluster acoustically meaningful patterns and
values to produce context-aware filters for speech restoration.

2.3. The DFDA-based SE
TFGridNet [6] is a state-of-the-art speech enhancement sys-
tem, combining convolution layers with self-attention mecha-
nisms. The attention-centric design allows direct integration of
DFDA’s discretized key-value attention, while preserving time-
frequency convolution benefits. In this part, DFDA is integrated
into TFGridNet by replacing its standard attention block with
discrete key-value attention in a subset of its layers. To com-
prehensively evaluate the performance of DFDA and explore its
complementarity with another structured attention mechanism,
we also incorporate HAtt for comparison and combination ex-
periments.

3. Experimental Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
We used 25k hours of speech corpus with DNS noise [20] and
Room Impulse Response (RIR) used in [21] to train the speech
enhancement model. We randomly selected another 400 speech
utterances from the same speech corpus as the seen speech, and
400 speech utterances from Emilia [22] and LibriSpeech [23]
as the unseen speech, respectively.
All speech, noise and RIR signals were either originally at
16 kHz or down-sampled to 16 kHz. During the training stage,
reverberation was applied to the speech signal with a probability
of70%, after which the signal was corrupted by noise at SNR
level randomly chosen from−5dB to10dB. During testing,
reverberation application probability was set to50%.
For model testing, as shown in table 4, we used a 4-layer
TFGridNet [6] as the model baseline, with an embedding di-
mension of 32 and 96 hidden units in the BLSTM. Considering
that the structured codebook might particularly affect the en-
hanced speech quality in the latter stages of the model, we pro-
pose replacing part of the attention block with the DFDA mod-
ule. In this experiment, the first two layers use the DFDA block,
denoted as TFGridNet+DFDA. Moreover, we incorporated the
HAtt structure [5] at the beginning of TFGridNet block to ex-
plore the potential combination of DFDA with other attention
mechanisms. These variants are denoted as TFGridNet+HAtt
and TFGridNet+HAtt+DFDA, respectively.
The details of these four models are shown in Table 4,
where the number indicates the quantity of initial blocks where
the standard attention is replaced by the corresponding mod-
ule (DFDA or HAtt). Meanwhile, the reported Multiply-
Accumulate Operations (MACs) (measured on a 1-second au-
dio input) show that the proposed DFDA method is computa-
tionally efficient, adding only 0.48 GMACs of overhead to the
baseline model. The FSQ dimensions for keys and values were
set to 15 and 20, respectively, each with 3 levels. Additionally,
the hidden size of the linear layer in DFDA is 256. The weight-
ing factorsαandβin the loss function are set to 70 and 60,
respectively. The compression exponents of magnitudecandd
are 0.3 and 0.7, respectively.
Acoustic features were extracted using a 512-point STFT
with a 32 ms Hann window and a 16 ms hop size. We used the
AdamW optimizer (initial LR=10 −3,β 1 = 0.9,β 2 = 0.999)
with an exponential decay (γ= 0.99) and gradient clipping
(max norm 5.0).
We use PESQ [24], ESTOI [25], SISNR [26], SSNR, SDR
[27], DNSMOS [28], and speaker similarity [29] as perfor-
mance metrics. Furthermore, to specifically validate our hy-
pothesis regarding unvoiced speech recovery, we introduce an
Unvoiced Segmental SNR (UV SSNR) metric, which is based
on the classic time-domain heuristics [30] and Segmental SNR.

3.2. Comparison with TFGridNet Method
Tables 1-3 present a comparative evaluation of speech enhance-
ment performance, specifically designed to quantify the contri-
bution of the DFDA mechanism. The results are benchmarked
against the TFGridNet baseline and its variant with HAtt across
both seen and unseen speech datasets. On the seen speech
dataset, integrating DFDA improved the baseline TFGridNet
by a notable margin, with key gains of 0.09 in PESQ, 1.3%
in ESTOI and 0.52 dB in SISNR. Crucially, the DFDA mecha-
nism provides distinct and complementary benefits to the HAtt
module. This is evidenced by comparing the combined model
(TFGridNet+HAtt+DFDA) against the model with only HAtt.
In the seen dataset, the addition of DFDA to the HAtt model
resulted in further gains of 0.10 in PESQ, 1.4% in ESTOI, and
0.56 dB in SISNR. Similar improvements are observed on the
unseen datasets. For instance, on the LibriSpeech dataset (Table
2), the combined model surpasses the HAtt-only model with a
0.06 gain in PESQ and a 0.66 dB gain in SISNR, confirming the
robust and complementary benefits of DFDA.
Fig. 3 presents a qualitative comparison using a challenging
noisy signal characterized by strong reverberation and a promi-
nent noise burst. The input spectrogram reveals significant re-
verberant smearing and broadband noise that obscure the under-
lying phonetic details.

cels at capturing and restoring stochastic unvoiced consonants.
4. Conclusions
In this paper, a novel dictionary-free discrete attention module
was proposed. Specifically, the DFDA and HAtt modules are
adapted to the TFGridNet block. The DFDA and HAtt mod-
ules could both improve the harmonic structures, while DFDA
could also improve the unvoiced segments. The synergistic
combination of DFDA, HAtt, and TFGridNet attention could
significantly improve quality and intelligibility, especially for
unvoiced speech. For future work, we plan to investigate the
integration of our DFDA module into the backbone networks of
emerging generative speech enhancement models to potentially
achieve even higher restoration quality.
