Abstract
With the rapid advancement of Text-to-Speech (TTS) and V oice
Conversion (VC), forged speech has reached a highly realistic
level, posing serious threats to voice biometrics. Traditional
passive detection methods suffer from limited generalization
against unseen synthesis models.
In this paper, we propose a phoneme-aware active water-
marking defense built on the Mamba framework. Leveraging
a Dual-column Bidirectional State Space Model, the system
captures long-range dependencies and covertly embeds iden-
tity bitstrings into speech. To resist diffusion-based purification
attacks such as PhonePuRe, we introduce a phoneme-guided
embedding mechanism that tightly couples the watermark with
speech semantics, ensuring transfer to forged speech during
speaker adaptation. Experiments demonstrate effective source
tracing with ultra-low latency (about 1 s) and high True Pos-
itive Rate under strong purification attacks. Source code is
available athttps://github.com/Silence-ai423/
phoneme-aware-mamba-watermark.
Index Terms: speech watermarking, deepfake defense, state
space models, adversarial purification, voice cloning
1. Introduction
The proliferation of zero-shot voice cloning technologies, ex-
emplified by models like YourTTS [1] and sv2TTS [2], has
drastically lowered the barrier to generating realistic speech
deepfakes, leading to rising identity spoofing risks. The re-
search community has primarily relied on passive Audio Deep-
fake Detection (ADD) systems. While ADD frameworks using
pre-trained models such as wav2vec 2.0 [3], XLS-R [4], and
Conformer [5] have shown success on benchmark datasets, they
often fail to detect novel artifacts generated by emerging diffu-
sion models such as DiffWave [6] and AudioPure [7].
Recent advancements introduce perturbation-based ac-
tive defenses to disrupt deepfake generation, including An-
tiFake [8] and V oiceGuard [9]. However, studies such as De-
AntiFake [10] show that surface-level adversarial perturbations
are highly vulnerable. Algorithms like PhonePuRe [10] can ef-
fectively erase these signals, causing a collapse of the protec-
tion mechanism. This vulnerability highlights the need for a
“shadow-like” defense paradigm—a signal that persists as an
intrinsic timbre feature and transfers into forged samples even
after purification and cloning.
To bridge this gap, we propose an active watermark de-
fense system driven by a State Space Model (SSM) [11]. The
main contributions of this work are three-fold:♦We pioneer

2. Related Work
2.1. Audio Deepfake Detection (ADD)
Audio deepfake detection has progressed from handcrafted
acoustic features (e.g., MFCCs and prosodic cues) with shal-
low classifiers to end-to-end deep models. Architectures such
as RawNet2 [15] and AASIST [16] learn discriminative rep-
resentations directly from raw waveforms, significantly im-
proving performance. More recently, self-supervised mod-
els like XLS-R [4] and its Mamba-based variants, including
XLSR-Mamba [12] and XLSR-MamBo [13], leverage large-
scale pretraining to extract robust phonetic and timbral fea-
tures. Meanwhile, the Mamba architecture [11] enables effi-
cient long-sequence modeling with linear complexity, making it
well suited for capturing subtle and distributed audio artifacts.
2.2. Active Speech Protection and Watermarking
Active speech protection, often framed as proactive audio de-
fense, aims to prevent voice cloning or impersonation. Methods
such as AntiFake [8], V oiceGuard [9], and timbre watermarking
approaches [17] introduce imperceptible perturbations or adver-
sarial noise to disrupt cloning models. While effective against
voice synthesis attacks, these approaches generally do not em-
bed retrievable information, limiting traceability. Recent timbre
watermarking methods, such as AudioMarkNet [18], adopt a
data-poisoning strategy: watermark patterns are learned during
model adaptation, enabling retrieval of speaker-specific identi-
fiers. However, these methods remain fragile under advanced
purification techniques, particularly phoneme-conditioned de-
noising or spectral manipulation [10, 19].
2.3. Adversarial Purification
Adversarial purification techniques counteract active audio de-
fenses. For example, De-AntiFake [10] introduces PhonePuRe,
a two-stage purification pipeline combining wave-level dif-
fusion denoising inspired by DiffWave [6] with phoneme-

3.3. Phoneme-Guided Watermark Embedding
To improve robustness against phoneme-conditioned purifica-
tion, watermark perturbations are aligned with acoustically sta-
ble phoneme regions. Phoneme boundaries are obtained using
Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA) [14], from which we construct
a spectral stability maskM(f,t)within the 100–1000 Hz band,
corresponding to formant-dominated steady-state regions.
Watermark perturbations are applied as:
∆spec = ∆⊙M(f,t)·α wm,(8)
whereα wm controls watermark strength and⊙denotes
element-wise multiplication. By enhancing low-variance
phoneme interiors and suppressing transition frames, water-
mark energy is concentrated in acoustic subspaces that are more
likely to survive purification.

3.5. Anti-Purification Adversarial Training
To ensure watermark survivability under purification, the train-
ing process incorporates purification perturbations [10] within
the optimization loop. During training, purified samples are pe-
riodically introduced so that the encoder learns perturbations
resilient to diffusion-based and spectral refinement operations.
The overall objective function is defined as
Ltotal =λ wmLwm +λδLδ +λ reconLrecon +λ orgLorg +λ purLpur

whereLwm enforces accurate watermark retrieval,Lδ con-

sistency,L org penalizes false positives on clean speech, andLpur
explicitly encourages retrievability after purification. Joint opti-
mization of these objectives yields watermark perturbations that
are both imperceptible and robust.
3.6. Detection and Redundancy
The binary watermark is encoded using Hamming (6,3) error
correction [20], producing a 36-bit sequence from an original
16-bit code. This sequence is repeatedly embedded over 1-
second audio segments sampled at 16 kHz. During detection,
segments are decoded independently and aggregated via major-
ity voting:

4.4. Real-World Threat Simulation
We simulate an end-to-end voice cloning attack. Eleven VCTK
speakers are watermarked using the adversarially-trained model
(1 s chunk-wise embedding). An attacker fine-tunes YourTTS
and sv2TTS on the watermarked data, then generates 15 cloned
utterances per speaker (11×15=165total). The defender per-
forms chunk-wise decoding on the cloned speech, matches de-
coded bits against the watermark pool via Hamming similarity
(threshold 0.7), and applies majority voting across chunks for
source tracing. Table 2 demonstrates the system successfully
traces the origin of forged audio sources with nearly 100% ac-
curacy and extremely low latency of approximately 1.1 seconds
when confronted with YourTTS and sv2TTS high-fidelity voice
cloning attacks.

5. Conclusion
This paper presents a phoneme-aware active watermark defend-
ing system utilizing the Mamba architecture. By intertwin-
ing the watermark signal with phonetic steady states and em-
ploying an adversarial training paradigm against PhonePuRe,
the system achieves highly robust, imperceptible, and ultra-
low-latency traceability. Experimental results confirm that the
phoneme-aware Mamba encoder successfully preserves speaker
identity strings through complex TTS cloning pipelines, offer-
ing a pragmatic defense mechanism against emerging speech
deepfakes.
