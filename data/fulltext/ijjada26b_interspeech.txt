Abstract
We presentWaveNorm AGC, a lightweight, low-latency
neural Adaptive Gain Control(AGC) system for real-time
speech normalization on edge devices. Operating in the time
domain, it learns a content-aware, level-invariant gain mapping
directly from raw waveforms. Unlike conventional rule-based
AGC, it avoids fixed control parameters and hand-crafted en-
velope tracking, providing stable and responsive gain adjust-
ment. WaveNorm AGC provides robust performance across a
wide dynamic range upto−70dB maintaining loudness com-
pliance with ITU-T P.56 and P.79 standards under noisy con-
ditions, while achieving∼6 dB noise reduction . The model
is speaker-independent and highly efficient (49 M MACs, 55
KB of memory), making it suitable for real-time deployment on
resource-constrained devices.

1. Introduction
Real-world speech signals exhibit large amplitude variations
due to speaker distance, room acoustics, and device charac-
teristics. These fluctuations degrade performance in real-time
applications such as automatic speech recognition (ASR), tele-
conferencing, and hearing assistance. Signals that are too weak
reduce intelligibility, while overly strong signals cause clipping
and distortion. AGC addresses this by dynamically regulating
signal levels to maintain consistent and perceptually appropriate
loudness.
Traditional AGC methods rely on heuristic signal pro-
cessing techniques such as envelope tracking and fixed at-
tack–release time constants. These approaches are widely used
in real-time DSP systems such as WebRTC AGC [1] due to their
computational efficiency. However, they rely primarily on sig-
nal energy without distinguishing between speech and noise,
which often leads to artifacts such as delayed gain adaptation,
clipping, and amplification of background noise under dynamic
and noisy conditions.
Recent learning-based approaches[2, 3] enable data-driven
gain control by learning mappings from speech signals. How-
ever, many operate in the time–frequency domain, introducing
additional latency, which limits their suitability for real-time
edge deployment. In contrast, time-domain approaches pre-
serve waveform structure and enable fully causal processing,
making them more appropriate for real-time deployment.

In this work, we propose WaveNorm AGC, a lightweight,
real-time neural AGC model designed for edge devices. The
model operates entirely in the time domain and combines tem-
poral convolution with a GRU-based bottleneck to capture
multi-scale speech dynamics and ensure smooth gain adapta-
tion. It learns a robust mapping directly from raw waveforms,
enabling consistent performance across diverse acoustic condi-
tions. WaveNorm AGC jointly performs loudness normaliza-
tion by attenuating near-field speech and amplifying far-field
speech, while achieving approximately 6 dB noise reduction
and preserving speech naturalness.
2. Architecture
Fig. 1 illustrates the block diagram of the proposed WaveNorm
AGC model. The system performs real-time adaptive gain con-
trol in the time domain, producing loudness-normalized speech
while suppressing background noise. It is fully causal and de-
signed for low-latency deployment on resource-constrained de-
vices. The architecture consists of three components: an en-
coder, a GRU-based temporal bottleneck, and a decoder.
The encoder processes the raw input waveform and converts
it into a compact latent representation capturing short-term am-
plitude and energy variations. The input signal is segmented
using 20 ms frames with a 10 ms frame shift to enable stream-
ing operation. A stack of causal Conv1D layers with kernel
size 3 and dilation rates of 2, 4, and 8 is employed to capture
multi-scale temporal context. Grouped convolutions are used
to improve parameter efficiency and enable the model to learn
diverse amplitude patterns across channels. This design allows
the encoder to simultaneously model rapid transients and slower
envelope dynamics without increasing latency or model depth.
The encoded features are passed to a GRU-based tempo-
ral bottleneck, which models long-term dependencies and en-
forces smooth gain adaptation. The GRU has a hidden size of
32 and maintains temporal context of the input signal, enabling
stable gain transitions during sustained speech while remaining
responsive to sudden level changes. This temporal modeling
is critical to avoid artifacts such as pumping and abrupt gain
fluctuations. The GRU output is further refined using fully con-
nected layers with 32 units to ensure well-conditioned feature
representations.
The decoder reconstructs the output waveform from the
bottleneck features while implicitly applying the learned gain
correction. It consists of Transposed Conv1D layers that mir-
ror the encoder, using a kernel size of 3 and reversed dilation
rates of 8, 4, and 2, enabling progressive refinement of tempo-

3. Experimental Setup
The dataset is designed to cover diverse real-world acoustic and
gain conditions. An in-house corpus at 48 kHz is used, com-
prising clean, noisy, and noise-augmented speech across vary-
ing speakers and environments. DNS3 clean speech is seg-
mented into 10 s utterances and scaled to RMS levels from−10
to−70dB under fixed and time-varying gain. Additive noise
(stationary, non-stationary, impulsive, and mixed) is applied at
SNRs from−5to+20dB. Training samples are formed by
concatenating clean, noisy, and noise-only segments to simu-
late streaming conditions.
The model is trained using a hybrid loss combining time-
domain Mean Squared Error (MSE) with a frequency-domain
Multi-Resolution Spectral Loss (MRSL):
Ltotal = 0.5∗L M SE + 0.5∗L M RSL (1)
with MRSL computed using STFT sizes
128,256,512,1024,2048. Optimization uses Adam (10 −3)
with a ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler, trained for 200 epochs
with batch size 16 on NVIDIA A6000 GPUs.
4. Results
WaveNorm is evaluated for both standalone AGC and down-
stream tasks under unseen conditions at 48 kHz and 16 kHz.
For standalone AGC, attenuation is tested on noisy V oice-
Bank+DEMAND (RMS−15to−30dB) and amplification
on clean TIMIT (RMS−25to−45dB). Compared to We-
bRTC [1] and Carnival [2], which show input-dependent gain
and higher variance, WaveNorm produces level-invariant out-
puts with smooth gain transitions, achieving∼6 dB noise re-
duction, while maintaining Active Speech Level near−26dBov
and loudness between−26and−28LUFS, satisfying ITU-T
P.56 and P.79 standards [4].
For downstream evaluation on noise suppression, TIMIT
is mixed with unseen DNS3 noise and RIRs. As a front-end,
WaveNorm improves perceptual quality across DeepFilterNet2,
DTLN, and GTCRN, with up to+0.35NISQA gain.
5. Conclusion
The proposed model operates in real time with minimal algo-
rithmic latency, making it well-suited for streaming and always-
on speech applications. With a compact footprint of 49 M
MACs and 55 KB memory, the system is highly efficient and
designed for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices.
Across diverse evaluation scenarios, the model consistently
provides stable loudness normalization over a wide dynamic
range upto−70dB, while achieving approximately 6 dB noise
reduction in noisy conditions. It maintains compliance with
ITU-T P.56 and P.79 standards and avoids common artifacts
such as clipping and noise amplification observed in conven-
tional AGC systems. These results demonstrate the robustness
of the proposed approach under varying acoustic conditions,
including low-level inputs, high-energy speech, and dynamic
background noise.
Overall, WaveNorm AGC offers an effective combination
of low computational complexity, real-time performance, and
reliable gain control. Its efficiency and robustness make it well-
suited for deployment in practical applications such as telecon-
ferencing, speech communication systems, and edge-based au-
dio processing in real-world environments.
