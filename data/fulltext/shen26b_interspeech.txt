Margin-Mixup for Robust Cross-Lingual Speaker Verification

Abstract
Cross-lingual speaker verification suffers from significant per-
formance degradation due to language mismatch. To effectively
maintain stable speaker recognition across diverse languages,
we propose layer-selected latent cross-attention adapters (LaS-
LCA), a novel framework designed to adapt frozen large-
scale pre-trained models. Specifically, we strategically select
backbone layers to isolate speaker traits from linguistic noise.
Within the adapter, shared latent cross-attention and convolu-
tional modeling distill multi-layer features into a consistent,
language-independent latent space. To further mitigate overfit-
ting on limited cross-lingual data, we additionally incorporate
an embedding-level margin-mixup strategy during the training
phase. Experimental results on the TidyV oiceX benchmark
demonstrate that LaS-LCA achieves an EER of 1.40%, signif-
icantly outperforming baseline models and exhibiting superior
robustness across diverse linguistic conditions.

1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV) aims to determine whether an enroll-
ment utterance and a test utterance belong to the same identity.
Earlier systems typically relied on statistical modeling, whereas
modern SV systems learn discriminative speaker embeddings
with deep neural networks. The X-Vectors framework popu-
larized a DNN-based embedding extractor that maps variable-
length utterances into fixed-dimensional embeddings via statis-
tics pooling and speaker classification training [1, 2]. Build-
ing on this paradigm, CNN/ResNet-based encoders trained on
large-scale in-the-wild corpora such as V oxCeleb [3, 4] have
significantly enhanced robustness.
However, as model architectures evolve in complexity, the
demand for large-scale labeled datasets has become a bot-
tleneck [5]. To address this challenge, recent research has
shifted to utilizing self-supervised learning (SSL) models pre-
trained on unlabeled data. Serving as powerful front-end en-
coders, these pre-trained models (such as WavLM [6] and
w2v-BERT [7]) can extract rich, general-purpose acoustic fea-
tures. Subsequently, a back-end model is employed to aggre-
gate the extracted features to yield a robust speaker embed-
ding [8, 9, 10]. In this paradigm, the pre-trained SSL encoder
is typically frozen or partially fine-tuned, while a task-specific
back-end is trained to aggregate frame-level representations into
utterance-level speaker embeddings [11, 12].

While SSL-based SV has demonstrated strong performance
on V oxCeleb benchmarks [13], V oxCeleb is predominantly
English-centric and may not fully reflect multilingual and cross-
lingual evaluation conditions [14]. In cross-lingual speaker
verification, the enrollment and test utterances may be spo-
ken in different languages, a condition commonly referred to
as language mismatch [15, 16, 17]. To facilitate evaluation
under language mismatch, the TidyV oice2026 Challenge intro-
duces Tidy-X [18], a novel benchmark tailored for robust cross-
lingual speaker verification. This benchmark aims to foster the
development of language-independent technologies that remain
accurate regardless of the language spoken.
In this paper, we proposeLayer-SelectedLatentCross-
AttentionAdapters (LaS-LCA), a novel framework that adapts
frozen large-scale pre-trained models for robust cross-lingual
speaker verification. To mitigate the language mismatch
problem, our approach incorporates three key contributions.
First, inspired by the Partial Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation
(PMFA) strategy [8], we employ a strategic layer selection
(LaS) mechanism. Rather than aggregating all representations
from the w2v-BERT 2.0 backbone [10], our experiments on
the adopted w2v-BERT 2.0 backbone show that using layers
19–24 gives the best performance among the evaluated contigu-
ous layer groups. Second, we propose a lightweight adapter ar-
chitecture that integrates shared latent cross-attention with local
convolutional modeling. By querying multi-layer backbone fea-
tures against a unified, learnable latent array, LaS-LCA acts as a
structural bottleneck to distill speaker-relevant cues into a con-
sistent latent space. This unified feature is further refined by a
convolutional feed-forward block within each adapter to capture
local spectro-temporal dependencies, thereby improving robust-
ness against language variability. Third, to enhance generaliza-
tion and mitigate overfitting on the limited Tidy-X benchmark,
we incorporate an embedding-level margin-mixup [19] strategy.
By interpolating latent embeddings, this technique effectively
regularizes the decision boundary, ensuring robustness in cross-
lingual speaker verification.

2.2. Layer Selection
Although SSL models provide rich acoustic representations,
they are primarily designed as general-purpose encoders rather
than specialized extractors for speaker embeddings. Conse-
quently, the extracted representations contain a complex mix-
ture of speaker-discriminative traits and identity-irrelevant in-
formation. While the layer-wise evolution of SSL represen-
tations has been extensively studied [21, 22, 23], its specific
impact on cross-lingual speaker verification remains under-
explored. Simply integrating all layers via concatenation or av-
eraging tends to introduce substantial redundant information,
leading to performance degradation.
To address this, we adopt a selective-layer strategy [8] that
evaluates contiguous layer segments to identify an appropriate
feature group for speaker verification. Specifically, given an
SSL encoder withLlayers, we consider a candidate set of con-

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Dataset
The experiments are conducted using the TidyV oiceX dataset, a
partition of the Mozilla Common V oice corpus. We utilize the
TidyV oiceX training and development partitions. The dataset
contains 4,474 speakers across 40 languages, totaling 457 hours
of audio. Specifically, the training set comprises 370 hours of
data from 3,666 speakers, while the development set includes
87 hours from 808 speakers. Both subsets cover the same 40
languages, providing a multilingual environment for model op-
timization and hyperparameter tuning. For evaluation, we adopt
the TidyV oice 2026 trial lists [29]:tv26eval-A, which pairs
seen-language enrollment with unseen-language test utterances,
andtv26 eval-U, where both enrollment and test utterances
consist of 38 unseen languages.
3.2. Model configuration
All experiments are implemented using the WeSpeaker frame-
work [30]. To enhance system robustness, three data augmenta-
tion techniques are applied across all models: additive noise
from the MUSAN dataset [31], reverberation from the RIRs
dataset [32], and speed perturbation [33] by scaling the audio
speed to 0.9 and 1.1 times the original rate. For baseline sys-
tems, we follow the standard WeSpeaker pipeline, where each
speech sample is randomly cropped into 2-second segments.
Acoustic features are extracted as 80-dimensional filter banks
(FBanks) using a25ms window and10ms frame shift.
The backbone of the proposed system is the Meta-released
w2v-BERT 2.0 model, utilizing pre-trained weights further op-
timized on V oxCeleb2 and V oxBlink2 via the Adapter MFA
framework [10]. To match the architecture’s input require-
ments, adjacent 80-dimensional FBanks are concatenated into
160-dimensional vectors. The 1024-dimensional representa-
tions output by the backbone are projected to 128 dimensions,
which then interact with a latent array of size64×128through
a cross-attention mechanism.
During the training phase, the weights of the w2v-BERT
2.0 backbone are frozen, and only the parameters of the adapter
are updated. Models are trained for 30 epochs using the Addi-
tive Angular Margin Softmax (AAM-Softmax) loss with a mar-
gin of 0.2 and a scaling factor of 32. For the embedding-level
margin-mixup strategy, the mixup operation is applied with a
probability of 0.5, and the interpolation weightλis sampled
from aBeta(α,α)distribution withα= 0.2. All experiments
are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU.
4. Results and Discussion
4.1. Performance analysis
Table 1 presents a comparison between the proposed sys-
tems and the TidyV oiceX baseline. All configurations em-
ploying the w2v-BERT 2.0 frontend produce lower EER and
MinDCF values than the SimAM-ResNet34 (ASP) baseline,
which records an EER of 3.07%. This indicates that large-
scale self-supervised (SSL) representations offer improved ro-
bustness for cross-lingual speaker verification compared to con-
ventional supervised models.
The choice of backbone initialization is critical for down-
stream performance. Initializing w2v-BERT 2.0 with weights
pre-trained on speaker-specific corpora (V oxBlink2 and V ox-
Celeb2) yielded a substantial performance gain over SSL
weights, reducing the EER from approximately 2.1% to 1.6%.
This suggests that speaker-discriminative priors encoded in the
backbone provide a performance advantage that cannot be fully
compensated by the adapter alone during fine-tuning.
Regarding adapter variations, the proposed LaS-LCA
achieved the lowest EER when combined with embedding-level
margin-mixup (EER 1.40%, MinDCF 0.66). The latent cross-
attention mechanism performed on par with the MFA baseline,
while adding 1D convolution modules yielded further improve-
ments. These results suggest that augmenting global attention
with localized spectro-temporal processing can be beneficial for
speaker verification across varied languages.

In Table 3, we investigate a fine-grained ablation of layer
selections within the top block. The 6-layer configuration

a rich context to extract nuanced speaker characteristics while
keeping the adapter’s parameter budget efficient for training.
Table 4 reports results for kernel size and margin-mixup.
For the non-mixup setting, a kernel size of 5 yielded the lowest
MinDCF (0.65) and competitive EER (1.44%). When mixup
is applied, the EER decreases further to 1.40% for kernel size
3. These differences suggest that moderate temporal receptive
fields are sufficient for local modeling and that embedding-level
mixup offers complementary gains.

5. Conclusions
In this paper, we proposed LaS-LCA, a novel framework de-
signed to mitigate language mismatch in cross-lingual speaker
verification. Our study demonstrates that rather than utiliz-
ing the entire depth of a pre-trained SSL model, focusing on
specific upper-layer representations provides a cleaner signal
for speaker identity. To effectively process these features, we
employed a shared latent cross-attention mechanism with local
convolutional refinement, mapping diverse multilingual inputs
into a consistent, language-independent embedding space. This
architectural design is further complemented by an embedding-
level margin-mixup strategy, which enhances the model’s ro-
bustness against limited data diversity. Achieving an EER
of 1.40% on the TidyV oiceX benchmark, our results suggest
that layer selection and adapter-based aggregation can improve
cross-lingual speaker verification with a frozen SSL backbone.
