Abstract
Speech recordings in regulated domains cannot be shared or
reused for AI development without suppressing two indepen-
dent re-identification channels: biometric speaker identity and
linguistically identifying content. Existing approaches address
each channel in isolation and require full re-synthesis, sacrific-
ing the in-domain acoustic characteristics that make recordings
valuable. We propose a unified acoustic token-space frame-
work that jointly anonymizes both channels via per-frame ad-
mixture of encoder-derived and phoneme-conditioned tokens,
with NER-triggered span replacement preserving surrounding
prosody. On the V oicePrivacy 2024 benchmark the system
achieves EER 42.54%, within one point of the challenge top
submission, with WER 3.73%.
Index Terms: speaker anonymization, speech privacy, voice
conversion, content privacy, speech synthesis, speech editing
1. Introduction
Speech recordings accumulate at scale in healthcare, legal pro-
ceedings, and enterprise telephony, carrying in-domain vocabu-
lary, acoustics, and speaking style that is uniquely valuable for
training domain-adapted automatic speech recognition (ASR)
systems and conversational analytics. Yet reuse creates compli-
ance risk: recordings expose personally identifiable information
through two independent re-identification channels, raising bar-
riers under the GDPR, HIPAA, PIPEDA, and sector-specific
regulations.
The first channel isbiometric. V ocal tract geometry, residual
prosodic habits, and energy distribution encode speaker identity
in features exploited by automatic speaker verification (ASV) [1].
Speaker anonymization addresses this by disentangling linguis-
tic content from identity before resynthesis. Signal-processing
methods such as McAdams coefficient shifting [2] are training-
free but weak against informed attackers [3]. Deep voice con-
version (VC) [4, 5] achieves stronger anonymization at greater
computational cost. Unlike training-free signal-processing meth-
ods (limited control) or entangled voice-conversion features,
RVQ offers a discrete, hierarchically factorized representation
whose staged residuals expose separable acoustic factors for
controllable anonymization [ 6, 7]. The V oicePrivacy Chal-
lenge (VPC) standardises evaluation through jointly measuring
privacy through ASV equal error rate (EER), utility through
ASR word error rate (WER), and emotion preservation through
unweighted average recall (UAR) [8].
The second channel islinguistic. V ocabulary, syntax,
function-word distributions, and named entities form a stylo-
metric fingerprint that persists in transcribed content, enabling
authorship attribution even after the acoustic signal has been fully
re-synthesised [9]. Attribution accuracy scales with transcript
volume, making long-form enterprise corpora particularly vulner-
able [10]. Within the speech community, stylometric obfuscation
of transcript content has been shown to reduce author identifica-
tion accuracy from 70% to 24% via paraphrasing [11], yet this
approach operates at text level and requires full TTS re-synthesis
to produce anonymised speech output. To our knowledge, no
existing speaker anonymization system jointly addresses both
channels in a unified framework; the acoustic and linguistic
re-identification risks have largely been treated as separate prob-
lems [12].
Content privacy has been studied independently through
three paradigms [13, 14]. Phone-code masking [15] makes sensi-
tive spans unintelligible, disqualifying the output for downstream
ASR or analytics. Named entity recognition (NER)-based substi-
tution [16] preserves intelligibility within replaced spans but is
bounded by NER recall and introduces acoustic discontinuities
at edited boundaries. Large language model (LLM) transcript
paraphrasing [17] provides the broadest content coverage, yet all
three paradigms ultimately require full text-to-speech (TTS) re-
synthesis, discarding the original acoustic character. At utterance
level, the JHU HLTCOE system [18] improved the EER-UAR
trade-off by stochastically routing between a VC system and
cascaded ASR-TTS; however, routing is a binary, utterance-wide
decision that still re-synthesises every frame and offers no mech-
anism for span-level content editing. The critical gap shared
by all prior work is that the output is not usable as in-domain
training data: re-synthesis erases the prosodic rhythm and voice
quality of the original recording.
We address this gap with a pipeline that operates within a
single acoustic token space, editing only the frames that require
modification and leaving the surrounding signal intact. The
system makes three contributions. First,NER-triggered frame-
level token replacement: sensitive spans are re-generated from
phoneme features and spliced back into the token stream without
touching surrounding frames, avoiding both full re-synthesis
and acoustic discontinuities at boundaries. Second,dual-stream
RVQ admixture for joint anonymization: encoder-derived and
phoneme-conditioned token streams are blended per-frame by
a scalarβ, jointly suppressing biometric identity and residual
stylometric fingerprints within a single synthesis stack. Third,
cosine similarity gatingrejects hallucinated phoneme-derived to-
kens, preserving intelligibility on noisy in-domain speech where
forced alignment is imperfect.1

3. Experiments
3.1. Data
We use the standard V oicePrivacy 2024 corpus as our primary
evaluation benchmark [26]. Training data for the speaker veri-
fication adversary consists of thelibri-train-clean-360split of
LibriSpeech [27], comprising 360 hours of read speech from
921 speakers. ASR utility is evaluated onlibri-dev-asrandlibri-
test-asr; speaker privacy is evaluated across four trial sets —
libri-dev-trials-f,libri-dev-trials-m,libri-test-trials-f, andlibri-
test-trials-m— with corresponding enrolment sets. Emotion
utility is evaluated on the IEMOCAP [28] development and test
partitions supplied with the challenge.
The raw training data for our T5 and BigVGAN mod-
els spans English and French. English sources include Com-
mon V oice 17 [29], LibriTTS (train-clean-100) [30], V oxPop-
uli [31], and VCTK [ 32]. French sources comprise Common
V oice 17, Multilingual LibriSpeech, V oxPopuli, and V oxForge.
Two filtering policies are applied: aMOS policy(wv-mos >3.3,
speaker duration <10 min) for diversity-critical components,
and aspeaker policy(10 min–5 hr per speaker) for the T5 and
BigVGAN models requiring robust per-speaker coverage.
Pseudospeaker embeddings are drawn from a pool of speak-
ers held out from all evaluation sets.
For the speech editing evaluation, we construct a test set of
213 utterances sampled fromlibri-test-asr, selecting sentences
containing at least one named entity of typePERSONorLO-
CATIONas identified by GLiNER [ 33]. Entity boundaries are
verified against force-aligned phoneme transcriptions.
3.2. Training
We introduce the RVQ-Whisper tokens described in Sec. 2.1.1 to
extract the linguistic information. The phoneme-conditioned T5
transformer is trained for 100 K iterations onlibri-train-clean-
360using the AdamW optimiser with a peak learning rate of
3×10−4 and batch size 16. BigVGAN is trained from scratch
for 800 K iterations (batch size 12, Adam β1=0.8, β2=0.99,
lr 10−4 with exponential decay γ=0.999996) to condition on
RVQ tokens, pseudospeaker x-vectors, and a log-F0 contour; all
other architectural choices follow the original implementation.
3.3. Evaluation Protocol
Privacy is measured by EER under the semi-informed attacker
protocol of VPC 2024 [ 26], using an ECAPA-TDNN ASV
model [34] re-trained on anonymised speech; higher EER (ceil-
ing 50 %) indicates stronger anonymisation. Linguistic utility
is measured by word error rate (WER) on thelibri-dev/test-asr
sets using Whisper medium.en [21]. Emotion utility is mea-
sured by unweighted average recall (UAR) on IEMOCAP using
the challenge’s pre-trained speech emotion recognition (SER)
model. Naturalness is assessed via an automatic mean opinion
score (MOS) predictor (wv-mos [35]). Speaker consistency (Edit
Sim) in the speech editing task is measured by the cosine simi-
larity between x-vector embeddings of the anonymised source
utterance and the NER-edited output.
3.4. Baseline Systems
We compare against two published systems.T12-5is the top-
ranked VPC 2024 submission (EER 43.23%), included as a
strong upper bound on speaker anonymisation under full re-
synthesis.HLTCOE[ 18] is the JHU HLTCOE utterance-level
admixture system, the closest architectural antecedent to our
work; it routes each utterance at probability p between a cas-
caded Whisper-VITS system and a kNN-VC system, and we re-
port its best published operating point atp= 0.4 (EER 40.81%,
UAR 47.09%, WER 3.33%).
All baselines are run with publicly available code and model

4. Results
4.1. V oicePrivacy 2024 Benchmark
Table 1 reports EER, WER, and UAR for our system across three
β values, alongside the baseline systems. Numbers are averages
over the female and male trial sets unless otherwise noted.
Our system achieves EER 42.54 % atβ= 0.7 , within one
point of the VPC top submission (43.23 %) while operating
entirely in a unified token space without secondary inference
passes. The WER of 3.73 % is within 0.4 points absolute of the
HLTCOE admixture system (3.33 %), confirming that operating
in a unified token space does not materially degrade intelligibil-
ity relative to the strongest comparable baseline. Table 1 also
reports two lower-β operating points without F0 transformation:
atβ= 0.0 the system reduces to pseudospeaker substitution
alone (EER 23.0 %), and at β= 0.3 partial admixture raises
EER to 30.0 % with WER remaining below 3 %.
UAR of 40.11 % is lower than the HLTCOE admixture sys-
tem (47.09 %), which preserves more emotional content by rout-
ing a fraction of utterances through voice conversion rather than
synthesis. This reflects a known tradeoff in token-prediction-
based systems: the phoneme-conditioned T5 generates tokens
consistent with the target text but does not model emotional
prosody, which is instead carried through the pseudospeaker em-
bedding and transformedF0. The full system scores MOS 3.84
(wv-mos) on the editing test set, with the admixture-only config-
uration scoring 3.91, indicating that span-level NER replacement
introduces only a modest naturalness cost.

EER–WER–UAR tradeoff governed byβ. EER rises monotoni-

4.2. Speech Editing Evaluation
Table 2 reports results on the speech editing evaluation set de-
scribed in Sec. 3.1, comparing the full system (NER replacement
+β-admixture) against an ablation that applies admixture only,
without NER-triggered span replacement.
The admixture-only system achieves anonymization sim-
ilarity 0.123 against the original speaker, confirming that β-
admixture alone effectively suppresses biometric identity. The
full system edit similarity of 0.959 confirms that NER-triggered
replacement preserves pseudospeaker consistency across edited
spans: the vocoder, conditioned on the same pseudospeaker em-
bedding throughout, produces a perceptually continuous output
even when the token stream switches source at entity bound-
aries. The MOS of 3.84 represents a modest 0.07-point decline
relative to the non-edited output (3.91), attributable to residual
boundary artefacts at frame-level transitions despite overlap-add
smoothing. The WER increase from 10.8% (admixture only) to
11.9% (full system) reflects the expected cost of span-level token
substitution on named entities.
4.3. Ablation: NER Coverage and Gating
The three components contribute distinct and complementary
roles. NER-triggered replacement targets explicit identifying en-
tities thatβ-admixture alone cannot suppress, since entity spans
may be reproduced verbatim even at high admixture rates when
the forced aligner assigns them high cosine similarity scores.
The admixture mechanism addresses the residual stylometric
fingerprint that persists between named entity spans. The cosine
similarity gate decouples anonymization strength from intelligi-
bility: without it, forced-aligner errors at highβ would propagate
hallucinated tokens directly into the vocoder, degrading WER
without benefit to privacy.
5. Conclusion
We presented a joint speaker and content anonymization frame-
work operating within a unified acoustic token space. NER-
triggered token replacement, per-frameβ-admixture, and cosine
similarity gating work in concert to suppress both biometric
identity and stylometric fingerprints without full re-synthesis.
On V oicePrivacy 2024 the system achieves EER 42.54 % and
WER 3.73 % at β= 0.7 , within one EER point of the chal-
lenge top submission, with MOS 3.84 and edit consistency 0.959
across NER-replaced spans. The primary limitation is UAR
(40.11 %): future work should explore prosodic conditioning on
source-utterance targets to recover emotion utility, and extend
NER coverage to discourse-level stylometric cues via LLM-
based paraphrase reranking.
