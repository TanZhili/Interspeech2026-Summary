An Investigation of Combining Geometric and Consistency Constraints for
Phase Estimation in Speech Enhancement

Abstract
We propose a novel iterative phase estimation framework,
termed the multi-source Griffin-Lim algorithm (MSGLA), for
speech enhancement under additive noise. The key idea is to
exploit the consistency constraint of complex short-time Fourier
transform (STFT) spectrograms to resolve the sign ambiguity
inherent in geometry-based phase estimation. We further in-
troduce a geometric formulation based on the law of sines and
cosines, enabling phase reconstruction using noise phase es-
timates. Oracle experiments validate the effectiveness of the
proposed approach under ideal conditions. Evaluations on VB-
DMD and WSJ0-CHiME3 show that MSGLA variants match or
slightly outperform existing methods, including direct phase es-
timation and DNN-based sign prediction, particularly in back-
ground noise suppression.

1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) aims at improving speech quality and
intelligibility by suppressing interfering additive noise. While
early approaches primarily concentrated on estimating the mag-
nitude of the target spectrogram [1–7], recent research has in-
creasingly emphasized the importance of accurate phase esti-
mation [8–10] in improving perceptual quality. In response,
a variety of model architectures and phase loss functions have
been proposed to enhance phase estimation [11–16]. However,
estimating phase accurately in noisy environments remains a
challenging task [17–20].
A notable strategy in phase estimation for speech enhance-
ment and separation is to leverage geometric constraints [21–
24]. By the law of cosines [25], the absolute phase differ-
ence between sources can be computed if their magnitudes are
known, reducing phase estimation to a binary decision: adding
or subtracting this difference from the noisy phase. One major
challenge in this framework lies in resolving the sign ambigu-
ity of the phase difference, as phase spectrograms lack clear
structure. To mitigate this, prior work has incorporated signal
processing constraints such as group delay and instantaneous
frequency, particularly in harmonic regions [21, 24]. More re-
cently, [22] proposed a sign predictor based on deep neural net-
work (DNN), trained to select the correct sign. However, this
approach suffers from the inherent randomness and low pre-
dictability of the sign targets [26], which can potentially result
in an even larger phase deviation when the sign is misclassified.
To address this, we propose the multi-source Griffin-Lim
algorithm (MSGLA), which exploits the consistency of both

GLA iteratively refines phases but alternates updates between
speech and noise. The first variant, noise magnitude-based MS-
GLA (NM-MSGLA), employs a DNN to estimate both speech
and noise magnitude spectrograms, followed by iterative phase
updates that converge to valid candidates under geometric con-
straints. This approach is conceptually related to the multi-
ple input spectrogram inversion (MISI) algorithm [29]. How-
ever, in contrast to MISI, which was restricted to cases with
either ground-truth magnitudes or Gaussian noise corruption,
our work is rooted in geometric-constraint-based phase estima-
tion and specifically addresses errors arising from DNN mag-
nitude estimation. A more recent study [30] extends MISI to
DNN-based speech enhancement, but its evaluation is limited
to signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), a metric misaligned with speech
perceptual quality.
To go beyond the conventional noise-magnitude formula-
tion, we further introduce a second variant: noise-phase based
MSGLA (NP-MSGLA). It utilizes noise phase estimates to re-
construct the speech phase via the law of sines [25], whose for-
mal formulation is shown in Section 3.2. To the best of our
knowledge, this is the first formulation of its kind. The moti-
vation comes from our empirical observation that noise phase
is often easier to estimate in low-energy regions where speech
phase is unreliable, suggesting that noise phase can provide a
complementary cue for accurate reconstruction.
We first validate the feasibility of both NM- and NP-
MSGLA variants through oracle experiments, where ground-
truth speech or noise spectrogram information are available for
phase updates. Results show that when either noise magnitude
or noise phase is accessible, MSGLA can accurately reconstruct
the phase, closely matching the ground truth. After establishing
the theoretical performance limit, we evaluate the method in
real-world SE tasks using estimated spectrograms on the VB-
DMD and WSJ0-CHiME3 data sets. Our results demonstrate
that MSGLA consistently matches or outperforms direct phase
estimation and shows superior performances compared with the
DNN-based sign predictor [22], particularly in background sup-
pression metrics. These findings underscore the effectiveness of
MSGLA in leveraging geometry insights and consistency con-
straints, indicating its strong potential as an effective framework
for accurate phase estimation in speech enhancement applica-
tions with additive noise.

gorithm (MSGLA) alternates GLA-style updates between the

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Data Sets
Two well-known SE benchmark data sets, the V oiceBank-
DEMAND (VB-DMD) [31] and WSJ0-CHiME3 [32], are
adopted for our experiments. For the VB-DMD data set, we
followed the commonly used training, validation, and testing
splits as detailed in [33], while for the WSJ0-CHiME3 data set
was prepared according to the instructions in [32]. TheRemix
andBandMaskdata augmentation methods proposed in [34] are
adopted. Our evaluation metrics include wide-band perceptual
evaluation of speech quality (PESQ) [35], extended short-term
objective intelligibility (ESTOI) [36], scale-invariant signal-to-
noise ratio (SI-SNR) [37]. and prediction of rating of back-
ground distortion (CBAK) [38].
4.2. Model Architecture
We use TF-GridNet [15] as the backbone model for all experi-
ments, adopting the architecture and hyperparameters from [15]
withD= 64,H= 128,I=J= 1, andB= 3. We
exclude the global attention layer, which results in about 1.3
million trainable parameters.

estimate the speech magnitude ˆAX, and the other to estimate

5. Experimental Results and Analysis
5.1. Phase Reconstruction with Oracle Noise Information
We first evaluate the effectiveness of NM-MSGLA and NP-
MSGLA using either oracle or estimated versions ofA X,A Z,
andP Z for speech phase reconstruction on the VB-DMD set.
Each method runs for 5 iterations, initialized with the noisy
phaseP Y . The resulting phase estimates are evaluated by their
average cosine similarity with the ground truthP X, and are
also combined with the estimated speech magnitude ˆAX for
standard speech quality metrics.
Table 1a presents the results for NM-MSGLA. As expected,
using oracle speech and noise magnitudes yields the best perfor-
mance, achieving a 0.87 cosine similarity with the ground truth
phase. Interestingly, we can observed strong results when the
oracle noise magnitude is used, with an SI-NSR of 21.02, and
a cosine similarity equal to 0.74. Moreover, the comparison of
the results obtained with only noise oracle magnitude and only
speech oracle magnitudes show that noise magnitude plays a
more important role in phase reconstruction. This highlights the
critical role of accurate noise magnitude in phase reconstruc-
tion. Finally, a performance drop is reported when both magni-
tudes are estimated, with a 0.09 degradation in PESQ compared
to the case with oracle speech magnitude.
Similar trends are observed in Table 1b for NP-MSGL.
With oracle speech magnitude and noise phase, the method
achieves 3.55 PESQ and 0.78 cosine similarity. Replacing
speech magnitude with its estimated counterpart results in mi-
nor drops in SI-SNR and similarity, but a more noticeable PESQ
decrease to 3.43. When both inputs are estimated, SI-SNR and
cosine similarity remain stable, though PESQ drops by 0.1 com-
pared to the oracle speech magnitude case. These oracle exper-
iments confirm that the proposed MSGLA variants—by using
both geometric and consistency constraints to provide a viable
framework for phase reconstruction.

phase estimation in SE.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a multi-source Griffin-Lim algorithm
(MSGLA), a novel approach to phase estimation for speech en-
hancement that unifies geometry and consistency constraints.
Unlike prior techniques that rely solely on magnitude-based
geometry formulations or direct phase regression, MSGLA it-
eratively refines phase estimates through alternating GLA-like
updates. Furthermore, we introduce NM-MSGLA and NP-
MSGLA, which, for the first time, leverages noise phase esti-
mates and the law of sines and cosines to derive valid speech
phase candidates. Experimental results under both oracle and
blind settings validate the effectiveness of our proposed tech-
niques, showing competitive or superior performance compared
to direct phase estimation and DNN-based sign prediction.
