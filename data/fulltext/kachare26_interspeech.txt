Abstract
Speech enhancement is widely used to improve speech intelligi-
bility in noisy conditions, while speech is increasingly investi-
gated as a non-invasive modality for neurological applications.
In telehealth and home-based assessments, speech enhancement
is used as a preprocessing step. Its effects on clinically rele-
vant speech biomarkers remain poorly understood. This demon-
stration presents an interactive system that allows users to ex-
plore the effect of speech enhancement methods on neurologi-
cal voice biomarkers and a downstream risk indicator. Simulat-
ing a noisy environment or using noisy speech input, multiple
enhancement methods are compared through spectrogram visu-
alizations, quantitative biomarker changes, and corresponding
shifts in a lightweight, interpretable risk score. The demo high-
lights that speech intelligibility improvement does not necessar-
ily imply biomarker preservation, and it motivates a clinically
informed design for future enhancements.
Index Terms: speech enhancement, speech biomarkers, speech
for health, trustworthy AI
1. General Description
Speech enhancement and speech-based health analysis are
widely studied but are typically addressed independently, de-
spite frequent interaction in real-world applications. Most en-
hancement methods are designed to improve perceived speech
quality or downstream recognition performance, without ex-
plicit consideration of health-oriented signal characteristics.
However, speech analysis for neurological applications focuses
on temporal, prosodic, and spectral patterns associated with
motor and cognitive control. In practical deployment scenar-
ios—including telehealth, mobile-based speech applications,
and home-based monitoring—speech is frequently recorded in
noisy environments and then enhanced before analysis.
The objective of this ClinAware demonstration is to sys-
tematically examine the interactions between commonly used
enhancement methods and neurological voice biomarkers under
controlled conditions. While enhancement improved intelligi-
bility, it may also alter acoustic patterns relevant to neurologi-
cal assessment, potentially affecting downstream interpretation.
Such effects are not always evident without parallel inspection
of signal representations and derived features. The proposed
system enables comparison of enhancement outputs, allowing
users to observe how design choices influence both acoustic
structure and downstream biomarker behavior. The system is
not intended for clinical diagnosis; rather, it serves as a proof-
of-concept to study the sensitivity of speech biomarkers to sig-

2.2. Speech Biomarkers
The system extracts a small set of interpretable biomarkers, us-
ing Python-based Librosa library [1] motivated by prior work
on neurological speech analysis [2, 3]:
•Pause ratio: Indicating hesitation and speech activity.
•Pitch mean: Reflecting vocal tension.
•Pitch standard deviation: Capturing prosodic variability.
•Spectral centroid standard deviation: Associated with ar-
ticulatory consistency and spectral stability.
•Jitter: Representing instability in vocal fold vibrations.
•Shimmer: Reflecting fluctuations invocal intensity.
•Speaking rate: Indicating articulation speecd and fluency.
These features are commonly reported to change in neuro-
logical conditions such as Parkinson’s disease and ALS, and are
also known to be sensitive to signal-processing operations.

3. Workflow and Implementation
Figure 1 illustrates the controlled experiment setup, where the
same noisy speech input is processed by multiple enhancement
strategies, a conservative spectral-gating baseline, an aggres-
sive smoothing approach, and a pretrained neural model (DE-
MUCS) [5], to isolate the effect of enhancement on downstream
features. For each signal condition, biomarkers are extracted
before and after enhancement, enabling direct comparison of
feature drift attributable solely to enhancement. The system
is modular and supports additional acoustic, linguistic, or ar-
ticulatory features, enabling extension to richer biomarkers. It
uses a FastAPI backend and a lightweight browser interface.
All processing runs offline on a standard laptop. Users can up-
load speech, listen to clean, noisy, and enhanced audio, inspect
spectrograms, and observe numerical biomarker shifts and cor-
responding risk changes in real time.
Figure 2 shows different speech enhancement strategies
are applied to the same noisy utterance can lead to substan-
tially different feature and risk outcomes, despite comparable
perceptual improvements. The baseline method maintains a
spectro-temporal structure similar to the noisy reference, with
only moderate increases in pitch variability and spectral dis-
persion, resulting in a limited risk score rise. The aggressive
method produces a visibly smoother spectrogram and reduced
fine-scale pitch variability, but introduces increased spectral in-
stability, contributing to a moderate risk shift. The DEMUCS
amplifies pitch dynamics and temporal energy variations, lead-
ing to a correspondingly large increase in the risk indicator.

4. Discussion and Conclusion
This demonstration presents an interactive framework for ex-
amining how speech enhancement affects neurological voice
biomarkers under realistic acoustic conditions. By applying
different enhancement methods to an identical noisy speech
sample, the system allows users to directly observe how sig-
nal processing choices affect spectro-temporal patterns, de-
rived biomarkers, and subsequent risk indicators. The obser-
vations suggest that improvements in perceptual clarity do not
necessarily translate to stability of health-relevant speech fea-
tures. By making the effects of enhancement transparent and
comparable, the demo facilitates more informed assessment of
noise-reduction strategies. The framework highlights the need
for clinically informed evaluation of speech enhancement and
provides insights for designing robust processing pipelines for
telehealth and speech-based monitoring applications.
