Abstract
Packet loss concealment (PLC) restores high-fidelity speech
from impaired signals in real-time communication. Existing
frequency-domain methods typically process real and imag-
inary spectra jointly. This coupled modeling overlooks the
distinct wrapped phase and magnitude characteristics, caus-
ing phase misalignment and structural artifacts. We pro-
pose a Wrapped-Phase-Aware Decoupled Dual-Stream Net-
work (DDSN) that explicitly separates magnitude and phase
modeling. Using independent streams, DDSN adapts to the spe-
cific characteristics of spectral energy and temporal alignment.
To facilitate interaction, our Scaled Asymmetric Residual Guid-
ance (SARG) mechanism uses magnitude priors to guide phase
reconstruction. A manifold-constrained trigonometric repre-
sentation maintains the topological consistency of the phase.
Experimental results demonstrate DDSN performs favorably
against established baselines, maintaining stable reconstruction
during long burst losses.

Stream Network, Phase Reconstruction, Burst Packet Loss
1. Introduction
Transmitting high-fidelity audio over wireless networks fre-
quently encounters issues like packet loss and jitter, which de-
grade speech quality in real-time communication. To mitigate
these impairments, packet loss concealment (PLC) is deployed
to reconstruct the missing speech segments. Strict algorithmic
latency constraints in V oice over IP systems limit access to fu-
ture frames, forcing models to rely heavily on historical rep-
resentations. This task is particularly challenging during long
burst losses, where continuous missing frames break short-term
dependencies, requiring models to leverage global context to
prevent discontinuous artifacts.
Early time-domain PLC methods [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8] en-
sure waveform continuity but struggle to model long-sequence
contexts and recover fine spectral details without prohibitively
large receptive fields. This limitation prompted a shift toward
spectral-domain modeling, including time-frequency GANs [9,
10, 11, 12] and models operating directly on concatenated real
and imaginary spectra [13, 14, 15].
Despite their success, joint real-imaginary processing over-
looks distinct signal characteristics; magnitude exhibits struc-
tured harmonics, whereas phase governs temporal alignment
with pseudo-random variations. Simultaneous optimization
thus biases networks toward easily converging magnitude fea-
tures [16], degrading phase recovery. Although previous
dual-stream networks like PHASEN [16] introduce amplitude-
to-phase communication, they lack causal streaming con-
straints. Under continuous burst losses, such feature modulation
severely attenuates phase details in lost or low-energy regions,
disrupting complex-plane topology and waveform coherence.
In this paper, we propose a Wrapped-Phase-Aware DDSN
for speech PLC. DDSN explicitly models magnitude and phase
through independent streams tailored to their specific character-
istics. To enable effective interaction without compromising the
phase structure, we introduce a SARG module, which uses the
predicted magnitude as a prior to guide phase reconstruction via
an additive residual connection. The main contributions of this
work are summarized as follows:
• We design a decoupled dual-stream architecture that sepa-
rately models spectral energy and temporal alignment. A
shared Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) bottleneck
is incorporated to capture global context, which is crucial for
handling missing information during long burst losses.
• We propose the SARG mechanism for asymmetric feature in-
teraction. By using a residual strategy(1+α·Mask), SARG
leverages magnitude priors to refine phase details while al-
lowing the original phase features to propagate without un-
natural attenuation.
• We utilize a continuous trigonometric representation
(cosθ,sinθ)for the phase, combined with a unit-norm con-
straint, to address the phase wrapping problem. Additionally,
applying power-law compression to the magnitude stream
enhances the recovery of low-energy speech components.

2.2. Wrapped-Phase-Aware Decoupled Architecture
As shown in Figure 1, we propose a decoupled dual-stream net-
work. Unlike conventional complex-domain models that project
magnitude and phase into a unified space, our dual-stream U-
Net architecture explicitly separates feature extraction to enable
independent modeling.
The parallel encoders,E mag andE phase, are constructed
by stacking Dense Encoder Blocks to facilitate feature reuse.
Tailored to their distinct structural attributes,E mag takes the
single-channel log-magnitude spectrum, whileEphase takes the
two-channel trigonometric map(cosθ,sinθ). Both encoders
utilize causal 2D convolutions with a stride of(1,2)to com-
press the frequency dimension while preserving temporal reso-
lution. We employ asymmetric kernel sizes: a local(3,3)ker-
nel for the magnitude stream to capture harmonic structures,
and a frequency-extended(3,5)kernel for the phase stream to
model the wider spectral dependencies required for group delay
estimation.
To address the potential lack of correlation caused by de-
coupling, a shared TCN bottleneck captures global temporal
context across both streams. The TCN employs stacked causal
depthwise separable convolutions (k= 3). The exponentially
increasing dilation factor (d= 2 k) expands the temporal re-
ceptive field, ensuring temporal alignment between the recon-
structed magnitude and phase.
The decodersD mag andD phase, composed of Dense De-
coder Blocks, reconstruct the respective magnitude and phase
estimates using concatenated features from the TCN and corre-
sponding encoder skip connections.
2.3. Scaled Asymmetric Residual Guidance
Phase recovery lacks structural constraints compared to the
magnitude spectrum. We propose the SARG mechanism to es-
tablish a unidirectional dependency, utilizing high-confidence
magnitude priors to guide the recovery of fine phase details.
Traditional Sigmoid gating fusion often truncates phase fea-
tures in low-energy or low signal-to-noise ratio regions as acti-
vation values approach zero, disrupting the continuous topolog-
ical structure on the complex plane. Instead of standard multi-
plicative gating, we model the interaction as a scaled residual
modulation:

2.4. Manifold-Constrained Trigonometric Representation
To ensure the phase-topological consistency of the dual-stream
network output, we construct a three-channel representation
that maps the prediction targets into a constrained wrapped
phase space. High-energy vowels in the speech magnitude spec-
trum tend to dominate the gradients of the loss function, causing
the network to ignore low-energy consonant details.
To mitigate this imbalance, we employ a power-law-based
perceptual compression strategy. The magnitude spectrumAis
compressed to|A| β (β= 0.5), simulating the nonlinear loud-
ness perception of the human ear. Since natural speech ex-
hibits a pronounced energy roll-off across the frequency axis,
this compression increases the relative weight of weak signals,
ensuring that high-frequency acoustic details are accurately re-
constructed.
Directly predicting the phase angleθ∈[−π,π)leads to
the phase wrapping problem; althoughπand−πdiffer signif-
icantly numerically, they represent the identical wrapped phase
state on the unit circle. This discontinuity can hinder gradi-
ent descent. Representing the phase as a continuous bivariate
vector(cosθ,sinθ)bypasses this issue and transforms phase
estimation into a smooth regression problem. During training,
L2 normalization maintains gradient stability. During infer-
ence, Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) orthogonalization
projects the predicted vectors onto the valid trigonometric man-
ifold. This projection is least-squares optimal for filtering resid-
ual estimation noise. To satisfy strict latency constraints, we
avoid standard iterative SVD solvers; instead, the2×2SVD is
computed analytically via closed-form quadratic roots and ba-
sic trigonometric identities. This deterministic implementation
avoids iterative overhead and accelerates real-time waveform
synthesis.
2.5. Loss Functions
To balance objective reconstruction fidelity with subjective per-
ceptual quality, we design a composite loss function,L total,
consisting of manifold constraint, multi-resolution frequency
reconstruction, phase consistency, and perceptual loss:
Ltotal =λ mcrLmcr +LMR +λphaLpha +LPQE (3)
where the weighting coefficients areλ mcr = 3.0andλ pha =
0.1. The larger weight forL mcr prioritizes the foundational
structural accuracy of the ternary representation, forcing the
network to converge onto the correct geometric manifold. Con-
versely,λ pha is kept small because phase derivatives exhibit
high variance in low-energy regions. This lower weight pre-
vents unstable phase gradients from overwhelming the primary
magnitude optimization while providing necessary continuity
regularization.
Manifold-Constrained Reconstruction Loss (L mcr): This
loss computes theL 1 distance in the ternary feature space
(|A|β,cosθ,sinθ), which promotes sparsity and sharper spec-
tral structures compared to mean squared error optimiza-
tion. Prior to calculation, the predicted phase vectors areL 2-
normalized along the channel dimension to explicitly project
them back onto the unit circle manifold.
Multi-Resolution STFT Loss (L MR ): To capture multi-
scale time-frequency structures,L MR computes the sum
of spectral convergence, log-magnitude, and linear magni-
tude losses across three different window lengths (240, 600,
and 1200). This combination simultaneously captures high-
resolution temporal transients and narrow-band frequency har-
monics.

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets and Settings
Datasets:We evaluated on the VCTK corpus[17] downsampled
to 16 kHz, split into non-overlapping training, validation, and
test sets. Training utilized online dynamic masking[18] with
packet loss rates (PLRs) uniformly sampled between 10% and
60%. We constructed two evaluation sets: (1) a Synthetic Test
Set using the Gilbert-Elliot model[19, 18] (10%-50% PLR) to
simulate burst losses; and (2) the INTERSPEECH 2022 PLC
Challenge Blind Test Set[20] to evaluate real-world generaliza-
tion.
Implementation:The STFT uses a 32 ms Hann window
(512 points) with a 4 ms hop length. Dense Blocks contain 16
hidden channels, and the TCN bottleneck comprises 4 stacked
modules (d∈{1,2,4,8}). Models were trained for 150 epochs
using Adam (lr= 5×10 −3→1×10 −3, batch size 16). The
loss weights areλmcr = 3.0andλ pha = 0.1.
3.2. Metrics and Baselines
We evaluated signal quality and intelligibility using PESQ[21]
and STOI[22], and perceptual fidelity via PLCMOS[23] and
DNSMOS[24] (SIG, BAK, OVRL). Downstream task perfor-
mance and speaker identity consistency were assessed using
Word Error Rate (WER) (faster-whisper[25]) and Speaker Sim-
ilarity (Sim, Resemblyzer[26]).
We compare DDSN against four representative baselines:
1) TFGAN[10]: employs dual-domain adversarial training for
joint waveform-spectrogram modeling; 2) FRN[8]: a predic-
tive full-band recurrent network mitigating artifacts without loss
mask priors; 3) cplx-bin2bin[13]: a generative model operating
directly on complex-valued spectrograms; and 4) LPCNet[3]:
the official time-domain challenge baseline combining linear
predictive coding with a WaveRNN backbone.

4.2. Ablation Study
We evaluated four configurations on the VCTK dataset (40%
PLR) to validate each proposed component (Table 3). Separat-
ing magnitude and phase (Dual-Stream) improves basic restora-
tion metrics over the Single-Stream (complex concatenation)
baseline. Adding standard Sigmoid fusion (Dual + Gating)
yields only marginal improvements, likely due to phase feature
truncation in low-energy regions. In contrast, integrating the
SARG module yields the largest improvements across all met-
rics, demonstrating that asymmetric residual guidance effec-
tively leverages magnitude priors without compromising phase
topology.

5. Conclusion
We proposed DDSN, a wrapped-phase-aware dual-stream PLC
network explicitly decoupling magnitude and phase. Via the
SARG module, it uses magnitude priors to guide phase re-
construction while preserving topological structure. Evalua-
tions on simulated and real-world burst losses show consistent
improvements over baselines in signal quality, perceptual fi-
delity, and recognition accuracy. While the current model prior-
itizes restoration fidelity to validate our decoupled theory, future
work will explore using phase group delay for reverse magni-
tude guidance to refine unvoiced spectra, alongside lightweight
adaptations (e.g., pruning and distillation) for edge deployment.
