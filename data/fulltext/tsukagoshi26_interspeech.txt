Abstract
Recent Speech Large Language Models (SpeechLLMs) have
emerged as a promising approach for end-to-end Spoken Lan-
guage Understanding (SLU). While adapting SpeechLLMs to
specific SLU tasks such as intent classification requires align-
ment with task-specific label schemas, conventional fine-tuning
still struggles to separate semantically adjacent intent cate-
gories. To address this, we propose Reasoning-Guided Fine-
Tuning (RG-FT), which distills structured reasoning trajectories
generated by DeepSeek-R1, comprising candidate enumeration,
rejection reasoning, and label prediction, into SpeechLLMs as a
multitask objective. Explicitly verbalizing why incorrect candi-
dates are rejected forces the model to capture fine-grained dis-
tinctions and sharpen inter-class decision boundaries. At infer-
ence, only direct label prediction is used, incurring zero addi-
tional computational cost. Experiments on multiple SLU bench-
marks and six SpeechLLMs demonstrate that RG-FT consis-
tently improves SLU performance.
Index Terms: spoken language understanding, speech large
language models, chain-of-thought reasoning, multitask learn-
ing
1. Introduction
Spoken language understanding (SLU) is a key component of
task-oriented dialogue systems, enabling a wide range of real-
world applications such as commercial voice assistants [1],
customer-service automation in contact centers [2], and sup-
porting front-line workers in operational environments [3].
Among the core SLU tasks, this work focuses on intent clas-
sification (IC) [4] and slot filling (SF) [5], which jointly map an
utterance to a structured semantic frame.
Recent Speech Large Language Models (SpeechLLMs),
including SALMONN [6], Qwen2-Audio [7], Qwen2.5-
Omni [8], and Audio-Flamingo [9], enable end-to-end SLU by
directly processing acoustic signals and generating structured
textual outputs. Prior work on SpeechLLMs for IC and SF has
actively explored zero-shot and few-shot approaches that elim-
inate the need for task-specific training [10, 11, 12]. However,
fine-tuning on task-specific data remains essential to achieve
competitive accuracy [13]. Choi et al. showed that fine-tuning
SpeechLLMs on paired speech and text data can recover most
of the performance gap between text-only and fully supervised
settings, even with a small amount of paired data [14]. Nev-
ertheless, correctly distinguishing semantically adjacent intents
that differ only in subtle aspects such as scope or granularity

remains a persistent challenge, as conventional fine-tuning fails
to provide sufficient separation between competing interpreta-
tions. Acquiring such fine-grained distinctions purely from data
demands a large number of near-boundary examples, which are
costly to obtain in practice.
One promising direction for addressing this challenge is to
supervise the reasoning process that underlies fine-grained in-
tent discrimination, rather than relying solely on label super-
vision. In text-based LLM research, rationale-based supervi-
sion [15, 16, 17] and reasoning distillation [18, 19, 20, 21, 22]
have demonstrated that injecting structured intermediate rea-
soning signals into the training objective can improve down-
stream performance. However, these results are largely estab-
lished in text-only settings, and it remains unclear whether such
supervision transfers to SpeechLLM-based SLU, where seman-
tic decisions must be formed from speech inputs exhibiting vari-
ability from speakers, prosody, and noise.
We address this gap with Reasoning-Guided Fine-Tuning
(RG-FT), a structured reasoning distillation framework for
SpeechLLM-based SLU. In this framework, structured reason-
ing trajectories generated by DeepSeek-R1 [23], consisting of
candidate enumeration, rejection reasoning, and label predic-
tion, are distilled into SpeechLLMs. The core idea is that ex-
plicitly verbalizing the grounds for rejecting incorrect candi-
dates serves as a structural regularizer, forcing the model to cap-
ture fine-grained distinctions and sharpen inter-class decision
boundaries. We formulate this as a multitask learning objective,
using only direct label prediction at inference with zero addi-
tional computational cost. We validate RG-FT on SLURP [24]
and Speech-MASSIVE [25] across six SpeechLLMs, and fur-
ther provide quantitative analysis of the resulting latent repre-
sentations via Silhouette coefficients and Fisher ratios.
Our contributions are as follows:
• We proposeRG-FT (Reasoning-Guided Fine-Tuning),
a reasoning distillation framework for SpeechLLM-based
SLU, in which reasoning trajectories from DeepSeek-R1
consisting of candidate enumeration, rejection reasoning, and
label prediction are distilled into SpeechLLMs as an auxiliary
regularizer under a multitask learning objective.
• Through experiments on SLURP [24] and Speech-
MASSIVE [25] across six SpeechLLMs, we demonstrate
that RG-FT consistently improves intent accuracy (up to
+2.4%) and SLU-F1 (up to+2.8) over standard fine-tuning,
with zero additional computational cost at inference.
• We provide quantitative evidence via Silhouette coefficients,
Fisher ratios, and centroid margin that reasoning supervision
enhances inter-class separability of latent speech represen-
tations, corroborating the mechanism by which RG-FT ad-
dresses confusion among semantically adjacent intents.

2. Proposed Method
Figure 1 provides an overview of the proposed framework. The
following subsections describe each component in detail. We
follow the intent and slot schema defined in SLURP [24].
2.1. Structured Reasoning Trajectory:T= (C,R,L)
We represent the SLU decision process as a structured reason-
ing trajectoryT(x) = (C,R,L). Although conceptually de-
composed into candidate enumeration (C), rejection rationale
(R), and final label (L), the trajectory is generated as a single
structured output sequence. The trajectoryT(x) = (C,R,L)
is operationalized through a structured prompt, as illustrated in
Figure 2.

Rejection Rationale (R):Let ¯CI =C I\{y∗}denote
the set of incorrect intent candidates, and let ¯CS denote the set

R={(c, r(c;x))} c∈ ¯CI∪ ¯CS.(2)
Compared to affirmation-based reasoning, explicitly rejecting
each competing hypothesis in a contrastive manner encourages
fine-grained differentiation among semantically adjacent intents
and slot types, thereby sharpening inter-class decision bound-
aries more effectively. This design is supported by empirical
evidence from contrastive explanation generation [26] and Con-
trastive Chain-of-Thought prompting [27], as well as theoretical
arguments that counterfactual explanations promote clearer un-
derstanding of decision boundaries [28].
Structured Label (L):The final structured output consists
of the predicted intenty∗ and a set of slot–value pairs(s k,vk),
wheresk is thek-th predicted slot type andvk its corresponding
value:
L={intent:y ∗,entities:{(s k,vk)}k}.(3)
2.2. Teacher-based Data Construction
To construct training data containingT, we employ DeepSeek-
R1 [23] as a teacher model. The teacher receives the speech
transcript, the ground-truth labels(y ∗,S∗), and a database of
all valid intent and slot definitions to generateT. Crucially,
the ground-truth labels are explicitly provided to the teacher,
reducing the risk of generating rejection rationales that contra-
dict the correct labels. We verified that 94.0% of the training
samples yielded well-formed trajectories conforming to the pre-
scribedC→R→Lformat; the remaining samples were dis-
carded. On average, DeepSeek-R1 enumerated 2.5 intent can-
didates and 1.2 slot-type candidates per utterance.

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Dataset
We evaluate our framework on two SLU benchmarks.
SLURP [24] is an English dataset comprising 11,514 training,
2,033 development, and 2,974 test sentences covering 18 do-
mains and 60 intent classes. Speech-MASSIVE [25] is a multi-
lingual speech dataset built upon the MASSIVE corpus; we use
its French subset, which provides the same split sizes and label
schema as SLURP.
3.2. Metrics
Following the evaluation protocol of SLURP [24], we report
Scenario accuracy, Action accuracy, Intent accuracy, and SLU-
F1. The first two measure correctness of domain classification
(e.g.,calendar) and intent action (e.g.,set), respectively, while
Intent accuracy evaluates their combination. SLU-F1 evaluates
entity prediction based on the correctness of the predicted slot
type and the textual overlap between the predicted and reference
slot values.

3.4. Implementation Details
All models are fully fine-tuned using BF16 precision with the
AdamW optimizer, a learning rate of4×10 −5 with cosine an-
nealing schedule, a batch size of 4, and 4 gradient accumulation
steps. Following [14], all models are trained on both speech and
gold transcription inputs to facilitate acquisition of fundamental
SLU capabilities. The maximum number of new tokens is set to
384, and all configurations are trained for up to 4 epochs with
the best checkpoint selected based on development set perfor-
mance. All experiments are conducted on 2×H200 GPUs, and
results are averaged over three random seeds.
For RG-FT, we setα=0.5for all main experiments. A sen-
sitivity analysis overα∈ {0.1,0.2,...,0.9}confirmed that
performance follows a bell-shaped trend across all six architec-
tures, withα=0.5consistently yielding competitive results (see
Section 4).

4. Results
4.1. RG-FT Consistently Improves SLU Performance
Across Architectures
Table 1 compares Direct-FT, Reasoning-FT, and RG-FT across
six SpeechLLMs on SLURP and Speech-MASSIVE (FR). RG-
FT outperforms both baselines on every model–metric–corpus
combination. The improvement is more pronounced for mod-
els with lower baseline performance; Music-Flamingo gains
+2.40%intent accuracy and+2.80SLU-F1 points, while
even the strongest baseline, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, shows consis-
tent improvements on both metrics. In contrast, Reasoning-
FT consistently underperforms Direct-FT, with larger degra-
dation in smaller models (e.g.,−3.06%intent accuracy for
Qwen2.5-Omni-3B on SLURP), consistent with prior findings
that reasoning supervision alone fails to stabilize smaller mod-
els [30, 31, 32, 21]. This contrast indicates that reasoning super-
vision is effective not as a replacement for label prediction, but
as an auxiliary regularizer that shapes the latent space without
overriding the primary classification objective.
4.2. Multitask Reasoning Sharpens Intent Clusters in La-
tent Space
Table 2 reports latent space separability metrics for Direct-FT
and RG-FT on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and 3B. RG-FT improves
all four metrics on both model sizes, indicating that reasoning
supervision increases between-class scatter relative to within-
class scatter (Fisher ratio) while pushing individual utterance
representations closer to their own class centroids and farther
from competing ones (centroid margin). The improvement is
consistent across cosine and Euclidean Silhouette coefficients,
suggesting that the effect is not an artifact of a particular dis-
tance metric. These results provide a representational account
of the accuracy gains observed in Table 1: by sharpening intent
cluster boundaries in latent space, RG-FT reduces confusion
among semantically adjacent intents even though reasoning is
absent at inference.

4.4. Balanced Mixing of Reasoning and Label Objectives
Yields Robust Gains
Figure 3 shows intent accuracy and SLU-F1 asαvaries from
0(Direct-FT) to1(Reasoning-FT) across all six models on
the SLURP test set. Both metrics follow a bell-shaped pro-
file across all model families, with the peak consistently lo-
cated between the two endpoints, suggesting that the combina-
tion of label prediction and reasoning supervision is more effec-
tive than either objective alone. Beyond the peak, performance
declines as the reasoning objective increasingly dominates, con-
sistent with the optimization difficulties of Reasoning-FT dis-
cussed in Section 4.1. Models with higher baseline performance
(Qwen2.5-Omni) show relatively flat curves, while models with
lower baselines exhibit larger gains with peaks concentrated
aroundα∈[0.4,0.6], suggesting that models with more room
for improvement benefit most from reasoning supervision. In
both cases,α=0.5falls within or near the optimal range, sup-
porting its use without per-model tuning.
5. Conclusion
We proposed RG-FT, a structured reasoning distillation frame-
work for moderate-scale SpeechLLMs, to address the chal-
lenge of distinguishing semantically adjacent intents in spo-
ken language understanding. By supervising a structured rea-
soning process (C→R→L) alongside direct label pre-
diction under a multitask learning objective, we consistently
improved performance across six models on both SLURP and
Speech-MASSIVE (FR). Latent space separability analysis pro-
vided quantitative evidence that reasoning supervision encour-
ages more separable intent clusters, and restricting inference to
direct label prediction eliminates additional computational cost.
Several open questions remain, including quality evaluation of
distilled reasoning trajectories and how acoustic features con-
tribute to the reasoning process. Future work will investigate
the effect of teacher model choice, generalization to more di-
verse corpora, and the impact of acoustic conditions such as
background noise on reasoning supervision.
