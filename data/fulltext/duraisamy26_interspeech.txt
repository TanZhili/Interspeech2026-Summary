Subject-Invariant Dynamic Graph Modeling for Cross-Subject EEG Imagined
Speech Decoding

Abstract
Decoding imagined speech using EEG-based Brain-Computer
Interfaces suffers from substantial performance degradation
when applied to unseen subjects due to strong inter-subject vari-
ability. We propose a subject-invariant dynamic graph model-
ing framework for cross-subject EEG-based imagined speech
decoding. The method integrates multi-view dynamic connec-
tivity priors with channel-wise graph attention and incorporates
adversarial subject disentanglement using a gradient reversal
layer to suppress subject-specific information. Evaluation un-
der a strict leave-one-subject-out protocol across 15 subjects
achieves average classification accuracies of31.20%±3.12%
and30.36%±4.05%on two independent datasets. The results
demonstrate consistent cross-subject generalization and high-
light the importance of structured connectivity modeling and
subject-invariant training for robust EEG-based speech decod-
ing.
Index Terms: silent/covert/inner speech; Brain-Computer In-

1. Introduction
Brain Computer Interfaces (BCIs) aim to decode internally
generated neural activity into meaningful external representa-
tions [1, 2]. Among emerging paradigms, imagined speech (aka
silent speech, covert speech, or inner speech) decoding from
scalp electroencephalography (EEG) has attracted considerable
attention due to its potential to enable non-invasive communi-
cation for individuals with severe speech impairments [3, 4, 5].
However, compared to paradigms such as motor imagery [6, 7]
or SSVEP [8, 9, 10], imagined speech decoding remains sub-
stantially more challenging. The neural correlates of covert
speech are subtle, spatially distributed, and exhibit low signal-
to-noise ratio, while inter-subject variability further complicates
model generalization [11, 12, 13]. Deep Learning has signif-
icantly advanced EEG decoding in recent years [14, 15, 16].
Convolutional and recurrent neural networks have been widely
adopted to capture spatiotemporal dependencies [17, 18, 19].
More recently, Transformer-based EEG foundation models such
as EEGPT [20], LaBraM [21], NeuroLM [22], and Brain-
BERT [23] have demonstrated strong performance across mo-
tor imagery, seizure detection, and sleep staging tasks by lever-
aging large-scale pretraining and long-range temporal model-
ing [24, 25, 26].
Despite these advances, reliable cross-subject imagined
speech decoding remains largely unresolved. Under strict
Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) protocols, pretrained EEG
Transformers often exhibit performance degradation toward

chance levels after fine-tuning. This limitation arises from two
fundamental factors. First, most EEG Transformers employ
patch-based tokenization that prioritizes temporal segmentation
while treating electrode channels as exchangeable inputs. Un-
like textual tokens, EEG channels correspond to fixed cortical
projections governed by neurophysiological constraints. Ignor-
ing spatial topology and cross-electrode interactions restricts
the model’s ability to capture meaningful cortical organization,
because neighboring electrodes often reflect correlated activity
arising from functionally related cortical regions [27]. Second,
imagined speech is characterized by pronounced inter-subject
variability in spectral dynamics and functional connectivity.
Differences in cognitive strategy, articulation imagery, and cor-
tical organization lead to subject-specific spatial–spectral signa-
tures [13]. Standard self-attention mechanisms, driven solely by
dot-product similarity, do not explicitly encode neurophysiolog-
ical relationships between electrodes. As a result, task-relevant
speech representations may become mixed with subject-specific
patterns, limiting cross-subject generalization.
Graph-based modeling provides a principled framework to
encode electrode topology and functional interactions [28, 29,
30]. By representing electrodes as nodes and defining edges via
spatial proximity or connectivity measures, graph neural net-
works have improved interpretability and robustness in various
EEG domains. However, existing approaches typically rely on
static connectivity matrices or are evaluated in subject-specific
settings, which limits their applicability to cross-subject imag-
ined speech decoding. Cross-subject imagined speech decod-
ing faces three key challenges: (1) preserving electrode-specific
spatial structure, (2) modeling dynamic rather than static con-
nectivity, and (3) reducing subject-specific variability under
LOSO evaluation. To address these challenges, we propose a
subject-invariant dynamic graph modeling frameworkfor cross-
subject EEG speech classification that integrates:
1.Electrode-aware Transformer tokenizationto preserve
spatial identity,
2.Dynamic multi-view graph priorsto model time-varying
functional connectivity,
3.Adversarial subject-invariant learningvia gradient rever-
sal to suppress subject-specific representations.
2. Methods
2.1. Datasets
We evaluate the proposed framework on two publicly avail-
able imagined speech EEG datasets, each containing five-word
classification tasks recorded from 15 subjects using 64-channel
EEG systems. Each dataset was processed independently to
avoid cross-dataset leakage.

Dataset 1[31] (BCI Competition 2020) comprises imag-
ined speech recordings from 15 participants. Subjects were in-
structed to internally rehearse five distinct words. Each word
was repeated 80 times, yielding 400 trials per subject. EEG
signals were acquired using a 64-channel cap following the in-
ternational 10–10 montage at a sampling rate of 500 Hz. The
imagined speech interval lasted two seconds per trial.
Dataset 2[32] (Overt/Covert Speech Dataset) contains both
overt and covert speech recordings collected from 15 healthy
participants using a 64-channel wireless EEG system at 500 Hz.
In this study, only the covert (imagined) speech recordings
were used, while all overt speech recordings were excluded
from analysis. The dataset includes five command words, each
repeated multiple times across blocks, resulting in 80 covert
speech trials per class per subject.
2.2. EEG Preprocessing
A unified preprocessing pipeline was applied to both datasets.
First, power-line interference was attenuated using a 50 Hz
notch filter. Signals were then band-pass filtered between
0.5–80 Hz to retain physiologically relevant rhythms while sup-
pressing slow drifts and high-frequency noise [33]. Indepen-
dent Component Analysis (ICA) was performed to remove oc-
ular and muscular artifacts [34]. Although these procedures
substantially mitigate artifacts, residual EEG noise remains an
inherent limitation of EEG recordings. EEG recordings were
segmented into 2-second epochs corresponding to the imagined
speech phase. A short 1 second pre-stimulus interval was used
for baseline correction.

2.4. Implementation Details
All experiments follow strict LOSO evaluation across 15 sub-
jects. In each fold, one subject is reserved for testing, and the
remaining subjects are used for training with an internal valida-
tion split drawn only from the training set.
Baseline fine-tuning.For baseline evaluation, each pre-
trained EEG Transformer (NeuroLM, LaBraM, EEGPT) is fine-
tuned under LOSO by attaching a linear classification head and
jointly optimizing the backbone and head.
Proposed fine-tuning.For the proposed framework, the
same pretrained backbone is jointly fine-tuned together with
electrode-aware pooling, the dynamic graph-biased attention
module, and the adversarial subject classifier (GRL head).
For each epoch, dynamic connectivity priors are computed
per window and injected into attention logits as described in
subsubsection 2.3.2. Optimization uses AdamW with cosine
learning-rate decay and early stopping based on validation loss.

2.5. Ablation Study Design
We quantify the impact of each contribution under identical
LOSO conditions by evaluating: (i) the Transformer baseline
without priors/GRL; (ii) priors without GRL (graph-biased at-
tention only); (iii) GRL without priors (adversarial invariance
only); and (iv) the full model (priors + GRL). This isolates the
roles of structured connectivity bias and subject-invariant opti-
mization in cross-subject EEG speech decoding.
3. Results
All results are reported under strict LOSO across 15 subjects per
dataset. We report mean classification accuracy (mean±stan-
dard deviation across held-out subjects). Statistical significance
is assessed using paired two-sidedt-tests against the fine-tuned
EEGPT baseline (the datasets are class-balanced).
3.1. Backbone Transformer Selection
Recent EEG foundation models such as EEGPT, NeuroLM,
and LaBraM have demonstrated strong performance on mo-
tor imagery and related tasks. To select an appropriate back-
bone for cross-subject imagined speech decoding, we fine-tuned
these pretrained Transformers under strict LOSO using identi-
cal training settings. The results are summarized in Table 2.

4. Discussion
The results demonstrate that cross-subject imagined speech de-
coding remains a challenging problem despite recent advances
in EEG foundation models. As shown in Table 2, NeuroLM
and LaBraM perform near chance level under strict LOSO eval-
uation, while EEGPT achieves comparatively stronger general-
ization. This suggests that large-scale EEG pretraining alone is
insufficient to overcome the substantial inter-subject variability
inherent in imagined speech EEG. The improvements reported
in Table 3 indicate that graph-based connectivity modeling and
adversarial subject-invariant learning provide complementary
benefits. While each component improves performance over
the fine-tuned EEGPT baseline, their combination achieves the
highest accuracy across both datasets. This suggests that ef-
fective cross-subject decoding requires both modeling neuro-
physiologically meaningful electrode interactions and reducing
subject-specific neural patterns. The comparison between static
and dynamic graph priors (Table 4) further highlights the im-
portance of time-varying functional connectivity. While static
priors provide only modest gains, dynamic window-wise priors
substantially improve performance, suggesting that imagined
speech is characterized by transient neural interactions that can-
not be captured by fixed connectivity assumptions. The subject-
invariance analysis further supports this interpretation. Subject
classification accuracy decreases from 18.23% to 7.00%, ap-
proaching the chance level of 6.67%, indicating that adversarial
optimization effectively suppresses subject-specific information
while preserving task-relevant speech representations. Finally,
Table 5 shows that conventional CNN, recurrent, and graph-
based models remain close to chance level under LOSO evalu-
ation, highlighting the intrinsic difficulty of cross-subject imag-
ined speech decoding. Although the proposed framework con-
sistently outperforms these baselines, the gap between within-
subject and cross-subject performance remains substantial, em-
phasizing inter-subject variability as a major obstacle for prac-
tical imagined speech BCI systems.
4.1. Limitations
Although our proposed method yields consistent gains, absolute
LOSO accuracy remains moderate for practical use (∼30%),
reflecting the intrinsic difficulty of imagined speech decoding
from EEG. Connectivity priors estimated from short windows
can be noisy and may depend on preprocessing choices (e.g., ar-
tifact removal and filtering). Future work should consider scal-
ing to larger vocabularies, use fewer EEG channels, and explore
different montages. Finally, while GRL reduces subject separa-
bility, complete invariance is not fully achieved, suggesting the
potential benefit of additional subject adaptation mechanisms.
5. Conclusion
We introduced a subject-invariant dynamic graph modeling
framework for cross-subject imagined speech EEG decoding.
By injecting time-varying multi-view connectivity priors into
graph-biased attention and combining them with adversarial
subject disentanglement, the proposed approach significantly
improves strict LOSO accuracy over fine-tuned pretrained EEG
Transformers and conventional CNN/RNN/GNN baselines.
