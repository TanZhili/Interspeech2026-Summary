Abstract
In Spoofing-Aware Speaker Verification (SASV), combining
Automatic Speaker Verification (ASV) and Countermeasure
(CM) subsystems is a key challenge. Existing approaches
mainly rely on static fusion schemes, which fail to fully reflect
the complementary strengths of the two subsystems. To address
this limitation, we propose a Soft Gating Score-Level Fusion
method that dynamically adjusts subsystem contributions based
on confidence. This trial-wise adjustment allows the subsystem
with higher confidence to contribute more strongly to the final
decision. The proposed method requires no additional training
and can be directly applied to existing SASV pipelines. Exper-
iments on two benchmark datasets under multiple model con-
figurations show that the proposed method achieves up to 90%
relative improvement in a-DCF compared to baselines. We fur-
ther analyze the conditions under which the proposed fusion is
most effective, providing practical insights for stable SASV de-
ployment.
Index Terms: soft gating, score fusion, spoofing aware speaker
verification
1. Introduction
ASV [1] systems have become widely deployed in security-
critical applications, including mobile authentication and bank-
ing services. However, ASV systems are vulnerable to spoofing
attacks [2] generated by speech synthesis techniques, including
text-to-speech (TTS) [3] and voice conversion (VC) [4]. To ad-
dress this vulnerability, SASV has been introduced [5], which
integrates a Countermeasure (CM) [6] subsystem into the veri-
fication pipeline. SASV has attracted increasing attention in re-
cent challenges, including ASVspoof5 [7] and Wildspoof Chal-
lenge [8].
A fundamental issue in SASV lies in how to combine
the outputs of ASV and CM. The two subsystems are trained
for different objectives: ASV discriminates between target
and nontarget speakers, while CM distinguishes bonafide from
spoofed speech. Prior works [9, 10] have explored various fu-
sion strategies, most commonly at the score level. Score-level
fusion is attractive because it enables the combination of inde-
pendently trained ASV and CM models without additional joint
training. Over time, score-level fusion has evolved from sim-
ple score summation [11] to weighted score fusion [12, 13],
and more recently to score-aware gated integration frame-
works [14].

Many existing score-level fusion methods rely on fixed
combination rules that apply the same weighting strategy across
all trials. This design leads to inherent limitations. First, such
static fusion does not account for the distinct functional roles
of ASV and CM. The relative importance of speaker discrimi-
nation and spoof detection can vary depending on the trial type
or attack scenario. For example, spoofing attacks may require
stronger emphasis on CM decisions, whereas zero-effort im-
postor trials primarily rely on ASV discrimination. Second,
fixed fusion schemes are sensitive to shifts in score distributions
across datasets or model configurations. When the score range
of one subsystem becomes dominant, the fused decision can be
biased toward that subsystem, leading to unstable performance.
To address these limitations, we propose a dynamicSoft
Gating Score-Level Fusionmethod. Instead of using fixed fu-
sion weights, the proposed approach dynamically adjusts the
contribution of each subsystem for every trial.
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
•Novel Fusion Method:We introduce a dynamic soft gating
score-level fusion strategy that overcomes the limitations of
conventional static score fusion.
•Training-Free Design:The proposed method requires no
additional training or learnable parameters, making it prac-
tical and easy to deploy in real-world systems.
•Practical and Stable Fusion:We demonstrate that the pro-
posed method maintains stable performance across different
ASV–CM model combinations and datasets, mitigating the
instability caused by score distribution shifts.

2.2. Score-Aware Gated Integration
To address these limitations, score-aware gated integration
frameworks have recently been proposed. For example,
ATMM-SAGA [14] uses the CM score as a multiplicative gat-
ing weight applied to ASV embeddings, suppressing spoofed
inputs while preserving bona fide samples.
Despite its effectiveness, this approach also presents sev-
eral practical limitations. First, it requires additional joint train-
ing with a specialized alternating optimization procedure. This
increases training complexity and limits its applicability when
only pre-trained subsystems are available. Second, the gating
mechanism is implemented as a simple scalar multiplication of
the CM score with ASV embeddings, which does not explicitly
model dynamic score-level interactions between the two sub-
systems. As a result, the gating behavior remains largely deter-
mined by a single score value rather than adaptively adjusting
the relative contributions of ASV and CM scores for each trial.
These limitations highlight the need for more efficient fu-
sion strategies that can adaptively combine ASV and CM scores
without additional training or complex architectural modifica-
tions.
3. Proposed Fusion Method
Lets asv ands cm denote the normalized scores produced by the
ASV and CM subsystems. Highers asv values indicate stronger
evidence for the target speaker, while higherscm values indicate
bona fide speech. Letτ asv andτ cm denote the Equal Error Rate
(EER) thresholds estimated on the development set.
The proposed Soft Gating Score-Level Fusion dynamically
adjusts the contribution of each subsystem based on a confi-
dence measure derived from these thresholds. The overall pro-
cedure is summarized in Algorithm 1.
3.1. Confidence Score
We define a confidence score using the margin between the sub-
system score and its EER threshold:
δasv =s asv−τ asv, δ cm =s cm−τ cm.(3)
The margin reflects how far the score lies from the EER
threshold. Scores far from the threshold indicate confident de-
cisions, while scores close to the threshold indicate uncertainty.
We therefore interpret the margin as a confidence measure of
the subsystem prediction. A large absolute margin corresponds
to high confidence, whereas a small margin indicates an am-
biguous decision.
Using this confidence measure allows the fusion process to
emphasize reliable subsystem outputs while reducing the influ-
ence of uncertain ones. In particular, when a subsystem pro-
duces a confident decision, its score receives a larger weight
in the fused result. This design overcomes a key limitation of
conventional static score fusion by dynamically adjusting sub-
system contributions according to their confidence, rather than
applying fixed weights regardless of output reliability.
3.2. Soft Gating Mechanisms
Based on the confidence scores, we define three gating mecha-
nisms that determine how the subsystem scores are combined.
CM Gating.In CM Gating, the CM margin controls the
fusion weight:
SCM-gate =s cm·δ cm +s asv·(1−|δ cm|).(4)
When the CM score is far from its threshold, the CM sub-
system is considered reliable and its contribution increases.
Conversely, when the CM decision is uncertain, the fusion gives
greater weight to the ASV subsystem. This formulation is par-
ticularly effective for spoof trials while still allowing ASV to
distinguish target and nontarget speakers.
ASV Gating.In ASV Gating, the ASV margin determines
the fusion weight:
SASV-gate =s cm·(1−|δ asv|) +s asv·δ asv.(5)
In this formulation, the influence of the ASV subsystem is
dynamically adjusted according to its confidence. This formu-
lation is particularly effective for nontarget trials while still en-
abling the CM subsystem to suppress spoofed speech.
Double Gating.Double Gating scales each subsystem in-
dependently according to its own confidence:
SDouble-gate =s cm·δ cm +s asv·δ asv.(6)
Unlike the previous variants, each subsystem is weighted
independently by its own margin. This allows both subsystems
to contribute strongly when their predictions are confident.

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Datasets
Experiments are conducted on ASVspoof 2019 LA (LA19) [15]
and ASVspoof5 [7]. We follow the official evaluation proto-
cols of both datasets and report results on their evaluation sets.
For ASVspoof5, experiments are performed under the Track 2
closed condition.
4.2. Models
We construct four SASV systems by combining two ASV mod-
els with two CM models, resulting in four ASV–CM combina-
tions. The ASV models are trained on V oxCeleb2 [16], while
the CM models are trained using the corresponding training and
development sets of each dataset.
•ASV models:ECAPA-TDNN [17] and ReDimNet [18].
•CM models:AASIST [19] and Conformer-TCM [20].
4.3. Score Normalization
Before fusion, the ASV and CM scores are normalized to a com-
mon range. ASV scores are computed as cosine similarity be-
tween enrollment and test embeddings and linearly mapped to
[0,1]using

probabilities using softmax:

wherez bf andz spf denote the logits corresponding to the
bonafide and spoof classes, respectively.
4.4. Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate system performance using SV-EER, SPF-EER, and
SASV-EER [5]. Additionally, we report a-DCF [21], a cost-
based metric that measures the expected cost of SASV decisions
under predefined class priors and error costs. The prior and
cost parameters are set according to the official ASVspoof5 [7]
evaluation protocol.

5.1. Results on LA19 Dataset
On the LA19 dataset in Table 1, our proposed methods gener-
ally improve over the Baselines across all four model combina-
tions. Compared to Baselines, the a-DCF is reduced by approx-
imately 90% on average. Among the three proposed methods,
Double Gating shows the best results in three out of four cases.
The only exceptions occur when using CM Gating in the
ECAPA+TCM and Redim+TCM combinations. In these two
specific cases, CM Gating fails to outperform the baselines, re-
sulting in a higher a-DCF. The reason for this specific perfor-
mance drop is highly related to the extreme EER threshold of
the CM model, which we will analyze in Section 5.3.
5.2. Results on ASVspoof5 Dataset
On the ASVspoof5 dataset in Table 1, the three proposed gating
methods also generally outperform the baselines, demonstrat-
ing that the proposed approach is still highly effective under
more challenging conditions. In most model combinations, the
proposed methods successfully reduce the a-DCF compared to
both Baselines.
However, similar to the LA19 results, there are specific
cases where our method clearly fails. The most notable excep-
tions occur when using Double Gating in the ECAPA+TCM and
Redim+TCM combinations. In these two specific cases, Double
Gating performs significantly worse than the baselines, result-
ing in a drastically higher a-DCF. We will explain why Double
Gating fails in these specific situations in Section 5.3, which is
directly related to the extreme EER threshold of the models.
5.3. Limitations and Analysis
This subsection analyzes a limitation of the proposed Soft-
Gating Score-Level Fusion observed in Sections 5.1 and 5.2.
Specifically, performance degradation occurs when the EER
threshold is extremely close to 0 or 1. Under these condi-
tions, the contribution of one subsystem may become exces-
sively large, leading to an imbalance in the fused score.
Fig 2-(a) corresponds to the ECAPA+TCM and
Redim+TCM configuration discussed in Section 5.1, where
the CM Gating yields higher SV-EER and a-DCF than the
baselines. In this case, the EER threshold of the CM subsystem
is located near 0. As a result, most bonafide scores lie above
the threshold, and the marginδ cm remains consistently large
and positive across trials. Under CM Gating, the CM score is
scaled directly by this margin, while the contribution of ASV
is reduced. Because the CM subsystem assigns high scores to
bonafide speech regardless of speaker identity, both target and
nontarget trials receive similarly amplified CM contributions.
Consequently, the contribution of ASV becomes small, making
it difficult to properly separate target and nontarget speakers,
which is confirmed by the increased SV-EER in Table 1.
Fig 2-(b) corresponds to the ECAPA+TCM and
Redim+TCM configurations discussed in Section 5.2,
where Double Gating shows increased SPF-EER and a-DCF.
It illustrates the opposite case, where the EER threshold
is located near 1. In this case, the margins of target and
nontarget trials remain small because their scores lie close to
the threshold, resulting in weak scaling in Double Gating. For
spoof trials, even when the CM score becomes large due to
misclassification, the margin remains small and limits the CM
contribution. Conversely, when the spoof score is far below the
threshold, the margin increases but the CM score itself is very
small, which again weakens its influence. Consequently, the
fused score becomes dominated by ASV decisions, leading to
an increase in SPF-EER.
These observations show that the effectiveness of the pro-
posed fusion depends on the positioning of the EER threshold.
Since the margin is computed directly from the threshold, ex-
treme threshold values can distort the dynamic weighting pro-
cess. In contrast, when the threshold lies within a reasonable
range, the margin varies meaningfully across trials and enables
stable fusion. Therefore, these findings indicate that the choice
of gating method should be adapted according to the threshold
position.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a training-free Soft-Gating score-level
fusion method that dynamically adjusts subsystem contributions
using the margin from each subsystem’s EER threshold as a
confidence measure. Experiments on multiple ASV–CM model
combinations and datasets show that the proposed method gen-
erally improves upon baselines. Our analysis further reveals
that the effectiveness of gating strategies depends on the posi-
tion of the EER threshold.
Overall, the proposed method provides a practical SASV
fusion framework that can be applied to diverse model combi-
nations without additional training. As future work, we plan to
develop improved gating strategies that better handle extreme
threshold conditions.
