Dual-Asymmetric Loss

Abstract
AI-based speech enhancement algorithms are prevalent in
speech transmission applications and consumer audio devices.
These algorithms typically utilize neural networks to predict fil-
ters or masks that attenuate noise in speech signals. However,
due to the often-overlapping frequency responses of speech and
noise, this process can degrade speech quality or result in resid-
ual noise artifacts. In this paper, we present a novel loss func-
tion with an adjustable parameter to balance the importance of
reconstructing the original speech and the effectiveness of noise
suppression. We demonstrate the efficacy of this loss with a
variety of state-of-the-art architectures. Experimental results
demonstrate improvements over baseline models for the tar-
geted outcome: higher speech quality or enhanced noise sup-
pression. Furthermore, the loss function exhibits a correla-
tion of 0.95 for speech and -0.96 for noise metrics when tested
across 13 distinct parameter values.

1. Introduction
The consumer electronics industry has significantly improved
background noise reduction in speech transmissions now that
AI-based speech enhancement (SE) [1] is available on phones,
headsets, wireless earbuds, and remote conferencing platforms
[2]. SE is even a built-in feature on many computer operating
systems (OS) or available as software plugins.
Studies have shown that memory-intensive, high-
computation SE can reduce noise in speech signals with high
fidelity [3, 4]. However, embedded deployment platforms are
both memory and computationally constrained and therefore
cannot support these state-of-the-art (SOTA) algorithms. Even
mobile phones or OS-based solutions, which have significantly
more memory and processing power, must limit computation
and memory usage to ensure seamless multitasking alongside
other complex applications [5]. Consequently, commercially
available SE remains below SOTA performance.
To be commercially viable, models must be memory and
computationally constrained using a variety of pre- and post-
training optimization strategies. Pre-training strategies often in-
volve reducing the number of model parameters or using less
computationally complex neural network operations [6, 7, 8].
Post-training strategies typically include techniques such as
weight pruning and quantization [9, 10]. However, without
performance-specific controls during the training process, these
optimizations can produce unexpected and undesirable results,
such as a SE model that produces low-quality speech or inad-
equate noise suppression, rather than a balanced reduction in
overall performance [11].
Some work has focused on reinforcing speech quality to
improve overall performance. In [12], voice activity detection
(V AD) is incorporated in the loss function to reduce speech ar-
tifacts. Another approach introduces an asymmetric loss term
to prevent speech attenuation [13]. However, SE models in-
herently trade off speech quality and noise suppression, mean-
ing that gains in one are typically accompanied by losses in the
other.
Given that constrained models often exhibit deficiencies in
speech quality and/or noise suppression performance, it may be
more desirable to tune SE models for task-specific objectives.
For instance, in-game chat applications may favor stronger
noise suppression, whereas emergency telecommunications re-
quires highly intelligible, lossless speech.
In [14, 15], the speech/noise trade-off is adjusted using a
weighted combination of the respective loss terms. These meth-
ods compute separate speech and noise errors by applying a
mask generated by a noisy mixture to constituent components
of the mixture; however, the phase of the stimuli used to gen-
erate the mask is inconsistent with that of its components. An-
other approach presents a single-term loss constructed as the
maximum of a weighted objective balancing under-suppressed
noise against over-suppressed speech [16]. This approach also
enables succinct tuning of one metric relative to another; how-
ever, at each step the model features are optimized for only one
objective rather than a weighted combination of both. This may
introduce oscillations in the loss, potentially steering the op-
timization away from convergence toward more desirable lo-
cal minima. Additionally, these methods that allow indepen-
dent tuning of speech and noise do not incorporate phase-aware
terms in their loss. Such terms are crucial for effectively train-
ing complex-valued models and have been shown to improve
the performance of magnitude-only models [17], particularly
when training enforces STFT consistency [18].
To enhance tunable control over SE behavior, we present
Dual-Asymmetric Loss, which balances the relative importance
of speech reconstruction and noise suppression during train-
ing. The proposed loss comprises two distinct components:
one exclusively targeting speech attenuation and one exclu-
sively targeting residual noise. The function incorporates an
adjustable parameter that governs the linear combination of
these components, enabling control over their relative impor-
tance. Each component includes magnitude and complex terms
to jointly enhance the amplitude and phase. Evaluations on a
compact, low-complexity real-time model show that the tuning
parameter exhibits strong correlations with speech-quality and
noise-suppression performance. Furthermore, the proposed loss
yields gains in either speech quality or noise suppression over
baseline performance for complex, non-causal SOTA models,
highlighting its generality and broad applicability.

2.2. Dual-Asymmetric Loss
Dual-Asymmetric Loss leverages asymmetry not only to penal-
ize speech attenuation but also to independently target resid-
ual noise. Swapping the operands of the difference prior to the
ReLU operation in the asymmetric loss function (4) introduces
a penalty that specifically targets residual noise in the enhanced
signal. Combined, this formulation yields two distinct asym-
metric penalty terms: one that targets speech attenuation and
one that targets residual noise. The former enforces that speech
is preserved, while the latter drives the model to suppress noise.
These are, however, contradictory terms. For the speech
term to approach zero, the model could produce a mask of all
ones, attenuating none of the original speech. The noise term
can approach zero by predicting a mask of all zeros, attenuating
all the signal and, consequently, all the noise. For these terms
to work in harmony, an adjustable parameterλis introduced to
balance the trade-off between speech reconstruction and noise
suppression. Low values ofλencourage the model to priori-
tize noise suppression, potentially at the expense of attenuating
frequencies in the speech signal. Conversely, high values ofλ
emphasize speech reconstruction, which may result in increased
noise leakage, particularly in regions where speech and noise
overlap. Whenλ= 0.5, the loss function is equally balanced
and reduces to a scaled-down version of the CCMSE (3).
Each term, the asymmetric speech loss (5) and the asym-
metric noise loss (6), comprises both a complex and a magni-
tude component. For the magnitude component of each term,
asymmetry is determined using a simple difference operation
that is lower-bounded by zero using a ReLU function. In the
speech term, frequency bins with less energy in the predicted
signal than in the target indicate speech attenuation. In the noise
term, frequency bins with more energy in the predicted signal
than in the target indicate residual noise. Therefore, the order
of operands in the difference operation ensures that each term
produces positive values only when its respective target condi-
tion is met. As a result, the speech term penalizes only speech
attenuation, while the noise term penalizes only residual noise.
Since speech attenuation and residual noise cannot be de-
termined by a sign difference in the complex plane, indicator
functions [20] are used to identify the frequency bins contribut-
ing to the complex components of the loss. ForL speech, the
indicator function1 |S|>| ˆS| returns a value of1for bins where
the target magnitudes exceed the predicted magnitudes, indi-

3. Speech Enhancement Models
To evaluate the efficacy of the proposed loss, four SOTA SE ar-
chitectures were selected for experimentation: LiSenNet [21],
DPCRN [22], SEMamba [23], and MP-SENet [24]. LiSen-
Net is a highly compact and lightweight model, comprising just
57K parameters and requiring only 56M multiply–accumulate
operations per-second (MACs). DPCRN is a U-Net–based ar-
chitecture featuring a dual-path bottleneck, comprising 800K
parameters and requiring 3.73B MACs. Both are causal mod-
els that produce excellent perceptual metrics relative to their
size and computational complexity. SEMamba uses a state
space model [25], comprising 2.25M parameters and requiring
32.73B MACs. MP-SENet is a transformer-based model com-
prising 2.05M parameters and requiring 23.75B MACs. Both
represent current SOTA, achieving some of the highest percep-
tual metrics on the V oiceBank-DEMAND noisy test set [26].
These four models serve to validate the effectiveness of the pro-
posed Dual-Asymmetric loss across a diverse range of model
sizes and computational complexities.
All models were trained using the V oiceBank-DEMAND
training set downsampled to 16 kHz. During training, each
batch consisted of eight 2-second audio segments. Each au-
dio frame contained 512 samples with 50% overlap and was
windowed using a Hann function. The proposed loss function
is applied with an STFT consistency constraint, using the same
STFT parameters. A learning rate of5×10−4 was used with an
exponential decay of 0.99 applied pre-epoch. All models were
trained for 200 epochs, and the checkpoint from the epoch that
yielded the lowest validation loss was chosen for testing.
To ensure unbiased results, all models were trained and
evaluated using identical codebases. The model code for LiSen-
Net1, SEMamba2, and MP-SENet3 was obtained from their of-
ficial open-source repositories and trained using configurations
provided by the original authors. Pre-trained weights for SE-
Mamba and MP-SENet were similarly obtained for reporting
baseline results. DPCRN was reproduced using the configu-
rations detailed in the original publication. Since pre-trained
weights for LiSenNet and DPCRN are not publicly available,
baseline results for these models are not reported.
4. Experiments
4.1. Methods
DPCRN, SEMamba, and MP-SENet were trained withλvalues
of0.35,0.5, and0.65, respectively, to demonstrate the effect of
the loss function under varying conditions. Moreover, LiSenNet
was trained withλvalues ranging from0.2to0.8in increments
of0.05to examine the correlation of this parameter with speech
and noise metrics.
To evaluate the models, we used the Deep Noise Suppres-
sion Mean Opinion Score (DNSMOS) [27], which produces
independent metrics for speech quality and noise suppression
performance. DNSMOS is an AI-based evaluation model de-
veloped by Microsoft for assessing SE models. It approximates
the ITU-T Rec. P.835 standard for the subjective evaluation of
speech transmission systems. DNSMOS predicts three mean
opinion scores: SIG (speech quality), BAK (background noise
intrusiveness), and OVRL (overall perceptual quality). For all
metrics, higher scores indicate better performance.

As a non-intrusive perceptual metric, DNSMOS cannot
reliably detect instances of speech being dropped or altered.
Therefore, traditional reference-based metrics Perceptual Eval-
uation of Speech Quality (PESQ), Short-Time Objective Intel-
ligibility (STOI), and Scale Invariant Signal-to-Distrotion Ratio
(SI-SDR) were used to evaluate how closely the model outputs
match the reference signals. Word Error Rate (WER) was used
as an additional intrusive metric to assess the impact of SE mod-
els trained with the proposed loss function on automatic speech
recognition (ASR) systems, such as speech-to-text. Transcrip-
tions of the enhanced audio were generated using Whisper [28],
a general-purpose speech recognition model developed by Ope-
nAI. These were then compared with human-annotated refer-
ences to calculate WER.
Evaluation metrics are reported across two datasets for all
trained models, including the baselines for SEMamba and MP-
SENet, as well as the original noisy signals. The first dataset is
the ICASSP 2023 DNS5 Challenge Track 1 Blind Test Set [29],
a crowd-sourced collection of 389 noisy audio files recorded in
real-world environments with authentic background noise. File
durations range from 10 to 365 seconds. However, due to the
high memory requirements of MP-SENet, evaluation is limited
to the first 30 seconds of each file, as processing longer seg-
ments exceeds the available 48 GB of GPU memory. The sec-
ond dataset is the V oiceBank-DEMAND noisy test set, which
was synthetically generated using clean speech from the CSTR
VCTK Corpus and noise samples from the DEMAND database.
It comprises a total of 824 audio clips, ranging from 1 to 10 sec-
onds in duration, mixed at various SNR levels.
4.2. Results
Evaluation results for the DNS and V oiceBank-DEMAND test
sets are presented in Table 1. All models trained using Dual-
Asymmetric loss are shown with the three selectedλvalues,
alongside the available baselines and noisy unprocessed signals.
The results of models trained withλ= 0.35reflect perfor-
mance when the loss is weighted more heavily toward noise
suppression than speech reconstruction. This effectiveness is
evidenced by the highest BAK scores achieved within each
model group, inclusive of the corresponding baselines, across
both datasets. Models trained withλ= 0.5demonstrate how
the loss function performs when the two terms are evenly bal-
anced. These models exhibit a good balance of SIG and BAK
metrics. They also achieved the highest SI-SDR scores, outper-
forming the respective baselines. Finally, models trained with
λ= 0.65reflect performance when the loss is weighted more
heavily toward speech reconstruction than noise suppression.
These models achieve the highest SIG scores across all three
λvalues for both datasets, with SEMamba and MP-SENet out-
performing their respective baselines on the DNS5 Blind Test
Set. The baseline models achieved higher scores across multiple
evaluation metrics on the V oiceBank-DEMAND test set, par-
ticularly in PESQ, for which they were specifically optimized.
However, the improved metrics of models trained with Dual-
Asymmetric loss on the out-of-distribution DNS5 Blind Test Set
demonstrate the generalizability of the proposed loss function.
Furthermore, all the models improved WER by32−45%rela-
tive to the noisy baseline, indicating that Dual-Asymmetric loss
improves speech intelligibility for ASR systems.

5. Conclusion
This study investigated the impact of a novel objective function,
Dual-Asymmetric Loss, which incorporates separate penalty
terms to independently target speech reconstruction and noise
suppression. An adjustable weighting parameter enables a SE
model to be tuned for either higher-quality speech or higher-
performance noise suppression. A single SE model was trained
across 13 distinct parameter values, yielding a PCC of0.95be-
tween the weighting parameter and speech quality, and−0.96
for noise suppression performance, both statistically signifi-
cant. These strong correlations were verified across four high-
performing causal and non-causal models evaluated on two dis-
tinct datasets. All models demonstrated increased speech qual-
ity metrics with higher parameter values and increased noise
suppression performance with lower values. Furthermore, the
proposed loss function achieved improvements in DNSMOS
SIG, BAK, and SI-SDR metrics compared to publicly available
SOTA baseline models.
