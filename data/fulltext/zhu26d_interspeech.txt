Abstract
Current music deepfake detectors rely on a discriminative
paradigm that fits decision boundaries to known fake distri-
butions. As generative algorithms evolve, these methods suf-
fer from structural vulnerability due to non-stationary distri-
bution shifts. We propose DASM, a framework that models
the stable distribution of authentic audio rather than tracking
evolving forgeries. A learnable memory bank is trained ex-
clusively on authentic samples via reconstruction loss to repre-
sent a compressed manifold prior. A dual-branch classifier uti-
lizes both original features and memory-reconstructed features
as complementary inputs, identifying forgeries as manifold-
space divergence from the authentic distribution. Furthermore,
a lightweight prompt tuning strategy adapts a frozen MERT en-
coder to the detection task with minimal parameters. Experi-
ments on the SONICS dataset demonstrate state-of-the-art per-
formance and robust resistance to acoustic degradations.
Index Terms: AI-Synthetic music detection, manifold learning,
memory augmentation, prompt tuning.
1. Introduction
AI-driven music generation platforms are reshaping digital au-
dio creation. Systems such as Suno [1] and Udio [2] can syn-
thesize music from textual prompts with quality increasingly
indistinguishable from authentic compositions. Such rapid de-
velopment has prompted recent legal actions from the Record-
ing Industry Association of America [3]. Although a substantial
body of work has emerged in the field of deepfake audio detec-
tion [4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9], and has gradually extended to the mu-
sic domain. Recent work has explored singing voice deepfake
detection, including SingFake [10] and the CtrSVDD bench-
mark [11]. Existing discriminative methods require both real
and forged samples during training, and the quality of their de-
cision boundaries is constrained by the coverage of forged sam-
ples in the training set. When confronted with novel generation
algorithms unseen during training, such methods face a struc-
tural blind spot: their decision boundaries encode generator-
specific artifacts rather than intrinsic authenticity properties.
This highlights a methodological issue: existing methods’
core reference frame is determined by the selection of forged
samples rather than the intrinsic properties of real audio. We in-
stead construct an explicit reference from real audio, redefining
detection as deviation measurement—the model depends solely
on real audio collection rather than specific forgery algorithms.
We propose DASM, anchored to real audio features. Its
core component is a learnable memory bank trained exclusively

Phase 2, the memory bank is frozen and a dual-branch clas-
sifier is trained: Branch 1 takes original features; Branch 2
takes their element-wise deviation from reconstructed features.
A lightweight Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) strategy

On SONICS [12], DASM achieves 0.13% Equal Error
Rate (EER), 99.89% Accuracy (ACC), and 99.98% Area Un-
der Curve (AUC) with strong robustness under acoustic degra-
dations. The main contributions are:
• A real-audio-anchored detection paradigm that reframes
forgery detection as deviation measurement from the real au-
dio manifold.
• A two-phase training strategy decoupling manifold learning
from discriminative learning.
• A dual-branch classifier encoding both absolute input rep-
resentation and its geometric departure from the real audio
manifold.
2. Related Work
Early audio anti-spoofing was driven by the ASVspoof chal-
lenge series [5, 6], employing handcrafted spectral features
combined with Gaussian Mixture Models. Deep learning
methods subsequently advanced the field: RawNet2 [9] in-
troduced end-to-end waveform processing; AASIST [8] ap-
plied graph neural networks for spectro-temporal modeling;
SSL Anti-spoofing [7] leveraged self-supervised speech rep-
resentations. Music-specific methods further improved detec-
tion, including lightweight CNN baseline M5 [13], graph-based
SingGraph [14], source-separated MOSS [15] and wavelet-
enhanced WPT-XLSR-AASIST [16]. Despite these advances,
all existing methods operate under a discriminative paradigm
whose decision boundaries are implicitly shaped by training-
time fake patterns, resulting in inherent vulnerability when
forged distributions shift.
Memory-augmented networks have demonstrated effective-
ness in anomaly detection by learning prototypical representa-
tions of normal samples [17, 18]. However, such approaches
remain unexplored in audio forensics. Moreover, existing
reconstruction-based methods typically reduce detection to a
scalar error threshold, discarding the directional structure of the
deviation signal and conflating the learning of normal audio rep-
resentations with discriminative classifier training.

3.1. Phase 1: Authentic Manifold Modeling
Phase 1 learns a latent feature space approximating the real mu-

thentic acoustic characteristics.
Music-Aware Feature Extraction.We utilise the frozen
MERT encoder to extract audio featuresF orig∈R T×D , where
TandDdenote time frames and feature dimension. Pre-trained

learning, MERT provides music-aware representations well-
suited to capturing the subtle timbral and structural properties
unique to musical content.

real music manifoldR real.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
4.1.1. Dataset
We evaluate DASM on the SONICS dataset [12], which serves
as the primary training benchmark. It contains 97,164 songs, in-
cluding 48,090 real tracks from YouTube and 49,074 fully syn-
thetic tracks generated by Suno and Udio. Audio is sampled at
24 kHz and split into training, validation, and test sets at a 7:1:2
ratio.
For cross-dataset evaluation, we use FakeMusicCaps [21],
which contains 39,056 audio clips. Among them, 5,324 are
authentic tracks sourced from MusicCaps, and 33,732 are syn-
thetic tracks generated by five state-of-the-art models: Music-
Gen, MusicLDM, AudioLDM2, StableAudio, and Mustango.
Audio is sampled at 24 kHz and split into training, validation,
and test sets at a 7:1:2 ratio.
SONICS features fully synthesized music with both vo-
cals and background instrumentation, providing complete fake
tracks for training. In contrast, FakeMusicCaps includes a
mix of fully synthesized tracks, vocal-only synthesis, and
instrumental-only synthesis, offering diverse generation scenar-
ios for cross-dataset evaluation.
4.1.2. Implementation Details
Audio inputs are resampled to 24 kHz and segmented into
10-second clips (240,000 samples). The memory bank uses
N= 2048manifold prior with 8-head cross-attention and top-
ksparse retrieval (k= 64); these values balance detection per-
formance and training stability, as largerkintroduces recon-
struction noise from irrelevant cores without consistent EER
improvement.
Training on SONICS follows two phases. Phase 1 pre-
trains the memory bank on 37,582 real clips via frozen MERT-
330M [19] with MSE reconstruction and orthogonality regular-
ization, optimized using AdamW (η= 1×10 −4, wd1×10 −5)
with cosine annealing for 10 epochs without augmentation.
Phase 2 freezes the memory bank and adapts MERT-
330M with 10 learnable prompt tokens per layer. Aug-
mentations—convolutive, impulse, and stationary noise (SNR
10–40 dB), time shifting (±20%), volume perturbation (0.8–
1.2×), Gaussian noise (σ= 0.005), and compression (64–128
kbps)—are applied at 50% probability. The classifier is jointly
optimized with Cross-Entropy and A-Softmax (m= 4) via
AdamW (η= 2×10 −5, wd1×10 −5), cosine annealing for
20 epochs, early stopping (patience 10), and batch size 16 on an
RTX 4090.
4.1.3. Evaluation Metrics
To assess the classification performance of the proposed method
against baseline models, we employ three standard metrics:
Equal Error Rate (EER), Accuracy (Acc), and Area Under the
Curve (AUC).
4.2. Comparative Evaluation
Table 1 compares DASM with state-of-the-art audio deepfake
detectors on SONICS. DASM achieves 0.13% EER, 99.89%
accuracy, and 99.98% AUC, outperforming all baselines. Tradi-

RawNet2) methods lag behind music-specific ones like Sing-
Graph (1.59% EER) and WPT-XLSR-AASIST (1.04% EER).
DASM’s memory-augmented design explicitly models authen-

4.3. Ablation Study
Table 2 presents the ablation results on the SONICS dataset.
Completely removing the memory bank increases the EER to
0.52%, confirming the importance of real manifold encoding.
Using only original features or only reconstructed features both
result in performance degradation, whereas the dual-branch de-
sign integrating the two signals achieves the best performance,
validating the complementarity of the two signals. Prompt tun-
ing is introduced only in Phase 2 to adapt the frozen MERT
encoder to the real-vs-fake discrimination task; removing it in-
creases the EER to 0.89% and reduces the accuracy to 98.27%,
indicating that it is indispensable for effective task adaptation.

4.5. Cross-dataset Evaluation
Cross-domain evaluation on FakeMusicCaps (Table 4) high-
lights the inherent difficulty of music deepfake detection under
distribution shift.
Despite this, DASM achieves 25.59% EER and 75.77%
accuracy, outperforming all baselines including the strongest,
SingGraph (30.92%), by a relative 17.2% EER reduction. Tra-
ditional methods (M5, RawNet2, SSL Anti-spoofing) degrade
substantially, with EERs above 39% and accuracy below 60%.
This relative advantage suggests that real-manifold model-
ing provides more generator-agnostic authenticity cues; nev-
ertheless, the absolute EER of 25.59% reflects the substantial
acoustic domain gap between SONICS and FakeMusicCaps,
indicating that cross-domain robustness remains an open chal-
lenge for all current methods.

4.6. Reconstruction Error Analysis
Figure 2 illustrates the discriminative signals from reconstruc-
tion. The left subfigure shows the CDF of Euclidean distances
between original and reconstructed features. Real audio has a
mean error of 390.84, while forged audio exhibits a higher mean
of 506.83, indicating that forged samples produce systematic
deviations due to their departure from the real audio manifold.
The right subfigure shows the CDF of cosine similarities.
Real audio achieves 0.2769 versus 0.1822 for forged audio,
demonstrating stable directional consistency for real samples
and geometric deviation for forged ones.
Together, both metrics confirm that the memory bank en-
codes a manifold boundary that separates real from forged audio
in complementary geometric dimensions.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we propose DASM, a music deepfake detection
framework that reframes detection by modeling the stable dis-
tribution of real audio instead of tracking the non-stationary
space of fake audio. At its core, the memory bank is trained
only on authentic samples to encode the real audio manifold as
learnable manifold prior. Detection is performed by measuring
reconstruction deviation from this manifold, allowing the sys-
tem to identify forgeries without explicit modeling of fake dis-
tributions during training. Experiments on SONICS show that
DASM achieves state-of-the-art performance and strong robust-
ness under diverse acoustic degradations, confirming that real-
audio-anchored detection is a stable and effective solution for
music deepfake detection. While primarily targeting fully AI-
synthetic music, DASM’s deviation magnitude naturally quan-
tifies AI involvement in AI-assisted content, providing a techni-
cal foundation for administration.
