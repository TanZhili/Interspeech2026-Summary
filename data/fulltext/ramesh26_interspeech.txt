Abstract
Hard boundaries are commonly used as ground truth targets
for text-independent supervised phone segmentation. However,
prior works do not explicitly model the uncertainty in phone
transition annotations. To address this limitation, we propose
the use of soft targets in place of hard boundaries. The am-
biguity of transition around boundary events is modeled using
two unnormalized zero-mean half Gaussian pulses centered at
each boundary indicated by the hard targets, with standard de-
viations proportional to neighboring segment durations. Hard
labels within two standard deviations of the boundary are re-
placed with Gaussian function values, while the other labels re-
main unchanged. Using soft targets, a baseline BiGRU model
achieves an R-val of 91.53% on the TIMIT test split. Experi-
ments on out-of-domain and multilingual data show statistically
significant improvements over hard targets.

1. Introduction
Segmentation in speech processing involves dividing the speech
signal into small meaningful units that are acoustically similar
within the marked boundaries. This process is crucial for var-
ious speech processing tasks such as keyword spotting (KWS)
and automatic speech recognition (ASR)[1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. In this
paper, we look into the problem of phone segmentation, where
the meaningful units are phones. Acoustic signals, being con-
tinuous in nature, pose a significant challenge. The same utter-
ance can have different acoustic realizations, and thereby lack
clear segment boundaries. They also encode orthogonal infor-
mation like style and content, further complicating the develop-
ment of a universal speech segmentor.
Despite these difficulties, numerous supervised and unsu-
pervised machine learning techniques for phone segmentation
have been proposed in recent years. These methods encompass
text-dependent and text-independent approaches, in which the
former effectively incorporates textual information in the tar-
gets while the latter focuses on including only non-textual as-
pects in their ground truth [3, 6, 7, 8]. This is in contrast to
unsupervised segmentation methods which detect variations in
speech sounds by examining natural data patterns in the absence
of pre-labeled examples [9, 10, 11]. Our method falls into the
category of text-independent supervised phone segmentation.
There are two principal aspects that govern the foundations
of any supervised learning paradigm: feature extraction, and the
learning technique [8],[6, 12]. Prior works in text-independent
supervised phone segmentation have extensively focused on

these aspects to improve the segmentation quality. But, there
exists a third component, namely the target quality, that isn’t
well explored in the literature. Our proposed method views the
phone segmentation problem from the viewpoint of the target
that provides the supervision.
A straightforward way to represent boundary information
is to encode frames corresponding to a boundary event as ones
and all other frames as zeros. We refer to these targets ashard
boundaries, which have been used in prior works [6, 7, 12].
Such targets frame phone segmentation as the detection of tran-
sition events by suppressing non-boundary frames and empha-
sizing boundary locations. However, phone transitions in natu-
ral speech occur gradually and are not as abrupt as implied by
hard boundaries. Consequently, representing boundaries using
a single frame provides only a limited depiction of the underly-
ing acoustic transition. To address this limitation, we propose
a mechanism that roughly models the uncertainty of boundary
transitions. Specifically, we replace non-boundary frames near
a hard boundary with values from two unnormalized zero-mean
half Gaussian pulses centered at the boundary, with a standard
deviation proportional to the neighboring phone durations.
The learning paradigm choice in our paper is influenced by
a prior work, where boundary events are encoded using hard la-
bels [7]. As seen earlier, these types of targets are weak depic-
tions of boundary events. Therefore, we extend their method to
use soft labels, which will be explained in detail in the method-
ology section.
The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 discusses
works related to phone segmentation. Section 3 details our
proposed methodology for generation of soft labels from hard
boundaries. Section 4 describes the experimental setup and
evaluations, followed by results and analyses. Section 5 con-
cludes the paper by discussing potential future works.
2. Related Works
Unsupervised phone segmentation aims to locate boundaries
without the use of labeled data. Kreuk et al. introduced a con-
trastive learning methodology, wherein representations of an-
chor frames are made similar to the adjacent frame and pushed
farther away from non-adjacent ones [9]. Boundaries are in-
ferred from changes in these representations, which encourages
similar representations for adjacent frames. Their proposed
paradigm serves as a good unsupervised baseline, but suffers
over-segmentation. Building on internal model signals, Wang
et al. showed that Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) gate activa-
tions carry boundary information, with peaks in gate dynam-
ics aligning with phone transitions [11]. The authors have also
shown that first layer GRU activations model the phonetic tran-
sitions better than the deeper layers. Another line of work relies

In contrast, supervised approaches rely on labeled phone

gets despite the inherently smooth nature of phone transitions.
Subsequent work introduced structured loss functions , which
incorporate global consistency constraints on predicted bound-
aries [6]. However, these structured objectives substantially in-
crease computational cost, making them less practical for large-
scale training.
Furthermore, an auto regressive model was proposed to pre-
dict the existence of a boundary event at timesteptbased on
boundary evidence fromt−1[12]. This model was trained
for more than1000epochs to achieve the best R-value. Results

supervised (SSL) models like HuBERT and wav2vec 2.0 to de-
cide the presence of a boundary event at any timestep [15]. Ex-
periments were performed with both pre-trained and finetuned
SSL models listed above. The aforementioned two approaches
are computationally expensive, as evident from their training
strategies. To model uncertain phone transitions efficiently, we
propose using soft boundaries to address the limitations of prior
approaches.

the input waveform. They are then passed through two layers of

activation function is applied on the outputs of the projection
head following the final layer of BiGRU to get probabilities of
boundaries at each timestep as outputs. The traditional binary
cross entropy loss between predicted and soft targets, with a
weight factor for the positive class is incorporated as the learn-
ing paradigm. Mathematically, our loss function is defined as

4. Experiments
Our model was constructed utilizing2BiGRUs, with a hidden
dimension of39units, succeeded by a linear projection from
a78-dimensional space to a1-dimensional scalar. The bound-
ary probabilities are obtained via a sigmoid activation function.
The learning rate was set to10 −3. Since there are more non-
boundary events than boundary events in the target, there is a
class imbalance. To address this issue, we setw + to4for mod-
els trained with soft boundaries, and5for models trained with
hard boundaries. This was done based on empirical evidences
suggestive of improved performance in both cases for the cor-
respondingw +. A39-dimensional Mel-frequency cepstral co-
efficient (MFCC) (inclusive of delta and delta-deltas), was em-
ployed using frame size of30ms and frame stride of10ms.

The spread ratioKwas set to0.2. A phonetic segment lasts for
10-15frames on average. Intuitively, the boundary transitions
may have gradually started2frames before the hard target, and
come to a halt2frames after the hard target. This led us to the
choice ofK. The training split of TIMIT and Buckeye corpus
were utilized for training, while out-of-domain testing involved
1hour of data from the test portion of the BUCKEYE corpus
for TIMIT trained model and vice-versa. We adhered to pre-
processing protocols similar to prior works to partition the data
into training, validation, and test sets, as well as to segment
longer sequences into manageable chunks [6, 9]. For multi-
lingual evaluation, publicly available datasets for Telugu and
Hindi were utilized, each featuring1-hour splits. The Telugu
dataset was made publicly available by Microsoft and Spee-
chOcean [17]. The Hindi dataset was obtained from an ASR
challenge conducted by the speech lab at IIT Madras [18]. Ad-
ditionally, multilingual evaluation was also done on German, a
close counterpart to English, using a3-hour split of the pub-
licly available Carina dataset [19]. The phonetic alignments for
Dravidian and Indo-European datasets were obtained through
kaldi-based forced aligners.

5. Conclusion
In this work, the role of target representation in text-
independent supervised phone segmentation was investigated.
Duration-aware soft targets, which model the uncertainty asso-
ciated with gradual transitions between phones instead of treat-
ing boundaries as single-frame events, were proposed. Across
in-domain, cross-dataset, and multilingual evaluations, the pro-
posed soft targets consistently outperformed conventional hard
targets. Ablation studies further showed that these gains arise
primarily from smoothing the boundary supervision rather than
from the choice of a particular kernel. These findings highlight
target representation as an important yet underexplored com-
ponent of supervised phone segmentation and suggest that su-
pervision should better reflect the continuous nature of speech
transitions. They demonstrate that better-designed supervision
targets can improve phone segmentation without modifying the
underlying segmentation architecture. Future work includes
making spread ratioKadaptive, and applying the proposed
framework to articulatory data, where transition modeling is
particularly important.
