Abstract
Deploying speaker retrieval systems requires robust cross-
domain embedding generalization. However, existing bench-
marks focus on verification metrics, leaving ranking stability
under retrieval constraints under-explored. This paper evalu-
ates six pre-trained embedding models across multiple cross-
domain scenarios. First, while supervised multi-scale models
resist channel filtering and aging drift, most architectures over-
fit to language-specific phonetic variations under cross-lingual
mismatch. Second, we leverage adaptive symmetric normaliza-
tion as a training-free backend strategy to improve retrieval per-
formance. By selecting high-scoring background cohorts to es-
timate localized score statistics, this strategy normalizes global
shifts caused by channel distortions and restores ranking consis-
tency across all models. These insights demonstrate that com-
bining multi-scale local features with adaptive backend calibra-
tion is effective for cross-domain speaker retrieval.

1. Introduction
Speaker retrieval (SR) is a core technology for large-scale au-
dio information retrieval. It supports applications such as au-
dio archive indexing, forensic voice tracking, and personalized
media services [1–3]. Driven by the growth of unconstrained
acoustic data, practical systems need to index speakers across
diverse and adverse environments [4–6]. To handle massive and
heterogeneous audio streams efficiently, real-world engineering
relies heavily on a plug-and-play architecture [7]. Model re-
training on target domains is often computationally expensive
and raises privacy risks. Therefore, practitioners deploy off-the-
shelf, pre-trained speaker models. These models extract identity
embeddings, which are then immediately funneled into high-
throughput vector databases for indexing.
Consequently, the viability of practical SR relies on the
cross-domain robustness of these frozen speaker representa-
tions. However, a gap persists between standard evaluations
and the requirements of real-world retrieval [8]. While speaker
models are continually evaluated on in-domain datasets, their
validation almost exclusively focuses on speaker verification
(SV) paradigms. These paradigms rely on binary metrics such
as the Equal Error Rate (EER) [9–11]. Crucially, SV operates
as a local hypothesis test that classifies pairs via a post-tuned
decision threshold. This threshold is typically optimized on a
specific development set to balance false acceptances and false
rejections. In contrast, SR is a threshold-agnostic, global 1:N

ranking task. It requires the embedding space to maintain con-
sistent similarity rankings and topological order across the en-
tire database. When encountering unseen distribution shifts, a
model with a low EER may still suffer from severe rank in-
version, where correct matches are displaced by out-of-domain
distractors.
In real-world scenario, domain shifts are triggered by
acoustic variations. These variations include transmission chan-
nel distortions, multilingual queries, aging drift, and ambient
noise [12, 13]. Such factors introduce systematic feature drift.
This drift skews similarity rankings, compresses the angular
margins between different speakers, and increases false alarms
within the database. Various supervised [14–16] and self-
supervised [17,18] embedding architectures demonstrate strong
verification performance under controlled conditions. However,
their resilience against ranking degradation in global retrieval
tasks is not yet fully understood. Most architectures are trained
using classification losses that do not explicitly optimize the
global distance topology required for large-scale SR. Conse-
quently, embeddings that are well-separated in local clusters
may still suffer from severe overlap when projected into an un-
constrained, large-scale search space.
To address this issue, this paper evaluates the generaliza-
tion boundaries of speaker embeddings under realistic retrieval
constraints. Different from standard binary verification metrics,
our protocol simulates a large-scale retrieval environment. We
evaluate queries against an unbalanced gallery that contains dis-
tractor speakers. This setup allows us to observe how domain
mismatches affect the relative ranking of both matching and
non-matching targets. The primary objective is to measure the
ranking distortion caused by different domain mismatches in the
embedding space. Furthermore, we investigate the viability of
the Adaptive Symmetric Normalization framework as a back-
end strategy to mitigate channel-induced performance drops.
This training-free calibration scheme dynamically selects top-
scoring background cohorts to standardize the score distribu-
tions. We evaluate whether this method can correct global shifts
in similarity scores and restore the rank order without fine-
tuning the underlying neural networks. This strategy provides a
computationally efficient alternative for real-world production
systems where frequent model updates are impractical.
The contributions of this work are three-fold:
• We establish an SR evaluation pipeline to test global similar-
ity ranking consistency across diverse domains.
• We analyze how supervised and self-supervised speaker em-
bedding models perform under distinct domain mismatches.
• We leverage the Adaptive Symmetric Normalization frame-
work as a training-free backend strategy to restore ranking
stability without model retraining.

uation framework around four common real-world domain
mismatch scenarios, using four corresponding open-source
datasets. The official statistics of these datasets are summarized

The specific acoustic variations for each dimension are defined

1) Channel Mismatch:This setup measures model re-
silience against hardware distortion and transmission filtering.
Using the wideband audio from V oxCeleb2 test set as the refer-
ence, the corresponding counterpart are processed by standard
telephone (G.711 A-law/µ-law) and network codecs [26]. This
creates a wideband-to-narrowband retrieval task with band lim-
iting and quantization noise.
2) Acoustic Environment Mismatch:This setup evalu-
ates the impact of room reverberation and ambient noise. The
reference query set consists of clean near-field recordings from
the VOiCES devkit subset, while the gallery contains far-field
recordings captured by microphone arrays at various angles.
This setup introduces room impulse responses and continuous
background interference.
3) Language Mismatch:This setup examines whether lan-
guage variations affect speaker identity features. Using the
TidyV oice evaluation set, we test the retrieval performance
across linguistic boundaries. The evaluation includes two cross-
lingual tasks:English Query→Non-English Gallery, andNon-
English Query→Unseen Foreign Language Gallery.
4) Age Mismatch:This setup evaluates the stability of
speaker representations against long-term physiological voice
changes. Using the longitudinal data from the voxAging
dataset, the gallery contains the middle-aged and late-stage
recordings of the target speakers. In contrast, utterances
recorded over a decade earlier are used as queries.
3. Experimental Results and Analysis
3.1. Experimental Setup
We evaluate six pre-trained architectures from the 3D-Speaker
Toolkit [27], including CAM++ [16], ECAPA-TDNN [14],
ERes2Net [15], x-vector [28], RDINO [17], and SDPN [18].
All models are pretrained on V oxCeleb2 training set and evalu-
ated directly in their pre-trained states without target-domain
fine-tuning. Embeddings areL 2-normalized, and scoring is
based on cosine similarity.
To ensure consistency, we use a randomized sampling pro-
tocol across all scenarios:
•Speaker Selection:We randomly select 100 mutually exclu-
sive target speakers for each scenario.
•Query-Gallery Partition:For each speaker, 10 utterances
are randomly chosen for the query setQ, and another 10 mu-
tually exclusive utterances form the galleryG.
•Background Distractors:All utterances from the remaining
unselected speakers in the dataset are added toGas out-of-set
distractors to simulate open-set retrieval.
As the ground-truth gallery size is fixed (G= 10) and
speaker identities are binary, we reportP@10to evaluate local
retrieval capacity, andmAPto evaluate global ranking stability.

3.4. Performance Enhancement via Score Normalization
To improve cross-domain retrieval performance without mod-
ifying the pre-trained embedding architectures, we leverage
an Adaptive Symmetric Normalization (ASN) strategy as a
training-free post-processing step [29]. This non-parametric
calibration scheme serves as an efficient backend remedy. It
aims to realign global geometric shifts and distortions of simi-
larity scores caused by acoustic drift without requiring expen-
sive model retraining or fine-tuning. The ASN resolves this by
dynamically selecting a speaker-dependent and test-dependent
cohort comprised of the highest scoring pseudo-impostor utter-
ances relative to the gallery and query vectors. This centering
and scaling dynamically standardizes the cosine similarity met-
rics onto a unified evaluation scale.

the strength of this adaptive selection protocol lies in its data-
driven cohort alignment mechanism. Under extreme domain
shifts, selecting the highest-scoring background files ensures a
cohort that predominantly shares the same language and biolog-

calibration eliminates local variations, successfully preserving
speaker-discriminative angular separation.
Self-supervised architectures such as RDINO and SDPN
display a distinct vulnerability to absolute cosine score atten-
uation due to their lack of explicit class-margin optimization
during pre-training. However, their relative ranking structure
remains effective. Under calibration, the global ranking capa-
bility of SDPN substantially improves, with its mAP increas-
ing from 17.48% to 28.59%. This phenomenon demonstrates
that identity-free pipelines capture robust speaker representa-
tions but suffer heavily from global domain shifts that distort
absolute score distributions. By deploying an adaptive cohort to
structurally estimate localized variances and means at the back-
end, the underlying rank topology is effectively projected back
onto a stable and calibrated space without altering the frozen
front-end embeddings.
4. Conclusion
This paper evaluates six pre-trained embedding models for
speaker retrieval across diverse cross-domain scenarios. Our
benchmark shows that while supervised multi-scale architec-
tures like ERes2Net resist channel filtering and biological ag-
ing, they overfit to English phonetic variations due to data
bias. To handle domain drift without altering front-end embed-
dings, we employ ASN as a training-free post-processing step.
This non-parametric calibration realigns distorted score distri-
butions through data-driven cohort selection, yielding universal
retrieval gains and improves ranking accuracy under channel
mismatch. These insights demonstrate that pairing multi-scale
feature extraction with adaptive backend calibration provides a
practical and effective framework for cross-domain SR.
