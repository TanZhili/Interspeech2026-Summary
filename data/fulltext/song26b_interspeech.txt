Abstract
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) benefits from large Self-
Supervised Learning (SSL) models (e.g., HuBERT, WavLM),
but their immense computational overhead hinders real-time
edge deployment. While mel-spectrograms offer a lightweight
alternative, traditional CNNs struggle to match SSL representa-
tion quality.
To bridge the gap between the two approaches, we propose
MSMC(Multi-Scale Masked Convolution), a highly efficient
architecture for spectrogram-based SER. A masked convolu-
tion encoder (MCE) extracts sparse spectral features without
information leakage, while a mean teacher framework enforces
multi-scale consistency, to effectively distil global semantic
context and micro-prosodic details into a compact model.
Evaluated on the IEMOCAP dataset, MSMC achieves
state-of-the-art accuracy among lightweight models (76.0%
W A), matching the performance of heavy SSL baselines. With
a significantly lower parameter count and computational com-
plexity, MSMC is suitable for real-time SER applications.

1. Introduction
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) is increasingly being de-
ployed as part of speech dialogue systems in call centres and
educational settings [1, 2]. Whenever real-time dialogue is
involved, latency is highly important, as is the computational
overhead required to support large numbers of concurrent chan-
nels. Recent state-of-the-art (SOTA) SER systems are dom-
inated by those using large-scale Self-Supervised Learning
(SSL) foundation models [3, 4]. While they perform very well,
such models often require hundreds of millions of parameters,
imposing prohibitive computational burdens for real-time appli-
cations, or resource-constrained edge devices.
Consequently, there has been resurgent interest in
lightweight architectures, such as those utilising log-Mel spec-
trograms [5]. Spectrograms compactly capture fundamental
acoustic descriptors such as pitch, energy and formants, and are
information-rich [6]. However, traditional Convolutional Neu-
ral Networks (CNNs) operating on spectrograms lack the capac-
ity to model long-range global dependencies effectively, a task
at which Transformer-based SSL models excel [7]. Emotion
in speech can be a long-range global attribute across an entire
utterance at times, disadvantaging short-time spectrogram anal-
ysis.
**indicates the corresponding author.
To overcome this fundamental limitation, we draw in-
spiration from recent advances in the vision domain, specif-
ically the Masked Convolution Meets Masked Autoen-
coders(MCMAE) [8]. MCMAE originally proposed masked
convolutions, building upon the foundational concept of par-
tial convolutions [9] to process corrupted image patches with-
out feature leakage. Adapting and evolving this principle for
the anisotropic nature of audio, we propose theMSMC(Multi-
Scale Masked Convolution) network, featuring a specialized
Masked Convolution Encoder (MCE) tailored for spectrogram-
based SER. The proposed MCE dynamically re-weights con-
volution operations strictly based on temporal-frequency mask
validity. This ensures that the extraction of fine-grained micro-
prosodic cues remains uncontaminated by artificial masking
boundaries, thus effectively preventing feature leakage.
However, while the MCE successfully extracts robust lo-
cal details, emotion in speech is inherently a long-range, global
attribute spanning an entire utterance. To capture overarch-
ing global semantic consistency without excessive architec-
tural scaling, we utilize a Mean Teacher knowledge distilla-
tion framework [10]. In our dynamic dual-branch architecture,
a lightweight student model processes heavily time-masked
spectrograms via the MCE, while an exponential moving aver-
age (EMA) teacher network processes the complete, unmasked
view.[11] This mechanism allows the lightweight student model
to align its representations with a global contextual view, forc-
ing it to reconstruct the global emotional trajectory from frag-
mented temporal segments. Ultimately, this self-distillation
process effectively distills profound long-range modeling capa-
bilities into a highly compact architecture.
MSMC is an efficient SER model that mitigates the need
for heavy SSL backbones with two distinct contributions:
(1) a leakage-free masked convolution encoder coupled with
visible-token-only transformers to maximize computational ef-
ficiency;
(2) a novel multi-scale consistency distillation strategy.
Results presented in Section 4 reveal that MSMC achieves
SOTA performance on IEMOCAP while drastically reducing
parameter count and multiply-accumulate operations (MACs)
over current SSL-based approaches.

Recent SER research performance has been driven largely by
the representation extraction ability of models such as wav2vec
2.0 [12], HuBERT [3], and WavLM [4] when coupled with con-
volutional network-based classifiers. Fine-tuning such mod-
els directly for SER typically requires processing raw wave-
forms through deep transformer stacks and is computationally

Masked Audio Modeling, inspired by MAE [14] and Au-

and distillation mechanism conducted during supervised fine-

Beyond resolving structural mismatches, a com-
pelling wave of empirical re-evaluations—often termed
the “CNN strikes back” movement—has fundamentally
challenged the presumed indispensability of Swin-based
self-attention.Pioneering vision studies, most notably the
ConvNeXt architecture [16], demonstrated that modernizing
standard convolutional blocks can comprehensively outperform
Swin Transformers without their quadratic computational
overhead.This paradigm shift has aggressively cascaded into
the audio domain. Recent breakthroughs provide undeniable
evidence that entirely replacing computationally exorbitant
Swin-attention modules with modernized depthwise CNNs not
only resolves the rigid truncation dilemma but actually elevates
representation quality. Crucially, these audio-adapted pure
CNNs have been shown to achieve superior or equivalent state-
of-the-art precision while demanding up to three times fewer
parameters than their heavy Transformer counterparts [17].
This emerging academic consensus perfectly validates our core
architectural motivation: anchoring our framework on a robust,
variable-length CNN foundation is not a structural regression,
but rather an empirically and mathematically superior strategy
for highly efficient acoustic feature extraction.
Another approach used in recent works is model com-
pression or distillation from SSL models into specialised
lightweight CNNs [18]. Our approach differs by fundamen-
tally improving the lightweight CNN feature extraction capabil-
ity through masked self-distillation rather than simple structural
compression.
3. Methodology
3.1. Overview
As illustrated in Fig. 1, the proposed MSMC framework adopts
a parallel dual-branch Mean Teacher architecture designed to
efficiently extract multi-scale emotional representations from
variable-length speech. Given an input log-Mel spectrogram,
the student branch receives a heavily corrupted view via con-
tinuous temporal and frequency masking (conceptually simi-
lar to the masking strategies in SpecAugment [19]) to simu-
late extreme acoustic information loss. This forces the network
to infer missing micro-prosodic phonetic chunks and overarch-
ing emotional pacing, while the teacher branch conversely pro-
cesses the complete, unmasked acoustic view to provide sta-
ble distillation targets. Both branches initially process their
respective inputs through a hierarchy of MCE blocks, which
safely extract pristine local micro-prosodic features dynami-
cally, completely preventing mask-induced information leakage
in the student branch. To bridge the gap between local convo-
lutional features and global sequence modeling, the MCE out-
puts are enriched with a Conditional Positional Encoding (CPE)
module that dynamically encodes relative spatial-temporal po-
sitions, crucial for preserving the sequential topology of speech.
Subsequently, the features are processed by a lightweight Trans-
former block where, for maximum computational efficiency, the
student branch employs physical token dropping to route only
the unmasked tokens. To comprehensively distill the teacher’s
long-range modeling capabilities into the highly compact stu-
dent, the framework is jointly optimized via a multi-scale con-
sistency mechanism. We enforce representation alignment at
both the local scale, by reconstructing masked regions of in-
termediate dense MCE feature maps, and the global scale, by
aligning the latent semantic vectors after the Transformer block.
Ultimately, the student network is updated via gradient descent
based on these consistency losses alongside the final supervised
class-wise cross-entropy(CE) loss, while the teacher network’s
parameters are updated smoothly via an exponential moving av-
erage (EMA) of the student’s weights without backpropagation.

C. Decoupled Optimization Strategy
Unlike a conventional joint summation of all objectives,
MSMC employs a staged, decoupled training paradigm at each
iteration.
First, thestudent networkis optimized exclusively via
self-supervised and distillation objectives, without direct access
to the ground-truth categorical labels. Its update objective is an
equally weighted sum (i.e., a 1:1 ratio) of the multi-scale recon-
struction loss and the global latent distillation loss:
Lstu =L rec +Lglob (5)
Second, to maintain long-range contextual stability, the
teacher networksoftly incorporates the student’s learned
micro-prosodic features via an Exponential Moving Average
(EMA) of the student’s parameters (θs). The EMA update rate
is set to0.01(i.e., momentumτ= 0.99):
θt←0.99θ t + 0.01θs (6)
Finally, to acquire task-specific emotional semantics, a lin-
ear classification head projects the unmasked teacher’s global
embeddingz tea to emotion logits. The supervised CE loss

This asymmetric design elegantly forces the student to
focus entirely on robust feature extraction from heavily cor-
rupted inputs, while the teacher forms precise emotional de-
cision boundaries on the clean views. During inference, the
teacher’s backbone, the EMA updates, and all masking oper-
ations are completely discarded. Only the lightweight student
network strictly processes the full, unmasked log-Mel spectro-
gram. Its extracted global representation is then passed directly
to the trained classification head. This results in an exception-
ally fast inference stage, making MSMC highly suitable for
real-time edge deployment.
4. Experiments and Results
4.1. Dataset and Experimental Setup
We evaluate the proposed MSMC network on the improvised
subset of the widely used IEMOCAP dataset [20], which con-
sists of 10 unique speakers across 5 recorded sessions. We use
four original emotion categories, namelyangry, happy, neu-
tral, andsad, without mergingexcitedutterances into thehappy
class. This improvised-only setting contains 2,280 utterances in
total.
To properly assess out-of-distribution (OOD) speaker gen-
eralization and prevent identity leakage, we adhere to the strict
10-fold LOSO cross-validation protocol.In LOSO, each fold in-
volves 9 speakers being used for training with 1 unseen speaker
reserved for testing. Performance is measured using both Un-
weighted Accuracy (UA) and Weighted Accuracy (W A) to ac-
count for class imbalance as well as overall performance.
The implementation uses 128-band log-Mel spectrograms
for acoustic features. These are extracted from mono audio at
16 kHz sampling rate. The model is trained from scratch for
80 epochs using the AdamW optimizer. To stabilize the hy-
brid architecture, we employ a differential learning rate strategy
(namely10−4 for the base encoder and5×10 −4 for the classi-
fication head) with a cosine annealing schedule. In addition, we
apply data augmentation during training to improve robustness

masking ratio is set to 60%.

4.2. Ablation Study: Feature Extraction Robustness
To better understand the performance gains yielded by our
main architectural innovations, we design a progressive ablation
study (see Table 1) to demonstrate the step-by-step improve-
ments from basic feature extractors to our full framework.
We first establish aBaseline (vanilla CNN), which con-
sists of a single convolutional layer followed by a custom-
implemented attention pooling module. While this self-
implemented lightweight architecture serves as a fundamen-
tal acoustic feature extractor, it yields a modest Weighted Ac-
curacy (W A) of∼58.5% and Unweighted Accuracy (UA) of
∼59.2%, reflecting its limited capacity to capture complex and
long-range temporal dynamics.
To enhance global sequential modeling, we introduce the
Baseline (CNN+Transformer). In this configuration, the
acoustic features extracted by the initial CNN undergo dimen-
sionality reduction before being fed into a standard Transformer
block. This structural upgrade explicitly models long-range
temporal dependencies, bringing a notable performance boost
to∼66.5% W A and∼60.3% UA.
Building upon these baselines, we integrate our core in-
novations: the MCE blocks and CPE. To rigorously assess
their standalone effectiveness, we evaluate theMSMC (teacher
only)configuration, which acts as a standalone network trained
from scratch without the global mean-teacher distillation frame-
work. Remarkably, even under an aggressive masking strategy
where 60% of the input spectrogram is physically masked and
discarded, this setup achieves 69.1% W A and 63.6% UA. Main-
taining such a highly competitive UA alongside the W A indi-
cates that the model genuinely captures discriminative micro-
prosodic boundaries across all emotion categories, rather than
simply collapsing to majority classes (a common pitfall in im-
balanced SER datasets).
This strong intermediate performance unequivocally
demonstrates that our proposed dynamic re-normalization
effectively prevents mask-induced information leakage,
while the CPE successfully preserves crucial time-frequency
topology. Finally, by incorporating the global mean-teacher
distillation framework, theMSMC (full)model further unlocks
the architecture’s potential, elevating the performance to
an impressive 76.1% W A and 68.8% UA. This consistent
progression validates the powerful synergy between our robust
feature extraction design and the semi-supervised distillation
paradigm.

4.4. Computational Efficiency & Physical Token Dropping
A fundamental limitation of applying standard Vision Trans-
formers to audio is the quadraticO(T 2)self-attention complex-
ity. Unlike previous works that merely zero-pad masked re-
gions (a solution that still requires a full matrix multiplication),
a sparse approach is employed via physical token dropping in
the proposed MSMC network
By strictly routing only the unmasked visible tokens (40%
of the sequence) to the Transformer blocks, the self-attention
sequence length is drastically truncated. This physical trun-
cation yields an authentic, reduction in Multiply-Accumulate
count (namely∼0.9G MACs) with 5.8M parameters), allowing
the MSMC network to be roughly23×more computationally
efficient than WavLM-base. Hence the resulting architecture is
more suitable for real-time deployment, especially on edge de-
vices.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a highly efficient network for speech
emotion recognition operating on log-Mel spectrogram fea-
tures called MSMC (multi-scale masked convolution network).
Combining a leakage-free masked convolution encoder (MCE)
with a multi-scale mean teacher distillation strategy, the pro-
posed lightweight architecture has been endowed with the
context-aware modeling strengths typically reserved for much
larger SSL-based models. Evaluations of MSMC on IEMO-
CAP demonstrate highly competitive speech emotion recogni-
tion performance (i.e. 76.0% weighted accuracy) compared to
SOTA solutions including those SSL models, when evaluated
with the same strict LOSO setting. MSMC’s significantly re-
duced parameter count and computational complexity make it
far more suitable for real-time deployments, especially on edge
devices.
