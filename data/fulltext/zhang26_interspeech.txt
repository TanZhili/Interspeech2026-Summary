AURA: Audio-Geometry Conditioned U-Net Refinement with Flow Matching
for High-Fidelity Monaural-to-Binaural Synthesis
Wenjie Zhang1,∗, Changjun He1,∗, Yinghan Cao1, Shiyun Xu1, Mingjiang Wang1,∗∗
1 Harbin Institute of Technology (Shenzhen), China

Abstract
Spatial audio is crucial for immersive listening, and binaural
rendering is particularly appealing due to its alignment with hu-
man auditory perception. However, synthesizing high-quality
binaural audio from monaural input remains challenging, as
spatial cues must be inferred while preserving both spatial re-
alism and fine-grained timbral detail, limiting its use in VR/AR
and telepresence. To address this issue, we propose AURA, a
two-stage binaural synthesis framework that explicitly models
the relationship between monaural audio and source direction.
The first stage combines global context modeling and local tex-
ture extraction to produce a coarse binaural estimate. The sec-
ond stage refines this estimate with a generative mechanism,
enhancing spatial cues and spectral details for high-fidelity out-
put. Extensive experiments show that AURA outperforms state-
of-the-art methods in both subjective evaluations and objective
metrics (Wave-L2: 0.123 vs. 0.128).

1. Introduction
Audio is central to human perception and interaction, and deliv-
ering high-quality listening experiences is increasingly impor-
tant for virtual reality, mixed reality, and telepresence systems
[1, 2, 3]. While monaural audio conveys content, it lacks the
spatial context required to infer a source’s distance and direc-
tion. Binaural audio, by contrast, encodes spatial cues via inter-
aural level and time differences (ILD/ITD), enabling a stronger
sense of externalization and spatial presence [4, 5]. These prop-
erties have spurred growing interest in binaural audio synthesis,
which aims to recover realistic spatial impressions from readily
available monaural recordings.
A substantial body of prior work relies on classical signal-
processing pipelines that render binaural audio using head-
related transfer functions (HRTFs) [6, 7, 8, 9]. In these ap-
proaches, binaural cues are typically modeled by sparsely mea-
suring responses at discrete spatial locations and then using the
resulting HRTF database to spatialize monaural signals. While
effective in principle, such measurements are expensive and in-
herently tied to fixed capture setups, which makes it difficult to
faithfully account for the relative pose between a (virtual) sound
source and a listener, their potential motion, and the presence of
environmental noise and reverberation in realistic scenes [10].
As a result, producing high-quality binaural renderings that re-
main robust under diverse and dynamic conditions continues to
be a challenging and largely unresolved problem [10, 11].

Given the strong success of deep neural networks in audio-
related tasks, learning-based binaural synthesis has attracted
growing attention for its potential to model spatial cues and
interaural differences more accurately, thereby enabling more
natural listening experiences. Some approaches further lever-
age auxiliary modalities, with visual information being the most
common choice, to facilitate binaural generation [12, 13, 14]. In
this work, however, we focus on the most fundamental setting
where only monaural audio is available. Richard et al. propose
WarpNet, which incorporates a time dynamic warping (TDW)
module and conditioned hyper-convolutions to fuse monaural
audio with the relative source–listener geometry for binaural
synthesis [11]. Leng et al. introduce BinauralGrad, a two-
stage framework that uses diffusion to generate a shared binau-
ral component from the TDW-based estimate and then synthe-
sizes the interaural difference component to produce the final
binaural signal [15]. Lee et al. perform binaural rendering in
the Fourier domain, improving interpretability and robustness
across source domains, leading to the NFS model [16]. He et
al. integrate positional cues via a dual-position attention module
and capture motion-induced Doppler effects [17]. Despite these
advances, most existing methods still fall short of producing
high-fidelity binaural audio that is perceptually indistinguish-
able from real-world recordings.
At the same time, the emergence of flow matching opens
a promising alternative for audio synthesis by learning control-
lable probability paths [18, 19, 20], which can help capture real-
istic acoustics and dynamic environmental noise. However, ap-
plying flow matching to binaural synthesis from monaural input
is intrinsically challenging, because a monaural signal does not
explicitly encode spatial pose, motion state, or other directional
attributes needed to disambiguate binaural cues. To address this
challenge, we cast binaural synthesis as a two-stage process: the
first stage predicts a coarse binaural signal from the monaural
input, while the second stage applies flow-matching-based re-
finement to inject fine-grained spatial and spectral details that
are critical for high-quality binaural rendering.
Overall, we proposeAURA, a two-stage framework for
mono-to-binaural synthesis conditioned on (virtual) source ge-
ometry. AURA first predicts a coarse binaural signal with a hy-
brid Transformer–CNN U-Net that jointly captures long-range
contextual dependencies and fine-grained local binaural tex-
tures while aligning audio content with spatial cues. It then
refines the coarse estimate with conditional flow matching to
recover subtle spatial micro-cues and stochastic acoustic details
that are difficult to infer from monaural input alone. Concretely,
our contributions are threefold: (i) we design a hybrid U-Net
backbone that integrates attention-based context modeling with
convolutional texture extraction for robust coarse binaural pre-
diction; (ii) we introduce an audio–pose interaction mechanism

2. METHODOLOGY
2.1. Overall Architecture
The overall architecture of the proposed AURA and its key com-
ponents are illustrated in Fig. 1. The input to AURA consists of
monaural audio along with the spatial information of (virtual)
sound sources, including position and orientation, hereafter re-
ferred to as Pos and Ori. We adopt Time Dynamic Warping [11]
to process the input audio. The Transformer-CNN Downsample
Blocks (TCDBs) are then employed to extract audio feature rep-
resentations, while the Spatial-Enhanced Residual Upsample
Blocks (SERUBs) generate coarse-grained binaural audio. The
Spatial-Awareness and Motion Attention Mechanism (SAMA)
is utilized to attend to the spatial location and motion state of
the (virtual) sound sources. Additionally, Flow Matching is ap-
plied to refine the coarse-grained binaural audio. The detailed
architecture of AURA is described in the following subsections.
2.2. Encoder-Decoder Architecture
To simultaneously capture both local features and long-range
dependencies in audio representations, the CNN and Trans-
former branches are incorporated within the TCDB, as illus-
trated in Fig. 1(b). Specifically, TCDB first performs downsam-
pling to obtain deep audio features. In the Transformer branch,
multi-head self-attention (MHSA) is employed to process these
features, while a learnable lightweight modulator is introduced
to enhance the Transformer’s capacity for effective feature rep-
resentation. Subsequently, a seamlessly integrated SimpleGate
(SG) [21] replaces the conventional feed-forward network, al-
lowing more efficient information propagation. Residual con-
nections are applied to both processes to compensate for poten-
tial information loss and to stabilize the learning of deep repre-
sentations.
In parallel, the CNN branch leverages 1×1 and 3×3 depth-
wise convolutions to encode channel-wise and spatial context,
effectively capturing local patterns and incorporating inductive
biases. Residual connections within the CNN branch further
ensure the integrity and stable transmission of information. Fi-
nally, the outputs from the Transformer and CNN branches are
fused, achieving a collaborative representation of both local and
global audio characteristics, which provides a robust foundation
for subsequent audio synthesis in the AURA framework.
To enhance the model’s ability to perceive spatial position-
ing during audio feature reconstruction, spatial information is
concatenated at multiple stages within SERUB, effectively im-
proving the model’s spatial awareness. Additionally, to pre-
serve low-level feature information and prevent loss of impor-
tant details during the downsampling process, SERUB incorpo-
rates the audio feature information from earlier stages, ensuring
that the model retains critical features throughout the processing
pipeline. The detailed structure is shown in Fig. 1(a).

sources is crucial for accurately presenting spatial percep-
tion. To enable the model to attend to the spatial positioning
and directional changes of (virtual) sound sources, we intro-
duce Spatial-Awareness and Motion Attention (SAMA) within
AURA. The structure of SAMA is illustrated in Fig. 2. It com-

2.4. Conditional Flow Matching Refinement
Learning a mono-to-binaural mapping requires not only inject-
ing geometric cues but also recovering fine-grained acoustic
details such as background ambience and subtle spatial micro-

ior in monaural recordings. To model such residual details be-
yond the coarse prediction, AURA employs Conditional Flow
Matching, abbreviated as CFM, as a refinement stage, as shown
in Fig. 1, subfigure d. Compared to diffusion-based generators
[22, 23, 24, 25, 26], CFM provides a continuous-time formu-
lation with stable optimization and a simpler training objective
[18].

3. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
3.1. Dataset
We evaluate our method using the dataset proposed in [11]. The
listeners are represented by a KEMAR mannequin equipped
with binaural microphones. Participants are instructed to move
around the KEMAR mannequin and speak within a radius of 1.5
m, resulting in a total of 2 hours of paired monaural and binau-
ral recordings sampled at 48 kHz. To the best of our knowledge,
this is currently the only publicly available dataset recorded out-
side an anechoic chamber. For consistency and comparability,
we follow the original training, validation, and test splits as de-
scribed in [16].

3.2. Evaluation Metrics
Considering factors such as auditory experience, we conduct
both quantitative and perceptual evaluations of the synthesized
binaural audio. Quantitatively, we report Wave-L2 (waveform
MSE to the ground truth) and, in the STFT domain, Phase-L2
and Amp-L2 (phase and magnitude MSE, respectively). For
perceptual evaluation, we recruit 15 listeners to rate test-set
samples on a 1–5 scale, including MOS (overall quality), Spa-
tial MOS (spatialization quality), and Similarity MOS (percep-
tual similarity to the ground truth).
3.3. Implementation details
In our experiments, each training batch contains 200 ms of au-
dio. Both TCDB and SERUB consist of 6 layers, withN en in
SERUB set to 3. For flow matching refinement,N down,Nmid,
andNup are set to 1, 3, and 1, respectively. We use a fixed-step
Euler solver with 10 steps, andσis set to 0.01. Moreover,λwav,
λPhase ,λAmp, andλCFM are set to 1.0, 1.0, 0.01, and 1.0, re-
spectively. Batch normalization is used for normalization, and
GELU is used as the activation function.
The optimizer used in the experiments is Adam [28], with
a maximum learning rate of1×10 −3 and a minimum learning
rate of1×10 −5. The learning rate is adjusted using a cosine
annealing strategy [29], with a linear warm-up over 1000 steps.
The model is trained for a total of 300,000 steps.

We conduct both quantitative and perceptual evaluations of the
synthesized binaural audio1, with the results presented in Tables

4.2. Ablation Studies
To analyze the contributions of different components in AURA,
we conduct an ablation study, as summarized in Table 3. Re-
moving the TDW module (w/o TDW) leads to a performance
drop across all metrics. Excluding the Transformer or CNN
branch (w/o Trans. branch or w/o CNN branch) results in Wave-
L2 increasing to 0.132 and 0.130, and Amp-L2 to 0.031 and
0.029, indicating that both branches contribute to feature repre-
sentation. When SAMA is omitted (w/o SAMA), the model’s
ability to capture the spatial position and orientation of (virtual)
sources is reduced, leading to Wave-L2 = 0.145 and Amp-L2
= 0.031. The removal of CFM (w/o CFM) has a pronounced
effect on synthesis quality, resulting in Wave-L2 = 0.183 while
Amp-L2 remains largely unchanged, highlighting CFM’s crit-
ical role in high-fidelity audio synthesis. Overall, the ablation
results demonstrate that all components (TDW, Trans. branch,
CNN branch, SAMA, and CFM) contribute jointly to achieving
higher synthesis quality and fidelity.
5. Conclusion
This paper presents a novel binaural audio synthesis model,
AURA, designed to provide a more immersive auditory experi-
ence. AURA synthesizes high-quality and high-fidelity binaural
audio in two main stages. In the first stage, AURA effectively
captures both global and local features through the Transformer
branch and CNN branch, improving audio quality and natural-
ness. SAMA enables the model to attend to the motion and
spatial positions of (virtual) sound sources by simultaneously
focusing on the interrelationship between position and orienta-
tion, thereby significantly enhancing spatial perception. In the
second stage, the use of CFM complements the acoustic de-
tails of sound propagation, achieving fine-grained binaural au-
dio. AURA outperforms state-of-the-art models in both audio
quality and spatial perception. Exploring more realistic binau-
ral audio synthesis will be the next step in our research.
