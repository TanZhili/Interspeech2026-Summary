Abstract
This study proposes a Transformer-based framework for Man-
darin tone recognition that relies solely on suprasegmentalF 0
information. A core-auxiliary branched architecture is intro-
duced, where the core contour encoder (C-Net) learns contextu-
alized tonal representations while detachable auxiliary branches
provide additional gradient pathways through cross-attention
and layer-specific attention pooling during training. By en-
coding structuredF 0 embeddings across syllable, word, and
chunk granularities and modeling rhythmic variability with a
rhythm encoder (R-Net), the proposed approach consistently
outperforms a single-branch baseline, even when the auxiliary
branches are removed during inference, thereby further improv-
ing computational efficiency.
Index Terms: suprasegmental information, tonal contour,
rhythmic variation, Transformers, cross-attention
1. Introduction
Suprasegmental information, or prosody, refers to the duration,
rhythm, meter, pitch and stress patterns that characterize spo-
ken words and utterances. In Mandarin, pitch serves as the
primary acoustic correlate of lexical tones, distinguishing word
meanings (e.g., m ¯a ”mother”, m ´a ”numb”, m ˘a ”horse” and m `a
”scold”). The pitch contours of Tone 1-4 are conventionally rep-
resented using numerical values to indicate pitch register and
direction: high level tone /55/, rising tone /25/, dipping tone
/214/, and falling tone /51/. The neutral tone (Tone 5) mainly
operates as a particle that expresses attitudinal meanings such as
certainty, surprise, or doubt. In connected speech, tones gener-
ally preserve their canonical contour shapes for lexical contrast,
although some undergo contextual tonal variation, leading to
diverse contour realizations influenced by adjacent tones [1].
In running speech, particularly in broadcast news or narra-
tive reading, automatic Mandarin tone recognition has achieved
notable progress in recent years with the advancement of deep
neural networks. It is widely acknowledged that supraseg-
mental features, especially tones, are primarily distinguished
by pitch and are also influenced to varying degrees by dura-
tion and energy. As evidence has emerged that spectral bal-
ance is another reliable acoustic correlate for lexical tone con-
trast, [2, 3] demonstrated that MFCCs can outperform tradi-
tional prosodic features for Mandarin tone recognition. Using
either a DNN–HMM framework [4] or or a convolutional neural
network with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) [5],
subsequent studies further confirmed the benefit of incorporat-
ing articulatory information into tone modeling. More recently,
[6, 7], learned more robust contextual tonal contour representa-
tions using a Transformer-based self-supervised learning frame-
work. Meanwhile, researchers as in [8] showed that an encoder-
decoder framework with a gating mechanism for improved pitch
tracking performs competitively on downstream tone recogni-
tion tasks, achieving results comparable to those obtained using
MFCCs, FFT spectrograms, and raw waveforms.
Mispronunciation detection and diagnosis (MDD) is a
key component of computer-assisted pronunciation training
(CAPT) systems for second-language (L2) learning [9–13].
Motivated by this need, and to facilitate future transfer learn-
ing for tone error detection and diagnosis in Chinese L2 learn-
ers, this study employs a Transformer-based framework to learn
contextualized tonal contours from native speech. Compared
with prior approaches that require higher computational re-
sources, our method provides two main contributions: (1) a
simplified input design that relies primarily on suprasegmental
features, and (2) detachable auxiliary networks that enhance in-
ference performance while improving computational efficiency
[14].
2. Methodology
As illustrated in Fig. 2 the proposed architecture adopts a core-
auxiliary branched framework to enhance the inference robust-
ness and efficiency of the core-branch Contour Network (C-
Net) for sequence tone classification. During training, the core
branch is supported by gradients propagated from two auxiliary
branches that model contextualized tonal contours and rhyth-
mic variability, the latter referred to as R-Net. The architec-
ture incorporates three key components: (1) to capture the influ-
ence of linguistic structure across multiple granularities, BERT-
style input embedding blocks parameterize learnable represen-
tations over syllable (Syl), word (Wrd), and chunk (Chk) units;
(2) the roles of multi-head self-attention in the core branch are
separated from the cross-attention mechanisms in the auxiliary
branches, whose backpropagated gradients guide tonal contour
learning in the core C-Net for final inference; (3) a layer-specific
attention pooling mechanism aggregates information from mul-
tiple intermediate layers to further enhance contour representa-
tion learning in the auxiliary branches.

3.2. Baseline Model: TNet-Full
While the proposed framework adopts a core-auxiliary
branched design, the compared TNet-Full [19] relies on a
single-branch architecture in which the contour encoder (C-
Net) integrates rhythmic information via cross-attention to the
rhythm encoder (R-Net) within the final two syllable-level
Transformer layers to consolidate tonal contour representations.
Under this design, the inputF 0 embeddings are limited to two
linguistic granularities, namely the syllable and word levels.
Nevertheless, both approaches use a two-layer MLP classifier
for tone prediction and share the same 80%, 10%, and 10%
train–development–test split on the FCU-VOICE-360 corpus.
3.3. Ablation Studies
As shown in Table 1, progressively incorporating additional lin-
guistic granularities, ranging from syllable and word to chunk
levels, for tonal contour representation learning in C-Net leads
to clear improvements in tone accuracy even without the use of
auxiliary branches. Additionally, across all three grouping set-
tings, the inference performance of the core branch (C-Net) is
consistently improved when trained with an auxiliary branch,
either C-Net or R-Net, whereas using both auxiliary branches
simultaneously does not provide further gains. Under compa-
rable model designs that differ only in the single-branch ver-
sus core-auxiliary branched architecture, apparently, the v2-m3
model surpasses the baseline TNet-Full. Both models employ
syllable- and word-level linguistic granularities, while v2-m3
further benefits from an auxiliary encoder (R-Net) that cap-
tures rhythmic influences on tonal contour representations, even
though the auxiliary branch is discarded during inference.
To validate the efficiency of the proposed core-auxiliary
branched framework, we measure the Multiply-Accumulate op-
erations (MACs) [22] and inference latency per IPU and per
tone (in milliseconds). As shown in Table 2, for models with
similar architectures but different training configurations, the
reported MACs and inference times demonstrate that v2-m3
achieves faster inference after the auxiliary R-Net is removed.
Consistently, the core-auxiliary branched framework enhances
model robustness and generalizability, while the reduced num-
ber of parameters further improves computational efficiency.
4. Conclusions
By progressively incorporating learnable embeddings across
syllable-, word-, and chunk-level granularities, the proposed
approach effectively captures contextualized tonal contour rep-
resentations and achieves consistent performance improve-
ments. The core-auxiliary branched architecture further en-
hances training robustness by introducing detachable auxiliary
networks that provide additional gradient pathways through
cross-attention and LSAP. Importantly, these auxiliary branches
can be removed during inference without performance degrada-
tion, thereby preserving computational efficiency. Overall, the
findings show that suprasegmental information alone enables
accurate and efficient tone recognition, providing a practical
foundation for tone mispronunciation diagnosis in Chinese L2
learning.
