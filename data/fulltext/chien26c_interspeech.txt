Abstract
Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) powers applications by in-
terpreting users’ emotions from speech. Ensuring fairness in
SER is inherently a two-sided challenge, as a truly fair system
requires neutrality to both speaker-side and rater-side biases.
However, existing debiasing methods rely on explicit attribute
labels for both sides, which are often unavailable. In this work,
we address the challenge of cross-dataset fairness transfer un-
der partial attribute supervision, where only one side (speaker
or rater) has gender labels in the target dataset. We present a
two-stage framework that first fine-tunes a pre-trained CLAP
model with an adversarial debiasing objective to build one-sided
gender-neutral models. We then introduce theATT2Fairtask
vector, derived from the difference between speaker- and rater-
side fair models in a source dataset, and apply it to infer the
absent-side fair model in the target dataset. Our results highlight
the practicality of fairness transfer for building gender-neutral
SER systems across diverse datasets.
Index Terms: speech emotion recognition, fairness, absent at-
tributes, task arithmetic
1. Introduction
Emotion AI facilitated by speech emotion recognition (SER)
is reshaping voice-based technologies by enabling more natu-
ral and emotionally responsive human-machine interactions [1].
As SER systems become embedded in our daily routines and
subject to stringent regulations (e.g., EU AI Act [2]), ensuring
fairness in these systems has become a critical concern [3]. Typ-
ical SER models are trained on data that involves both human
speakersandraters, and thus inherently reflect biases rooted
in human expression and perception [4, 5]. Fairness in SER is
therefore not a “one-sided” concern but fundamentally a “two-
sided” problem, requiring attention to bothspeaker-sideand
rater-sideperspectives [6]. Prior work [7] has demonstrated
that balancing these two perspectives can be achieved by align-
ing the outputs of separately trained one-sided fair models.
While the notion of two-sided fairness offers a principled
solution to mitigate bias in SER, it remains difficult to im-
plement in scenarios where sensitive attributes are unavailable
[8, 9]. Most existing debiasing strategies rely on access to
explicit attribute information to define group membership and
enforce fairness constraints during training [10]. For exam-
ple, Gorrostieta et al. [11] proposed an adversarial framework
to mitigate speaker-side bias related to age and gender, while
Chien et al. [12] addressed rater-side bias by reducing distri-
butional disparities across rater gender groups. More recently,

Upadhyay et al. [13] explored fairness considerations under
cross-domain settings, highlighting the challenges of transfer-
ring bias mitigation strategies across datasets. In contrast, real-
world deployment scenarios often involve partially labeled data,
where attributes are available on only one side (speaker or rater),
and the other side remains unannotated. These approaches
therefore assume that such attributes are available for the side
being debiased [14, 15]. Our work tackles this problem, partic-
ularly when adapting a fair SER model from a source domain
with attributes on both sides (speaker and rater) to a target do-
main with attributes only on one side (speaker or rater).
Our idea is to leverage paired speaker- and rater-side fair
models from an attribute-known source domain to guide the
inference of absent-side fair models in partially labeled target
domains [16]. This follows an analogical reasoning: just as a
speaker-side fair model transforms into a rater-side model in
the source domain, we apply the same transformation to infer
the absent counterpart in the target domain [17]. Our approach
draws inspiration fromtask arithmetic, which enables the trans-
fer of model behavior through vector arithmetic in parameter
space [18]. As illustrated in Fig. 1, we compute the difference
between the parameters of speaker-side and rater-side gender-
neutral models in the source domain to derive anATT2Fairtask
vector that captures the fairness transformation between them,
which is then applied to a one-sided fair model in the target do-
main to obtain the corresponding absent-side fair model. In this
work, we show that simple arithmetic operations can be applied
to transfer fairness knowledge and further infer the absent-side
fair model.

Building on the concept of fairness transfer through task
vectors, we propose atwo-stageframework for inferring two-
sided fair models in domains with only partial attribute avail-
ability. In the first stage, we apply an adversarial debiasing
strategy to CLAP-based models [19] trained on source datasets
with known attributes (e.g., gender), independently construct-
ing speaker-side and rater-side fair models. In the second stage,
we derive theATT2Fairtask vector from the source domain
to infer the absent-side gender-neutral model in the target do-
main, without requiring its gender information. Our contribu-
tions are twofold: (1) defining a cross-dataset fairness trans-
fer setting where only one side has gender labels, and (2)
showing thatATT2Fairachieves gender-neutral fairness trans-
fer across datasets, maintaining recognition accuracy while im-
proving fairness.
2. Research Methodology
In the context of fair SER domain adaptation, we frame the
problem as across-dataset fairness transfertask, where fair-
ness knowledge learned from one dataset is generalized to an-
other dataset under domain shift (Fig. 2). Since a complete fair
SER model requires both speaker-side and rater-side compo-
nents, our focus is on transfer settings where only one side (e.g.,
speaker or rater) has gender labels in the target dataset, while the
other side remains unlabeled. In this case, fairness on the absent
side must be inferred.
To address this problem, we propose a two-stage frame-
work for constructing two-sided gender-neutral SER models
even when attribute supervision is incomplete (Fig. 3). Stage
1 fine-tunes a CLAP model on attribute-known data with adver-
sarial debiasing to obtain speaker- or rater-side gender-neutral
models. In Stage 2, we derive a task vector,ATT2Fair, from
paired fair models in the source domain and apply it to the one-
sided model in the target dataset.
2.1. Stage 1: FairCLAP with Adversarial Learning
Our goal is to mitigate bias originating from either the speaker
or rater perspective. Stage 1 consists of two key components:
(1) fine-tuning CLAP models as foundation emotion encoders,
and (2) incorporating a debiasing objective to enforce attribute
neutrality. The model in the first stage we defined as FairCLAP
in the rest of the paper.

3.1.1. Study Sets
We define two primary datasets based on related work [7]:
S1includes the entire speaker set from each dataset, used for
speaker-side fairness analysis (for gender).S2, known as the
rater-gender biased set, comprises samples where ground truth
labels align exclusively with emotion annotations from either
male or female raters.
3.2. Experimental Settings
3.2.1. Text Query Generation and Implementation Details
We generate queries in the format: “the utterance is{e class
/ note class}.” For example, to ascertain if an utterance ex-
presses happiness, the query would be: “the utterance is{happy
/ not happy}.”
The initial step is to fine-tune CLAP models using text
queries that correspond to specific emotions as our baseline,
which is refined without any fairness constraints to establish a
reference point. We conduct the fine-tuning over 30 epochs,
setting the learning rate to 1×10 −4, with a batch size of 32,
employing Adam as the optimizer. The hyperparameterλis
grid-searched over [10 −3,10 −2]. Subsequently, for the train-
ing of the FairCLAP domain classifier, the speaker-side models
use the S1 set, while the rater-side models utilize the S2 set.
The training strategy follows the methods described in [12].
All models are implemented in PyTorch and trained using an
NVIDIA A100 GPU with 40GB.
3.2.2. Performance Metrics
We report the recognition performance and fairness metrics for
both one-sided fair models. Our target emotion labels are de-
rived from voted ground truths, with emotion recognition per-
formance on S1 assessed using the weighted F1-score. The fair-
ness metrics, denoted as∆SP, are evaluated using the statistical
parity score [28] (ideal value = 0) on both study sets, S1 for
speaker-sideand S2 forrater-side, which measures fairness by
ensuring that the likelihood of a prediction outcome is indepen-
dent of the attribute [29].

Table 1 reports the recognition and fairness performance of

4.3. Embedding Trajectory Alignment underATT2F air
To validate whether the task vectorτinduces a consistent fair-
ness transformation in the target domain, we conduct an embed-
ding trajectory alignment analysis. Specifically, we examine
whether the representation shift caused by fairness modeling
follows a similar direction across source and target domains.
For each utterance, we extract the utterance-level embedding
from the same encoder layer and define the fairness shift direc-
tion as the difference between the embeddings produced by the
two one-sided models. In the source domain, this shift is de-
fined as∆ S, computed from the difference between the rater-
side and speaker-side fair models. In the target domain, the
corresponding shift∆ T is obtained using the inferred absent-
side model generated via theATT2Fairtask vector. To quantify
the directional consistency between the two shifts, we compute
the cosine similarity between∆ S and∆ T for each utterance.
A higher cosine similarity indicates that the inferred fairness
transformation in the target domain follows a direction similar
to that observed in the source domain. The distribution of align-
ment scores is visualized in Fig. 4.

Podcast, as indicated by the dotted vertical lines. This posi-
tive alignment indicates that the representation shifts induced
byATT2Fairin the target domain follow a direction similar to
the fairness transformation observed in the source domain. The
consistency of this alignment across datasets suggests that the

5. Conclusion and Future Work
In this work, we address the problem of fairness in SER from a
two-sided perspective, requiring fairness considerations on both
speaker and rater sides. Specifically, we introduce a two-stage
framework that first constructs one-sided gender-neutral models
via FairCLAP, which fine-tunes a pre-trained CLAP model with
an adversarial debiasing objective, and then infers the absent-
side model by applying anATT2Fairtask vector derived from
attribute-known domains. Our results across multiple datasets
offer interesting insights: 1) FairCLAP maintains strong recog-
nition performance while substantially improving fairness met-
rics; 2)ATT2Fairenables effective cross-dataset fairness trans-
fer even when only partial gender labels are available, providing
a scalable and practical solution to broaden fairness coverage in
SER systems; 3) Further analysis reveals a clear positive shift
alignment across datasets, suggesting thatATT2Fairtransfers a
consistent fairness correction rather than introducing arbitrary
changes. Future work plans to extend our approach to a broader
set of attributes under partially observed or missing supervision.
