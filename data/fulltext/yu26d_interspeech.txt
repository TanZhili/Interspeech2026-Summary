Sweep-RSE: Streaming Region-of-Interest Speech Extraction in Multi-Talker
Scenarios via Explicit Spatial Sweeping

Abstract
Region-of-Interest (ROI) speech extraction is crucial for
AR/VR and hearing aids but challenging in streaming multi-
talker scenarios. Existing methods rely on implicit boundary
conditioning, often failing to isolate targets under dense inter-
ference or suppress noise in empty regions. We propose Sweep-
RSE, a lightweight, fully causal framework utilizing an explicit
Align & Sweep mechanism. By actively scanning the ROI, our
model locks onto target signals via phase coherency and explic-
itly models spatial discrepancies to reject outliers. Integrated
with a Region Speech Detector, it ensures real-timeO(1)pro-
cessing. Experiments demonstrate Sweep-RSE achieves pre-
cise target extraction in complex scenarios and effectively sup-
presses false alarms in silent regions, operating efficiently with
only 1.66M parameters and 3.05 GMACs.
Index Terms: Region-customizable speech extraction, array
signal processing, spatial audio, causal processing
1. Introduction
Region-of-Interest Speech Extraction (R-SE) has emerged as
a critical technology for AR/VR interfaces and smart hearing
aids, enabling users to selectively attend to sounds within a
customizable spatial window. Unlike traditional fixed beam-
forming [1, 2, 3, 4, 5], which relies on discrete sectors or
fixed look directions [6, 7, 8], R-SE requires handling contin-
uous and variable user-defined regions. To provide this flexi-
bility, recent state-of-the-art methods such as ReZero [9] and
DPARNet-RSE [10] introduced continuous windowing mecha-
nisms. These approaches typically formulate region represen-
tations by either aggregating directional features from sampled
grid points or encoding boundary angles into attention queries
to condition the extraction network.
However, these methods rely onimplicit conditioning: they
depend on the network to learn the mapping between spatial
features and target signals without enforcing physical geomet-
ric constraints. This lack of explicit guidance often leads to sub-
optimal performance in dense multi-talker scenarios or spatial
leakage when the target region is empty (i.e., the number of tar-
get sourcesQ= 0). Furthermore, while coordinate alignment
techniques simplify the extraction task [11, 12], they typically
align only to the window center, leaving the spatial variance of
off-center sources to be handled by implicit network learning.
To overcome these limitations, we propose Sweep-RSE, a
lightweight streaming framework that replaces implicit condi-
tioning with anExplicit Spatial Sweepmechanism. Instead of
relying on the network to learn spatial mappings, our approach
actively transforms the coordinate system by virtually steering

2. Proposed Method
This section details the proposedSweep-RSE, a causal frame-
work designed for speech extraction within a variable Region-
of-Interest (ROI). As illustrated in Fig. 1, the system comprises
three key components: a Boundary-Aware Encoder to explic-
itly scan and lock onto targets, a Dual-Path Block for spectro-
temporal refinement, and a Region Speech Detector to handle
empty regions. Crucially, the entire pipeline processes input
frames without referencing future context to guarantee low-
latency streaming.
2.1. Problem Formulation
Given a multi-channel signal captured byMmicrophones, its
Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) representationYm(t,f)
at them-th microphone can be modeled as:
Ym(t,f) =X m,IN(t,f) +X m,OUT(t,f) +N m(t,f)(1)

2.3. Boundary-Aware Encoder
Unlike previous implicit conditioning methods that treat spatial
boundaries as passive features [10, 14], our Boundary-Aware
Encoder actively sweeps the ROI to explicitly identify the opti-
mal steering direction.
2.3.1. Complex Hybrid Feature Expansion (CH-SDB)
The aligned signals are processed by the ComplexHybrid-SDB
(CH-SDB), which redesigns the efficient subgroup process-
ing [15] for the complex domain operations [16]. To preserve
spatial phase relationships, we employ complex 2D convolu-
tions where real and imaginary parts share weights. Specif-
ically, a complex convolution with a3×1kernel (tempo-
ral×spectral) first extracts frequency-independent spatial cues.
To balance spatial and spectral expansion, the feature channels
of both real and imaginary parts are then split in half and fed
into a dual-branch Complex SDB2d: a Strict Branch (3×1
kernel) operates exclusively along the temporal axis to main-
tain phase consistency, while a Context Branch (3×3kernel)
captures spectro-temporal harmonics. The outputs are concate-
nated and fused via LayerNorm and Mish activation, yielding
robust phase-preserved representations.

2.4. Dual-Path Block
We adapt the Dual-Path architecture of SpatialNet [13] into a
causal framework by replacing standard attention with Causal
MHSA and T-ConvFFN. Leveraging the spatial pre-processing
of our encoder, we employ a significantly lighter separator than
the original model to focus solely on spectro-temporal refine-
ment. To support infinite streaming, all temporal layers main-
tain step-wise internal caches, ensuringO(1)processing com-
plexity per frame without future look-ahead.
2.5. Complex Mask and Region Speech Detector (RSD)
The refined representations are fed into two parallel task-
specific heads to generate the final target complex mask and
verify the presence of the speaker. Specifically, the RSD head
is designed to robustly handle empty regions (Q= 0). The
RSD modulates frame-level features using a lightweight gating
mechanism to suppress silent frames and predicts the probabil-
ity of active speech within the target window.
3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset and Simulation Protocol
Following recent studies [9, 10], we generated spatial audio
mixtures on-the-fly.Acoustic Environment:We generated
RIRs using the Image Source Method [19] via gpuRIR [20].
The room dimensions were randomly sampled within[3,10]×
[3,8]×[2.5,4]m, with the reverberation time (T60) ranging
from0.05to0.7s. We simulated a 4-channel square micro-
phone array (r≈5.65cm). To ensure realistic spatial config-
urations, all sources and microphone array were positioned at
least0.5m from walls.Source Material:We used the VCTK
corpus [21] for speech, split into train/val/test sets (8:1:1) for
speaker-independent evaluation. WHAM! noise [22] was added
at SNRs between[5,15]dB. All signals were resampled to 16
kHz.
3.2. Implementation Details
We use a window size of 32 ms and a hop size of 8 ms. To
ensure real-time efficiency, we significantly compress the back-
bone compared to SpatialNet [13], usingB= 3blocks and
C= 32channels with an FFN expansion of64. The tar-
get window width is randomly sampled between10 ◦ and90 ◦.
For the spatial sweep, we employ a fixed-resolution scanning
strategy (5.625◦) with a maximum ofL= 16candidates with
τ= 10. The model is trained for 200 epochs using AdamW
(lr= 3×10 −4) to minimize the joint lossL SI-SDR + 0.1LBCE.
During inference, the RSD detection threshold is set to 0.5.
3.3. Evaluation Scenarios
We evaluate our model on two distinct datasets: a Standard Set
(3k samples) mimicking benchmark conditions [9, 10], and our

3.4. Comparative Methods
To rigorously evaluate Sweep-RSE, we compare it against two
categories of baselines:Standard Benchmarks:We bench-
mark against Oracle MVDR [26, 27, 28, 29], BSRNN [30], Spa-
tialNet [13], and CoS [11].SOTA Proxies for Fair Compar-
ison:To isolate spatial mechanism efficacy, we implemented
proxiesusing our Dual-Path Block backbone replacing only
the spatial conditioning module: 1) ReZero-Proxy [9] adopts
the grid-based spatial representation by aggregating steering
vectors from uniformly sampled grid points within window,
which is then injected via feature-wise addition; 2) DPARNet-
Proxy [10] implements the Boundary-Conditioned Attention,
which projects trigonometric boundary encodings into attention
queries to implicitly condition the extraction network.

4. Conclusion
We presented Sweep-RSE, a strictly causal framework for
Region-of-Interest speech extraction. By introducing an explicit
Align & Sweep mechanism and a Region Speech Detector, it ef-
fectively overcomes the limitations of implicit conditioning in
dense multi-talker and empty-region (Q= 0) scenarios. Cru-
cially, Sweep-RSE achieves this superior spatial selectivity with
a highly compact footprint (1.66M parameters) and minimal
computational cost (3.05 GMACs). Combined with itsO(1)
streaming complexity, our approach provides a highly efficient
and practical solution for real-time edge applications. Future
work will extend this mechanism to separate multiple speakers
within the ROI by leveraging fine-grained phase coherency to
disentangle overlapping sources.
