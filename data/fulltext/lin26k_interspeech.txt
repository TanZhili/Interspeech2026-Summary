Abstract
Existing sound event detection (SED) systems often suffer se-
vere performance degradation in complex noisy environments.
To address this, we propose a noise-robust Boundary-Guided
Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network (BG-CRNN). We in-
troduce a dynamic boundary attention module that constructs
masks by predicting event boundaries. This restricts self-
attention strictly within individual event segments, prevent-
ing target sound features from being contaminated by adjacent
background noise. Furthermore, a Boundary-Guided Attention
(BGA) module utilizes these boundary features to generate gat-
ing weights, adaptively enhancing active event regions. Experi-
ments on the WildDESED dataset demonstrate that BG-CRNN
consistently outperforms baselines across signal-to-noise ratios
(SNRs) from 10 dB to -5 dB. Notably, it achieves a PSDS1 of
0.191 even under the extreme -5 dB condition, confirming its
strong noise robustness.
Index Terms: sound event detection, Boundary-Guided Atten-
tion, noise robustness
1. Introduction
Sound Event Detection (SED) [1, 2] aims to jointly catego-
rize sound events and localize their onset and offset timestamps
within audio recordings [3, 4], holding promising applications
in smart cities [5], intelligent surveillance [6, 7], and health
monitoring [8, 9, 10, 11]. Mainstream methods rely heavily on
large-scale strongly labeled data to train deep neural networks.
To mitigate data scarcity, semi-supervised learning paradigms
leveraging weakly labeled and unlabeled data have gained in-
creasing traction [12, 13]. In the baseline system for DCASE
2023Challenge Task4, the pre-trained self-supervised BEATs
model [14] was combined with a CRNN architecture, achieving
remarkable effectiveness. Subsequently, Shao et al. [15] fur-
ther introduced the ATST-Frame model, which achieved state-
of-the-art performance due to its finer temporal resolution and
superior frame-level feature representations.
However, real-world acoustic environments are complex
and dynamic, leading to significant performance degradation
of existing systems under noise interference. To improve the
performance of SED under diverse real-world noise conditions.

Xiao et al. [16] utilized Large Language Models [17] to select
various noises, such as bird calls, vehicle traffic, and fan sounds.
They extended the DESED dataset [18, 19] by categorizing
noises into four types (environmental, human, mechanical, and
natural) based on acoustic characteristics and simulating real
domestic sound fields through varying intensities, frequencies,
and insertion methods. Xiao et al. demonstrated that the per-
formance of the baseline CRNN on WildDESED deteriorates
drastically as the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) decreases. Yin
et al. [20] introduced a noise-robust SED framework integrating
LLMs with Language-Query Audio Source Separation [21]. In
this method, an LLM intelligently selects noise types for data
augmentation during fine-tuning. The fine-tuned model then
generates text queries to drive LASS for target sound separa-
tion, leading to substantial performance improvements in noisy
scenarios. On the other hand, auxiliary tasks such as Event Ap-
pearance Detection (EAD) [22] and sound separation [23, 24]
are also employed to enhance the model’s detection capability
in noisy environments.
In addition to utilizing external knowledge or signal pro-
cessing techniques, leveraging fine-grained temporal cues has
also proven effective. Boundary information has garnered in-
creasing attention as vital auxiliary knowledge in Sound Event
Detection [25, 26]. Inspired by recent work on boundary-aware
attention mechanisms [27], this work proposes a boundary-
guided approach to address complex real-world scenarios. By
constructing segment-specific dynamic masks, the model re-
stricts self-attention to the same event segment, thereby block-
ing the propagation of interference from background noise and
irrelevant segments. Furthermore, the Boundary-Guided Atten-
tion (BGA) module is introduced to utilize boundary features
for generating gating weights, and adaptively enhance active
event regions via residual connections. This dual mechanism
integrates feature extraction and temporal localization, signifi-
cantly improving the model’s noise robustness and localization
accuracy in low-SNR scenarios.

2.1. Feature Extractor
We employ a CNN and a pre-trained ATST-Frame model as fea-
ture extractors. Self-supervised on large-scale unlabeled data,
ATST-Frame generates high-precision frame-level representa-
tions of complex patterns. To reconcile their differing temporal
resolutions, adaptive pooling aligns the ATST-Frame features
with the CNN outputs. Concatenating these features yields a
semantically rich input representation.
2.2. Dynamic Boundary Transformer
After obtaining the high-level semantic features, we introduce
the Dynamic Boundary Transformer as illustrated in Figure
1(b). This module consists of six Dynamic Boundary Atten-
tion Blocks. Among them, three Dynamic Boundary Atten-
tion Blocks are employed in the boundary detection branch to
predict boundaries. The remaining three Dynamic Boundary
Attention Blocks utilize the predicted boundary information to
dynamically construct attention masks, ensuring that the self-
attention mechanism operates exclusively within the same event
segment. Finally, the outputs of the two branches are concate-
nated to obtain boundary-enhanced features.

2.4. Loss Function
To train the framework end-to-end, we employ a multi-task
objective function that optimizes sound event detection and
boundary localization. The SED loss governs supervised learn-
ing, employing Binary Cross Entropy to minimize classification
errors for frame-level strong and clip-level weak labels. For
unlabeled data, the consistency loss adopts the Mean Teacher
framework, aligning student predictions with the teacher via
Mean Squared Error under perturbations.
Given the high sparsity of boundary frames within the entire
time series, the standard cross-entropy loss tends to cause the
model to favor predicting ”non-boundary” frames. To address
this, we have designed a weighted boundary loss functionL b.

3. Experimental
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate our method on the DESED and WildDESED
datasets. DESED is the standard benchmark for DCASE Chal-
lenge Task 4, designed to simulate typical domestic environ-
ments across 10 sound categories (e.g., Alarm, Speech, Dog,
Vacuum cleaner). The training set consists of 1,000 synthetic
strongly labeled [29], 3,470 real strongly labeled, 1,578 weakly
labeled, and 14,412 unlabeled clips. The synthetic mixtures
were generated using the Scaper library [30].
To assess model robustness under complex acoustic condi-
tions, the WildDESED dataset was further employed. As an
extension of DESED, this dataset aims to reflect diverse noise
variations found in real-world settings. WildDESED was con-
structed by leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to se-
lect contextually relevant noise samples from AudioSet, thereby
creating diverse and challenging domestic scenarios. For all
datasets, audio recordings were resampled to 16 kHz.
3.2. Experimental Setup
The ATST-CRNN is adopted as the backbone architecture. The
input audio is first converted into 128-dimensional log-mel
spectrograms using a frame length of 128 ms and a hop size of
16 ms. These spectral features were subsequently processed by
a 7-layer CNN. We also use a frozen pre-trained ATST model to
obtain 768-dimensional semantic representations. Batch sizes
for synthetic strongly labeled, real strongly labeled, weakly la-
beled, and unlabeled data are set to 24, 24, 16, and 32, respec-
tively. The model is trained using the Adam optimizer cou-
pled with a cosine annealing learning rate scheduler, following
a warm-up period of 5,000 steps. Data augmentation is per-
formed using Mixup [31] with a probability of 0.5. During the
post-processing stage, class-dependent median filtering is ap-
plied to smooth the frame-level predictions. Furthermore, we
fine-tune the ATST model on the WildDESED dataset using in-
terpolation consistency training (ICT) [32] in conjunction with
Mixup to enhance its detection capability, and systematically
evaluate the model’s performance.
Our primary evaluation metrics are the threshold-
independent Polyphonic Sound Detection Scores (PSDS) [33,
34], specifically PSDS1 and PSDS2. PSDS1 imposes strict
temporal constraints on detection and serves as the stan-
dard metric for time-sensitive SED applications. Conversely,
PSDS2 emphasizes the system’s ability to distinguish between
classes, where reaction time is less critical compared to the
first scenario. PSDS1 evaluates temporal localization precision,
whereas PSDS2 is more sensitive to class confusion issues.

4. Results
4.1. WildDESED Results
Table 1 presents the results of our proposed method and base-
lines under various SNR levels. Table 1(a) reports the perfor-
mance when models are trained on the clean DESED dataset.
As the SNR decreases, all models experience a performance
drop. However, our proposed method consistently outperforms
the baseline ATST-CRNN. Specifically, the fine-tuned version
achieves PSDS1 of 0.442 and PSDS2 of 0.690 at 10 dB.
Table 1(b) shows the results when models are trained on
the noisy WildDESED dataset. While the inclusion of noisy
training data improves the overall detection performance for all
models, our approach still achieves the best results across all
four noise levels. At 10 dB, the fine-tuned BG-CRNN achieves
an impressive 0.483 on PSDS1. These direct comparisons in-
dicate that the dynamic boundary guidance and dynamic mask-
ing mechanisms can effectively limit background noise interfer-
ence, thereby improving the temporal localization accuracy of
sound events.

4.3. Ablation Study
To understand the contribution of each proposed component,
we conducted an ablation study on the standard DESED dataset
(Table 3). The baseline ATST-CRNN achieves a PSDS1 of
0.502 and a PSDS2 of 0.750. When only the boundary detection
branch composed of three Dynamic Boundary Attention Blocks
is introduced, which solely learns to locate event boundaries,
the PSDS1 significantly jumps to 0.544. This highlights the
importance of boundary awareness for time-sensitive detection.
Subsequently, further introducing the Boundary-Guided Atten-
tion Module results in a slight decrease in PSDS scores. This
may be attributed to the module causing the model to overly
focus on event boundaries. After incorporating the Dynamic
Boundary Attention, our full proposed pipeline achieves the
best performance with 0.566 PSDS1 and 0.791 PSDS2.
5. Conclusion
We present BG-CRNN, a boundary-guided framework for ro-
bust sound event detection in complex noisy environments.
A Dynamic Boundary Transformer generates intra-segment
masks to confine self-attention within individual events, while
a Boundary-Guided Attention module adaptively enhances ac-
tive event regions. Experiments on DESED and WildDESED
demonstrate consistent improvements across diverse SNR lev-
els, confirming its strong potential for real-world deployment.
