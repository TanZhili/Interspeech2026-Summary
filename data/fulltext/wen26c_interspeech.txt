Abstract
Direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation with compact linear mi-
crophone arrays exhibits pronounced performance degradation
when the source is located near end-fire directions. Through
theoretical analysis, we show that this degradation arises from
an ill-conditioned nonlinear mapping between time-difference-
of-arrival (TDOA) and azimuth under the far-field plane-wave
model, where small TDOA perturbations near end-fire di-
rections can be amplified into large angular errors. Based
on this analysis, we propose two improved methods within
a reliability-aware phase transform framework: a weighting-
enhanced steered response power (SRP) approach and a refined
TDOA estimation scheme. In addition, we collect a real-world
dataset to enable quantitative evaluation of DOA estimation per-
formance near end-fire directions. Experimental results demon-
strate that the proposed methods significantly outperform the
baseline methods. The code and dataset are publicly avail-
able athttps://github.com/WwHhYy666/ULA_End_
Fire_Robust_ASL.

1. Introduction
Direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation is fundamental in acous-
tic applications like speech capture and spatial audio. Among
various geometries, uniform linear arrays (ULAs) are widely in-
tegrated into consumer devices such as televisions, where reli-
able far-field DOA estimation is required [1]. While data-driven
deep learning currently dominates mainstream DOA estimation,
traditional signal processing methods, for example SRP, remain
indispensable for such resource-constrained edge devices due to
their low complexity, interpretability, and zero-training nature
[2,3]. Focusing on these conventional frameworks, a critical is-
sue is that the estimation accuracy of ULAs near the array axis
is consistently lower than in side-fire directions. This degrada-
tion becomes more pronounced for compact arrays with small
apertures operating in indoor environments, where reverbera-
tion and background noise further distort inter-channel phase
information [4]. Although this effect has been reported in prior
studies, such as the end-fire minimum variance distortionless
response (MVDR) analysis and end-fire DOA bias characteri-
zation [5,6], it is typically regarded as an empirical observation
or practical limitation rather than being attributed to any under-
lying theoretical constraints.

This paper leverages the inherent theoretical limitations of
TDOA-to-azimuth mapping to explicitly address end-fire degra-
dation. Under the commonly adopted far-field plane-wave
model, DOA estimation with ULAs relies on inverting this non-
linear mapping. It is shown that this inversion becomes severely
ill-conditioned near end-fire directions, resulting in dramati-
cally increased sensitivity of the estimated angle to TDOA per-
turbations. As a consequence, even small estimation errors
caused by noise, reverberation, or discretization can lead to
large angular deviations. This analysis reveals the fundamen-
tal reason why conventional DOA estimators, including steered
response power with phase transform (SRP-PHAT) [7,8] and its
variants [9], MVDR beamformer [10], and multiple signal clas-
sification (MUSIC) [11], exhibit unstable and biased behavior
near end-fire directions, particularly for compact arrays.
This paper builds upon a reliability-aware PHAT-based
framework [12-14] to explicitly address the ill-conditioned na-
ture of DOA inversion near the end-fire directions [5]. Without
increasing the array aperture or relaxing the far-field assump-
tion [5, 6], we develop two complementary methods to miti-
gate end-fire degradation in compact ULAs. The first method
enhances an SRP-based DOA estimation scheme [10, 15] by
incorporating an inverse-variance-inspired weighting strategy,
thereby improving robustness in the end-fire regions. The sec-
ond method stabilizes the inversion stage by refining TDOA es-
timation prior to angle recovery through oversampling, a phys-
ically constrained delay search space, and weighted cosine-
domain fusion, thereby suppressing error amplification induced
by the ill-conditioned inversion near the end-fire directions.
To evaluate this phenomenon under realistic acoustic con-
ditions, this work further addresses a critical gap in existing
studies: the absence of publicly available real-world datasets
specifically designed to assess end-fire robustness. To this end,
a dataset is collected using a compact linear microphone array
deployed in a reverberant indoor environment. Several widely
used DOA estimation methods and two proposed improved al-
gorithms are systematically evaluated on this dataset. Experi-
mental results demonstrate improved localization accuracy and
stability in end-fire regions, while maintaining competitive per-
formance across the full azimuth range.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental setup
We conduct experiments in a rectangular room measuring5m×
4m×3m, using home-based equipment with an embedded
compact linear microphone array. The array consists of four mi-
crophones with an inter-element spacing of3.5cm (total aper-
ture10.5cm). It is deployed at a height of1.5m and positioned
0.2m from a wall.
A loudspeaker playing clean speech signals is placed on the
same horizontal plane as the array. Azimuths from20 ◦–160◦
are evaluated in10◦ increments at source-to-array distances of
1m and2m. For each condition (i.e., each distance-azimuth
pair), approximately 100–200 non-overlapping 1-second speech
segments are recorded. The environment contains typical
household noise and moderate reverberation, with a reverber-
ation time comparable to that of an average home and an SNR
between10and20dB. The experimental setup is illustrated in
Fig. 2. The end-fire region is defined asθ EF = [20◦,40◦]∪
[140◦,160◦].
All recordings are resampled to16kHz. STFT is computed
using a 1024-point Kaiser window with 75%overlap. The fre-
quency range isf min = 800Hz tof max = 4500Hz, with
a frame selection ratio ofρ= 0.3is applied. In (7),α= 2
andη= 2(aligning with the theoretical optimum in Sec.3.1),
andγ= 1in (9) - (10). SRP scanning is performed over0 ◦–
180◦ with a resolution of0.2◦, and GCC-WLS uses 16× over-
sampling. Because practical deployment often demands imme-
diate, out-of-the-box localization without environment-specific
data for model pre-training, we benchmark exclusively against
training-free, zero-shot baselines (SRP-PHAT, SRP-MVDR).

5. Conclusion
This paper theoretically traces end-fire degradation in compact
linear arrays to an ill-conditioned TDOA-to-azimuth mapping
that significantly amplifies small delay perturbations. To mit-
igate this without expanding the array aperture, we propose
two improved algorithms: W-SRP-PHAT and GCC-WLS. Ex-
periments on a newly collected real-world reverberant dataset
demonstrate that our methods significantly enhance localiza-
tion accuracy and stability in end-fire regions while maintaining
competitive overall performance.
