Self-supervised Speaker Verification with High-Confidence Pseudo-Label
Selection and DINO-Style Self-Distillation Based on Pre-trained Models
Yishuang Li, Yi Yu, Wanli Dong, Weihao Gan∗∗
Malanshan audio&video laboratory, China
liyishuang@mlslabs.com.cn, ganweihao@mlslabs.com.cn
Abstract
In this paper, we propose a self-supervised speaker verifica-
tion (SS-SV) method based on pre-trained models (PTMs),
which improves performance via high-confidence pseudo-label
selection and DINO-style self-distillation. We select the top-
K most speaker-discriminative Transformer layers from PTMs
and cluster their representations separately to generate multiple
pseudo-label sets. Using label alignment, we retain samples
with consistent clustering as high-confidence pseudo-labeled
data, while others are treated as unlabeled. We perform pseudo-
supervised learning with label noise correction loss on the high-
confidence set;for unlabeled data we use an EMA teacher to
provide soft distillation targets and apply a consistency loss for
regularization. These objectives are jointly optimized to train
the SV model. Experiments on the V oxCeleb dataset show that
our SS-SV method achieves performance comparable to current
state-of-the-art approaches, demonstrating its effectiveness.
Index Terms: self-supervised speaker verification, pre-trained
model, pseudo-label selection, DINO-style self-distillation,

1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV) aims to determine whether two
speech segments are spoken by the same person [1]. Deep learn-
ing has substantially advanced SV in recent years [2], but cur-
rent high-performance SV systems typically rely on large-scale,
carefully labeled speaker data. Such annotations are costly and
time-consuming to obtain, and growing privacy concerns fur-
ther restrict data access and sharing, creating a major bottleneck
for continued progress [3].
To reduce the dependence on labeled data and better ex-
ploit abundant unlabeled speech, self-supervised learning (SSL)
has become a promising direction [4]. By constructing pre-
text tasks, SSL learns structured representations from unlabeled
data, often capturing semantic and speaker-discriminative infor-
mation [5, 6]. Recent self-supervised speaker verification(SS-
SV) methods commonly adopt an iterative two-stage frame-
work: (i) training an encoder to extract speaker embeddings
with an SSL objective, and (ii) generating pseudo-labels (PL)
via clustering and iteratively refining the encoder or down-
stream SV model [7, 8, 9, 10]. In the first stage, contrastive
learning typically relies on positive and negative pairs [7, 9, 11],
but negatives may come from the same speaker and thus mis-
lead SS training. To remove this dependence, negative-free self-
distillation methods such as DINO [12] have been proposed and
shown notable gains [10, 13, 14, 15]. However, the quality of
pseudo-labels remains critical: noisy pseudo-labels can inject

erroneous supervision and degrade discrimination [16]. To mit-
igate this issue, prior work filters unreliable samples based on
the observation that low-loss samples tend to be more reliable,
e.g., loss gating and its dynamic variants [9, 10, 17].
Meanwhile, pre-trained models (PTMs) for speech repre-
sentation learning, such as HuBERT [18], UniSpeech-SAT [19],
and WavLM [20], have rapidly progressed and provide high-
quality universal features from large-scale unlabeled speech.
Chen et al. [21] further extend PTMs to SS-SV in PTM-LC
by clustering PTM representations to obtain stronger initial
pseudo-labels and applying label correction to improve perfor-
mance and robustness. Despite these advances, an open chal-
lenge remains: under unlabeled conditions, how can we lever-
age hierarchical PTM representations to construct more reli-
able supervision while suppressing pseudo-label noise without
sacrificing data efficiency? To address this challenge, we pro-
pose a PTM-based self-supervised SV method that jointly op-
timizes the overall system via high-confidence pseudo-label se-
lection (HCPLS) and DINO-style self-distillation.Our con-
tributions are three-fold:(1) We propose a high-confidence
pseudo-label selection strategy based on multi-layer clustering
consistency of PTMs, which improves pseudo-label quality via
cross-layer alignment and consistency filtering. (2) We de-
velop a joint training framework that couples pseudo-supervised
learning with DINO-style self-distillation, fully exploiting high-
confidence samples while effectively leveraging low-confidence
samples as unlabeled data. (3)Extensive experiments on the
V oxCeleb dataset validate the effectiveness of the proposed
method, achieving an EER of 1.09% on V oxCeleb1-O.

2.4. Clustering and Offline Label Correction
We use Infomap [24], a graph-based clustering method that
treats each utterance embedding as a node, connects nodes by
cosine similarity, and minimizes the random-walk description
length, making it effective for discovering complex speaker
communities. To further improve pseudo-label reliability, we
apply offline label correction [21]: each utterance is split into
chunks and predicted at the chunk level. If one label appears in
more than50%of chunks, it is assigned to the utterance; other-
wise, the utterance is considered ambiguous and discarded.

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics
To validate the proposed self-supervised speaker verifica-
tion framework, we conduct extensive experiments on V ox-
Celeb2 dev (V ox2 dev) [25], which contains 5994 speakers
with 1,092,009 utterances. During training, we segment each
utterance into non-overlapping 2 s chunks. We perform online
data augmentation using MUSAN [26] and RIR [27], including
wav drop, speed perturbation, adding noise, and adding rever-
beration. Note that no ground-truth speaker labels are used dur-
ing training. We evaluate performance on V oxCeleb1-O (V ox1-
o), using EER and minimum normalized detection cost function
(MinDCF). For MinDCF, we setP target = 0.01.
3.2. System Configuration
We evaluate theLargevariants of three widely used pre-
trained speech models—HuBERT [18], UniSpeech-SAT [19],
and WavLM [20]—and select layers based on validation EER.
Each PTM comprises a 7-layer convolutional feature extractor
followed by a 24-layer Transformer. Fig. 2 shows the layer-
wise validation EERs. WavLM attains the three lowest EERs
at layers 10, 11, and 9, possibly due to its speaker-oriented
pre-training that uses simulated noise/overlapped speech as in-
put and predicts pseudo-labels of clean speech in masked re-
gions. Accordingly, we adopt the first 10 Transformer lay-
ers of WavLM as the PTM-sub network. We train the model
with AdamW and a cyclic learning-rate schedule [28]. Follow-
ing [22], training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 updates all
parameters except the PTM-sub, and Stage 2 fine-tunes the en-
tire network. The maximum learning rates are set to5×10 −4
and1×10 −5 for the two stages, with 10 and 5 epochs, respec-
tively. Hyperparameters are set toβ t=0.7inL lnc,T=2.0and
τ=0.6inL kd,λ=0.5, and EMA decayα=0.999.

4.2. Iteration-1 Performance Comparison
As shown in Table 2, in Iteration-1 our method outperforms pre-
vious representative self-supervised SV methods on V ox1-O. S1
is our implemented baseline, configured the same as PTM-LC
[21], which performs pseudo-supervised training only onH 1
without HCPLS or self-distillation, achieving an EER of 2.64%.
Compared with S1, S2 and S3 apply only the HCPLS strategy
and train onH 2 andH 3, lowering the EER to 2.44% and 2.40%,
respectively. This confirms that HCPLS markedly improves
pseudo-label reliability in the first Iteration, thereby reducing
the sensitivity of pseudo-supervised training to label noise. In
addition, S3 outperforming S2 indicates that a stricter cross-
layer consensus (K=3) yields a more stable supervision sig-
nal.Building on HCPLS, S4 and S5 further incorporate DINO-
style self-distillation and treat the low-confidence subsetLK as
unlabeled data, resulting in additional EER reductions to 2.17%
and 2.10%. Notably, S5 performs best, delivering a 20.5% rel-
ative EER reduction over S1. This suggests that forcing hard
pseudo labels on low-confidence samples can exacerbate noise,
whereas leveraging them as unlabeled data through distillation
and consistency regularization enhances representation learning
without introducing unreliable supervision. Overall, combining
high-confidence pseudo supervision with low-confidence unla-
beled self-distillation in Iteration-1 provides a stronger initial-
ization and a better foundation for subsequent iterations.

4.4. Iterative Refinement and Performance Comparison
As shown in Fig. 3, our method consistently reduces EER across
iterations and achieves an EER of 1.09% on V ox1-O after 4 it-
erations, outperforming most previous iterative self-supervised
SV approaches. Compared with the PTM-LC [21] baseline,
which obtains an EER of 1.25%, our method achieves a relative
EER reduction of 12.8%. Notably, IPL [30] requires 11 itera-
tions to reach an EER of 1.14%, whereas our method achieves
a lower EER in fewer iterations. This highlights both the effec-
tiveness and the iteration efficiency of the proposed method.
5. Conclusion
In this study, we propose an efficient self-supervised speaker
verification method based on PTMs. By combining high-
confidence pseudo-label selection based on PTM multi-layer
clustering consistency with DINO-style self-distillation strat-
egy, we significantly improve pseudo-label quality and effec-
tively utilize low-confidence data. Experiments on the V ox-
Celeb dataset validate the effectiveness of our method, which
delivers promising performance.
