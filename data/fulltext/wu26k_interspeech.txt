Leveraging Temporal Redundancy via Layer-wise Key-Value Pooling
Attention for Efficient ASR

Abstract
Transformer-based models have revolutionized Automatic
Speech Recognition. However, the quadratic complexity of
self-attention and feature redundancy necessitate deep stack-
ing, limiting long-sequence efficiency. To address this, we pro-
pose Key-Value Pooling Attention (KV-Pooling), which lever-
ages high temporal redundancy in speech by average pooling
to compress Key/Value tensors, smoothing feature distribution
spikes while retaining Query resolution to maintain modeling
precision. This reduces reliance on network depth. Building
on this, we introduce KV-Pooling-Zipformer, integrating this
mechanism into Zipformer by pre-setting differential pooling
strides based on layer-wise feature abstraction. Compared to
the native Zipformer, RNN-T experiments show absolute re-
ductions of 0.2% CER on AISHELL-1 and 0.3% WER on Lib-
riSpeech. Notably, it improves inference RTF by 10% on an
AMD EPYC 7763, achieving synergistic gains in accuracy and
efficiency.

1. Introduction
In recent years, Transformer-based architectures [1], which el-
egantly integrate Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and
self-attention mechanisms, have become the mainstream in
end-to-end Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and achieved
State-of-the-Art (SOTA) performance [2, 3, 4]. However, as
model sizes scale and processing long speech sequences be-
comes necessary, the O(T 2) computational complexity of self-
attention leads to severe memory footprint and inference la-
tency issues. To alleviate this bottleneck, existing methods
typically reduce network depth or apply macro-level temporal
down-sampling [3, 5, 6]. These approaches inevitably sacri-
fice the original temporal resolution and are prone to temporal
aliasing. Consequently, directly combining aggressive down-
sampling with reduced network depth significantly degrades
recognition accuracy [7].
Existing attention mechanisms overlook the stark differ-
ence in information density between text and speech. Unlike
discrete, semantically dense text, speech exhibits extreme tem-
poral redundancy, where a single phoneme often spans mul-
tiple highly similar frames [7, 8]. Standard self-attention re-
lies on dense, frame-by-frame dot-product interactions. While
this high temporal resolution enables Queries to precisely lo-
cate acoustic boundaries, Keys and Values—serving merely as
contextual references—do not necessitate such fine granularity,
rendering the full-sequence computation massively redundant.

To address this, we propose the Key-Value Pooling (KV-
Pooling) Attention mechanism. Breaking the equal-length con-
straint of standard attention, it adopts an asymmetric resolu-
tion strategy. Specifically, we apply temporal pooling to down-
sample Keys and Values before computing attention weights.
This merges adjacent similar frames, cutting complexity from
O(T 2) to O(T 2/s) (where s is the stride) and smoothing fea-
ture distribution spikes, while Queries retain original resolution
to preserve fine-grained details. This approach allows the model
to focus on salient acoustic representations, significantly reduc-
ing reliance on network depth and accelerates both training and
inference while maintaining high accuracy even in shallower ar-
chitectures.
We further propose the KV-Pooling-Zipformer architecture
to implement this plug-and-play mechanism, systematically ad-
dressing three challenges: 1) A Center Alignment strategy cor-
rects relative positional encoding shifts caused by length vari-
ation; 2) A Layer-wise Differential Pooling strategy pre-sets
larger strides in shallow layers with high adjacent-frame simi-
larity and redundancy, and gradually narrows them in semantic-
dense deep layers; 3) A specific causal masking mechanism
seamlessly supports streaming inference. This synergistic de-
sign optimizes both efficiency and performance.
2. Related Work
In recent years, Transformer-CNN hybrid architectures, such
as the Conformer [1], have dominated end-to-end Automatic
Speech Recognition (ASR). However, the O(T 2) computa-
tional complexity of the self-attention mechanism severely re-
stricts their deployment in long-sequence and streaming scenar-
ios. Existing optimization trajectories primarily fall into two
categories. The first is input down-sampling (e.g., Squeeze-
former [6]), risks losing fine-grained acoustic information, such
as phoneme boundaries, due to its coarse-grained compres-
sion. The second involves linear or sparse attention mecha-
nisms [9, 10], which often suffer from compromised long-range
modeling capabilities or difficulties in hardware adaptation.
Drawing inspiration from Grouped Query Attention
(GQA) [11] in NLP and the Pyramid Vision Transformer
(PVT) [12] in CV , recent ASR studies attempt to compress
Keys and Values in the temporal dimension [13]. However,
they predominantly employ fixed-stride strategies—neglecting
layer-wise redundancy variance—and inevitably introduce rel-
ative positional misalignments. To address this, we propose the
Key-Value Pooling Attention mechanism, which pairs a hierar-
chical layer-adaptive pooling strategy with a Center Alignment
method. This tightly resolves the aforementioned bottlenecks,
drastically reducing computational overhead while rigorously
preserving fine-grained acoustic details.

3. Proposed Method
This section first introduces the down-sampling mechanism for
Key and Value vectors, constructing a generic, plug-and-play
KV-Pooling Attention module to replace standard attention in
ASR models. Subsequently, taking Zipformer [3] as an exam-
ple, we quantify information redundancy by analyzing adjacent-
element cosine similarity and information entropy across en-
coder layers. Guided by these metrics, we pre-set layer-
wise pooling strides to ultimately formulate the KV-Pooling-
Zipformer encoder model.
3.1. KV-Pooling Attention
Given an input sequence X ∈ RT×d, standard self-attention
linearly projects it into Query ( Q), Key ( K), and Value ( V )
matrices (Q, K∈ RT×dk , V ∈ RT×dv ) to compute the out-
put Y = Softmax(QK T /√dk)V . However, computing the
pairwise correlation strictly requires O(T 2) time complexity,
causing a severe computational bottleneck for long speech se-
quences.
Due to frame overlapping and extended phoneme durations,
speech representations in ASR inherently exhibit high local
temporal redundancy. As a result, aggregating adjacent K and
V vectors incurs negligible information loss. Beyond simple
compression, this local pooling serves as a crucial structural in-
ductive bias [8, 14]: it smooths fine-grained feature distribution
spikes in shallow layers, broadens the context receptive field,
and steers the model toward capturing global semantic depen-
dencies. Simultaneously, maintaining the original temporal res-
olution of Q ensures the precise localization of acoustic bound-
aries.

mechanism (Fig. 1). We compress K and V along the time di-

By decoupling the temporal resolution of Q and K/V , this

the core temporal alignment logic, thereby ensuring high plug-

feature distribution spikes. Consistent with similar practices in
NLP and CV [12, 15], this mechanism demonstrates superior
performance and robustness compared to vanilla attention, par-
ticularly in memory-constrained scenarios.
3.2. Center-Aligned Relative Positional Encoding
Modern ASR architectures [1, 16, 17] predominantly employ
Relative Positional Encoding (RPE), which fundamentally as-
sumes identical temporal granularity (frame length) for Queries
and Keys. However, KV-Pooling reduces the Key/Value tempo-
ral resolution, creating a granularity mismatch that breaks con-
ventional index alignment.
To provide a structural correction for this mismatch, we
propose a Center Alignment mapping strategy (Fig. 2). For
a pooled Key at index j (with stride s), we project it back to
the temporal center of its original acoustic window. The recal-
ibrated discrete index is computed as j′ =⌊(j + 0 .5)× s⌋.
The relative distance to a Query at original index i becomes
i− j′, guiding the dynamic extraction of relative embeddings.
This strictly preserves the geometric consistency of the attention
mechanism, ensuring positional information accurately reflects
true temporal distances despite multi-scale down-sampling.

3.3. Causal and Padding Mask Adaptation
As KV-Pooling reduces Key/Value resolutions, the original
causal and padding masks become dimensionally incompatible.
For the causal mask, while relative distances are computed us-
ing the temporal center, strict causality must be enforced us-
ing the right boundary of the pooling window. Specifically, a
pooled key at index j with stride s encapsulates original acous-
tic frames up to index (j + 1)× s− 1. To strictly prevent any
future information leakage in streaming scenarios, this pooled
key is explicitly masked out if the query index i is strictly less
than this right boundary (i.e., masked if i < (j + 1)× s− 1).
For the padding mask, we utilize a strict logical reconstruc-
tion: a pooled token is deemed valid only if all original tokens
within its corresponding window are unpadded. Any presence
of padding elements masks the entire pooled position. This ef-
ficiently eliminates boundary noise, ensuring the model attends
exclusively to pristine acoustic features.

Two key observations emerge from the statistics:

high compressibility. Conversely, deep layers maintain high en-
tropy despite temporal similarity, implying they encode dense,
discriminative semantics sensitive to excessive compression.
These findings establish a qualitative guideline for stride
selection: layers with high similarity and low entropy toler-
ate larger strides, whereas high-entropy layers require smaller
strides to preserve critical semantics. The exact discrete strides
are architectural hyperparameters, optimized empirically via
subsequent experiments.
4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
To comprehensively evaluate the efficacy of the proposed
method, we conducted systematic experiments on the Mandarin
AISHELL-1 [18] (178 hours) and English Librispeech [19]
(960 hours) datasets. For feature extraction, all audio data were
converted into 80-dimensional Log-Mel spectrograms with a
10 ms frame shift. The analysis frame length was set to 25 ms
for AISHELL-1 and 20 ms for Librispeech. During training, we
employed SpecAugment [20] and speed perturbation [21] (with
factors of 0.9 and 1.1) for data augmentation.

4.2. Results and Analysis
As shown in Tables 2 and 3, integrating KV-Pooling improves
inference efficiency (RTF) while generally maintaining or im-
proving recognition accuracy. On AISHELL-1, our models
consistently outperform the baselines across all scales. On
LibriSpeech, the Large (L) model improves on both test sets,
whereas the Medium (M) and Small (S) models achieve partic-
ularly notable gains on the more challenging test-other set, with
minor test-clean regressions. This is expected: KV-Pooling acts
as a temporal smoothing filter that mitigates feature distribu-
tion spikes. For highly clean speech, this smoothing may ob-
scure subtle phonetic details that smaller-capacity models heav-
ily rely on. Nevertheless, consistent improvements on test-other
demonstrate that KV-Pooling effectively filters irrelevant acous-
tic variations, enhancing robustness in complex environments
alongside computational speedups.

4.3. Ablation Study
To validate our Layer-wise Differential Pooling strategy
and pooling operators, we conduct ablation studies on the
Zipformer-L and Zipformer-S architectures (Table 4). Evalu-
ated on the AISHELL-1 dataset, we test various pooling meth-
ods and stride configurations, each mapping to the 4 macro-
stages defined in Table 1.
1) Efficacy of Layer-wise Configurations: Compared to the
stride-{1,1,1,1} baseline, our {4,2,1,2} configuration yields
optimal CER and RTF. The degradation of the fixed-stride
({2,2,2,2}) model corroborates our guidelines in 3.4 : while
redundant shallow layers tolerate aggressive down-sampling,
compressing high-entropy middle layers impairs accuracy. No-
tably,{4,2,1,2} also outperforms the monotonic{4,2,1,1}. We
attribute this to semantic crystallization (Fig. 3): as deep repre-
sentations converge into highly homogeneous tokens, applying
a moderate stride (s = 2) at the tail stage introduces no seman-
tic loss. Instead, it effectively smooths token representations
and reduces the decoder’s sequence length, achieving the best
accuracy-speed trade-off.
2) Impact of Pooling Operators: Replacing Average Pool-
ing with Convolutional Pooling (Conv {4,2,1,2}) leads to a
noticeable performance drop (CER rising to 7.85/8.41% on
Zipformer-S). This confirms our hypothesis: while convolution

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose the KV-Pooling Attention mechanism
to address the quadratic computational bottleneck in ASR by
decoupling the temporal resolution of Queries from Contexts.
By integrating a Center-Aligned positional encoding with strict
causal masking and a Layer-wise Differential Pooling strat-
egy, our method effectively filters temporal redundancy while
preserving fine-grained acoustic boundaries. Experiments on
AISHELL-1 and LibriSpeech demonstrate synergistic improve-
ments in both recognition accuracy and inference speed. While
inherently compatible with streaming ASR architectures, ex-
ploring its full deployment in strict streaming applications re-
mains a focus for future work.
