Abstract
Singing V oice Conversion (SVC) faces a trade-off between
singer feature disentanglement and singing quality. To address
this, this paper proposes the MinFlow-SVC framework, a con-
ditional flow matching-based SVC method enhanced by min-
pooling adversarial training. We adopt a KNN-based approach
to map source singer features into a shared singer space, remov-
ing source timbre to obtain content features like pitch, phonetics
and singing expression. To further enhance generation quality,
we introduce a min-pooling adversarial training strategy, which
can detect and correct KNN-induced feature inconsistencies and
improve conditional flow matching generation quality with har-
monic awareness. Experiments show our method outperforms
existing state-of-the-art SVC baselines in naturalness, intelligi-
bility, timbre similarity and singing stability.
Index Terms: Singing voice conversion, k-nearest neighbor,
conditional flow matching, adversarial training
1. Introduction
Singing V oice Conversion (SVC) aims to convert the timbre
and vocal style of a source singing voice while strictly pre-
serving its linguistic content, pitch contour, and melody, thus
achieving timbre transfer across singers[1, 2]. Its core chal-
lenge is the efficient disentanglement of content and timbre,
separating the signal into content representations (phonetics,
pitch, melody) and timbre-style representations linked to singer
identity[3, 4]. Existing timbre modeling approaches mainly
include extracting singer identity embeddings via verification
models or pre-trained encoders[2, 5], and integrating reference
audio features during reconstruction[6, 7]. For content model-
ing, Vector Quantization (VQ) is widely used in mainstream
SVC frameworks, mapping continuous features into a finite
codebook by content similarity to remove singer-specific tim-
bre. However, VQ has an inherent trade-off between disentan-
glement and reconstruction quality[8, 9]: small codebooks lack
fine-grained content and cause artifacts, while more codevec-
tors may reintroduce timbre and lower utilization. Adversarial
training[10, 11] and mutual information minimization[12] only
offer limited disentanglement gains[13]. Overall, most explicit
disentanglement methods face a dilemma: insufficient disentan-
glement leads to source timbre leakage, while excessive disen-
tanglement loses key content information, degrading generated
audio quality.
To mitigate the trade-off between content-timbre disentan-
glement and generation quality in singing voice conversion,

2. Method
Fig.1 illustrates the architecture of MinFlow-SVC, which pri-
marily consists of a content encoder and a vector field estimator,
enhanced respectively by discriminatorsD T andD V . WavLM
[15] is used to extract semantic features from the input singing
waveform, which are then converted into singing content fea-
turesx cont by the content encoder. The singing content features
xcont and the target singer timbre embeddingytar serve as con-
ditions for the vector field estimator to progressively transform
Gaussian noise into the target singer Mel-spectrogram. Finally,
a vocoder [16] synthesizes the singing waveform from the gen-
erated Mel-spectrogram.
2.1. Content Encoder
The core goal of the content encoder is to map the semantic fea-
tures of the source speaker into a shared speaker-independent
space, thereby effectively stripping away the original timbre
information while preserving the speech content. Inspired by
KNN-VC [17], we leverage the rich content information con-
tained in self-supervised learning (SSL) features [15] to estab-
lish frame-level alignment between source and a shared speaker
space based on content similarity, thereby constructing paired
data to train the content encoder.
Specifically, when training on a multi-speaker dataset, we
designate a speaker with a moderate amount of data as the

directly using the outputs ofContentEncoder(·)can still pro-

This likely arises from the frame-by-frame KNN pairing used
to obtainx knn, which lacks contextual information. Thus, iden-
tifying and correcting these discontinuous segments is crucial.
In WeKWS [18], the max-pooling loss allows the models
to autonomously learn keyword boundaries without pre-aligned
labels by emphasizing high-probability frames within a seg-
ment. Inspired by this, we introduce a min-pooling adversar-
ial training strategy. This guides the discriminator to identify
and localize unnatural artifacts caused by discontinuities in the
transformed featuresx cont, thereby enhancing feature consis-
tency and improving perceptual output quality. For each input,
the discriminatorD T outputs a continuity probabilityp∈[0,1]

lowest-probability patch as the positive samplep pos. Optimiz-

2.4. Harmonic-Aware Min-Pooling Adversarial Loss

finement of harmonic quality. We design a dynamic harmonic
mask using the predicted fundamental frequency (f0), since the

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
3.1.1. Dataset
We adopt the widely-used multi-speaker dataset, the M4singer
corpus [20], to train our model. For each speaker, 10 sen-
tences are used for validation and the rest are used for training.
For both subjective and objective evaluations, we randomly se-
lect 6 speakers from the OpenSinger[21], with 10 sentences per
speaker, to assess the model’s zero-shot conversion ability. All
audios were downsampled to 16kHz for simplicity.
3.1.2. Implement Details
We extract WavLM [?] features from the final layer of the pre-
trained WavLM-large model. For both the content encoder and
vector field estimator, we adopt a U-Net [22] architecture simi-
lar to that used in Diff-VC [23], except that we add extra projec-
tion layers to the content encoder to compress the input features’
dimensionality to half of its original size and then restore them
to their original dimensions in the output. The content encoder’s
discriminatorD T and the vector field estimator’s discriminator
DV follow the structure used in StyleGAN [24]. Additionally,
we apply an extra sigmoid function to the output of the dis-
criminator to produce probability values. A pretrained speaker
encoder [25] is used to extract the speaker timbre embeddings,
while a HiFi-GAN [14] vocoder synthesizes audio from the Mel
spectrogram.σ min is set to1×10 −4. In the actual training
process, we first train the content encoder and then freeze its
parameters to train the vector field estimator.
We trained the content encoder with a batch size of 10 using
the Adam optimizer at a learning rate of1×10 −4 for 35K steps
on two NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs. The vector field estimator is
trained with a batch size of 32, using the Adam optimizer at a
learning rate of1×10 −4 for 70K steps on the same devices.
3.1.3. Baselines
We compare our MinFlow-SVC with DiffSVC [7], So-Vits-
SVC, and NeuCoSVC [26] using their official implementations.
To ensure a fair comparison, we re-trained the models using
their official code on the m4singer dataset.
3.1.4. Evaluation metrics
We comprehensively evaluate the model performance from
three dimensions: naturalness, timbre similarity, and prosody
similarity, using both subjective and objective metrics. For
subjective evaluation, we invite 15 listeners to conduct sub-
jective tests, where a 5-scale naturalness mean opinion score
(NMOS) is used to assess speech naturalness, and a 4-scale
similarity mean opinion score (SMOS) is adopted to evaluate
timbre similarity, with95%confidence intervals computed.For
objective evaluation, we utilize a pre-trained model [27] to ex-
tract F0 information from the audio, and calculate the Pearson
correlation coefficient between the original and converted au-
dios(F0CORR) to measure prosody similarity. We further as-
sess timbre similarity via the speaker embedding cosine sim-
ilarity (SECS) obtained from a pre-trained speaker verification
model [28], and measure inference speed by computing the real-
time factor (RTF). In addition, we employ MOSNet [29] to as-
sist in naturalness evaluation, as our experiments show that it
can effectively reflect speech quality to a reasonable extent.
3.2. Experimental Results
3.2.1. Analysis on Speaker Timbre Conversion
To evaluate our content encoder’s conversion ability, we trained
a WavLM-based speaker classifier. The classifier uses a 256-
unit LSTM followed by a linear layer and a classification head.
It is trained using 86 speakers from M4singer and OpenSinger.
We evaluated the speaker classifier’s accuracy using test setxsrc
from these 86 speaker, and then tested the content encoder’s
conversion ability by convertingx src tox cont. Results in Ta-
ble 1 show95%accuracy on originalx src features and98%
accuracy on converted content featuresx cont. These results in-
dicate that our content encoder effectively performs timbre con-
version from both seen and unseen source speakers to the shared
speaker, thereby significantly reducing the source speaker’s tim-
bre information.

3.2.3. Ablation Study
Ablation experiments indicate that removing the content en-
coder leads to a decrease in the timbre similarity score, as the
timbre information residual in the source speaker features is not
effectively stripped away, resulting in source timbre leakage in
the converted audio.
To investigate the importance and effectiveness of our pro-
posed methods, we conducted three ablation experiments with
the inference steps fixed at 10. First, we removed the min-
pooling adversarial training strategy in the content encoder to
assess whether our approach effectively alleviates frame dis-
continuities caused by KNN independent pairing. As shown
in the third-to-last row of Table2, removingL min-pooling notably
lowers NMOS scores, and unnatural artifacts can be heard
in some speech segments. Further spectral analysis supports
this finding: Fig. 2a presents the source speech’s spectrogram,
while Fig. 2b shows the conversion result withoutL min-pooling.
Comparing the highlighted red sections reveals noticeable dis-
continuities in the converted spectrogram. However, apply-
ingL min-pooling restores consistency in these areas, as shown in
Fig. 2c. Through quantitative and visualization analysis, we
demonstrate that the proposed min-pooling adversarial training
strategy effectively alleviates discontinuities caused by KNN.
Subsequently, we removedL adv-harm and trained the vector
field estimator only withL CFM. As shown in the penultimate
row of Table 2, this modification leads to obvious decreases in
both naturalness metrics including NMOS and F0-CORR. Fi-
nally, we removed the DHM operation fromL adv-harm, reverting
it to conventional adversarial training without harmonic mod-
eling. As shown in the last row of Table 2, the F0-CORR
score drops, indicating that the DHM operation affects har-
monic modeling during training.
We further verify the effectiveness ofL adv-harm through vi-
sualization comparison: Fig. 3a shows the spectrogram of the
source speech, while Fig. 3b shows the conversion result with-
outL adv-harm, where the red rectangular regions exhibit degraded
harmonic structures, especially in high-frequency components,
leading to unnatural perception. After applyingL adv-harm, this
problem is effectively alleviated, as shown in Fig. 3d, where
the harmonics in the red rectangles become much clearer and
sharper with restored high-frequency details. When the DHM
operation is removed fromL adv-harm, the spectrogram in the red
rectangles of Fig. 3c shows significantly fewer details than that
in Fig. 3d. Both quantitative analysis and visual comparison
confirm that the proposed harmonic-aware min-pooling adver-
sarial training enhanced conditional flow matching can generate
more natural speech.

4. Conclusion
This paper presents MinFlow-SVC, a conditional flow matching
framework with min-pooling adversarial training for voice con-
version. It employs KNN-based mapping to project source fea-
tures into a shared, timbre-free space. The min-pooling mech-
anism corrects frame-level discontinuities and enhances flow
matching to produce natural Mel-spectrograms. Experiments
show our method surpasses strong baselines in zero-shot con-
version on naturalness, timbre similarity, and objective metrics.
Ablation studies validate its key components.
