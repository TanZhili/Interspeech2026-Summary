Beyond Symmetric Interaction: Capability-Aware Asymmetric Multi-Agent
Collaboration for Audio Deep Reasoning

Abstract
Audio deep reasoning demands expert-level perception and
multi-step reasoning. Vanilla multi-agent paradigms struggle
here due to three challenges: (1) underutilization of the com-
plementary abilities of diverse Large Audio-Language Mod-
els (LALMs); (2) neglect of role differentiation and capability
bias; and (3) sampling instability inherent to LALMs. To ad-
dress these, we proposeAsymAudio, a novel Capability-Aware
Multi-Agent Audio Reasoning framework with three key com-
ponents: (1) an Inter-Agent Collaborative Interaction Module
that fuses complementary acoustic strengths across LALMs;
(2) a Role-Adaptive Asymmetric Strategy that assigns hierar-
chical roles and tailored feedback to reduce synergistic hallu-
cination from the bucket effect and textual compensation; and
(3) an Intra-Agent Consistency Refinement Module that uses
resampling and self-correction to enhance stability. AsymAu-
dio ranked 3rd in the Interspeech 2026 Audio Deep Reasoning
Challenge (Agent Track).
Index Terms: Audio Deep Reasoning, Large Audio-Language
Models, Multi-agent, Capability-Aware
1. Introduction
Audio deep reasoning [1, 2, 3] has emerged as a critical chal-
lenge in audio understanding, extending beyond traditional
tasks like sound event detection [4] or speech recognition [5]. It
requires models to not only execute expert-level perception but
also to perform multi-step reasoning. This capability is pivotal
for real-world applications, including autonomous systems [6]
and next-generation interactive assistants [7].
In the literature, great progress in audio reasoning has been
achieved by prior work [8, 9, 10, 11]. However, audio deep rea-
soning remains a significant challenge. Recently, the autoagent-
based paradigm [12, 13, 14] has shown promise in handling
complex reasoning by orchestrating multiple Large Language
Models (LLMs). However, directly applying vanilla autoa-
gent paradigm to audio deep reasoning suffers from three main
challenges:Firstly, existing approaches fail to fully exploit
the complementary capabilities of diverse agents. The repre-
sentative work in audio reasoning, AudioGenie-Reasoner [15],
adopts a single Large Audio-Language Model (LALM) to iter-
atively query audio and translate information into text for text-
based reasoning. This single-perspective approach inevitably
encounters a modality bottleneck and fails to leverage the com-
plementary acoustic capabilities inherent in different LALMs.
Secondly, current multi-agent systems overlook the inherent
role differentiation and capability bias among agents. Stan-
dard frameworks typically employ symmetric voting or debate,

2. Method
We propose a Capability-Aware Multi-Agent Audio Reasoning
framework, AsymAudio, to fully exploit multi-agent collabora-
tion potential for audio deep reasoning. As shown in Figure 2,
AsymAudio operates in three stages: (1) Intra-Agent Con-
sistency Refinement, where individual agents self-correct via
resampling; (2) Inter-Agent Collaborative Interaction, where
agents collaborate through a role-adaptive asymmetric strategy;
and (3) Final Decision and Reasoning Collection, where a holis-
tic explanation is synthesized from aligned reasoning paths.
2.1. Role-Adaptive Asymmetric Strategy
AsymAudio consists of three heterogeneous agents, denoted as
A={α 1,α 2,α 3}. To address the capability mismatch among
agents, we discard the traditional symmetric debate format. In-
stead, we assign distinct roles and interaction schemes based on
the intrinsic characteristics of each model.
2.1.1. Hierarchical Agent Roles
Treating models with disparate capabilities as equals often leads
to a “bucket effect", where the system’s overall performance is
capped or even degraded by the limitations of weaker agents.
Thus, we categorize them into two hierarchical tiers:
• Decision agents (α 1,α 2): These agents actively drive the
decision-making process. They participate in voting and
evolve other agents’ reasoning through interaction.
• Auxiliary evidence agent (α 3): This agent serves as the ev-
idence provider. Due to relatively weaker audio reasoning
capabilities, it does not vote on the decision. Instead, it is
utilized to substantiate the final reasoning with grounded au-
ditory evidence. This agent is also equipped with external
tools [31] to augment fine-grained acoustic details.
2.1.2. Distinct Interaction Schemes
We observe that during collaboration, some models are prone
to “textual compensation", where they abandon deep acoustic
analysis and instead rely on pure textual logic to blindly align
with the outputs of other agents. To mitigate this, we propose
two distinct feedback schemes:
• Implicit objective acoustic guidance (G imp): To prevent bias,
we strictly withhold the other agent’s specific answer. In-
stead, an LLM acts as a moderator to identify the core point
of disagreement and generates a neutral listening guide. This
guide highlights specific acoustic conflicts and clarifies ob-
jective scientific principles without revealing a conclusion,
forcing the agent to re-examine the audio signal.
• Explicit peer context injection (C exp): This scheme adopts
a demonstrative approach, providing the peer’s reasoning
and answer as direct context. This functions like a teacher-
student dynamic, allowing these agents to utilize the stronger
model’s output as reference material for critical calibration.
Rather than blindly mirroring the output, this context empow-
ers them to re-evaluate their own logical deductions against
superior acoustic perception in an explicit manner.

3. Experimental Results
3.1. Experimental Setup
Evaluations.Experiments are conducted on two widely used
audio deep reasoning benchmarks: MMAR [1] and MMAU-
mini [2]. Since existing SOTA methods only predict the final
answer, we use accuracy as the primary metric, with the reason-
ing score [32] reported only in ablation studies.
Implementation Details.We employ Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-
Instruct [30] and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Thinking [30] as the
decision agentsα 1 andα 2, respectively. The auxiliary evidence
agentα 3 integrates Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Captioner [30]
with Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 [33], the latter of which
also serves as the LLM in our framework. Whisper-large [5] is
used within the auxiliary evidence agent for tool-based actions.
The maximum number of interaction rounds is set to three.

Analysis of Asymmetric Strategy.We perform ablation stud-
ies on our role-adaptive asymmetric strategy in Table 3. First,
comparing①with②highlights the necessity of assigning roles
based on agent capability. In②,α 1 andα 2 act as decision

in①suffers from a “bucket effect”. Treating all agents equally
allows the weaker agentα 3 to drag down the overall perfor-
mance. In contrast, the role-specialized design in②alleviates
this issue and improves both accuracy and reasoning quality.
Next, comparing②with④, we explicitly feedα 2 with peer
context fromα 1 in④, whereasα 2 receives an implicit guide in
②. Due toα 2’s reasoning characteristics, the explicit context in
④allows the stronger agentα 1 to effectively guide the weaker
agentα 2, resulting in improved overall performance.
Finally, comparing③with④, we explicitly feedα 1 with
peer context fromα 2 in③, whereas this guidance is replaced
with an implicit cue in④. We observe thatα 1 tends to per-
form “textual compensation” in④, over-relying onα 2’s guid-
ance instead of grounding its reasoning in the audio. Sinceα 2
is relatively weaker and its response may contain errors, this ex-
plicit information exchange in③propagates noise and leads to
degraded performance.

4. Conclusion
We propose AsymAudio, a capability-aware multi-agent frame-
work for audio deep reasoning. By integrating asymmetric col-
laboration with intra-agent consistency refinement, AsymAudio
effectively leverages complementary agent strengths while mit-
igating instability and the bucket effect. Experiments demon-
strate substantial improvements over both single model and con-
ventional multi-agent paradigms. Our analysis further high-
lights the impact of role-adaptive strategies, offering insights
for future multi-agent audio reasoning systems.
