ML-KD-DRI-GAN: Teacher-Guided Denoising and Triplet-Adversarial
Training for Robust Spoken Language Understanding

Abstract
Automatic speech recognition (ASR) errors substantially
degrade Spoken Language Understanding (SLU) by distorting
semantic representations. We propose Multi-level Knowledge-
Distillation DRI-GAN (ML-KD-DRI-GAN), a teacher-guided
adversarial denoising framework that maps noisy embeddings
onto a clean semantic manifold. A teacher model is first trained
on clean transcripts, after which the student model is learned
through (i) multi-level latent alignment at the generator to de-
noise noisy embeddings and (ii) Bi-discriminator-cooperated
distillation (Bi-DCD) at the discriminator to transfer teacher
discriminative knowledge. We further introduce a synthetic
generator to produce hard negatives and enforce triplet sep-
aration among clean, denoised, and synthetic samples to im-
prove robustness. Experiments on noisy SLU benchmarks show
consistent gains over GAN-BERT baselines. On the SLURP
dataset, ML-KD-DRI-GAN achieves absolute improvements of
4.49% and 6.46% under moderate and severe ASR noise.
Index Terms: Automatic Speech Recognition, Natural Lan-
guage Understanding, Large Language Models
1. Introduction
Spoken Language Understanding (SLU) systems typically rely
on an automatic speech recognition (ASR) front-end followed
by downstream intent classification and slot filling [1]. In real-
world deployments, ASR outputs often contain substitution, in-
sertion, and deletion errors, resulting in a distribution mismatch
between clean training data and noisy inference inputs [2, 3].
This mismatch significantly degrades SLU performance, par-
ticularly in low-resource settings where labeled data is limited.
While end-to-end SLU [4] models jointly trained on speech sig-
nals have been explored, they require large amounts of anno-
tated speech data and remain sensitive to unseen linguistic and
acoustic variations [5, 6]. As a result, modular SLU pipelines
that leverage pre-trained language models remain a practical
choice. Recent studies have highlighted the effectiveness of
pre-trained language models for intent classification and slot-
filling tasks in few-shot SLU settings [7]. However, despite
their strong performance on clean text, such models are highly
vulnerable to ASR noise, as transcription errors propagate di-
rectly to downstream intent prediction [8].
To address this issue, prior studies have proposed
representation-level alignment strategies that encourage robust-
ness by reducing discrepancies between clean transcripts and
noisy ASR hypotheses. Methods based on contrastive learning
and consistency objectives, such as SpokenCSE [9], and CCL

[10], improve robustness by enforcing representation similarity
during training. Nevertheless, these approaches primarily fo-
cus on deterministic encoders and representation consistency,
without explicitly modeling denoising or transferring task-level
decision boundaries.
In parallel, adversarial learning has been explored to im-
prove robustness against linguistic noise in NLP tasks. GAN-
based frameworks have been applied to text correction and
robustness-oriented learning, including lexical adversarial train-
ing, GAN-like sequence labeling, and adversarial grammatical
error correction [11, 12, 13]. While effective for modeling noisy
or corrupted text, these approaches primarily operate at the sur-
face text level and are not designed to explicitly address ASR-
induced noise or task-level decision boundaries in SLU. Semi-
supervised GAN-based approaches such as GAN-BERT [14]
further demonstrate the effectiveness of adversarial learning
for low-resource text classification by combining GAN train-
ing with pre-trained language models. However, GAN-BERT is
not designed to explicitly handle ASR corruption and does not
exploit clean–noisy correspondence beyond adversarial feature
matching. More recent GAN-based SLU methods [15, 16] in-
troduce distribution-level objectives and metric learning to im-
prove robustness, but they still lack a unified teacher–student
distillation mechanism that aligns clean and noisy utterances at
multiple semantic levels. Hu et al. [17] proposed Discriminator-
Cooperated Distillation (DCD), showing that discriminator rep-
resentations enable effective knowledge transfer in GAN. Sim-
ilarly, recent work [18] explored embedding-space distribution
alignment between teacher and student generators via latent dis-
tillation to enhance GAN efficiency. Inspired by such recent ad-
vances in GAN [18, 17], and Noisy-SLU [19], we propose ML-
KD-DRI-GAN, a generative adversarial teacher–student frame-
work that extends DRI-GAN to explicitly address ASR noise.
Our approach first trains a teacher model on clean transcrip-
tions, and subsequently guides a student model trained on noisy
inputs through multi-level latent alignment at the generator,
bi-discriminator cooperative knowledge distillation at the dis-
criminator, and triplet-based metric learning. By jointly per-
forming denoising, decision-boundary transfer, and geometry
preservation within an adversarial learning paradigm, ML-KD-
DRI-GAN enables robust intent classification under severe ASR
noise. Our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose ML-KD-DRI-GAN, a teacher-guided adversar-
ial denoising framework that projects noisy SLU embeddings
onto a clean semantic manifold via multi-level latent align-
ment to improve robustness under ASR corruption.
• We introduce Bi-discriminator-cooperated distillation (Bi-
DCD) by adapting DCD-style knowledge transfer [17] to the
discriminator, enabling effective transfer of teacher discrimi-
native knowledge and enhanced decision boundaries.

2. Methodology
Our proposed framework, ML-KD-DRI-GAN, is designed to
improve SLU from noisy ASR transcripts by jointly perform-
ing denoising, distribution alignment, and knowledge distilla-
tion in the embedding space. Figure 1-a (Overview) illustrates
the complete pipeline, while Figures (b)–(d) decompose the
teacher training and student learning into generator-side and
discriminator-side objectives.
2.1. Problem Formulation
LetX clean denote the clean transcription andX noisy its ASR-
corrupted version. Lety∈{1,...,K}be the intent label. We
use a BERT encoderE(·)to obtain continuous embeddings:
hclean =E(X clean)∈R d,(1)
hnoisy =E(X noisy)∈R d,(2)
Althoughh noisy retains same intent information, it typi-
cally exhibits semantic distortion, distributional shift, and re-
duced class separability due to ASR errors. The goal of ML-
KD-DRI-GAN is therefore to learn a transformation that: i)
Mapsh noisy toward the clean semantic manifold defined by
hclean, ii) Preserves intent-discriminative structure, and iii) Im-
proves robustness against unseen noise.
2.2. Architecture Overview
ML-KD-DRI-GAN extends the DRI-GAN framework [15] by
integrating multi-level latent alignment, knowledge distillation,
and metric learning into a unified training scheme. The pro-
posed architecture explicitly denoises ASR-corrupted embed-
dings through multi-level latent alignment between a frozen
teacher generator(G T )and a trainable student generator(G S),
guiding the student to recover noise-invariant semantic repre-
sentations. While generator-side latent alignment focuses on
denoising representations, Bi-DCD transfers task-level discrim-
inative knowledge from the teacher discriminator(D T )to the
student discriminator(D S). This is achieved by aligning both
intermediate bottleneck features and class logits, enabling the
student discriminator to learn more stable and noise-robust de-
cision boundaries. In parallel, a synthetic generator continues
to produce noise-driven embeddings, preserving the adversar-
ial min–max game between the student discriminator and the
synthetic generator(G S2). Consequently, although adversar-
ial training dynamics are maintained, the student’s real embed-
dings are now teacher-aligned and semantically denoised, lead-
ing to more robust intent classification under noisy ASR condi-
tions.
ML-KD-GAN contains: (i) a frozen teacher generatorG T
and teacher discriminatorD T , (ii) a trainable teacher-aligned
student generatorG S, and student discriminatorD S, and (iii) a
synthetic student generatorG S2.

3. Datasets and Implementation Details
We evaluate the proposed ML-KD-DRI-GAN method on the
intent detection task using the SLURP dataset [22]. SLURP is
a large-scale SLU dataset covering diverse domains, speakers,
and recording environments, with intent labels defined as (sce-
nario, action) pairs. SLURP contains 60 intent classes with an
average utterance length of 6.9 tokens. The dataset consists of
50,628 training samples, 8,690 validation samples, and 10,992
test samples. To simulate realistic noisy SLU conditions, we
use ASR hypotheses generated by the Google Web API and
Wav2Vec 2.0 [23], obtained from the SpokenCSE 1 [9]. The
median Word Error Rates (WER) are approximately 25% and
60%, respectively.
For the noisy SLU task, we employ contextualized text rep-
resentations extracted using BERT [24] embeddings. The archi-
tectures of the teacher and student generators and discrimina-
tors are MLP-based with leaky ReLU activations, following the
design described in Section 2.3. The additional synthetic gen-
erator used to produce synthetic embedding of 768-dimension
from random noise follows the same configuration as GAN-
BERT [14]. We adopt a staged training strategy commonly
used in knowledge distillation and metric learning frameworks
[25, 26]. First, the teacher model is trained independently us-
ing clean supervision. Next, the student model is initialized and
trained for 10 epochs using only adversarial losses, without ap-
plying any distillation or metric learning objectives, allowing it
to learn a stable representation. After this warm-up phase, la-
tent alignment and multi-level knowledge distillation losses are
introduced, together with contrastive metric learning, to further
guide the student toward the teacher’s representation space.
All experiments are conducted with a batch size of 32 on
NVIDIA A100 (80 GB) GPUs. To ensure robustness, each ex-
periment is repeated five times with different random shuffles,
and the average performance is reported.

Table 1 compares the proposed method with prior ap-
proaches and the GAN-BERT baseline on the noisy SLURP
intent detection task. Over the GAN-BERT baseline, ML-
KD-DRI-GAN consistently delivers absolute gains of 4.49%
and 6.46% under moderate and severe ASR noise, respectively.
These results demonstrate the effectiveness of incorporating
multi-level knowledge distillation for robust SLU under noisy
transcriptions. When compared with other competitive meth-
ods such as SpokenCSE, DRI-GAN, and CCL, our approach
achieves the best overall performance across noisy conditions.
This improvement highlights the advantage of incorporating ad-
versarial learning with multi-level knowledge distillation.
Table 2 presents a progressive ablation study of the student
model. Starting from a student DRI-GAN without distillation,
adding generator-side latent alignment yields substantial gains,
particularly under higher WER. Introducing discriminator-side
knowledge distillation further improves performance, while the
addition of triplet loss provides the final boost, resulting in the
full ML-KD-DRI-GAN model. This confirms that each compo-
nent contributes incrementally to robustness under noisy ASR
conditions. Figure 2 illustrates the sensitivity of the student
model to generator-side distillation weight (α), discriminator-
side distillation weight (β), and triplet loss weight (γ). Per-
formance consistently improves with increasing supervision
strength up to an optimal range, after which gains saturate, with
larger improvements observed under higher ASR noise. This
highlights the importance of balanced distillation and metric
learning for stable training.
Figure 3 provides a t-SNE visualization of the embeddings
on a subset of SLURP intent classes. Compared to the student
without distillation, the novel approach produces more compact
intra-class clusters and clearer inter-class separation. This indi-
cates that knowledge distillation and metric learning effectively
transfer discriminative structure from the teacher to the student.
4. Conclusion
This paper presented ML-KD-DRI-GAN, a multi-level knowl-
edge distillation framework for robust SLU under noisy ASR
conditions. By progressively distilling latent representations,
discriminator knowledge, and metric structure from a teacher
model, the proposed approach significantly improves intent de-
tection performance over strong baselines. Extensive experi-
ments on the SLURP dataset demonstrate consistent gains, sup-
ported by ablation studies. These results highlight the effec-
tiveness of combining adversarial denoising, knowledge distil-
lation, and metric learning for noise-robust SLU, while future
work will explore its generalization across more diverse real-
world acoustic conditions and ASR systems.
