GradHarmony: A Gradient Alignment and Magnitude Normalization
Strategy for Audio Deepfake Detection

Abstract
In audio deepfake detection, data augmentation improves model
generalization by exposing them to diverse data variations.
However, gradients computed from clean and augmented sam-
ples may become directionally misaligned and differ in mag-
nitude. These discrepancies may be amplified in multi-
augmentation settings, leading to unstable optimization. In
this paper, we propose GradHarmony, a gradient alignment and
magnitude normalization strategy for stable multi-augmentation
training. GradHarmony computes gradients separately for clean
data and each augmentation type, and aligns conflicting aug-
mented gradients using the clean gradient as a reference. Then
adaptively normalizes their magnitudes using exponential mov-
ing average (EMA)-based statistics to prevent dominance. Ex-
perimental results show performance improvements across a va-
riety of models, with two state-of-the-art models achieving an
average 22% reduction in equal error rate compared to the base-
line on out-of-domain datasets.
Index Terms: audio deepfake detection, data augmentation,
gradient alignment, magnitude normalization
1. Introduction
In audio deepfake detection (ADD), data augmentation has been
widely adopted to better reflect realistic conditions during train-
ing [1, 2, 3]. By allowing models to learn from more diverse
audio variations, augmentation significantly contributes to im-
proved generalization performance [4]. Despite its effective-
ness, multi-augmentation training introduces non-trivial opti-
mization challenges. Augmented data often exhibit character-
istics different from clean data [5, 6], affecting both the magni-
tude and direction of their corresponding gradients. As a result,
gradients derived from clean and augmented samples may be-
come misaligned during backpropagation, potentially slowing
down optimization or leading the model toward suboptimal so-
lutions. Here, the clean gradient denotes the gradient computed
from the original input sample, while augmented gradients are
computed from inputs transformed through augmentation op-
erations. In multi-augmentation training, multiple augmented
samples are included within a single iteration, producing multi-
ple gradients that jointly influence the parameter update.
In multi-task learning, several studies have addressed simi-
lar issues by explicitly aligning gradients across tasks [7, 8, 9],
demonstrating improved optimization efficiency. However, in
ADD, gradient misalignment has received limited attention.
Although recent work [10] has shown performance improve-
ments by aligning augmented gradients with their correspond-
ing clean gradients, it primarily focuses on single-augmentation

Furthermore, while directional alignment has been explored,
the impact of imbalanced or dominant gradient magnitudes [11]

To address these limitations, we propose GradHarmony, a
gradient alignment and magnitude normalization strategy for
stable multi-augmentation training. GradHarmony consists of
a Clean-referenced Gradient Alignment (CGA) mechanism and
an Exponential Moving Average (EMA)-based [12] Magnitude
Normalization (EMA-MN) scheme. CGA treats the clean gra-
dient as a stable optimization anchor and aligns conflicting aug-
mented gradients accordingly. In addition, it employs EMA-
MN to normalize gradient magnitudes and mitigate dominance
from imbalanced augmentation signals. By jointly control-
ling directional conflicts and magnitude discrepancies, Grad-
Harmony accelerates convergence and improves generalization
in multi-augmentation training across both in-domain and out-
of-domain datasets.

In ADD, previous work [10] demonstrated that aligning
clean and augmented gradients using methods such as PCGrad
[7] or GradVac [8] improves generalization. However, these ap-
proaches primarily consider a dual-path setting that aligns gra-
dients between a clean sample and a single augmentation, and
do not explicitly address scenarios involving clean data with
multiple augmentations. PCGrad and GradVac were originally
developed for multi-task learning, where each task is associ-
ated with a distinct objective. In contrast, multi-augmentation
training in ADD does not involve independent objectives for
each augmentation type. When multiple augmentations are
present simultaneously, pairwise alignment lacks a criterion
for determining a consistent reference direction and does not
guarantee a coherent alignment direction across gradients. To-
gether, these considerations highlight the need for an optimiza-
tion strategy that maintains directional consistency with respect
to a clear reference while controlling magnitude imbalance in
multi-augmentation training for audio deepfake detection.

which consists of two components: (1)Clean-referenced Gra-

(2)EMA-based Magnitude Normalization (EMA-MN)for
mitigating magnitude imbalance.

3.2. Clean-referenced Gradient Alignment
As discussed in Section 2, pairwise alignment can reduce con-
flicts between gradient pairs, yet it does not establish a consis-
tent optimization reference when multiple augmented gradients
are present. To address this, we adopt a Clean-referenced Gradi-
ent Alignment (CGA) mechanism that explicitly uses the clean
gradient as the reference direction. Because augmented sam-
ples are generated through label-preserving transformations of
the original clean data, clean and augmented samples share the
same supervision signal rather than representing independent
objectives [14, 15]. From this perspective, the clean objective
provides the principal optimization direction, while augmented
objectives introduce transformed variations intended to enhance
robustness. Accordingly, the clean gradient is treated as a fixed
reference anchor, and each augmented gradient is aligned with
respect to it.
CGA can be implemented using different gradient align-
ment operators. In this work, we instantiate CGA using PCGrad
as a baseline due to its simplicity and strong empirical perfor-
mance in ADD [10]. Unlike standard PCGrad, which performs
pairwise alignment among all gradients, we restrict the align-
ment operation to occur only between each augmented gradient
and the clean gradient. The clean gradient itself remains un-
changed and serves as a fixed optimization anchor. Specifically,
when a negative inner product is detected, we remove the con-
flicting component of the augmented gradient:

4. Evaluation
4.1. Experimental Setup
We evaluate our method on both non-self-supervised learn-
ing (non-SSL) and self-supervised learning (SSL)-based mod-
els. Non-SSL models include AASIST [13], RawNet2 [16],
and RawGATST [17], while SSL-based models include SSL-
AASIST [18] and SSL-Conformer [19]. All models are trained
on the ASVspoof 2019 Logical Access dataset [20]. Evaluation
is conducted on ASVspoof 2021 DeepFake (DF21) including
hidden track [21], In-the-Wild (ITW) [22], DSD-Corpus (DSD)
[23], and Fake-or-Real (FoR) [24]. DF21 serves as in-domain
benchmark, whereas ITW, DSD and FoR assess generalization
under real-world and diverse deepfake conditions.
For multi-augmentation training, we employ commonly
adopted augmentation techniques in ADD, including RawBoost
[1], MUSAN [3], and Room Impulse Response (RIR) [2]. Per-
formance is evaluated using the Equal Error Rate (EER).
4.2. Implementation details
We trained the models using NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 GPU. For
all models, we follow the official open-source implementations
and retain their default architectural and training configurations
unless otherwise specified. Each mini-batch is composed of
50% clean samples and 50% augmented samples. The aug-
mented portion is evenly distributed across augmentation types
to ensure balanced exposure to different perturbations within
each training step. For RawBoost augmentation, we adopt Al-
gorithm 4, which integrates the effects of Algorithms 3 and 5
and generates more diverse distortion patterns. Rather than op-
timizing augmentation settings for individual models, we use
a unified configuration across all experiments to ensure fair
and consistent comparison. We also use fixed hyperparameters
across all models (β= 0.99,α= 2andN w = 100) without
model-specific tuning to ensure fair comparison.

4.4. Ablation Study
We conduct an ablation study, evaluating CGA and EMA-MN
individually and in combination. From Table 2, applying only
CGA generally improves performance on several out-of-domain
datasets. This suggests that resolving directional conflicts en-
hances robustness under diverse acoustic conditions. How-
ever, for SSL-AASIST, applying only CGA slightly degrades
in-domain performance. This implies that directional align-
ment alone may not fully prevent optimization bias when large-
magnitude gradients dominate the update. Such dominance can
lead the optimization trajectory toward particular augmented
views. In contrast, using only EMA-MN yields limited and
sometimes inconsistent improvements. Although magnitude
normalization mitigates gradient dominance, it does not resolve
directional conflicts. These unresolved conflicts can still hinder
effective optimization. When combined, CGA and EMA-MN
achieve the best performance across models and datasets. This
demonstrates that directional alignment and magnitude normal-
ization provide complementary benefits, jointly improving ro-
bustness and stability in multi-augmentation training.

4.5. Effect of EMA-based Thresholding
Table 3 compares magnitude normalization with and without
EMA smoothing. Overall, applying EMA leads to more consis-
tent performance across datasets. Without EMA, per-iteration
median normalization can achieve competitive results on certain
datasets, but its performance varies substantially across condi-
tions. This variability suggests that directly using the median as
a threshold is sensitive to fluctuations in gradient magnitude,
which may result in suboptimal scaling. In contrast, EMA-
based thresholding stabilizes the normalization process and pro-
vides more reliable performance under diverse evaluation con-
ditions.
4.6. Experiments with Different Alignment Methods
Table 4 presents the results when GradVac [8] is used as the gra-
dient alignment operator within GradHarmony. Even with this
alternative alignment method, GradHarmony improves perfor-
mance over baseline and augmentation training while reducing
the number of training epochs required for convergence. These
results indicate that the effectiveness of GradHarmony are not
specific to a particular alignment operator. Consequently, the
proposed strategy can be flexibly integrated with different align-
ment methods, demonstrating its extensibility and practical ap-
plicability in multi-augmentation training.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced GradHarmony, which combines
Clean-referenced Gradient Alignment with EMA-based Mag-
nitude Normalization for stable multi-augmentation training in
audio deepfake detection. By jointly addressing directional con-
flicts and magnitude imbalance, the proposed strategy improves
generalization and accelerates convergence across various mod-
els and evaluation datasets.
While the clean gradient serves as an effective reference in
our experiments, investigating adaptive or data-driven reference
selection strategies remains a direction for future work. In addi-
tion, exploring the scalability of the proposed strategy in more
diverse augmentation settings may further enhance its applica-
bility. Finally, although the clipping scale factorαis treated
as a fixed hyperparameter in this work, future research may in-
vestigate adaptive normalization strategies that adjust clipping
strength based on training statistics or data conditions.
