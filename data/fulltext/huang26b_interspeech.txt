Abstract
Individualized head-related transfer functions (HRTFs) are cru-
cial for immersive audio, yet their acquisition remains challeng-
ing. Existing deep-learning approaches are often constrained by
discrete sampling grids and lose fine morphological details dur-
ing mesh preprocessing. In this work, we propose a geometri-
cally informed spherical neural operator (GISNO), a framework
that reformulates HRTF prediction as a continuous operator-
learning problem. By integrating a discretization-invariant
neural operator with a differentiable Helmholtz-integral ren-
derer, our model learns to map 3D head geometry to contin-
uous boundary pressure fields. Experiments on the HUTUBS
dataset demonstrate that our method outperforms represen-
tative baselines in log-spectral distortion. Furthermore, the
physics-integrated architecture enables multi-dimensional zero-
shot generalization across unseen spatial densities, distances,
and frequencies, offering a robust solution for scalable HRTF
personalization.

1. Introduction
Head-related transfer functions (HRTFs) characterize the
acoustic filtering process wherein a listener’s anatomy, com-
prising the pinnae, head, and torso, shapes sound propaga-
tion through frequency-dependent scattering and diffraction [1].
These functions are indispensable for high-fidelity binaural ren-
dering in immersive virtual and augmented reality (VR/AR)
systems. In practice, spatial audio is frequently rendered us-
ing generic HRTFs measured from dummy heads. However,
the use of non-individualized HRTFs often leads to perceptual
degradations, such as front–back confusions [2], reduced exter-
nalization [3], and diminished localization accuracy [4].
High-quality immersive experiences thus necessitate indi-
vidualized HRTFs. While direct acoustic measurements in
anechoic chambers [5] provide high accuracy, the process is
time-consuming and requires specialized equipment, hindering
large-scale deployment. Alternatively, computational acoustics
can estimate subject-specific HRTFs from 3D head scans us-
ing numerical methods such as the boundary element method
(BEM) [6, 7]. Nevertheless, the fidelity of these simulations
heavily relies on high-quality mesh generation and precise
boundary condition assumptions. Furthermore, the substantial
computational cost of full-wave solvers limits their utility for
real-time or resource-constrained applications.
To circumvent these challenges, data-driven HRTF person-
alization has gained significant traction. Early research focused

capture richer morphological details, subsequent studies lever-

More recently, deep learning architectures such as CNNs and U-
Nets have been applied directly to 3D geometric data to predict
HRTFs, aiming to better capture the intricate geometry of the
ear that governs high-frequency cues [12, 13]. Other approaches
exploit the inherent spatial-spectral correlations by jointly pre-
dicting HRTFs across multiple directions or planes [14].
Despite these advancements, most existing data-driven
models are intrinsically tied to fixed, discrete sampling grids.
Consequently, their output spatial resolution is constrained by
the measurement directions present in the training dataset, ne-
cessitating additional interpolation for off-grid directions. Fur-
thermore, the common practice of resampling irregular 3D
meshes onto regular grids during preprocessing can smear local-
ized geometric features (e.g., pinna folds), thereby limiting the
model’s predictive accuracy and its ability to generalize across
different mesh resolutions.
In this work, we propose a geometrically informed spher-
ical neural operator (GISNO), a novel framework for HRTF
personalization that bridges neural operator learning with
classical computational acoustics. Unlike traditional neural
networks, neural operators learn mappings between infinite-
dimensional function spaces, offering a discretization-invariant
representation that is independent of the underlying grid reso-
lution [15]. By leveraging the spherical Fourier neural operator
(SFNO) [16] and graph neural operator (GNO) [17], GISNO
casts the HRTF prediction task as learning the mapping from
the 3D head geometry to the boundary complex pressure func-
tionp(y)on the surfaceΓ. By integrating this learned boundary
distribution with a differentiable Helmholtz integral renderer,
we can analytically determine the sound pressure (and thus the
HRTF) at any arbitrary field location in a physically consistent
manner. Experimental results demonstrate that GISNO not only
improves prediction accuracy but also enables zero-shot gen-
eralization across multiple dimensions including spatial super-
resolution, radial extrapolation, and spectral refinement beyond
the discrete configurations of the training set.

3. Proposed Method
The architecture of GISNO is designed to bridge unstructured
geometric feature learning with continuous acoustic field propa-
gation. As illustrated in Figure 1, the framework couples a neu-
ral operator front-end with a physics-based differentiable ren-
dering pipeline, comprising three core modules:
•GNO-based Encoder–Decoder: It facilitates discretization-
invariant feature transfer between unstructured head meshes
and a regular spherical latent grid.
•SFNO-based Latent Mapper: It models global acoustic
scattering interactions on the sphere using spherical convo-
lutions.
•Differentiable Helmholtz Renderer: It analytically maps
the predicted boundary field to sound pressures at arbitrary
field locations via Eq. (3).

the original mesh vertices.

3.3. Differentiable Helmholtz Rendering
The boundary complex pressureˆp(y)predicted by the neural
operator is not directly supervised. Instead, we pass it through a
differentiable renderer that implements the Helmholtz Integral
Formula (Eq. 3). This renderer propagates the predicted bound-
ary field to any set of evaluation pointsx∈Ω c.
To handle complex-valued computations in standard deep
learning frameworks, we represent all complex quantities as
two-channel real tensorsp= [ℜ{p},ℑ{p}] ⊤. The Green’s
function and its normal derivative are decomposed into their re-
spective real and imaginary components. The entire rendering
process is fully differentiable, allowing the loss computed at
the field points to back-propagate through the integral operator
to the front-end network.
This design offers two primary advantages. First, it en-
forcesphysical consistency: the predicted HRTF is guaranteed
to satisfy the Helmholtz equation by construction. Second, it
enableszero-shot spatial generalization, as the renderer can
evaluate the field at any distance or angular resolution without
retraining the network.

4. Experiments
4.1. Implementation Details
The GISNO front-end employs a65×60equiangular spherical
latent grid. The latent mapper consists of three SFNO blocks
with a downsampling factor of 2, while the GNO-based en-
coder and decoder handle the feature transfer between the la-
tent grid and the mesh. To optimize computational efficiency,
we exploit theacoustic reciprocity principle. By placing a vir-
tual monopole source at each ear entrance, the front-end net-
work only needs to perform two forward passes per subject to
3330
predict the boundary fields. The Helmholtz-integral renderer
then analytically computes the HRTFs for all target directions.
The model is trained using the AdamW optimizer with an initial
learning rate of2×10 −4 and a cosine annealing schedule.
4.2. Evaluation on the HUTUBS Dataset
We evaluate our framework on the HUTUBS dataset [18],
which provides 3D head meshes for 55 human subjects. The
original head-related impulse responses (HRIRs) are trans-
formed into the frequency domain via a 256-point fast Fourier
transform (FFT) to obtain complex-valued HRTF spectra.
From the 55 available subjects, 40 are selected for train-
ing and 15 are reserved for testing. To mitigate overfitting
and improve the model’s robustness to 3D scanning artifacts,
we implement a geometry-based data augmentation strategy on
the training set. Specifically, we perturb the mesh vertices by
adding zero-mean Gaussian noise along their local outward nor-
mal directions. This stochastic jittering simulates the measure-
ment uncertainty inherent in 3D scanning while keeping the cor-
responding ground-truth complex pressures constant. By gener-
ating three such augmented variants per subject, we effectively
expand the training set from 40 to 160 meshes.

4.3. Multi-dimensional Zero-shot Generalization
A key advantage of the proposed GISNO framework is its abil-
ity to generalize across spatial and spectral dimensions without
retraining. To verify this, we use Mesh2HRTF [21] to generate
a high-fidelity ground-truth dataset with two distinct configura-
tions:
•Training Set:1,550 evaluation points on a 1.2 m sphere
(ARI protocol [22]) with a frequency step∆f= 150Hz.
•Zero-shot Test Set:7,800 evaluation points on a 1.5 m
sphere with a finer frequency step∆f= 100Hz.
This setup requires the model to simultaneously perform
spatial super-resolution(1,550 to 7,800 points),radial extrap-
olation(1.2 m to 1.5 m), andspectral refinement(150 Hz to
100 Hz). As shown in Figure 3, the predicted pressure distribu-
tion on the 1.5 m sphere atf= 1000Hz is in excellent agree-
ment with the ground truth. The normalized magnitude error
over the entire sphere yields a mean of 0.0139 and a standard
deviation of 0.0264. These results confirm that by learning the
boundary operator and employing a physics-based renderer, the
model captures the underlying continuous acoustic field rather
than merely interpolating between training samples.

5. Conclusion & Discussion
We presented GISNO, a neural operator framework that shifts
HRTF personalization from discrete regression to continuous
operator learning. Operating directly on 3D meshes, GISNO
preserves detailed morphological cues often lost in standard
preprocessing. Furthermore, its discretization-invariant nature
allows HRTF prediction at arbitrary spatial resolutions and
frequencies without retraining, merging data-driven efficiency
with computational flexibility.
Crucially, GISNO enforces physical consistency. Unlike
black-box models, it constrains the predicted boundary pres-
sure fields to satisfy the Helmholtz equation. This physics-
informed inductive bias drives the model to learn underlying
scattering phenomena rather than merely interpolating training
data. Our zero-shot experiments—demonstrating strong gener-
alization across unseen spatial densities, radial distances, and
frequencies—confirm that GISNO learns a robust representa-
tion of acoustic wave propagation.
Despite these promising results, several limitations remain.
Our evaluation relies on the relatively small HUTUBS dataset,
and zero-shot capabilities were primarily validated on simulated
data. Future work will extend validation to larger, more diverse
datasets and real measured HRTFs.
Finally, GISNO’s mesh-independent parameterization un-
locks scalable training across heterogeneous datasets. By de-
coupling the model from specific measurement grids, it seam-
lessly integrates diverse data sources toward universal acoustic
modeling. Overall, GISNO provides a physically grounded and
scalable approach to HRTF personalization, offering a promis-
ing step toward bridging data-driven learning and computa-
tional acoustics.
