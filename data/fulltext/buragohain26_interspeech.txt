Decoder Framework for High-Fidelity Speech Reconstruction

Abstract
This paper presents a novel speech synthesis framework that
reconstructs audible speech directly from invasive electroen-
cephalogram (EEG) recordings, enabling a communication op-
tion for individuals with severe speech difficulties. The frame-
work exploits time–frequency features extracted from the high-
gamma band (70–170 Hz), strongly linked to speech produc-
tion and perception, and maps them to speech representations
using a WaveNet-based decoder. This framework comprises
stacked causal dilated convolutional residual blocks with gated
activations to capture both short-term articulatory patterns and
long-range temporal relationships while maintaining causal-
ity. Residual and skip connections enhance feature propagation
and stabilize training. Experimental results demonstrate strong
correlations between predicted and actual spectrograms, high-
lighting both robustness and inter-subject variability, thereby
advancing state-of-the-art neural speech decoding for brain-
computer interfaces (BCIs).
Index Terms: Speech synthesis, brain-computer interfaces,
deep neural networks, EEG.
1. Introduction
Speech problems, caused by damage to the neuromuscular or
vocal tissues hinder sound production and can substantially de-
grade communication and overall quality of life [1, 2]. These
diseases, such as stuttering and apraxia, are widespread glob-
ally, especially in youngsters; recent research report that around
7.7% of U.S. children aged 3-17 years and nearly 11% of those
aged 3-6 years encounter speech-related issues each year [3–5].
Neuroprosthetic technologies, known as brain-computer inter-
faces (BCIs), offer a promising method for recovering com-
munication by directly connecting with the neural system to
circumvent damaged speech musculature. This is particularly
pertinent for disorders like amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS),
where speech muscles are compromised while cognition re-
mains unaffected [2, 6–8]. These devices, whether implanted
or external, facilitate more natural and successful interactions
compared to traditional communication aids, offering essential
support for patients with significant motor impairments [9, 10].
Automatic speech recognition (ASR) and speech synthe-
sis are the two primary approaches for decoding speech from
brain signals. The ASR-based method [11–13] employs spe-
cialized software and models to transform brain signals into
textual representations, whereas the speech synthesis method
seeks to produce audible sound directly from the neural signals.
In this study, we concentrate on the second approach. Con-
ventional methods for decoding and synthesizing speech spec-
trograms from brain signals, including linear models and for-
mant synthesis with Kalman filters [14], demonstrated subop-
timal performance. This yields low-quality synthesized audio
and Pearson correlation coefficients (PCC) below 0.7 [15–17].
Recent advances utilize deep neural networks (DNNs), includ-
ing convolutional and recurrent architectures, which enhance
intermediate representations and the quality of speech [18–20].
However, speech synthesis from neural signals remains chal-
lenging due to their inherently noisy and redundant nature, low
signal-to-noise ratios, and temporal mismatches between neu-
ral activity and speech representations. These factors hinder
the reliable extraction of fine-grained speech attributes, such
as prosody and articulation [21]. The authors of [22] devel-
oped a foundational neural-to-spectrogram decoding paradigm
employing linear regression (LR). Building upon this, the au-
thors of [23] developed a deep learning (DL) model using fully
connected networks (FCN) and convolutional/locally connected
networks (CNN/LCN) to restore WORLD vocoder [24] param-
eters, illustrating the advantages of nonlinear approaches over
LR. Recently, the authors of [25] introduced a NeuroIncept ar-
chitecture that combines multi-scale Inception CNN modules
with Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) to efficiently describe in-
tricate spectral and temporal neural patterns. Despite these ad-
vancements, existing approaches are limited in their modeling
capability, especially in their ability to capture long-range tem-
poral correlations and inter-subject variability. As a result, they
have difficulties in producing high-fidelity, temporally consis-
tent speech representations from complex brain processes.
Motivated from the aforementioned works, we propose a
novel neural speech decoding system that integrates logarith-
mic Mel-scaled spectrogram (logMel) representations with a
WaveNet-based decoder to directly reconstruct audible speech
from neural activity. The use of logMel offers a concise and
perceptually grounded acoustic representation that emphasizes
speech-relevant spectral features while minimizing redundancy
and dynamic range, making it ideal for learning from noisy and
high-dimensional neural signals. To effectively represent the in-
tricate temporal dynamics of speech production and perception,
we utilize a WaveNet Decoder consisting of stacked causal di-
lated convolutional residual blocks. Each block employs gated
activation units to selectively regulate the information flow,
allowing the network to capture fine-grained temporal details
and long-range relationships while maintaining causality. The
dual residual and skip connections facilitate robust deep learn-
ing and enable the effective aggregation of multi-scale tempo-
ral features at the output. This architectural design addresses
the constraints of previous recurrent or fixed-receptive-field ap-
proaches by offering a flexible, scalable, and entirely convo-
lutional framework for temporal modeling. The combination
of the logMel representation with the gated, dilated WaveNet
Decoder form a robust and innovative approach to neural-
to-speech decoding, advancing the state-of-the-art (SOTA) in

2. Proposed Methodology
2.1. Dataset Description
In this work, we use a publicly accessible dataset [22] of stereo-
tactic EEG (sEEG) recordings from 10 Dutch participants (5
males and 5 females, mean age: 32 years) diagnosed with phar-
macoresistant epilepsy. Depth sEEG electrodes were implanted
in participants as a component of their clinical treatment. The
electrode’s placement was determined exclusively by clinical
necessities, primarily focusing on the superior temporal sulcus,
the hippocampus, and the inferior parietal gyrus. Consequently,
the number and placement of the electrodes differed among the
subjects. sEEG signals were captured at either 2048 Hz or 1024
Hz, synced with participants’ speech (sampling rate (fs)=48
kHz), as they read aloud a list of 100 words from the Dutch IFA
corpus [26]. The sEEG recordings were subsequently down-
sampled to 16 kHz for further analysis. To maintain participant
identity, pitch modulation was employed to the audio recordings
utilizing the Librosa package [27].
2.2. Signal Preprocessing
The sEEG signals for each subject were represented using time-
frequency features derived from the high-gamma band (70-170
Hz) (see Fig. 1 (a)). This band was selected based on prior
research indicating that it encompasses information related to
speech and language production and perception [21,23,28]. The
raw sEEG data was initially detrended to eliminate linear trends.
A Butterworth bandpass filter (70-170 Hz) was subsequently
employed to isolate high-gamma frequency components, while
a notch filter was utilized to remove line noise (50 Hz) and its
first two harmonics (100 Hz, 150 Hz), therefore further puri-
fying the signal. Following filtration, the Hilbert transform was
employed to calculate the analytic signal, facilitating the extrac-
tion of the signal envelope that captures amplitude variations
within the high-gamma band. The processed sEEG data were
segmented into overlapping temporal windows of 0.05 seconds,
with a frame shift of 0.01 seconds. For each window, the mean
amplitude was calculated, resulting in a feature matrix for fur-
ther analysis. To integrate temporal dynamics, each 0.05s win-
dow was expanded by incorporating the features from both the
current and adjacent time windows. The process used a sliding
window technique with a model order of 4 and a step size of
5, thereby improving temporal resolution and capturing inter-
dependence across various time intervals.
On the other hand, the audio samples were transformed
intologMelwith 128 spectral bins, as depicted in Fig. 1 (a).
The logMel spectrograms were derived from 0.05 seconds with
a frame shift of 0.01 seconds, employing a Hanning window.
The neural data for each participant was standardized using
z-score normalization, which improves comparability between
data points and optimizes subsequent model training processes.
2.3. Proposed WaveNet Decoding Model Architecture
The proposed WaveNet Decoder model, depicted in Figure 1
(b), aims to simulate long-range temporal dependencies in se-
quential data through the utilization of causal and dilated con-
volutions. In contrast to recurrent (e.g., Recurrent Neural Net-
works (RNNs)) architectures, the model exclusively utilizes
convolutional operations, facilitating parallel processing while
maintaining strict temporal causality. The decoder network
comprises three main components: an initial projection layer, a
series of stacked dilated WaveNet blocks featuring residual and
skip connections, and a fully connected (FC) decoding stage.
The input sequence is initially processed using a 1×1 con-
volutional layer with 64 filters to transform the input features
into a higher-dimensionality latent space. This linear projection
synchronizes the input dimensionality with the next WaveNet
blocks while preserving temporal resolution. The core of the
proposed architecture consists of four layered WaveNet mod-
ules, each with five dilated convolutional blocks with exponen-
tially increasing dilation rates (such as, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16). Each
WaveNet block utilizes a gated activation method, wherein par-
allel causal convolutions with “tanh” and “sigmoid” activations
are multiplicatively integrated. This gating mechanism allows
the model to control information flow and capture intricate tem-
poral dynamics. Each block incorporates residual connections
that promote stable gradient propagation and enhance model-
ing capacity, as well as skip connections that maintain multi-
scale temporal information across layers. When the dimensions
of the input and output channels are disparate, a 1×1 convolu-
tion is employed to maintain dimensional consistency in resid-
ual summation. All skip connections from the dilated blocks
are combined through element-wise addition and subsequently

processed by a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation layer.
A Global Average pooling (GAP) layer subsequently reduces
the temporal dimension, yielding a concise representation that
encapsulates the acquired long-term temporal dependencies.
The aggregated features undergo processing via a series of FC
layers with diminishing dimensionality (512→256→128→32).
Dropout regularization (DP=0.2) is employed between layers to
reduce overfitting and improve generalization. The final linear
output layer maps the refined representation into the target out-
put dimensions, generating the decoder’s prediction. The pro-
posed decoder architecture efficiently captures both short- and
long-term temporal relationships using dilated causal convolu-
tions and gated activations, while circumventing the computing
burden associated with recurrent models. This makes it espe-
cially appropriate for real-time and low-latency sequence de-
coding jobs. The model comprises 2.17M trainable parameters.
2.4. Network Optimization and Training Paradigm
For each participant, the dataset included roughly 5 minutes of
synchronized sEEG and audio recordings obtained while read-
ing aloud a predetermined list of 100 Dutch words. The data
were split into training and validation sets with an 80:20 ra-
tio, ensuring a rigorous separation of samples to avert any data
leakage. During training, the settings of the proposed WaveNet
Decoder were tuned utilizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE)
loss function between the predicted and the original audio spec-
trograms. Early stopping was used with a patience of 5 epochs
to alleviate overfitting. Model training was performed for up
to 100 epochs with the Adam optimizer with a learning rate
of 0.0001 and a batch size of 128. For evaluation, 1000 sam-
ples were randomly chosen from the validation set for each
participant and analyzed using 10-fold cross-validation. Per-
formance was computed using the PCC to measure the simi-
larity between the spectrograms produced by the WaveNet De-
coder and the original audio spectrograms. Furthermore, the
Spectral-Temporal Glimpsing Index (STGI) [29] was used to
assess the retention of temporal and spectral patterns in the
reconstructed spectrograms. The main aim of this work is
neural-to-spectrogram; hence, subjective hearing tests were not
performed. Subsequent research will integrate vocoder-based
waveform reconstruction to facilitate perceptual assessment of
the neural-to-audio decoding. The experiments were performed
on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 GPU (12 GB VRAM), RAM
64 GB, and running Ubuntu 24.04.3. Model was implemented
using Python 3.10 and TensorFlow-Keras version 2.15.0.

3.2. Comparative Analysis
Table 2 presents a comprehensive performance comparison of
the proposed WaveNet Decoder model with several SOTA mod-
els documented in the literature. The performance evaluation is
conducted using two primarily reconstruction quality metrics:
PCC and STGI, in conjunction with computational efficiency
indicators, including training time, Giga Floating Point Opera-
tions per Second (GFLOPs), inference latency, and frames per
second (FPS). The Linear Regression (LR) model [22] attained
a relatively low PCC of 0.70 and a STGI of 0.33, indicating lim-
ited reconstruction capabilities despite minimal computing cost.
The FCN model [23] enhances performance, achieving a PCC
of 0.86 and a STGI of 0.39, while the CNN model [23] further
improves intelligibility with a STGI of 0.46 and a PCC of 0.89.
Similarly, the NeuroIncept Decoder model [25] exhibits better
reconstruction accuracy, attaining a PCC of 0.90 and a STGI of
0.49, while it incurs a greater computational cost. The proposed
WaveNet Decoder outperformed all other SOTA models, attain-
ing the highest PCC of 0.93 and STGI of 0.51, while also en-
hancing computational efficiency, seen by diminished inference
latency (177.30 ms) and the greatest FPS (574.20). The find-
ings underscore the efficacy of the proposed model in achieving
a balance between reconstruction accuracy and real-time viabil-
ity.

4. Conclusion and Future Work
This work advances neural decoding by demonstrating the via-
bility of reconstructing high-fidelity speech directly from neu-
ral activity through advanced deep learning techniques. Our
proposed method uses high-gamma features derived from in-
vasive EEG signals and correlates them logMel audio repre-
sentations via an innovative neural network architecture, the
WaveNet Decoder. The proposed method demonstrates sig-
nificant correlations between predicted and actual audio spec-
trograms, ranging from 0.90 to 0.95, signifying a good abil-
ity to capture critical audio properties. However, the compara-
tively small STGI scores indicate that additional enhancements
are required to more accurately represent the intricate tempo-
ral dynamics of speech processing in the brain. Future work
will focus on pretraining and representation-learning techniques
for data-scarce environments, including approaches that utilize
EEG data linked to language units like words or sentences in
the absence of corresponding audio. These directions seek to
improve real-time speech creation from neural signals and to
facilitate practical applications in speech restoration and BCI
systems.
