Abstract
Automatic speech quality assessment (SQA) is essential
for evaluating modern speech generation systems. However,
human listening tests are costly and difficult to scale. The UR-
GENT Challenge introduced a multi-metric evaluation proto-
col that assesses diverse absolute quality metrics, including
human Mean Opinion Score (MOS) and objective measures,
together with comparative human preference tests. This pro-
tocol highlights the need for unified modeling under heteroge-
neous supervision. Despite this need, most existing approaches
focus on either absolute quality prediction or preference pre-
diction. Motivated by the URGENT protocol, we propose
URGENT-MOS, a unified SQA framework that jointly models
multi-metric absolute quality prediction and pairwise preference
prediction within a shared architecture. By leveraging diverse
metrics and learning from heterogeneous data, the framework
improves performance and cross-domain robustness in both ab-
solute quality and preference prediction tasks. Code is available
athttps://github.com/vvwangvv/URGENT-MOS.
Index Terms: speech quality assessment, preference learning,
multi-metric supervision
1. Introduction
Automatic speech quality assessment (SQA) plays a central
role in evaluating modern generative speech systems, including
speech synthesis, enhancement, and coding [1 –6]. While hu-
man listening tests remain the gold standard, they are costly and
difficult to scale [7–11], motivating the development of reliable
objective alternatives. Early objective metrics relied on signal
processing and psychoacoustic models [12–15]. More recently,
neural Mean Opinion Score (MOS) predictors have enabled non-
intrusive perceptual quality estimation [16–20]. Nevertheless,
annotations are often inconsistent across datasets, limiting ro-
bustness under heterogeneous supervision.
Recent community evaluations, such as the URGENT Chal-
lenge, adopt a multi-metric protocol that reports multiple ab-
solute quality metrics together with human preference tests,
highlighting the need for unified models supporting both ab-
solute quality and preference prediction. Moreover, as gen-
erated speech approaches natural quality, inter-system differ-
ences become subtle and listener judgments grow more sensitive
to subjective preference and context [21 –25]. Consequently,
comparison-based paradigms that directly model pairwise pref-
erence have gained increasing attention [26, 27]. While recent
studies [28 –31] explore ranking-based supervision or derive
preference signals from human MOS, they typically optimize ab-
solute quality and preference separately, missing complementary
supervision across datasets. Since practical pipelines require
both, this motivates a unified formulation.
In this work, we propose URGENT-MOS, a unified SQA
framework motivated by the multi-metric protocol of the UR-
GENT Challenge. Building upon prior multi-metric absolute
quality frameworks such as Uni-VERSA [32], URGENT-MOS
jointly models multi-metric absolute quality prediction and pref-
erence prediction within a shared architecture. By learning
from datasets with heterogeneous annotations, it improves cross-
domain robustness while aligning training with modern evalua-
tion protocols. Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose URGENT-MOS, a unified SQA framework that
jointly models multi-metric absolute quality prediction and
pairwise preference prediction within a shared architecture.
• We introduce a multi-metric training strategy that learns from
diverse datasets with heterogeneous supervision.
• We construct and release a preference-annotated SQA dataset
derived from existing human MOS-annotated datasets for
training and evaluation of preference prediction.
• Experiments across diverse domains show strong performance
in both absolute quality and preference-based evaluation.
2. Method
URGENT-MOS jointly models absolute multi-metric quality
prediction and preference prediction within a shared representa-
tion space. This unified formulation allows the two supervision
signals to regularize each other during training while supporting
heterogeneous datasets with inconsistent annotation schemas.
Given a pair of speech inputsXA andXB, a shared feature
extractor produces acoustic representations that are used for both
absolute quality and preference prediction modeling (Fig. 1).
Absolute quality is estimated by theAbsolute Metric Prediction
Module(AMPM), while preference prediction is modeled by the
Naturalness-Conditioned Preference Module(NCPM).

2.1.2. Absolute Metric Prediction Module
To support multi-metric absolute quality prediction, URGENT-
MOS predicts multiple complementary quality metrics simulta-
neously, following the paradigm introduced in Uni-VERSA [32].
These include signal-level measures (e.g., SDR, PESQ), learned
perceptual metrics (e.g., DNSMOS, UTMOS), and human MOS,
as summarized in Table 1.
2.1.3. Naturalness-Conditioned Preference Module
Preference prediction is modeled by theNaturalness-
Conditioned Preference Module(NCPM, the middle block of
Fig. 1). NCPM operates on representations corresponding to the
Naturalnesscategory in Table 1. Given paired representations,
NCPM employs cross-attention to condition the representation
of one sample on that of the other (i.e., treating one sample as
the query and the other as the key/value), and subsequently out-
puts a preference prediction score. This design aligns preference
prediction with naturalness-related representations while sharing
the feature space with absolute quality prediction.
2.1.4. Range-Constraining Activations (rcAct)
Each metric head is followed by a range-constraining activation
to ensure predictions respect the valid ranges in Table 1.
For metrics with both lower and upper bounds (e.g., hu-
man MOS, DNSMOS, PESQ), we apply a scaled sigmoid. For
predictionxwith bounds[v min,v max]:
y=v min + (vmax−v min)σ(x).(1)
For metrics defined on[v min,∞), such as LSD or MCD:
y=v min + softplus(x−v min).(2)
For metrics with only an upper bound (e.g., LPS), we apply:
y=v max−softplus(v max−x).(3)

2.2.2. Preference Supervision from ACR Annotations
Datasets collected via Comparison Category Rating (CCR) re-
main limited in scale, whereas human-annotated MOS datasets
obtained through Absolute Category Rating (ACR) are more
abundant. To leverage this broader supervision, URGENT-MOS
derives preference labels from human MOS by constructing
sample pairs and assigning targets based on score differences.
Given two samplesXA andXB with human MOS scores
sA andsB, preference pairs are generated using three strategies:
(i) arbitrary matching (Dany); (ii) corpus-level matching within
the same dataset (Dcorpus); and (iii) reference-level matching
across systems that share identical content (e.g., the same text
prompt or clean reference signal) (Dref). A tie thresholdδ >0
is introduced to account for annotation uncertainty:

3. Experiment Setup
3.1. Datasets
3.1.1. Training
URGENT-MOS is trained on a diverse collection of speech qual-
ity datasets spanning text-to-speech (TTS), voice conversion
(VC), speech enhancement (SE), V oice over Internet Protocol
(V oIP), telephony and quality impairment domains, as summa-
rized in Table 2. The training corpus includes samples annotated
for absolute quality prediction and pairs annotated for preference
prediction, enabling unified learning of both tasks.
3.1.2. Evaluation
We evaluate the model using a combination of officially released
test sets and constructed preference pairs to reflect both absolute
quality and preference prediction scenarios. For datasets that pro-
vide standard evaluation splits, we directly adopt the official test
sets. To further align evaluation with practical preference-based
assessment, we additionally construct pairwise comparison data
usingreference-scope matching(Section 2.2.2). This construc-
tion is applied only to datasets that contain sufficient information
to support such pairing, as summarized in Table 3. In Table 5
and Table 4, for abbreviation, CHiME-7 denotes the CHiME-7-
UDASE-Eval test set. FOR and LIVETALK refers to the NISQA
FOR and LIVETALK test sets, respectively.
3.2. Metrics
We evaluate URGENT-MOS using absolute quality and prefer-
ence prediction settings. For preference prediction, we report
preference prediction accuracy over{A≻B,B≻A} . For
absolute quality prediction, we report utterance-level Pearson
correlation (LCC) and Spearman rank correlation (SRCC).

3.3. Training Details
Each shared metric encoder in URGENT-MOS is implemented
as a Transformer with 6 layers, 8 attention heads, a model dimen-
sion of 768, and a feed-forward dimension of 2048. The model
is trained using a constant learning rate of 3×10 −5. We adopt
a dynamic batching strategy based on audio duration, with each
batch containing approximately 400 seconds of speech. Training
is conducted for 60k optimization steps on a single NVIDIA
A100 GPU. Depending on the number of feature extractors, the
total training time ranges from 12 to 48 hours.

The choice of preference construction strategy has limited
impact on URGENT-MOS. Models trained with reference-level
(Dref), corpus-level (Dcorpus), or unconstrained (Dany) pairing
achieve comparable results across evaluation sets. This indicates
that the training threshold δ= 0.5 ( (6) in Section 2.2.2 ) is
sufficient to separate perceptually distinct samples, even in the
presence of cross-corpus inconsistencies.
Furthermore, incorporating naturalness-related multi-metric
supervision (M5) slightly improves performance over MOS-only
training (M1), suggesting that complementary perceptual cues
enhance preference discrimination. However, extending supervi-
sion to all 15 metrics does not consistently yield additional gains.
In particular, F1C1M15 degrades on several datasets, likely due
to weak or inconsistent correlations among certain metrics when
a single shared feature encoder is used. This degradation is likely
due to weak or inconsistent correlations among LSD and MCD
metrics, as evidenced by the correlation analysis in Figure 2.
Nevertheless, the broader multi-metric capability of the M15
variants provides richer multi-aspect quality signals that may
benefit LLM-based speech assessment frameworks.
4.2. Correlation-based Evaluation
Table 5 presents LCC and SRCC on datasets with absolute
quality annotations. The correlation results are consistent
with the preference-based findings. URGENT-MOS achieves
strong correlations across all datasets, indicating improved cross-
domain robustness compared to conventional MOS predictors.
Naturalness-focused multi-metric supervision (M5) and multi-
feature extraction (F4) provide consistent gains, while full multi-
metric supervision (M15) does not always further improve perfor-
mance. The F4C1M5 model shows the best overall performance
and robustness across evaluation domains.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we presented URGENT-MOS, a unified speech
quality assessment framework that jointly models absolute qual-
ity prediction and pairwise preference prediction. By integrating
heterogeneous objective and perceptual metrics while tolerating
missing annotations, URGENT-MOS enables robust training
on large-scale, multi-source datasets with inconsistent supervi-
sion. This unified formulation bridges human MOS prediction
and preference prediction within a single model. Experiments
across diverse domains demonstrate consistent improvements
over strong baselines in both absolute quality prediction and
preference prediction settings.
