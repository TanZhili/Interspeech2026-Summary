Abstract
Speaker verification (SV) systems often experience perfor-
mance degradation in real-world environments due to diverse
noise. A common solution is to add a speech enhancement (SE)
frontend; however, most SE modules are trained to reconstruct
predefined “clean” reference signals that still contain remain-
ing noise and channel variability. This can lead the SE mod-
ule to restore non-discriminative factors as well, thereby de-
grading SV performance. To address this limitation, we pro-
pose a Mixture Consistency Learning–based Speaker Verifica-
tion (MCL-SV) system. Instead of relying on reference sig-
nals, MCL-SV enforces that the sum of the separated speaker
and background representations matches the original input mix-
ture, so that the enhanced signal is aligned with the input itself
in a self-supervised manner. Experiments on multiple datasets
show that MCL-SV consistently outperforms strong baselines
and achieves state-of-the-art results among recent noise-robust
SV systems.
Index Terms: speaker verification, noisy environment, mixture
consistency
1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV) aims to determine whether an input
utterance corresponds to a claimed speaker. Recent advances in
deep learning have enabled SV systems to achieve outstanding
performance in clean environments, demonstrating strong relia-
bility in capturing speaker-discriminative characteristics [1–4].
However, real-world scenarios inevitably expose speech signals
to various noise factors, which significantly degrade the perfor-
mance of SV systems.
To mitigate this issue, a number of studies have introduced
speech enhancement (SE) modules as frontend preprocessors
for noise removal [5–8]. These modules typically adopt a U-Net
architecture [9] that compresses and encodes a noisy input, then
expands it to reconstruct enhanced features. During training, re-
construction objectives such as a mean squared error are used
to drive the mapping from noisy inputs to their corresponding
“clean” signals, thereby refining the input representations and
effectively suppressing noise interference. In particular, ExU-
Net [7] jointly optimizes SE and SV objectives and has achieved
outstanding performance in noisy environments.
More recently, noise-aware approaches have been devel-
oped under the premise that explicitly recognizing or model-
ing noise can further improve performance [10–13]. NA-ExU-
Net [10] separates the encoder into parallel branches to indepen-

beyond simple noise suppression, structurally disentangling or
explicitly exploiting noise information can substantially rein-

Despite recent advances, many prior studies that employ SE
modules primarily optimize supervised losses that force the re-
constructed output to match predefined “clean” reference data.
However, such target-dependent learning may have limitations
that could degrade speaker verification performance. Target ref-
erences regarded as “clean” often retain undesirable factors aris-
ing from recording conditions and channel variability [14, 15].
When such targets are used as ground truth, the SE models tend
to reproduce not only speaker-relevant information but also ir-
relevant characteristics, which then propagate into the features
consumed by the SV system. Consequently, downstream perfor-
mance can degrade and the variance of the speaker embedding
distribution may increase.
An alternative to target reconstruction is to enforce consis-
tency between the input mixture and the outputs of a decompo-
sition model [16–19]. For example, Wisdomet al.introduced
differentiable STFT and projection-based constraints that im-
pose mixture consistency in speech enhancement, stabilizing
training and improving separation quality [16]. MixIT further
enables unsupervised separation by using a self-supervised ob-
jective in which separated outputs are reassigned and re-mixed
so that their recombination explains the original mixture, even
without ground-truth sources [18]. These methods commonly
leverage the constraint that the sum of multiple outputs should
match the input mixture, demonstrating the feasibility of learn-
ing without relying on explicit targets.
Building on this perspective, we propose aMixture Con-
sistency Learning–based Speaker Verification (MCL-SV)
system that applies consistency-based learning to noise-robust
speaker verification. MCL-SV adopts a dual-decoder SE archi-
tecture that explicitly separates the output space into a speech
(speaker) path and a noise (nuisance) path, instead of using a
single-decoder SE module. Moreover, by introducing a consis-
tency constraint that enforces the sum of the two path outputs
to reconstruct the original mixture input, our framework pro-
vides a target-free training signal without relying on predefined
“clean” references.
In addition, we explicitly couple the SV objective to
the speech path output, encouraging it to preserve speaker-
discriminative cues while absorbing SV-irrelevant variations
into the noise path. Unlike prior approaches based on mix-
ture consistency or re-mixing, which primarily aim to recover
ground-truth sources, our method does not pursue source recov-

a baseline that uses a conventional SE module as the frontend
and an independent SV module as the backend.

To address this issue, we propose Mixture Consistency
Learning–based Speaker Verification (MCL-SV) system. MCL-
SV addresses the following issue through structural modifica-
tions and the introduction of a consistency-based loss function.
3.1. Architecture
Under the assumption that even “clean” reference data may
still contain non-discriminative components, we design a Dual-
Decoder SE (DDSE) module to explicitly accommodate these
elements. As shown in Fig. 1, DDSE shares a U-Net encoder
and branches the decoder into two paths. Here, SE path is de-
signed to preserve speaker-discriminative factors, while noise
extraction (NE) path serves to absorb non-discriminative cues.
Formally, the DDSE operates as follows:
ˆS=D s(E(X)),(5)
ˆN=D n(E(X)),(6)
whereX, ˆS, and ˆNdenote the noisy input, the enhanced spec-
trogram, and the estimated noise, respectively. The functions
E(·),D s(·), andD n(·)represent the shared encoder, the SE
decoder, and the NE decoder, respectively. The latent repre-
sentation compressed by the shared encoder is passed to two
decoders with identical structure. The decoders then produce
the reconstructed spectrograms, which are optimized using loss
functions described in Section 3.2.
3.2. Loss functions
In this section, we describe the loss functions used to train the
proposed MCL-SV system. MCL-SV integrates the DDSE ar-
chitecture with a consistency-based objective called Mixture
Consistency (MC) loss. The MC loss is defined as follows:
LMC =∥X−( ˆS+ ˆN)∥ 2,(7)
whereXdenotes the original mixed input, and ˆSand ˆNare the
spectrogram outputs produced by the DDSE structure. Unlike
conventional training that relies on predefined reference targets,
the MC loss is independent of explicit targets. Instead, it en-
forces mixture consistency by constraining the sum of the SE
and NE outputs to match the original mixed input.
However, the mixture-consistency constraint alone does not
clearly specify which information should be routed through the
SE path and which should be captured by the NE path. To re-
solve this routing ambiguity and to encourage a decomposition
that is directly beneficial for speaker verification, we couple the
SV objective explicitly to the SE-path output ˆS. As shown in
Fig. 1, we feed ˆSinto the SV backend and optimize it using the
speaker verification lossL SV, thereby encouraging ˆSto pre-
serve speaker-discriminative cues. At the same time, the NE
path is naturally pressured to absorb SV-irrelevant variations
into ˆN. Consequently, the overall training objective of MCL-
SV is defined as follows:
LProposed =L MC +L SV.(8)
In this formulation,L MC acts as a reconstruction-oriented
regularizer that stabilizes DDSE decomposition without targets,
whileL SV serves as a task-driven constraint that defines the
roles of the two paths from the SV perspective. Unlike tradi-
tional speech enhancement that aims to recover “clean” target
or source separation methods that pursue decomposition as an
end goal, our approach learns a decomposition optimized for
SV performance.
By integrating (i) the dual-path output space of DDSE, (ii)
the target-free mixture consistency constraint, and (iii) the di-
rect coupling of the SV objective, the proposed framework en-
ables structured learning in which speaker-relevant information
is retained in ˆSand speaker-irrelevant variations are encouraged
into ˆN. This joint learning aligns the enhanced frontend rep-
resentation with the downstream SV objective and is expected
to yield robust speaker verification performance under diverse
noise conditions.
4. Evaluation Setup
V oxCeleb1 (V ox1) [14] and V oxCeleb2 (V ox2) [15] were used
as sources of clean utterances and training data. Noise samples
were drawn from the MUSAN dataset [20], which was divided
into two non-overlapping subsets for training and testing. To
create noisy speech, clean utterances were mixed with noise at
SNR levels uniformly sampled between 0 and 20 dB, following
the protocol in [7]. Room impulse response (RIR) reverberation
[28] was also applied for data augmentation. For out-of-domain
evaluation, we employed Nonspeech100 [29], V oxSRC23 [30],
and VC-Mix [31].
Model training was performed using the Adam optimizer
[32] together with a cosine-annealing learning rate schedule
[33]. For SphereFace2 [22], we setλ=0.7 andt=3, and the mar-
gin and scale parameters were set to 0.2 and 32 when trained on
V ox2, and 0.1 and 24 when trained on V ox1, respectively. Eval-
uation was based on the equal error rate (EER). Additionally,

5. Evaluation Results
5.1. Overall Effectiveness
Table 2 presents a comparison of the proposed MCL-SV with
recent noise-robust speaker verification models under a com-
mon protocol on the V ox1 evaluation data. When trained on
V ox1, MCL-SV shows relatively lower performance in clean
and high-SNR conditions but maintains stable results across all
environments. Notably, it achieves significant improvements at
the most challenging 0 dB condition and records an average
EER of 3.03%. Considering that real environments are closer to
low SNR scenarios with diverse noise distributions, this robust-
ness in noisy conditions provides a more practical advantage.
On V ox2, the baseline system that integrates an SE fron-
tend module with our reproduced ReDimNet-B2 [3] backend
achieves a lower average EER than the backend-only sys-
tem in noisy conditions, indicating that the SE module con-
tributes to improved noise robustness. MCL-SV further ad-
vances this trend, achieving an average EER of 1.19% while
remaining competitive in clean conditions. It demonstrates
relative improvements of 16.2% and 12.5% over our repro-
duced ReDimNet-B2 and the baseline, respectively. In particu-
lar, MCL-SV also improves clean performance by about 14.4%
compared to the baseline, suggesting that explicitly discarding
non-discriminative factors from the original signal contributes
to performance gains.

5.3. Out-of-Domain Generalization
We further examined the generalization ability of MCL-SV
on out-of-domain datasets. On the Nonspeech100 evaluation
(Table 4), MCL-SV achieved an average EER of 3.17%, out-
performing not only the baseline but also larger models such
as WavLM + NAW-SV [26]. A similar trend is observed on
V oxSRC23 and VC-Mix (Table 5), where MCL-SV consistently
surpasses the baseline and achieves competitive results against
the recent advanced SEED system [35]. Although SEED sys-
tem performs slightly better on the VC-Mix dataset, MCL-SV
achieves a 3.60% EER on average across the two datasets, out-
performing both the baseline and SEED. Overall, these results
demonstrate that, even in more realistic scenarios involving do-
main shifts and changes in noise distributions, our proposed sys-
tem exhibits stable speaker-discriminative performance across
diverse environmental conditions.
6. Conclusion
This paper proposed MCL-SV , a novel speaker verification sys-
tem that combines a dual-decoder speech enhancement with
mixture consistency learning. The system constrains the sum
of the two decoder outputs to match the noisy input and directly
optimizes the speech branch for speaker verification, thereby
reducing reliance on target labels and focusing the module on
speaker discriminative features. In our experiments, MCL-SV
achieves the best average performance under both in-domain
and out-of-domain conditions. Ablations on loss configurations
further show that replacing target–based losses with the mix-
ture consistency objective is the primary source of the gains. In
future work, we will closely analyze how decoder outputs that
separate speaker-relevant and speaker-irrelevant factors differ
from those of conventional speech enhancement systems.
