Abstract
Given a set of unlabeled reverberant speech signals, the re-
cent USDnet model can learn to effectively perform unsuper-
vised single-speaker speech dereverberation. This paper pro-
poses USDnet++, which extends USDnet by leveraging signal
processing based dereverberation results to improve the train-
ing of USDnet. The rationale is that the dereverberation re-
sult produced by signal processing algorithms typically has a
higher signal-to-noise (SNR) of the target speech and hence
could be leveraged as a weak supervision to improve the train-
ing of USDnet. In this paper, we employ weighted prediction
error (WPE) and convolutional beamforming (WPD) to derive
the signal processing based dereverberation result for improving
USDnet. Evaluation results on the WSJ0CAM-DEREVERB
dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of USDnet++.
Index Terms: Unsupervised speech dereverberation
1. Introduction
Dereverberation is a crucial and meaningful task, due to the
negative impact of room reverberation on speech quality, in-
telligibility, and the performance of applications like automatic
speech recognition (ASR) and speaker identification [1, 2]. To
address this issue, many methods have been proposed, ranging
from conventional signal processing techniques [3, 4] to deep
neural network (DNN) based approaches [5–9].
Conventional dereverberation methods often estimate a lin-
ear inverse filter to process the reverberant mixture [1, 10, 11].
A representative algorithm in this category is weighted pre-
diction error (WPE) [3, 12, 13], which suppresses late rever-
beration while preserving direct sound and early reflections.
In multichannel scenarios, beamforming is also widely used,
with the MVDR beamformer [11] being a classical exam-
ple. Recent studies combine these methods, either in a cas-
cade fashion [14, 15] or within joint optimization frameworks
[4,16–18]. Among them, the convolutional beamformer (WPD)
[4] achieves strong dereverberation and noise suppression by
jointly optimizing spatial and temporal filtering.
With the development of deep learning, deep neural net-
works (DNNs) have also been applied to speech dereverber-
ation and have shown significant improvements over conven-
tional signal processing based methods. Supervised learning,
which trains the DNN on pairs of reverberant and clean speech,
has become a widely adopted paradigm in this field [7, 19, 20].
These pairs are typically generated through simulation, which
cannot simulate sufficiently realistic signals, and collecting such
paired data from real-world recordings is often challenging,
even impossible. As a result, DNNs trained purely on simu-

Unsupervised learning [22–25] enables training directly on

Some prior studies [26–28] leverage outputs of conventional al-

results as the training targets [28]. However, these methods are
limited by the accuracy of the conventional algorithms.
To overcome this limitation, USDnet [22] avoids directly
using the outputs of conventional algorithms as the training tar-
gets. Instead, it enforces a reconstruction constraint. That is,
the DNN output, after passing through a linear filter, should
reproduce the original mixture. Signal processing-based dere-
verberation (SPD) algorithms, such as WPE [3] and WPD [4],
can usually provide a reliable enhancement of the target speech
and increase the SNR of the target speech by several deci-
bels. Intuitively, SPD results could be utilized as weak su-
pervision to train DNNs for dereverberation. With this idea in
mind, we propose USDnet++, which further incorporates SPD
results as additional guidance for model training. Besides en-
couraging the linearly-filtered DNN estimates to approximate
observed mixtures, USDnet++ further encourages them to ap-
proximate SPD results. Evaluation results on the WSJ0CAM-
DEREVERB dataset [8] show the effectiveness of USDnet++.
The contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:
1. We refine USDnet by adding a new loss term that encourages
the filtered DNN estimates to reconstruct the SPD results.
2. We develop a multi-stage refinement framework for USD-
net++, where DNN-based and signal-processing-based dere-
verberation are applied alternately to improve each other.
3. We further enhance USDnet++ by including SPD results as
additional inputs to a re-trained DNN, enabling the model to
exploit information afforded by SPD to improve dereverb.

3. Review of USDnet
We review USDnet [22] (shown in Fig. 1(a)), on which we
build USDnet++. Unsupervised speech dereverberation is a
classic blind deconvolution problem [29, 30]. There are in-
finite solutions to the source and filter pairs, where, in each
pair, the source and filter, after linear convolution, can well-
approximate the observed reverberant signal, but not respec-
tively corresponding to the true direct-path signal and filter. To
address this, USDnet proposes various constraints to regularize
its output to have the output approximate the direct-path signal.

[31], using the real and imaginary (RI) components of the mix-

4. USDnet++
Our proposed USDnet++, shown in Fig. 1(b), improves USD-
net in Fig. 1(a) by including new losses defined on dereverber-
ated mixtures computed by conventional SPD algorithms such
as WPE [3] and WPD [4]. This section presents the deriva-
tion of SPD results and the training procedure of USDnet++,
which includes three strategies: (a) SPD-constrained training;
(b) multi-stage refinement; and (c) including SPD results as in-
put features.
4.1. SPD-Constrained Training of USDnet++
Given a pre-trained USDnet [22], we first leverage its estimate
of the direct-path signal to derive an SPD result (denoted as
Y SPD) and then leverage the SPD result to compute an extra loss
for fine-tuning USDnet, yielding USDnet++. The procedure for
obtaining the SPD result will be detailed later in Section 4.4.
Fig. 1(b) illustrates how USDnet++ leverages the SPD re-
sult. The SPD result can be considered as a mixture signal
recorded by a virtual microphone. It can be utilized to design a

4.2. Multi-stage Refinement for USDnet++
Via SPD-constrained training, USDnet++ is expected to obtain
a better estimate of the direct-path signal than USDnet. The
better direct-path signal can in turn lead to better SPD results,
which can be leveraged to fine-tune the previous USDnet++
model. This procedure can be iterated multiple times to pro-
gressively improve SPD results and USDnet++.
4.3. Including SPD Results as Input Features for USDnet++
We further propose a system with two USDnet++ models. After
the first USDnet++ model is trained, we use it to compute the
direct-path signal and derive an SPD resultY SPD. The SPD
result is then used as an input feature in addition to the original
mixture to train the second USDnet++ model. Notice that at
inference time, the system needs to run the two models to obtain
the final dereverberation result.

5. Experimental Setup
We evaluate USDnet++ on WSJ0CAM-DEREVERB [8], a
popular dataset for supervised dereverberation. Based on
WSJ0CAM, which provides7861,742, and1088clean signals
for training, validation, and testing, respectively, WSJ0CAM-
DEREVERB simulates39293(∼77.7h),2968(∼5.6h), and
3262(∼6.4h) reverberant mixtures for training, validation,
and testing, respectively. Each utterance is convolved with
simulated RIRs generated using random room configurations,
speaker-array distances (0.75–2.5m), and T60 values (0.2–1.3
s). An8-microphone circular array (with a diameter of20cm)
is used. Diffuse air-conditioning noise randomly sampled from
the REVERB dataset [33] is included, with SNRs randomly
drawn from the range[5,25]dB. The sampling rate is16kHz.
For STFT, we use a32ms window size, an8ms hop size,
and a square-root Hann analysis window. The DNNs adopt TF-
GridNet [8, 9] with hyper-parametersD= 128,B= 4,I= 4,
J= 4,H= 200,L= 4,E= 2. The batch size is fixed at
2. For complex ratio masking, the predicted RI components are
clipped to the range[−5,5]. The values ofαandβin Eq. (7)
are tuned to3/8and1, respectively.
We compare USDnet++ with two unsupervised baselines:
WPE [3] and USDnet [22]. For8-channel WPE and WPD, we
set∆ d = 3and∆ l = 5, following [32]. All methods use
the same STFT configuration. The direct-path signal at the ref-
erence microphone is used as the reference signal for metric
computation. The evaluation metrics include PESQ [34], eS-
TOI [35], and SI-SDR [36].

7. Conclusions
We have proposed USDnet++, which leverages signal process-
ing based dereverberation results to improve the training of US-
Dnet. Experiments on WSJ0CAM-DEREVERB show that US-
Dnet++ consistently outperforms conventional algorithms such
as WPE and WPD, achieving higher PESQ, eSTOI, and SI-SDR
scores. The gains are consistent across evaluation metrics, indi-
cating improvements in perceptual quality, speech intelligibil-
ity, and distortion reduction simultaneously. Multi-stage refine-
ment further improves performance by iteratively updating the
WPD results and the DNN model, with the WPD outputs used
to compute the MC loss. Incorporating SPD results as extra fea-
tures of USDnet++ enables the network to exploit complemen-
tary cues for superior dereverberation. These results confirm
the effectiveness of USDnet++. Future research would extend
USDnet++ to real-recorded, multi-speaker scenarios.
