Modality Importance is Not Static: Temporal Dynamics via Gating in
Multimodal Emotion Recognition

Abstract
Multimodal emotion recognition is inherently dynamic, as the
importance of text, speech, and visual signals changes across
time. However, most existing systems rely on static fusion,
implicitly assuming time-invariant modality contribution and
ignoring temporal variation. We present a temporal fusion
framework that models modality importance as a time-varying,
class-conditional distribution. A GRU-based module applies
emotion-query gating to adapt modality weights over dialogue
context. Under 6-class IEMOCAP LOSO evaluation, temporal
modeling improves over static fusion (+9.01 in mean(F1, UA)),
and gating further improves over temporal modeling with-
out gating (+1.63), while outperforming Transformer-based fu-
sion with fewer parameters. Perturbation-based occlusion and
Area Over the Perturbation Curve(AOPC) provide evidence that
modality importance is non-stationary across time, suggesting
multimodal emotion recognition as a dynamic decision process
rather than a static fusion problem.
Index Terms: multimodal emotion recognition, temporal dy-
namics, explainable AI
1. Introduction
Emotion in dialogue is not expressed instantaneously; it unfolds
across conversational turns. Context-aware dialogue models
consistently show that emotion cues are distributed across mul-
tiple turns [1, 2, 3]. The same lexical content may convey differ-
ent affect depending on preceding context, vocal prosody shifts,
or subtle facial cues. Despite this inherently temporal nature, a
substantial portion of multimodal emotion recognition (MER)
still performs static per-utterance fusion and reports only ag-
gregate endpoint metrics. Such practice implicitly assumes that
modality importance is stationary over time [4].
We challenge this assumption and show that modality im-
portance is non-stationary over dialogue time. Our goal is
not to outperform state-of-the-art, but to provide empirical
and attribution-based evidence of temporal modality dynamics.
This reframes multimodal SER as a sequential decision process
rather than a static fusion problem.
We investigate a focused hypothesis: Emotion recognition
benefits from modeling modality importance as a time-varying,
class-conditional distribution over dialogue context.
Rather than introducing a heavy end-to-end multimodal
Transformer, we decouple unimodal representation learning and
fusion learning. This design enables controlled comparison
between static fusion, temporal modeling, and progressively
richer gating mechanisms under an identical leave-one-session-
out cross-validation (LOSO) protocol. The formulation is min-

Our findings highlight that modeling temporal modality dynam-

for multimodal emotion recognition.
2. Related Work and Positioning
2.1. Temporal Modeling in Dialogue Emotion Recognition
Dialogue emotion recognition commonly employs recurrent or
attention-based context modeling [1, 2, 3]. Recent ERC models
also explore graph-based interaction modeling and contextual
reasoning over dialogue structure [5, 6]. While such models
demonstrate the importance of conversational history, modal-
ities are typically fused before temporal modeling, implicitly
assuming stationary modality importance. We focus on test-
ing this assumption directly, rather than on scaling the temporal
backbone.
2.2. Multimodal Fusion and Dynamic Weighting
Early fusion (feature concatenation) and late fusion (logit stack-
ing) remain dominant strategies [4]. Beyond these methods,
multimodal fusion has evolved toward transformer-based inter-
action modeling [7, 8, 9]. Recent ERC studies further report
strong gains with correlation-aware fusion, facial-expression-
aware multi-task learning, and contrastive objectives [10, 11].
Other lines of work improve modality quality itself, such
as ASR error compensation with LLM refinement [12], and
parameter-efficient adaptation of pre-trained speech models
[13]. MMER additionally combines multimodal interaction
with multi-task objectives for SER [14]. Taken together, these
approaches mainly strengthen representations, but they do not
directly test how modality importance evolves across dialogue
time. Attention mechanisms are often used to mediate cross-
modal interaction, yet attention weights are frequently treated as
explanations even though their faithfulness is limited [15, 16].

Dynamic gating has been explored in multimodal learning,
but most methods still rely on per-utterance static weighting

modality importance systematically varies over dialogue time
under strict protocol-matched evaluation.
2.3. Positioning of This Work
This work isolates a specific inductive bias: model modality im-
portance as a class-conditional, time-varying distribution. By
decoupling unimodal encoding and fusion learning, we enable
transparent ablation: static vs temporal, non-gated vs gated,
and progressively richer gating variants. The focus is not ar-
chitectural scale, but dynamic modality distribution modeling.
Our contribution is therefore analytical: a controlled study that
tests a hypothesis about non-stationary modality importance us-
ing attribution-based analysis rather than attention-only expla-
nations.

4. Experimental Setup
We use IEMOCAP with six emotion classes (ang, hap, neu, sad,
fru, sur), mergingexcintohap[25]. Evaluation follows 5-fold
LOSO (Session1–5 held out once each), and validation is sam-
pled only from the training sessions to avoid leakage. We report
Macro-F1 and UA (mean class recall) and select checkpoints by
the average of these two scores on validation. The random seed
is 2026. Temporal fusion uses context lengthK= 8, hidden
size 128, dropout 0.2, AdamW [26], and 30 epochs; static fu-
sion also uses 30 epochs. Video-new uses 16 frames at 224 res-
olution with face crop enabled, inverse-frequency class weights,
and partial backbone unfreezing. All experiments were run on
a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU.
5. Results
5.1. Main Results
Table 1 summarizes the main LOSO results, including both
internal ablations and protocol-matched Transformer-family
models. Here, Transformer, MulT, and MMER are used as se-
quence encoders in the temporal modeling stage under the same
training protocol. The performance gap between static and tem-
poral models (+7.38) confirms that context modeling is a pri-
mary factor. Under the same data split and training protocol,
Temporal GRU (no gate) outperforms Transformer, MulT, and
MMER, while the additional gain from gating (+1.63 over no-
gate GRU) isolates the effect of dynamic modality weighting.
Parameter comparison from checkpoint weights further shows
efficiency: our emotion-query model uses 0.076M parameters,
versus 0.452M (Transformer), 1.840M (MulT), and 1.939M
(MMER). We report these gains as hypothesis-supporting ev-
idence rather than as a state-of-the-art claim. This indicates that
explicit modality weighting can be more effective than simply
increasing model capacity: our model achieves higher perfor-
mance while being about 5.9×smaller than Transformer and
about 24–26×smaller than MulT/MMER. Our evaluation fo-
cuses on controlled comparison within a unified framework to
isolate temporal modality dynamics. Rather than benchmarking
against heterogeneous external systems, we focus on internal
comparisons under identical conditions, ensuring that observed

6. Temporal Modality Dynamics
Figure 2 provides the central analysis artifact of this paper:
modality importance changes over relative time, and the dynam-
ics are reflected in the magnitude of temporal variation rather
than static weights. While text maintains the highest average
weight, the temporal variation (std) reveals that modality contri-
butions are not fixed but fluctuate across dialogue positions. The
non-zero variance across all modalities confirms that modality
importance is inherently time-dependent rather than static. This
addresses a major limitation of static fusion analyses that pro-
vide only utterance-level scalar importance.
Figure 3 provides quantitative faithfulness evidence: The
monotonic increase in AOPC indicates that the identified tem-
poral positions are important, not merely correlated. This in-
dicates that a small subset of temporal positions drives model
decisions, supporting the non-stationary nature of modality im-
portance. The narrow variance band suggests the temporal im-
portance pattern is consistent across folds. Text shows stronger

7. Conclusion
We studied multimodal emotion recognition from the perspec-
tive of temporal modality dynamics. Temporal modeling sub-
stantially outperforms static fusion, and dynamic gating further
improves performance, with the proposed emotion-query for-
mulation achieving the best results with minimal additional pa-
rameters. Perturbation analyses suggest that modality impor-
tance varies across temporal positions, supporting the view of
multimodal SER as a dynamic decision process rather than a
static fusion problem. Future work includes evaluation on ad-
ditional dialogue models and more naturalistic conversational
datasets beyond the partially scripted IEMOCAP corpus.
