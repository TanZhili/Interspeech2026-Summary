Abstract
Audio-language models (ALMs) have shown strong gener-
alization in standard audio classification tasks, yet their few-
shot adaptation remains constrained by text-centric prompt
learning, which under-adapts the audio encoder and struggles
to distinguish subtle differences between acoustically simi-
lar classes. This text-dominated adaptation leads to imbal-
anced optimization across modalities and limits the discrim-
inative capacity of ALMs under low-data regimes. To ad-
dress this limitation, we propose MALP, a multi-modal prompt
learning framework that jointly optimizes audio-specific, text-
specific, and shared prompts. Specifically, MALP first enables
modality-specific adaptation to capture complementary charac-
teristics of audio and text, and then introduces shared prompts to
strengthen cross-modal alignment while preserving modality-
specific discriminability. Experiments on eleven benchmark
datasets demonstrate consistent improvements over multiple
strong baselines, achieving average gains of 7.21% over CoOp,
4.80% over CoCoOp, and 1.77% over PALM. Ablation stud-
ies further confirm the complementary roles of audio-specific
and shared prompts, validating the effectiveness of multi-modal
prompt learning for few-shot audio classification.

1. Introduction
In recent years, audio-language models (ALMs) have achieved
remarkable performance across diverse audio classification
tasks [1, 2, 3]. Through large-scale cross-modal pre-training,
these models learn transferable representations that generalize
well to unseen categories [4], making them particularly attrac-
tive for few-shot audio classification [5, 6, 7, 8], where la-
beled data are scarce and costly to obtain [9, 10]. This set-
ting frequently arises in real-world scenarios involving rare
sound events, environmental recordings, and domain-specific
signals such as underwater acoustics and medical audio anal-
ysis [11, 12, 13]. By leveraging prior knowledge acquired from
large-scale corpora, ALMs provide a promising foundation for
rapid adaptation to new audio classes with minimal supervision.
Despite this effectiveness, adapting ALMs to few-shot au-
dio classification remains challenging due to the limited avail-
ability of labeled data. To enable efficient adaptation under such
constraints, prompt learning has become a prevalent strategy
that adjusts task-specific parameters while keeping backbone
encoders frozen [5, 14, 15, 16]. Representative approaches such
as CoOp [14] learn continuous prompt embeddings in the text

Prompt Learning (MALP), a unified framework that reformu-

with distinct functional roles: audio-specific prompts that adapt
the audio encoder to capture task-relevant acoustic variations,
text-specific prompts that refine class-level semantic represen-
tations, and shared prompts that bridge the two modalities in
a shared embedding space. Instead of enforcing immediate
cross-modal coupling, MALP adopts a progressive optimization
strategy: first enabling modality-wise specialization, and subse-
quently promoting alignment through shared prompt interaction

2.2. Overview of Multi-Modal Prompt Learning
Few-shot adaptation of audio-language models requires balanc-
ing two objectives: enhancing modality-specific discrimination
and preserving cross-modal alignment. Existing prompt learn-
ing approaches mainly optimize the text branch, which may re-
sult in imbalanced cross-modal adaptation and limited separa-
bility for acoustically similar categories.
To address this issue, we propose Multi-Modal Audio-
Language Prompt Learning (MALP), a unified framework that
decomposes prompt adaptation into modality specialization and
cross-modal alignment (see Figure 2). MALP introduces three
learnable prompts: an audio-specific promptP audio ∈R d,
a text-specific promptP text ∈R d, and a shared prompt
P shared∈R d. The modality-specific prompts perform resid-
ual adaptation within each encoder, while the shared prompt
conditions both modalities to encourage representation consis-
tency. All prompt parameters are jointly optimized with frozen
backbone encoders, enabling balanced cross-modal adaptation
under few-shot settings.
2.3. Modality-Specific Prompt Adaptation
Text-only prompting is often insufficient for capturing subtle
acoustic variations that are weakly reflected in high-level se-
mantic descriptions. To enhance intra-modal discrimination
while preserving the pre-trained representation space, we in-
troduce modality-specific prompts into both audio and text
branches. Given frozen encodersf A andf T , the prompt-

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate MALP on eleven publicly available datasets
spanning diverse audio classification tasks. These bench-
marks cover multiple semantic levels and acoustic proper-
ties, including instrument recognition (Beijing-Opera [17],
NS-Instruments [18]), environmental sound classification
(ESC50 [19], ESC50-Actions [19], UrbanSound8K [20]),
speech emotion recognition (CREMA-D [21], RA VDESS [22]),
vocal sound classification (V ocalSound [23]), surveillance
sound classification (SESA [24]), acoustic scene classification
(TUT2017 [25]), and music genre classification (GT-Music-
Genre [26]). These datasets vary in semantic granularity, acous-
tic variability, temporal complexity, and category scale. Evalu-
ating across such heterogeneous settings provides a comprehen-
sive assessment, where both discriminative modeling and cross-
modal alignment are essential.
3.2. Baselines
We compare MALP with four representative baselines:
PENGI [2] as the zero-shot reference, CoOp [14] and Co-
CoOp [15] as unimodal prompt tuning methods, and PALM [5]
as an audio-language few-shot prompt learning approach.
These baselines cover zero-shot transfer and prompt-based
adaptation, enabling a structured evaluation of our multi-level
prompt design.
3.3. Implementation Details
All experiments were implemented in PyTorch and conducted
on a single GPU with computational capacity comparable to an
NVIDIA RTX 3090. We adopt a 16-shot setting, randomly sam-
pling 16 instances per class for training. The unimodal prompt
fusion coefficientλis set to 0.2. Models are optimized using
stochastic gradient descent with a batch size of 16, a learning
rate of 0.05, and trained for 50 epochs. All experiments are re-
peated with three random seeds, and we report both per-seed
results and the averaged performance. For zero-shot evalua-
tion, we use a fixed template, “This is a recording of CLASS
NAME,” without handcrafted prompt engineering. All exper-
imental settings are kept consistent across baselines to ensure
fair comparison.
3.4. Results
Table 1 summarizes the performance comparison across eleven
benchmark datasets. MALP achieves the best average accu-
racy of 78.35%, consistently outperforming all baselines. In
particular, MALP improves over CoOp, CoCoOp, and PALM
by 7.21%, 4.88%, and 1.77% on average, respectively, demon-
strating the effectiveness of multi-level prompt learning in few-
shot audio classification. Performance gains are especially pro-
nounced on datasets such as Beijing-Opera, CREMA-D, and
RA VDESS, where fine-grained acoustic cues and subtle seman-
tic distinctions are critical. These results suggest that incor-
porating audio-specific prompts enhances intra-modal discrim-
inability, while shared prompts facilitate cross-modal align-
ment, leading to more robust representations under limited su-
pervision. On datasets such as ESC50 and GT-Music-Genre,
improvements are comparatively smaller but remain consistent,
indicating stable gains across tasks with varying semantic gran-
ularity and acoustic complexity.

3.5. Ablation Experiments
To analyze the contribution of each component, we conduct
ablation studies on the audio-specific and shared prompts, as
shown in Table 2. Introducing the audio-specific prompt im-
proves the average accuracy from 76.58% to 77.33%, while
adding the shared prompt yields a gain of 0.31%. When both
components are combined, MALP achieves 78.35%, outper-
forming each individual variant. These results indicate that the
audio-specific prompt primarily enhances intra-modal discrim-
inability, whereas the shared prompt contributes to cross-modal
alignment. Their combination provides complementary bene-
fits, leading to the best overall performance.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed MALP, a multi-level prompt learning
framework for few-shot audio classification. Unlike prior meth-
ods that focus primarily on text-side prompt tuning, MALP in-
troduces audio-specific prompts together with a shared prompt
to enhance intra-modal discrimination and cross-modal align-
ment. This design enables flexible modality adaptation while
maintaining semantic consistency across audio and text rep-
resentations. Extensive experiments on eleven benchmark
datasets demonstrate consistent performance gains over strong
baselines under the 16-shot setting. Ablation studies further
confirm that both audio-specific and shared prompts contribute
complementary benefits to the overall performance. Future
work will investigate more challenging scenarios, including
ultra-low-shot and cross-domain settings, to further evaluate ro-
bustness under limited or shifted data distributions. We also
plan to explore more parameter-efficient prompt designs to im-
prove scalability for large-scale applications.
