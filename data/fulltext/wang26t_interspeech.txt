Abstract
Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) often suffer from
audio-textual attention imbalance, prioritizing text over acous-
tic information during multi-modal fusion. This bias lim-
its the utilization of acoustic cues and degrades audio rea-
soning performance. To mitigate this, we proposeMA TA, a
novel training-free method that dynamically pushes LALMs to
payMoreAttentionToAudio tokens within the self-attention
mechanism. Specifically, MATA intervenes after raw atten-
tion scoring, targeting only the last token in intermediate lay-
ers without adding parameters or computational overhead. Ex-
periments on MMAU and MMAR benchmarks confirm consis-
tent performance gains. Furthermore, integrating MATA with
the Qwen3-Omni-Thinking model secured second place in the
Single Model Track of the Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning
Challenge. As the only training-free approach among top solu-
tions, MATA offers a highly efficient strategy to mitigate atten-
tion bias and advance LALM reasoning.

1. Introduction
In the realm of artificial intelligence, deriving logical conclu-
sions or uncovering implicit intentions from perceived informa-
tion, which is known as reasoning, is a key ability that sup-
ports advanced cognitive tasks [1, 2, 3, 4]. In the field of audio
processing, audio reasoning tasks require models to deeply un-
derstand aural information, such as speaker intent, emotional
tone, and subtle environmental cues [5, 6, 7]. For instance, in a
complex soundscape, a reasoning model must be able to reason
that a sudden, high-pitched scream indicates distress or danger,
while the consistent hum of an engine suggests an ongoing pro-
cess. Effectively performing these tasks demands a comprehen-
sive grasp of all available audio information.
Large audio language models (LALMs) integrate large lan-
guage models (LLMs) with audio encoders [8, 9, 10, 11], show-
ing great promise for complex audio-textual tasks ranging from
audio captioning to question answering. Despite their impres-
sive performance, a critical challenge remains: the attention
bias between audio and text modalities. While previous re-
search has demonstrated similar issues in large vision language
models (LVLMs) and LLMs [12, 13, 14, 15], analogous inves-
tigations into the attention dynamics between audio and text in
LALMs remain scarce. This oversight is non-trivial because if

In this work, we systematically investigate the attention al-
location of LALMs when processing multi-modal audio and
text inputs. Our analysis, performed on the Qwen2.5-Omni-7B
model [16], reveals a significant attention disparity: the model
allocates considerably more attention to text tokens than to au-
dio tokens throughout the answer generation process. This bias
is particularly pronounced in the intermediate layers of the de-
coder, which are responsible for the critical fusion of multi-
modal information [17, 18]. As shown in Fig. 1, we compute
the mean attention weight for each decoder layer by averaging
the attention distributions across all auto-regressive decoding
steps. The results show that in the intermediate layers, atten-
tion to audio tokens consistently remains low, while attention
to system and instruction text tokens stays high. We argue that
this imbalance prevents the model from effectively utilizing the
rich information embedded in the audio signals during the de-
coding process, which can lead to poor reasoning performance
and hallucinations.
To address this challenge, we propose MATA, a novel,
training-free technique to enhance the model’s focus on the au-
dio modality during inference. By directly increasing the atten-
tion weights allocated to audio tokens within the intermediate
layers of the LALM’s decoder, our method encourages a more
audio-centric reasoning process. Evaluations on two prominent

2. Related Work
2.1. Large Audio Language Model
The rapid evolution of LLMs has driven the development of
LALMs, which extend the strengths of LLMs into the multi-
modal audio-textual domain. Early works like LTU [8] and
GAMA [9] aligned pre-trained audio encoders with LLMs
via fine-tuning, laying the groundwork for basic audio under-
standing. Qwen2-Audio [11] marked a significant milestone
by integrating Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback
(RLHF), enhancing the model’s ability to understand and re-
spond to audio inputs in a more human-aligned manner. Build-
ing upon Qwen2-Audio, subsequent works like Audio-CoT [7]
and Audio-Reasoner [5] introduced Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
techniques to the audio-language domain. By breaking down
complex audio reasoning tasks into sequential, interpretable
steps, these methods partially improved the models’ reasoning
capabilities. More recently, omni-modal models like Qwen2.5-
Omni [16] and Baichuan-Omni-1.5 [23] have demonstrated ex-
ceptional performance in audio-textual scenarios. Pushing the
boundaries further, Kimi-Audio [24] adopts an advanced gener-
ation mechanism to balance low latency with high-fidelity ex-
pressive audio processing. As the latest advancement, Qwen3-
Omni [22] significantly scales the architecture and leverages op-
timized training objectives to further elevate the performance
ceiling of multi-modal alignment and complex reasoning. De-
spite these substantial architectural advancements, a critical
challenge remains: the inherent attention imbalance between
the audio and text modalities within these models.
2.2. Attention Weight Bias for Multi-modal Input
Cross-modal attention bias refers to the phenomenon where
multi-modal models disproportionately allocate attention to one
input modality, such as text, while under-attending to another,
such as audio or vision. Although this bias has not been system-
atically studied in the field of LALMs, it has been extensively
explored in LVLMs. In LVLMs, this bias is often a root cause of
hallucinations, stemming from an inherent imbalance between
visual and textual knowledge priors [12, 15]. Most LVLMs are
built upon pre-trained LLMs, which are inherently biased to-
wards textual information due to their training on vast text cor-
pora. Even with subsequent fine-tuning on image-text pairs for
cross-modal alignment, pre-trained textual knowledge remains
dominant. To mitigate this modality bias in LVLMs, two main
strategies have been adopted. The first uses re-alignment train-
ing with high-quality annotated data to reduce the dominance of
the textual modality [25]. The second strategy focuses on visual
enhancement and textual weight reduction [13]. These methods
either boost focus on visual tokens or weaken text influence dur-
ing inference, promoting balanced multi-modal integration.
In contrast, the LALM domain has yet to see a systematic
investigation into this kind of audio-textual attention bias. A re-
cent study by [26] indirectly highlights this gap, showing that
LALMs can perform competitively on audio reasoning tasks
even with the audio input completely removed, relying solely
on text instructions. This finding strongly suggests that current
LALMs have a tendency to neglect the audio modality and rely
excessively on textual information, underscoring the need for
deeper understanding and effective solutions.
3. Methodology
To address the audio-textual attention imbalance in LALMs
without the prohibitive costs of full retraining, we propose a
approach referred to as PayMoreAttentionToAudio (MATA).
MATA is a training-free technique that dynamically increases
the model’s focus on audio tokens within its self-attention
mechanism. The core motivation for MATA stems from our
key finding: in the intermediate layers of the Transformer ar-
chitecture, the primary locus of multi-modal information inte-
gration, LALMs exhibit a significant disparity in attention al-
location between text and audio tokens. By intervening at this
critical stage, we can guide the model towards a more balanced
multi-modal representation before the information propagates
to deeper layers for output decoding.
Current LALMs typically employ an autoregressive decod-
ing paradigm, generating tokens sequentially based on the con-
text of all preceding and multi-modal input tokens. The self-
attention mechanism, a core component of Transformer-based
LALMs, computes raw attention weights through a dot-product
operation between queryQand keyKstates. To prevent nu-
merical instability from large dot-product values, these raw
weights are scaled by the square root of the key dimension, with
the full attention computation formulated as:

4. Experiments
4.1. Setup
To systematically evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed
MATA method, we conducted experiments on two mainstream
audio reasoning benchmarks: MMAU 1 and MMAR 2, as well
as the Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge. For the
MMAU dataset, since the initial version is no longer available,
we used the Test-mini set with 1k audios of the updated ver-
sion (v05.15.25). We selected three state-of-the-art LALMs,
specifically Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni, and the recently in-
troduced Qwen3-Omni-Thinking, as our primary baseline mod-
els. These models were chosen for their superior performance
across various audio-textual multi-modal tasks, providing a rig-
orous benchmark for evaluating the improvements offered by
MATA. For hyperparameter settings, the enhancement strength
αwas defaulted to 0.1, and the MATA intervention was applied
to the LLM decoder layers from 10 to 20.
4.2. Experimental results on the MMAU dataset
Table 1 presents accuracy comparisons between our MATA
method and mainstream baselines on the MMAU (v05.15.25)
benchmark. The dataset categorizes audio inputs into three dis-
tinct tasks: sound, music, and speech. Given that the ground-
truth answers for the full MMAU 9000 test set are not pub-
licly available, all models were evaluated on the 1000 subset.
As shown in Table 1, Qwen2.5-Omni achieves the best overall
performance, with an average accuracy of 71.5%. In contrast,
Qwen2-Audio, once the top-performing open-source model in
its original publication, exhibits a noticeable performance gap
relative to the latest LALMs, reflecting the rapid advancement
of multi-modal architectures.
When integrated with our MATA method, both base-
line models demonstrate significant performance improvements
across all categories, which validates the effectiveness of our
approach. Specifically, applying MATA to Qwen2-Audio in-
creases the average accuracy from 59.4% (reproduced) to
64.8%. For the stronger baseline Qwen2.5-Omni, MATA main-
tains consistent enhancements, raising the average accuracy
from 71.1% (reproduced) to 73.6%. Notably, the magnitude
of improvement on Qwen2.5-Omni is slightly less pronounced
compared to Qwen2-Audio, a phenomenon we attribute to its
already robust baseline performance. Nonetheless, the MATA-
enhanced Qwen2.5-Omni achieves state-of-the-art results in
most categories, confirming the value of our method even for
cutting-edge models.

4.4. Results on the Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning
Challenge
As introduced in our experimental setup, we further validated
MATA within the framework of the Interspeech 2026 Audio
Reasoning Challenge [21]. It is worth noting that the final stage

MMAR-Rubrics, to rigorously evaluate the generated Chain-of-

completeness. For a comprehensive overview of the evaluation
pipeline, detailed metric definitions, and the complete compe-
tition leaderboard, we encourage readers to consult the official
challenge report [21].

5. Conclusions
In this paper, we investigate the critical problem of audio-
textual attention imbalance in LALMs, identifying that they dis-
proportionately allocate attention to text over audio, particularly
in the crucial intermediate layers for multi-modal fusion. To
mitigate this issue, we propose MATA, a training-free technique
that dynamically amplifies attention to audio tokens within
the self-attention mechanism. Extensive experiments demon-
strate that MATA consistently improves the reasoning accuracy
of diverse LALM architectures on the prominent MMAU and
MMAR benchmarks. Furthermore, evaluating our method in
the Interspeech 2026 Audio Reasoning Challenge highlighted
its advanced capabilities in real-world competitive scenarios.
By integrating MATA with the Qwen3-Omni-Thinking model,
we secured second place in the Single Model Track. Notably,
under the stringent rubric-based evaluation protocol, MATA sig-
nificantly enhanced the quality, factuality, and logic of the inter-
mediate reasoning chains. For future work, we plan to extend
the MATA method to a broader range of LALM architectures
and explore its application in other multi-modal models to fur-
ther improve their audio processing capabilities.
