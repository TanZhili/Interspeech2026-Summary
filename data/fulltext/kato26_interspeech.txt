Abstract
In telephone conversations, speakers lack the means to know
how clearly their voice reaches the listener. This degradation
in naturalness stems not only from the acoustic and communi-
cation environments but also from the speaker’s unintelligible
timbre. To address this issue, our real-time voice conversion
(VC) system, Coco-VC, performs on the listener side. It is built
with telephony data augmentation and multi-teacher distillation
to extract robust representations. The interactive demonstration
showcases a real-time, low-latency streaming VC on a standard
laptop, allowing users to seamlessly switch VC settings via an
intuitive GUI. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that Coco-
VC maintains high quality even for degraded telephony speech
compared to existing streaming VC systems.
Index Terms: voice conversion, streaming processing, tele-
phone speech, knowledge distillation
1. Introduction
Traditional voice conversion (VC) is typically deployed on the
speaker’s side, where the speaker uses it to transform their own
voice. In this setup, the speaker can optimize their recording en-
vironment and speaking style to ensure the highest VC quality.
In contrast, we focus on listener-side VC, a system that trans-
forms the speaker’s voice based on the listener’s preferences
and requirements.
A primary use case for such a system is telephone conver-
sations. In these scenarios, speakers are often unaware that
their voice reaches the listener with poor clarity due to their
own voice timbres, adverse acoustic environments, or com-
munication constraints. To be effective in these scenarios, a
VC system must operate as a streaming process that is robust
against telephony-specific degradations while seamlessly con-
verting the speaker’s voice into the desired target (Figure 1).
To address this objective, we present Coco-VC. It combines
telephony data augmentation with multi-teacher distillation to
capture degradation-invariant features. In this demonstration,

2. Interactive Listener-Side VC Demo
Interactive demonstration overview:The demonstration
places attendees in the role of a listener struggling to under-
stand heavily degraded voices via telephone. Using the Coco-
VC standalone application (Figure 2) running locally on a stan-
dard laptop, attendees experience a real-time VC system that
transforms unintelligible telephony speech into clear, natural-
sounding output.
Attendee interaction flow:The demo proceeds in three stages
to maximize hands-on engagement. First, attendees are asked to
play a listener role and listen to raw degraded telephone voices
to establish an unprocessed baseline. Second, the VC toggle is
activated live, immediately converting the stream into intelligi-
ble target-speaker speech for direct A/B comparison. Third, at-
tendees can serve as the speaker and speak into the microphone,
experiencing real-time conversion of their own voice under ar-
tificially introduced telephony degradations.
Target speaker switching:A key interactive element is the
ability to switch between multiple predefined target speaker
profiles mid-stream without interruption. Attendees can select
different conversion patterns via the GUI and immediately hear
the output shift, illustrating the system’s flexibility as a listener-
centric assistive tool.
Hardware and deployment setup:The demo runs entirely
on a consumer-grade laptop (e.g., Apple M2 MacBook, CPU-
only), achieving approximately 80 ms end-to-end latency. Al-
ternatively, it can run on a Windows laptop leveraging an in-
tegrated GPU (iGPU) at 160 ms latency; this difference is pri-
marily due to OS-specific audio stack buffering, while the core

3. System Development
Client-side real-time execution:To power the real-time VC
experience described in Section 2, Coco-VC employs a highly
efficient architecture (Figure 3). The core component is a stu-
dent content encoder built with Causal ConvNeXt blocks. By
applying zero look-ahead padding, the model processes 20 ms
frames with a 20 ms base algorithmic latency. Incorporating
overlap-add processing brings the total algorithmic latency to
40 ms. The encoded features are then converted into wave-
forms by a lightweight V ocos-based decoder trained on large-
scale in-domain data, synthesizing clean, target-speaker speech
on a standard laptop.
Multi-teacher distillation:Relying on a single self-supervised
learning (SSL) model often limits conversion quality due to
inherent teacher biases. Coco-VC overcomes this by fusing
the strengths of two complementary teacher models into a sin-
gle lightweight student encoder. We distill representations
from ContentVec, which excels at extracting speaker-invariant
prosodic information, and the Whisper encoder, which provides
highly robust linguistic features trained on massive datasets.
This dual-teacher distillation ensures the lightweight encoder
captures rich, high-fidelity content representations comparable
to large-scale non-streaming models.
Telephone-quality augmentation:To bridge the gap between
ideal training data and real-world remote speech, we utilize
an asymmetric training strategy. During training, the frozen
teacher models process clean 16 kHz speech to extract ideal
target features, while the student model processes heavily de-
graded 8 kHz speech. The student’s input is dynamically cor-
rupted using a telephony degradation pipeline that simulates
codec artifacts, signal distortions, reverberation, and noise.
By predicting clean, fused features from corrupted inputs, the
model simultaneously acts as a speech enhancer and feature ex-
tractor, acquiring robust representations that are invariant to se-
vere acoustic environments.

Real-World Robustness on Private Data:To validate
practical readiness, we evaluated the models on the private
dataset of simulated complaint calls (Table 2). We compared
our proposed Coco-VC model, trained on 61,840 hours of in-

960 hours of public data. Under severe telephony degradations,
the public-data variant struggled significantly (WER: 0.363). In
contrast, by leveraging in-domain training data, the proposed
model achieved a robust WER of 0.125 and a UT-MOS of
3.35. This improvement (a 23.8% absolute reduction in WER)
demonstrates that our domain-aware training pipeline is essen-
tial for delivering reliable, barrier-free communication in chal-
lenging real-world environments.
5. Conclusion
We presented Coco-VC, an environment-robust streaming voice
conversion system for telephone-quality remote speech. Com-
bining telephony data augmentation with multi-teacher distilla-
tion, our model extracts degradation-invariant representations.
The demonstration highlights its practical viability, proving that
high-quality, low-latency VC runs efficiently on standard lap-
tops. Coco-VC offers a powerful tool for inclusive, barrier-free
telecommunication in severely degraded acoustic environments.
