Abstract
A human face conveys rich cues about speaker identity,
enabling face-based zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) for unseen
speakers. However, in modular face-based TTS systems, the
acoustic model is typically trained on speech-derived embed-
dings, while face-derived representations are introduced only
at inference time, often resulting in identity drift. We propose
Dual-Space Constrained TTS (DSC-TTS), a modular frame-
work that enforces identity consistency during acoustic model
training in both the speaker embedding space and a shared iden-
tity space learned through face-voice alignment. By constrain-
ing representations across these complementary spaces, the
proposed framework improves speaker identity stability while
preserving speech quality. Experiments demonstrate higher
speaker similarity and stronger identity consistency than exist-
ing face-based TTS methods.
Index Terms: face-based TTS, face-voice alignment, zero-shot
TTS, speaker consistency
1. Introduction
Generating speaker-consistent speech from a single face image
is a central goal of face-based zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS),
as it enables speech synthesis for previously unseen individuals
without requiring enrollment audio. This capability is particu-
larly valuable in scenarios where reference speech is unavail-
able, such as dubbing for silent films, virtual avatar animation,
and face-based human–computer interaction. Despite recent
progress, stable speaker identity from a single face image re-
mains difficult to achieve. In the zero-shot setting, the absence
of enrollment audio makes it difficult to reliably ground speaker
embeddings, a problem further aggravated by heterogeneous
datasets and domain shifts. As a result, high similarity between
face-derived embeddings does not necessarily lead to percep-
tually consistent speaker identity in synthesized speech, often
leading to unstable identity and timbre inconsistency. This gap
highlights a fundamental challenge in face-based TTS: speaker
identity must be inferred and preserved without any acoustic
reference for speaker grounding.
Existing face-based TTS methods can be broadly divided
into two paradigms. End-to-end approaches integrate facial rep-
resentations directly into acoustic model training to improve
speaker consistency [1, 2, 3]. However, these methods are of-
ten limited by the scale and quality of available audio–visual
datasets, and they typically fall short of the naturalness and
speaker fidelity achieved by TTS models trained on large-scale
speech-only corpora. Modular approaches, in contrast, de-
couple cross-modal representation learning from acoustic mod-

eling, using face-derived representations to predict speaker

Face2Speech [4] pioneered this paradigm, followed by work
on improved alignment objectives [5], residual-guided super-
vision [6], structure-preserving cross-modal alignment [7], and
disentanglement of speaker identity from prosody [8]. While
modular systems benefit from strong speech synthesis back-
bones, they remain prone to speaker inconsistency when con-
ditioned on face-derived representations.
Several factors contribute to this limitation in modular face-
based TTS systems. Speaker representations learned from het-
erogeneous corpora often exhibit corpus-dependent variation,
which undermines identity stability when transferred across
datasets. In addition, face–voice alignment is typically learned
in a weak or unidirectional manner, making it sensitive to
modality mismatch and domain shift. More importantly, face-
derived conditioning is usually introduced only at inference
time, whereas the acoustic model is trained exclusively with
speech-derived speaker representations. This mismatch be-
tween training and inference representations further exacerbates
identity inconsistency during face-conditioned synthesis.
To address these challenges, we propose a three-stage mod-
ular framework termedDual-Space Constrained TTS(DSC-
TTS). The key idea is to constrain speaker identity repre-
sentations in two complementary spaces: a speaker embed-
ding space learned from speech and a shared identity space
learned through face–voice alignment. Specifically, we first
learn speaker embeddings that reduce corpus-dependent varia-
tion while preserving speaker-discriminative structure. We then
train a symmetric, bidirectional face–voice alignment module
to map facial and speech representations into a shared latent
space, enabling cross-modal identity consistency. Finally, dur-
ing acoustic model training, identity representations are jointly
constrained in both spaces, which improves compatibility with
face-derived conditioning at inference time and mitigates iden-
tity drift in synthesized speech. Audio samples are available on
our project page.1
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
• We propose a speaker embedding learning strategy that re-
duces corpus-dependent variation while preserving speaker-
discriminative structure for modular face-based TTS.
• We design a symmetric, bidirectional face–voice alignment
method to learn a shared identity space that enhances cross-
modal consistency.
• We introduce DSC-TTS, a modular framework that en-
forces consistency in both the speaker embedding space
and the face–voice shared identity space, thereby mitigating
training–inference mismatch and reducing identity drift.

2. Method
We propose DSC-TTS, a three-stage modular framework for
face-based zero-shot TTS, consisting of a speech encoder, a
face–voice alignment module, and a dual-space constrained
training strategy for the TTS acoustic model. As shown in
Fig. 1, the speech encoder and the face–voice alignment mod-
ule are trained in the first two stages. In the final stage, the
learned identity representations are used to constrain acoustic
model training.
2.1. Speech Encoder
The speech encoderE s extracts fixed-dimensional,ℓ 2-
normalized speaker embeddings from log-Mel spectrograms
and is trained using speaker classification as the primary objec-
tive, augmented with auxiliary objectives for corpus invariance
and speaker-discriminative structure preservation.
Speaker discrimination is optimized using AAM-
Softmax [9]. To mitigate corpus-dependent variation, we adopt
domain-adversarial training via a gradient reversal layer [10].
However, enforcing domain invariance may weaken speaker-
discriminative structure. To counteract this effect, we apply
supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) [11], which promotes
intra-speaker compactness and inter-speaker separability.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
V oxCeleb2 [13] is a large-scale audio–visual dataset col-
lected from unconstrained online videos and is used to train
both the face–voice alignment module and the speech en-
coder. LibriTTS [14] is a multi-speaker English speech cor-
pus with aligned transcripts; itstrain-clean-100and
train-clean-360subsets are used for multi-speaker TTS
training and for training the speech encoder. LJSpeech [15] is
used for single-speaker TTS pretraining. All speech signals are
downsampled to 16 kHz.
For evaluation, 24 unseen speakers (12 male and 12 fe-
male), disjoint from the training set, are randomly selected
from V oxCeleb2. For each speaker, face images are paired
with text prompts sampled from the LibriTTStest-clean
set. LRS2 [16] is a tri-modal face-speech-text dataset collected
under different recording conditions and used for sample-level
evaluation under corpus mismatch.
3.2. Implementation Details
We use an ECAPA-TDNN [17] backbone for speaker embed-
ding extraction and a pretrained AntelopeV2 2 for face embed-
ding extraction. For each video,K= 5face frames are uni-
formly sampled and aggregated with attention pooling. Face
and speaker embeddings are projected into a shared latent space
of 256 dimensions.
The face–voice alignment network is trained with AdamW
using a learning rate of10−4, weight decay of10−6, and a batch
size of 256. For TTS, we use YourTTS [18], which is pretrained
on LJSpeech for 120K steps and fine-tuned on LibriTTS for
165K steps. Loss weights are set toλDomain =λ SupCon = 1.0,
λREC = 0.5,λ LC = 0.1,λ SCL = 9.0, andλ SICL = 3.0. All
experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU

with 24 GB memory.
3.3. Evaluation Metrics
Speech intelligibility is evaluated using word error rate (WER)
and character error rate (CER), computed from Whisper tran-
scriptions [19]. Speaker-related properties are evaluated us-
ing Resemblyzer 3, which is used to compute speaker embed-
ding cosine similarity between synthesized speech and the
corresponding reference speech (SECS), speaker embedding
consistency (SEC), and speaker embedding diversity (SED).
SEC measures the average intra-speaker embedding consis-
tency across synthesized utterances, while SED reflects inter-
speaker embedding separability across different speakers.
Subjective evaluation is conducted using Similarity Mean
Opinion Score (SMOS) tests on a five-point scale, which eval-
uate perceived speaker similarity between synthesized speech
and the target speaker. Each sample is rated by 10 listeners.
3.4. Comparison with Face-Based TTS Methods
We compare DSC-TTS with one end-to-end baseline,
FaceTTS [1], and three modular methods: Face2Speech [4],
SYNTHE-SEES [6], and Face-StyleSpeech [8]. For the mod-
ular baselines, face–voice alignment components are imple-
mented following the original designs, while the remaining
components are evaluated under a common setting.
Table 1 shows that modular methods generally produce
lower WER and CER than the end-to-end baseline FaceTTS on
both V oxCeleb2 and LRS2. On V oxCeleb2, FaceTTS reports
a WER of 0.113, whereas all modular systems achieve WERs
below 0.10. Despite better intelligibility, modular baselines ex-
hibit weaker speaker identity consistency, as reflected by their
lower SEC scores.

3.5. Ablation Study
Table 2 examines the effects of speaker embedding learning and
dual-space consistency constraints in DSC-TTS. Incorporating
domain-adversarial and supervised contrastive objectives im-
proves speaker-related metrics, especially SECS. The gains are
more pronounced on LRS2, suggesting improved generalization
of the learned speaker embeddings under corpus mismatch.
Introducing dual-space consistency constraints during
multi-speaker fine-tuning further improves identity consistency
across synthesized utterances. In particular, SICL improves
SEC by enforcing consistency in the shared identity space, and
combining SICL with SCL yields additional gains in the cross-
corpus setting. Meanwhile, SED remains largely unchanged,
indicating that the constraints enhance identity stability without
degrading inter-speaker separability. These results show that
speaker embedding learning and dual-space constrained train-
ing provide complementary improvements.

4. Conclusion
We propose DSC-TTS, a modular face-based zero-shot text-to-
speech framework that mitigates speaker identity drift from a
single face image. By integrating corpus-invariant speaker em-
bedding learning, symmetric face–voice alignment, and dual-
space constrained training, DSC-TTS alleviates the training–
inference mismatch in speaker identity representations. Ex-
periments on V oxCeleb2 and LRS2 demonstrate that the pro-
posed method improves speaker similarity and identity consis-
tency while preserving intelligibility compared with existing
face-based TTS approaches. Ablation and qualitative analyses
further validate the complementary contributions of each com-
ponent to the overall performance.
