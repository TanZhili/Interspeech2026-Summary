Abstract
We present a Computer-Assisted Pronunciation Training
(CAPT) system that incorporates a CARFAC (Cascade of
Asymmetric Resonators with Fast-Acting Compression) and
SAI (Stabilised Auditory Image)-derived pitchogram as an
alternative to conventional spectrogram-based visualisation,
which often fails to clearly represent temporal fine structure.
The system features a dual-screen interface that provides real-
time pitchogram feedback alongside reference pitchograms
generated using Google Text-to-Speech. This design enables
learners to perceive and produce the four Mandarin tones by
comparing their speech with reference patterns. Our approach
highlights the potential of pitchogram-based visualisation to
support intuitive, real-time pronunciation practice for second
language (L2) learners.
Index Terms: tonal language, CAPT, CARFAC, SAI
1. Introduction
Mandarin Chinese is a tonal language with four traditional tones
(see A in Figure 1) and one neutral tone [1]. Non-tonal lan-
guage speakers often struggle to understand and speak the four
traditional tones due to difficulty mapping pitch variations to
tone categories [1], while the neutral tone is recognised as
an easy tone [2]. Computer-Assisted Pronunciation Training
(CAPT) systems commonly address tonal pronunciation chal-
lenges through visualisation, with spectrograms being a widely
adopted tool for displaying tone contours [3, 4]. However, the
fine temporal details in spectrograms change rapidly, so begin-
ners or learners without prior experience often struggle to inter-
pret them in real time [5].
We demonstrate how theCARFAC (Cascade of Asym-
metric Resonators with Fast-Acting Compression) and SAI
(Stabilised Auditory Image)-derived pitchogramsupports
learners in learning the four Mandarin Chinese tones. We de-
signed the CAPT system to support both tone perception and
tone production practice for self-learning since these two are
closely linked [3]. Our goal is to enable learners to intuitively
recognise and correct their own tone production errors using
only the pitchogram.
This demonstration: (1) introduces a novel visualisation
method for CAPT systems in tonal languages, and (2) explores
the potential of pitchogram-based prototypes for future CAPT
systems.

2. Theoretical Background
Visualisation-based CAPT systems often employ spectrograms,
particularly mel-spectrograms, because they emphasise fre-
quency components that are more closely aligned with human
auditory perception [6]. In a typical mel-spectrogram pipeline,
the input audio is segmented into short frames, transformed into
the frequency domain using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT),
and then passed through a mel filter bank followed by loga-
rithmic compression to produce a time–frequency representa-
tion [7]. While such representations are widely used in speech
and pronunciation learning, their rapidly changing temporal fine
structure can still be difficult for novice learners to interpret in
real time.
To address this limitation, we draw on SAI, which was pro-
posed to demodulate and stabilise temporal fine structures into
more slowly varying functions of time lag [5]. Compared with
mel-spectrograms, SAI provides a more temporally stable rep-
resentation of periodic structure, making it a promising basis
for visual feedback in tone learning. We further build on CAR-
FAC, a biologically inspired auditory model that approximates
cochlear processing [8, 9].
In this work, we demonstrate a CARFAC/SAI-derived
pitchogram, implemented using Google’s CARFAC frame-
work1 [8]. As illustrated in Figure 1 (A), the pitchogram is
derived from the combined CARFAC/SAI outputs and serves
as the basis of our visual feedback design.

3. Workflow and Implementation
We integrate the pitchogram into a CAPT system designed to
support Mandarin tone perception and production. The system
is run from the command line on Windows and requires no setup
or calibration. Reference audio is generated using Google Text-
to-Speech (TTS) 2. The system consists of two Python-based
prototypes (see Figure 2. Audio is taken as input at 16 kHz and
segmented into short frames of 450 samples (approximately 28
ms). A single button allows switching between the two proto-
types, making it easy to move between perception and produc-
tion tasks during a session. All main functions can be controlled
with a single keypress, and no setup or calibration is required.
In thetone perception prototype(see Figure 2 (A)), the
learner listens to a reference audio sample while viewing its
pitchogram. They select the correct tone using on-screen but-
tons or number keys. For two-syllable words, two selections are
required. The system immediately shows whether the answer is
correct using a coloured indicator, and the learner must identify
the tones shown in the visualisation before moving on.
In thetone production prototype, a dual-screen layout is
used (see Figure 2 (B)): the left screen displays a real-time
pitchogram of the learner’s own speech via the Google CAR-
FAC pitchogram web tool, 3 while the right screen hosts the
Python application where the learner plays a reference audio
sample and views its pitchogram before repeating the word
aloud. This side-by-side arrangement allows direct visual com-
parison of the learner’s own tonal contour and the reference.

words from HSK Levels 1 and 24 for the demonstration. Learn-

4. Conclusion
This demonstration presents a CAPT system that uses
CARFAC/SAI-derived pitchograms for real-time pronunciation
practice. This suggest that pitchogram-based visual feedback
can serve as a valuable component of CAPT systems for tone
learning. Future work should explore the integration of auto-
matically generated TTS to enable automatic expansion of the
vocabulary set. In addition, this could be further enhanced by
incorporating AI-generated textual feedback to provide more
explicit instructional guidance. Finally, the system could be ex-
tended to other tonal languages as well.
