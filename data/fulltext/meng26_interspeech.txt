Abstract
In speaker recognition tasks, acquiring data requires signifi-
cant technical and financial resources for individual organiza-
tions, while also posing risks of privacy breaches. Applying
federated learning architectures can address these issues, but
model performance may degrade when confronting data het-
erogeneity challenges. In this paper, we propose a federated
learning-based speaker recognition method with dual classifi-
cation heads. By introducing a more knowledgeable classi-
fication head, local models can acquire more comprehensive
global knowledge, thereby improving model performance. Our
method achieved an average EER improvement of 46.5% over
local training and 7.6% over FedAvg when trained on the V ox-
Celeb2 dataset. When jointly trained on the V oxCeleb and CN-
Celeb datasets, the average EER improved by 68.8% over local
training and 5.4% over FedAvg.

1. Introduction
Speaker recognition [1] stands as a pivotal technology within
identity verification and security systems. This technique
achieves speaker identification by converting speech signals
into acoustic features, from which distinctive feature vectors ca-
pable of distinguishing different speakers are extracted. Speaker
recognition primarily encompasses two distinct approaches:
speaker identification and speaker verification. Speaker iden-
tification addresses the question of ‘who is speaking’, deter-
mining the speaker’s identity from a set of known individuals;
whereas speaker verification addresses the question of ‘whether
it is the same speaker’, determining whether two speech seg-
ments originate from the same individual. Presently, speaker
recognition technology is primarily implemented using deep
neural networks [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]. With advancements in deep
neural networks, this technology has found extensive appli-
cation across various practical scenarios, achieving significant
progress. Within the deep learning framework, the quality of
training data plays a decisive role in model performance, and
large-scale, high-quality datasets are crucial for driving the ad-
vancement of speaker recognition systems [8, 9].
The prevailing speaker recognition systems predominantly
employ a centralised training paradigm, wherein a single entity
aggregates multi-source data to complete model training within

sensitivity. Should any single institution attempt to accumulate
large-scale speaker voice data, it would not only require sub-
stantial economic investment and technical resources but would
also face severe challenges posed by increasingly stringent pri-
vacy compliance regulations.
Federated learning [13] is a distributed machine learning
approach that has emerged in recent years, offering a privacy-
aware learning paradigm that does not require the sharing of
raw data. Participants in federated learning typically comprise
servers and clients. Clients receive a global model from the
server and utilise their local private datasets for training. Upon
completion, clients upload only their local models to the server,
which then updates the model without requiring the transfer of
raw data.
The current federal speaker recognition framework typi-
cally positions the client as an individual terminal device, as-
suming each client holds only a limited amount of speaker data.
This configuration is not only constrained by the computational
capabilities of terminal devices but also heavily reliant on users’
active authorisation and cooperation. This is unrealistic. We
have established a more practical scenario by defining the client
as an organisation or company possessing a certain scale of
data, which better aligns with real-world requirements. In this
scenario, the data distributions held by individual clients are
more likely to exhibit significant heterogeneity. Specifically,
each client’s private dataset typically contains distinct sets of
speakers, with speaker categories remaining mutually exclusive
across different clients. This data heterogeneity, arising from
inconsistent data distributions, similarly exerts a detrimental ef-
fect on speaker recognition tasks within the federated learning
framework [14, 15, 16, 17], consequently leading to a decline
in model performance.
Data heterogeneity has long been a focus of attention. Fed-
Prox [18] proposes a regularization method. MOON [19] en-
ables local models to learn from the global model to acquire
more knowledge, while FedCDA [20] selects the model with
the smallest loss for aggregation during the model aggregation
process. However, for most optimisation methods, the models
within these approaches are trained under the guidance of lo-
cal classification heads. As local classification heads only have
access to the private data of the current client, they lack ex-
plicit awareness of the global data distribution. Consequently,
the locally trained models can only indirectly acquire knowl-
edge from other clients through subsequent model aggregation
[21, 22, 23]. This mechanism limits the effective integration of

In response to this, we propose a Federated learning-based
speaker recognition method with Dual Classification Heads
(FedDCH), which intuitively obtains global knowledge by in-
troducing another encyclopedic classification mind. The global
classifier and local classifier work together to guide the training
of the back-end feature extractor, achieving excellent results. In
summary, our contributions are as follows:
We introduce a global classifier to participate in local train-
ing, intuitively acquiring global knowledge to address the chal-
lenges posed by data heterogeneity to model performance. Dur-
ing local training, the global classifier collaborates with the lo-
cal classifier to guide the optimisation of the backend network.
Upon completion of local updates, the global classifier is up-
loaded to the server alongside the local model to participate in
the aggregation of global knowledge. To fully account for vari-
ations in data volume across different speakers within the dis-
tribution, we incorporate a speaker-ID-based weighted aggre-
gation mechanism during global classifier aggregation. Specif-
ically, when the global classification head is more thoroughly
trained on a particular speaker’s data, it gains greater influence
during aggregation, thereby more effectively preserving that
speaker’s discriminative information. Extensive experimenta-
tion and testing across diverse datasets validate the resulting
improvements in model performance.

2.2. Dual Classification Heads Training
Since the local classifierhk(·) merely divides embeddings into
Ck classes, subsequently guiding the backpropagation to train
the parameters of the backend embedding extractor, under these
circumstances, the local model can only acquire global knowl-
edge through model aggregation. Consequently, the model’s
knowledge acquisition regarding the entire dataset participating
in federated learning remains incomplete, leading to subopti-
mal model performance. During subsequent model aggregation
stages, the aggregation of models bearing distinct distributional
characteristics may adversely affect the performance of the final
trained model. To address this, we introduce a global classifi-
cation head during local training. This classifier decomposes
embeddings intoC classes and collaborates with the local clas-
sifier to guide the learning of the embedding extractor. This
enables the local model to acquire global knowledge more di-
rectly, thereby enhancing overall model performance.

3.2. Implementation Details
Data Preparation. We randomly and uniformly split V ox-
Celeb2 intoK sub-datasets according to speaker ID.
Training Details. For all experiments, we employed an
ECAPA-TDNN with a channel size of 1024 as the embedding
extractor w. The input is the 80-dimensional log mel-
spectrogram, and the speaker embedding is 512-dimensional.
The local loss functionllocal consistently utilizes AM-Softmax
Loss (m = 0.2,s = 30 ) [31]. The initial learning rate was
set to 0.001, decaying by 0.97 per epoch. The optimizer used
was Adam. l = 5,T = 20. For experiments on the V oxCeleb2
dataset, we set α = 0 .4,β = 0 .4,µ = 1 . Experiments
were conducted using Cross-Entropy loss, AM-Softmax
loss (m = 0 .2,s = 30 ) [31], and AAM-Softmax loss
(m = 0.2,s = 30 ) [32] as lglobal. For joint experiments on
V oxCeleb and CN-Celeb, we setα = 0.2,β = 0.1,µ = 5. The
global loss is the same as the local loss.

3.3. Results and Analysis
Results on VoxCeleb2. For experiments on V oxCeleb2, we
evaluated different methods across multiple test sets, as shown
in Table 1. Our proposed FedDCH method demonstrates strong
performance on V ox-O and V ox-E, showcasing its effectiveness
and stability in enhancing local model performance within fed-
erated learning scenarios. When confronted with the challeng-
ing V ox-H speaker, our method achieves optimal performance
on only a subset of clients, indicating room for optimization
when handling difficult validation sets. In contrast, the stan-
dard method exhibits relatively poor performance. While other
federated learning approaches show improvements, this con-
firms that collaborative learning via federated methods can help
clients enhance local model performance. Additionally, exper-
iments with different global loss functions were conducted to
assess method stability, as shown in Table 3. Our method con-
sistently achieves favorable performance across these experi-
ments. Collectively, these results demonstrate that our proposed
method holds significant advantages in federated speaker recog-
nition.
Results on VoxCeleb and CN-Celeb. To enhance the persua-
siveness of our experiments, we simulated more realistic scenar-
ios. V oxCeleb1, CN-Celeb1, V oxCeleb2, and CN-Celeb2 were
deployed as private datasets across four distinct clients for ex-
perimentation purposes. As shown in Table 2, even when clients
possess private datasets with distinct characteristics, the perfor-
mance of locally trained models improved through collaborative
learning via federated learning. Furthermore, the overall perfor-
mance improvement achieved by our proposed FedDCH is the
most significant.

4. Conclusion
In this work, we have adjusted the setting to a more realistic sce-
nario where participants in federated learning are no longer in-
dividuals but organisational entities or corporate bodies. Within
this framework, we propose FedDCH, a dual-classification
header-based speaker recognition method grounded in feder-
ated learning. By introducing collaborative training between
a global classifier and local classifiers, we more intuitively cap-
ture global data distribution knowledge, enhance local model
performance, and directly acquire global knowledge to address
the challenges posed by data heterogeneity to model perfor-
mance. Following local updates, the global classifier is up-
loaded to the server alongside local models to participate in
global knowledge aggregation. To fully account for variations
in data volume across different speakers within the distribu-
tion, we incorporate a speaker-ID-based weighted aggregation
mechanism during global classifier aggregation. This further
optimises local models. Our experiments on V oxCeleb and CN-
Celeb demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach for speaker
recognition tasks. We also explore its performance across dif-
ferent test sets and with varied loss functions, confirming its ro-
bustness. Future work will investigate more efficient federated
speaker recognition frameworks.
