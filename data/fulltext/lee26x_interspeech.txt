AGENT: A Black-box Adversarial Attack
Exposing the Achilles' Heel of SASV Systems

Abstract
Spoofing-Aware Speaker Verification (SASV) enhances Auto-
matic Speaker Verification (ASV) by integrating countermea-
sure (CM) modules to defend against spoofing attacks. Re-
cent studies show that SASV improves robustness against both
spoofing and adversarial attacks on standalone ASV . However,
attacks targeting the full ASV–CM pipeline remain underex-
plored. We propose AGENT, a black-box adversarial attack
that jointly deceives both ASV and CM modules. AGENT is
built upon two key designs: (1) a score-maximization objective
that amplifies ASV confidence beyond decision margins, and
(2) a directional-selective gradient fusion strategy that explic-
itly resolves gradient conflicts between modules. Experiments
demonstrate that AGENT achieves up to 99.62% Attack Suc-
cess Rate (ASR) across diverse SASV architectures. These re-
sults reveal critical vulnerabilities in current SASV systems and
highlight the need for stronger defenses in real-world voice au-
thentication systems.
Index Terms: spoofing-aware speaker verification, adversarial
attack, biometric security
1. Introduction
ASV [1] systems are widely adopted for access control and
secure communication, valued for their convenience and non-
intrusiveness. Despite this, they remain vulnerable to various
threats, most notably spoofing attacks [2], where artificial or
manipulated speech is used to imitate a target speaker and gain
unauthorized access [3]. Another major threat is adversarial at-
tacks [4], which add imperceptible perturbations to input signals
in order to mislead the system’s decision.
To address spoofing, SASV systems [5] have been proposed
by integrating ASV with CM [6] modules. Zhang et al. [7]
show that SASV not only improves robustness against spoof-
ing attacks but also mitigates adversarial attacks on standalone
ASV models. For instance, FAKEBOB [8], a state-of-the-art
(SOTA) adversarial attack on ASV , suffers a 70% performance
drop when transferred to a cascaded SASV structure [9], with
only 38% of adversarial examples successfully bypassing the
CM module.
Despite these advances, most adversarial methods [8, 10,
11, 12, 13] focus solely on ASV models, overlooking the joint
decision boundary of SASV . To the best of our knowledge, only
two studies [7, 14] have attempted to attack SASV , but their
approaches depend on auxiliary networks or speech synthesis
models, which increase attack complexity. Moreover, prior
evaluations have been restricted to cascaded architectures, leav-
ing their applicability to other SASV configurations uncertain.

3.1. Score Maximization Objective (SMO)
Most existing adversarial attacks use threshold-based objectives
that only aim to push the score above a decision boundary. As
a result, the generated examples often lie close to the boundary,
making them less transferable across different architectures.
To address this limitation, we directly maximize the ASV
similarity score:
LASV =s ASV,wheres ASV =f ASV (x(t),yt).(6)
Instead of simply crossing the decision boundary, AGENT
maximizes the similarity score to push adversarial examples
further into the acceptance region. This leads to stronger trans-
ferability across different models in black-box scenarios.
To avoid rejection by the CM module, we include the fol-
lowing objective:
LCM =s CM−τCM,(7)
wheres CM =f CM (x(t))denotes the output score of the sur-
rogate CM model. This objective encourages the adversarial
example to remain above the CM decision threshold. Unlike
the ASV objective, the CM objective merely prevents rejection
and does not require aggressive score amplification.

conflicting gradient directions. Directly summing gradients can
therefore hinder optimization and degrade transferability.

4. Experimental Setup
The source code used in our experiments is publicly available
at https://github.com/2oil/AGENT.git.
•Hyperparameter settings.We setT= 30,α=ϵ/10, and
β= 1, and varyϵfrom 0.001 to 0.016.
•Datasets.All experiments are conducted on the ASVspoof
2019 LA dataset [20], using 4,000 random non-target trials
from the evaluation set.
•Models.We employ three ASV models (ECAPA-
TDNN [21], NeXt-TDNN [22], and ResNet34v2 [23])
and four CM models (AASIST [24], AASIST-SSL [25],
RawNet2 [26], and ResNet-OC [27]).
For SASV systems, we adopt two widely used architectures:
cascading [9] and score fusion [5]. Thresholds are calibrated
on V oxCeleb1 [28] for ASV models, and on the ASVspoof
2019 LA evaluation set for CM models and score fusion, all
set at the Equal Error Rate (EER) operating point.
•Evaluation metrics.Attack effectiveness is measured by the
ASR, ASR(%)=N s/Nt, whereNs andNt denote the num-
ber of successful and total adversarial examples. Addition-
ally, perceptual quality is measured by the signal-to-noise ra-
tio (SNR), SNR= 10·log 10(Px/Pδ), withP x andP δ de-
noting the power of the original signal and perturbation.

5.3. Attack performance across different SASVs
Tables 3 and 4 present the ASRs across different surrogate–
victim combinations and SASV architectures. AGENT con-
sistently achieves high ASRs under both cascading and score
fusion systems. In cascading configurations, ASRs exceed 95%
in most settings and reach up to 99.62%. Score fusion archi-
tectures appear slightly more robust. Unlike cascading systems
that apply independent thresholds, score fusion imposes a joint
constraint on the combined score. As a result, insufficient in-
crease in one module may require additional increase in the
other. Nevertheless, AGENT still achieves up to 94.95% ASR
under score fusion.
High attack success is preserved across different surro-
gate–victim model pairs, demonstrating strong transferability
under black-box settings. Even at high ASRs, SNR remains
above 22 dB, indicating that the perturbations do not signifi-
cantly degrade perceptual audio quality. These results high-
light the effectiveness and practicality of AGENT across diverse
SASV architectures.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed AGENT, a black-box attack that
jointly targets ASV and CM modules in SASV systems. Ex-
tensive experiments demonstrate consistently high ASRs across
diverse architectures, revealing overlooked adversarial vulnera-
bilities in SASV frameworks. Future work will extend AGENT
to diverse real-world attack scenarios, including physical and
over-the-air settings. By analyzing these vulnerabilities from
multiple perspectives, we aim to contribute to the development
of more resilient and secure voice authentication systems.
