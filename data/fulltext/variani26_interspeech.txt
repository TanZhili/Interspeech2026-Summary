Representational Instability in Decoupled Audio Encoders
Ehsan Variani, Tom Bagby, Georg Heigold, Ke Wu, Cyril Allauzen
Google Research, USA
{variani, tombagby, heigold, wuke, allauzen}@google.com
Abstract
Modern audio processing uses neural encoders to generate dis-
crete or continuous representations for downstream models
(e.g., LLMs, ASR). While optimized for the Rate-Distortion
(RD) trade-off to ensure waveform reconstruction, their stabil-
ity is often overlooked. We conceptualize the Stability-Rate-
Distortion (SRD) trade-off, highlighting representational sta-
bility as a third evaluation axis. Optimizing strictly for re-
construction leaves representations vulnerable to acoustic nui-
sances, creating a Stability Penalty. To evaluate this, we in-
troduce the Continuous Edit Distance (CED) to quantify ge-
ometric drift. Analyzing state-of-the-art encoders (EnCodec,
SoundStream, Whisper) reveals catastrophic instability. While
continuous latents resist minor perturbations, the vector quanti-
zation (VQ) bottleneck shatters them into disparate discrete se-
quences—the Quantization Penalty. Evaluating 26 dialects ex-
poses a cross-lingual ”Language Tax,” where stability collapses
on low-resource dialects due to weak linguistic priors. High
waveform fidelity is empirically at odds with representational
stability in low-bitrate regimes, highlighting a critical vulnera-
bility in current audio tokens.
Index Terms: speech recognition, human-computer interac-
tion, computational paralinguistics
1. Introduction
The mathematical foundation of audio compression is tradi-
tionally rooted in the Source Coding Theorem, which dictates
the fundamental limits for the trade-off between the informa-
tion rateR—typically expressed as the transmission bitrate in
kilobits per second (kbps)—and the reconstruction distortionD
[1, 2]. For decades, the speech and audio processing commu-
nity has focused on the minimization of waveform or percep-
tual distortion at increasingly aggressive bitrates. This long-
standing pursuit has recently culminated in the emergence of
neural codecs utilizing Vector Quantization (VQ) [3, 4] and
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [5] to achieve near-
transparent reconstruction in the sub-kilobit regime [6, 7, 8]. In
theseclosed-loopsystems, the latent representation is treated as
a transient intermediate state; the encoder-decoder pair is jointly
optimized as a coupled system where the end-to-end fidelity of
the output waveform ˆXis the primary metric of success.
However, the recent shift toward multi-modal interleaving
and large-scale generative modeling has led to the widespread
adoption ofdecoupledoropen-looparchitectures. In these
frameworks, compressed latent manifolds or discrete token se-
quences are leveraged as universal “audio words” to facilitate
training across disparate modalities and tasks [9, 10, 11]. In this
paradigm, the encoder’s output is a permanent representation
that must facilitate semantic reasoning and preserve structural
consistency across highly variable acoustic environments. Un-
like a coupled decoder, which can learn to invert complex non-
linear mappings to reconstruct a signal, downstream consumers
of these tokens—such as Large Language Models (LLMs) or
Speech-to-Speech Translators—require representational invari-
ance. The mappingE:X → Zmust project semantically
equivalent signals onto stable latent coordinates, regardless of
the nuisance variables present in the input.
Despite achieving state-of-the-art reconstruction, high-
fidelity neural codecs exhibit a profound Discrete Representa-
tion Inconsistency (DRI) [12]. Empirical evidence suggests that
these encoders—often driven by adversarial pressures to pre-
serve high-frequency “textures”—are hyper-sensitive to acous-
tic nuisances that do not alter the underlying semantic mani-
fold [13, 14]. While recent works such as StableToken [14] and
NAST [15] attempt to empirically mitigate this fragility through
noise-aware tokenization, a unified, architecture-agnostic met-
ric to evaluate this latent drift has been lacking. For decoupled
downstream systems, this sensitivity induces catastrophic repre-
sentational drift. In discrete regimes, a perturbation with negli-
gible perceptual impact can trigger a near-total reconfiguration
of the token sequence, effectively presenting the downstream
model with a disparate linguistic identity for the same acous-
tic event. We posit that this instability persists because existing
multi-objective optimizations—even those incorporating auxil-
iary tasks like ASR—fail to explicitly constrain the stability of
the mapping under perturbation. When an encoder is primar-
ily governed by reconstruction distortionD, it is mathemati-
cally incentivized to preserve all mutual information between
the sourceXand representationZ, including unstable nuisance
components.
To conceptualize this conflict, we propose the Stability-
Rate-Distortion (SRD) trade-off. We argue that open-loop ar-
chitectures require a distinct evaluation axis designed specifi-
cally for downstream machine consumption: representational
Stability(S). We hypothesize that optimizing strictly for the
traditional reconstruction bound fundamentally leaves represen-
tations vulnerable to noise, as the encoder must allocate its rate
budget to track nuisance textures. Consequently, achieving sta-
bility in the representation space incurs a conceptual “Stability
Penalty” (∆S)—a mandatory empirical trade-off between se-
mantic consistency and waveform reconstruction that current
state-of-the-art models fail to address. The specific contribu-
tions of this work are as follows:
•Conceptual Framework & Metric: We introduce the SRD
trade-off to conceptualize tokenizer fragility, and propose the
Continuous Edit Distance (CED), an architecture-agnostic
metric that robustly quantifies representational drift across
both continuous latent manifolds and discrete codebooks.
•Isolating the Quantization Penalty: We present an empir-

2. Conceptualizing Representational
Stability
The design of traditional audio codecs has historically been
treated as a signal reconstruction problem, where the objective
is to transmit a waveform through a capacity-constrained chan-
nel with minimal loss of fidelity. However, as these codecs tran-
sition into foundational encoders for downstream AI tasks, the
objective shifts from signal recovery to representational consis-
tency. In this section, we conceptualize the conflict between
reconstruction quality and representational stability. We begin
by revisiting the classical Rate-Distortion (RD) theorem and
subsequently extend it conceptually into a three-dimensional
Stability-Rate-Distortion (SRD) framework.

A critical limitation of this classical view is thatZis treated

framework does not penalize an encoder where a clean signalX
and a perturbed variantg(X)map to radically different points
in the latent spaceZ, provided both points allow for a valid re-
construction within the error boundD. In our SRD framework,
we argue that this topological indifference is the primary cause
of representational collapse in AI-facing encoders.
2.2. Information Decomposition and Semantic Mapping
To move beyond reconstruction, we must distinguish between
the semantic signal and the stochastic noise. We model the
sourceXas being generated by two latent processes: a se-
mantic processY(e.g., phonemes, melodic motifs) and a nui-
sance processN(e.g., reverberation, additive noise, or chan-
nel effects). Formally, we define a semantic mapping function
fY :X →Ysuch that any transformationg:X →Xis a
semantic-preserving perturbation iff Y (X) =f Y (g(X)).
Following the chain rule of mutual information, the total
bitrateR=I(X;Z)is exactly decomposed as:
I(X;Z) =I(Y;Z) +I(N;Z|Y)(3)
whereI(Y;Z)is the Semantic Information andI(N;Z|Y)is
the Nuisance Information. In modern high-fidelity codecs, the
termI(N;Z|Y)is significantly inflated. To satisfy perceptual
realism, adversarial discriminators [5] force the encoder to track
high-frequency “textures.” This anchors the representationZ
to specific realizations of the nuisanceN, rendering the map-
ping hyper-sensitive to perturbations and destroying its utility
for downstream machine reasoning.
2.3. The Stability-Rate-Distortion (SRD) Trade-off
We conceptually extend the classical 2D Shannon curve into a
3D SRD formulation by introducing a constraint on representa-
tional drift. LetX T denote the space of input acoustic signals
of temporal lengthT, andZ K denote the space of compressed
latent sequences of lengthK(whereK≤Tdue to the en-
coder’s temporal striding). For an encoderE:X T →Z K,
the operational bitrateR(D,S)can be conceptualized as being
constrained by the intersection of two feasible sets:
•Fidelity SetD D: Mappings that satisfy the traditional ex-
pected reconstruction error boundD.
•Stability SetS S: Mappings that satisfy a strict representa-
tional drift boundSunder a semantic-preserving perturbation
g(X), ensuringE[d Z(E(X T ),E(g(X T )))]≤S, whered Z
is a sequence-level distance metric measuring geometric drift
in the latent space (formalized in Section 3).
Because the restricted intersection (D D∩S S) is a sub-
set of the unconstrained fidelity spaceD D, the minimum re-
quired information rate must logically exceed or equal the un-
constrained Shannon limit. We term this conceptual gap the
Stability Penalty(∆S). As we demand higher stability (reduc-
ing the allowable driftS), the encoder must either “pay” with a
higher bitrate to insulate the latent manifold from nuisances, or
“sell” its reconstruction fidelity by allowingDto increase. Cur-
rent high-fidelity neural audio codecs are typically optimized to
approach the Shannon floor (ignoringS), which achieves high
reconstruction quality at low bitrates but places the model on a
fragile region of the SRD manifold.
There have been previous efforts to expand classical RD
theory by introducing a third dimension. Most notably, the
Rate-Distortion-Perception (RDP) framework [16] introduces
a Perception axis that optimizes forstatistical realism(mini-
mizing distributional divergence) to ensure reconstructed sig-
nals sound natural to human observers. In contrast, the dimen-
sion we introduce focuses strictly on representationalstability
(deterministic geometric invariance) for downstream machine
consumption. In fact, these two axes are often at odds: opti-
mizing for RDP’s perceptual realism actively requires models
to track high-frequency nuisances, which is precisely the mech-
anism that degrades geometric stability and causes representa-
tional shattering.

2.4. Consequences for Neural Pipelines
The conceptual framework established above suggests that an
encoder operating without stability constraints is mathemati-
cally incentivized to produce fragile latent representations. In
modern audio processing, where the encoder is often the first
stage of a larger decoupled pipeline, this instability propagates
and amplifies downstream. Incontinuous manifolds, uncon-
strained geometric drift under noise shifts the latent coordi-
nates. For downstream tasks relying on spatial locality (such
as contrastive retrieval or continuous LLM prefixes), this dis-
placement fundamentally degrades semantic matching.
2.5. Semantic Discontinuity in LLMs
When these continuous representations are discretized intodis-
crete tokensZ K
q and used as input for an Autoregressive Trans-
former (e.g., Audio-LLMs), the instability is severely amplified.
If the encoder lacks representational stability, a minor continu-
ous drift crossing a quantization boundary can cause a “token
flip”—a discrete jump in the index sequence. This vulnerability
is particularly catastrophic in modern codecs employing Resid-
ual Vector Quantization (RVQ). In an RVQ bottleneck, a geo-
metric drift that causes a boundary crossing in the first quan-
tizer layer fundamentally alters the residual vector passed to all
subsequent layers. Consequently, a single micro-perturbation
can trigger a cascading failure, replacing the entire token hi-
erarchy with an orthogonal discrete sequence. Because LLMs
are highly sensitive to sequence context, these flipped tokens
can easily divert the attention mechanism, leading to semantic
hallucinations or degraded synthesis in downstream generative
tasks.
2.6. The “Fragility-Efficiency” Trade-off
We postulate a fundamental trade-off inherent in current en-
coder design across both continuous and discrete paradigms, a
structural tension which we empirically validate through com-
prehensive perturbation experiments in Section 4:
•Efficiency-Focused (HighS): Models like EnCodec at low
bitrates maximize reconstruction fidelityDby utilizing the
full expressive capacity of the continuous bottleneck or dis-
crete codebook without explicitly imposing stability con-
straints. However, this leaves no “geometric margin” for con-
sistency, making the resulting representations highly fragile
to minor, everyday nuisance perturbations.
•Stability-Aware (LowS): By internalizing a tight stabil-
ity constraint, the encoder learns a mapping where seman-
tic clusters inX T map to reliable, invariant regions inZ K.
Mathematically, increasing this geometric margin requires
widening the V oronoi cells of the quantizer or allocating re-
dundant codebook indices to capture the same underlying se-
mantic concept across diverse acoustic conditions. While this
requires a higher bitrate to maintain the sameD, it ensures
that downstream generative models receive a highly consis-
tent and predictable signal.
Ultimately, this conceptual framework suggests a critically
necessary paradigm shift in how we evaluate neural codecs
and encoders. Rather than focusing solely on the “Rate-
Distortion” curve (reconstruction quality), we must instead con-
sider the broader, multidimensional “Stability-Rate-Distortion”
manifold. Fundamentally, an encoder is only as good as the in-
herent stability of the representations it provides to subsequent
models in the processing chain.

3.2.1. Physical Logic of CED Operations
The choice of weights and costs in CED directly reflects the
physical nature of representational drift in audio. In neural
codecs, nuisances like clock drift or micro-temporal shifts can
cause the encoder to “skip” a frame or produce an extra latent
frame. The deletion (w del) and insertion (w ins) penalties act as
the cost for these temporal elasticities.
Ifw→0, the metric becomes overly permissive, allow-
ing the representation to drift in time without penalty. Con-
versely, ifw→∞, the metric degrades into a rigidL p com-
parison. For our standard continuous evaluations, we strictly set
wins =w del = 2.0. Because the maximum possible geometric
substitution cost on the unit hypersphere is2.0, this ensures that
discarding a valid frame or hallucinating an extra frame carries a
penalty exactly equivalent to the maximum possible geometric
displacement, preventing the alignment algorithm from spuri-
ously skipping frames to artificially lower the distance.

4. Experiments and Results
We evaluate the representational stability of various audio en-
coding paradigms using the Stability-Rate-Distortion (SRD)
framework defined in Section 2. Our goal is to empirically
map the SRD manifold by measuring the divergence between
“clean” latent representations and those subjected to controlled,
stochastic acoustic nuisances.
4.1. Dataset and Nuisance Protocol
We utilize the clean studio-quality reference audio from the
Massive Sound Embedding Benchmark (MSEB) [18] Simple
V oice Questions (SVQ) dataset. To evaluate the fragility of the
latent manifold, we subject these clean samples to a frequency-
domain SpecAugment protocol [19].1
Unlike standard feature-space SpecAugment applied to
Log-Mel filterbanks, we implement an FFT-Augment-iSTFT
pipeline directly on the waveform. The audio is transformed via
STFT (N-FFT = 2048, Hop Length = 512). We then apply ran-
dom continuous masking to the linear magnitude spectrogram:
•Time Masking: Suppressing up to 20% of the temporal steps
(maximum width of 100 frames).
•Frequency Masking: Suppressing up to 15% of the fre-
quency bins (maximum width of 27 bins).
The masked spectrogram is then inverted back to the time do-
main via inverse-STFT. This linear-domain approach guaran-
tees mathematically perfect reconstruction of the unmasked re-
gions, ensuring that any representational drift is strictly a re-
action to the localized information loss, rather than processing
artifacts.
Justification of Synthetic Perturbations:We explicitly
isolate our evaluation to this stochastic masking protocol rather
than incorporating real-world additive background noise. Eval-
uating representations on real-world noise intrinsically alters
the global acoustic envelope, conflating true global signal loss
with representational drift. Furthermore, comprehensive real-
world noise evaluations across 26 languages would require
strictly parallel datasets, which are unavailable. By guaran-
teeing that the unmasked regions remain bit-perfect, our FFT-
SpecAugment pipeline strictly isolates representational drift as
anarchitectural fragilityof the quantizer.
