Abstract
Speech acquisition using through-barrier radar sensing is chal-
lenging due to severe band limitation, distortion, and inter-
ference. Conventional deep learning models struggle to cap-
ture long-range temporal dependencies and recover missing
high-frequency components. To address these challenges, we
developed the Cascaded Attention Fusion Transformer (CAF-
Former), which integrates progressive spectral expansion with
a cascaded attention hierarchy. Temporal structure is modeled
via multi-query self-attention that diversifies contextual repre-
sentations while sharing a common basis. The resulting features
are fused through a frequency-domain attention module that re-
calibrates spectral relations and models cross-frequency inter-
actions. This design enables more effective high-frequency re-
covery and achieves consistent improvements over transformer
baselines and state-of-the-art models under severe degradation.
Index Terms: Speech acquisition, radar-acquired speech,
through-wall sensing, bandwidth extension, cascaded attention
1. Introduction
Speech acquisition in real-world scenarios often faces condi-
tions where conventional microphones become ineffective, such
as in the presence of strong noise, reverberation, or physical
obstructions like walls and debris [1, 2]. These limitations
have motivated the exploration of alternative sensing modalities
that do not rely on direct acoustic propagation. Among them,
radio-frequency (RF) sensing [3–5] has attracted considerable
attention, since electromagnetic waves can penetrate barriers
and capture subtle surface vibrations induced by speech [6, 7].
In particular, frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW)
radar possesses the ability to estimate displacement by analyz-
ing the echo phase of reflected signals [8], making it possible to
recover speech even under through-barrier conditions. Despite
this capability, radar-acquired speech often exhibits poor qual-
ity: signals are easily corrupted by clutter and noise, and high-
frequency components (>1kHz) that are crucial for intelligi-
bility are severely attenuated [9]. These challenges motivate the
development of advanced learning-based approaches for radar-
acquired speech enhancement [10], including noise suppression
and reconstruction of missing spectral details.
Consequently, deep learning approaches have been ex-
plored for speech enhancement, particularly for denoising and
bandwidth extension under severe noise and band-limiting con-
ditions. Early models such as the radar acoustic network
(RANet) [11] and Wave-V oice Net [12] adopt convolutional en-
coder–decoder architectures with residual connections to sup-
press clutter and reconstruct high-frequency components. How-
ever, their reliance on local convolutional operations limits the

severely degraded regions. More recently, transformer-based
architectures, including Conformer variants [13–15], have
demonstrated strong performance by capturing global tempo-
ral context and complex spectral dynamics. Dual-path designs
such as DPTNet [16] and TF-Locoformer [17] further enhance
dependency modeling through hierarchical time–frequency at-
tention, jointly exploiting local and global structures for speech
separation and enhancement. Nevertheless, these models do not
explicitly account for the severe sensing degradation inherent to
radar modalities. To address this gap, EBENet [18] was pro-
posed for radar-acquired speech, jointly performing enhance-
ment and bandwidth extension within a unified transformer-
based framework by incorporating temporal context to improve
high-frequency spectral consistency. Despite this, conventional
multi-head self-attention (MHSA) remains suboptimal under
severely degraded radar sensing conditions. Specifically, con-
catenated attention heads are linearly projected [19], causing
individual heads to fragment towards noise-dominated or cor-
rupted sub-bands, particularly in high-frequency regions with
extremely low SNR, as also observed in MHSA-based enhance-
ment frameworks [17, 18]. This dilutes informative cues and
limits cross-band dependency modeling during reconstruction.
In this work, we propose a sensing-aware framework for
through-wall radar speech enhancement, tailored to address se-
vere modality-induced degradation. Building upon this for-
mulation, we introduce the Cascaded Attention Fusion Trans-
former (CAF-Former), a transformer-based instantiation of this
sensing-aware framework. To address the severe spectral degra-
dation inherent to radar sensing, the model adopts a progres-
sive reconstruction strategy that gradually expands reliable low-
frequency representations toward full-band recovery. Within
each progressive layer, we reformulate the attention process
into a cascaded structure that sequentially models temporal de-
pendencies and inter-frequency relations. In the first stage, a
temporal multi-query attention mechanism (TMQA) employs
parallel query projections with shared keys and values, pro-
ducing diverse temporal attention maps under a unified spec-
tral basis. While this shared key–value formulation is sim-
ilar to multi-query attention (MQA) [20], TMQA is specifi-
cally tailored for temporal modeling, enabling the capture of
complementary temporal patterns critical for radar speech re-
covery. Compared with conventional MHSA [19], the pro-
posed TMQA alleviates head fragmentation under degraded
radar conditions. In the second stage, these representations
are fused through a frequency-attention fusion (FAF) module
to explicitly model cross-frequency interactions and integrate
complementary query outputs. Unlike linear aggregation in
MHSA [19], FAF performs attention-driven spectral recalibra-

2.2. Temporal Multi-Query Attention

only a small subset of low-frequency bins contains useful in-
formation, a single projection within each head cannot ade-
quately capture the temporal dependencies conditioned on spec-
tral structure. To address this, we employ several independently
parameterized queries that attend to a shared set of keys and val-
ues. This concentrates representational capacity while encour-
aging complementary temporal views over a common spectral
basis, thereby mitigating the head fragmentation typical of con-
ventional multi-head attention.

3. Simulation and Experiment Results
We conducted experiments on speech data collected using a cus-
tom radar sensing platform. Specifically, a custom-designed
FMCW system operating at 5.31 GHz with a 40 MHz band-
width was employed. The transmitted FMCW signal was radi-
ated through a horn antenna, and echoes from the vibrating sur-
face were received by another horn antenna. A speech signal,
played through an ASX10108-SPD-R PUI Audio sound exciter
with a thin metal plate diaphragm, served as the vibrating sound
source. To capture speech through a barrier, the radar was po-
sitioned on one side of a 15 cm concrete wall, while the sound
exciter was placed on the opposite side, as shown in Fig. 2.
Figure 2:Through-wall data collection setup. The 5.31 GHz
FMCW radar is positioned facing a 15 cm concrete wall, with
the sound exciter placed on the other side.
LibriSpeech [26] was used as the clean source speech cor-
pus for both recorded and simulated data. All signals were re-
sampled to 8 kHz and segmented into 4 s utterances. For sim-
ulation, each utterance was first convolved with an impulse re-
sponse estimated from the data recorded using the custom radar,
after which additive noise was added to achieve uniformly dis-
tributed SNR levels of{−5,0,5,10,15}dB. These simulated
data were used for training and validation, amounting to ap-
proximately 312 hours of training data and about 6 hours of
validation data. Testing was performed on both simulated data
and real recordings collected using the radar platform.
To compute the STFT coefficients, each 4 s speech utter-
ance sampled at 8 kHz was segmented into 512-sample frames
with 75% overlap. The frames were then windowed using a
Hamming window before a 512-point FFT was applied. Due to
the complex conjugate symmetry of the FFT, only the first 257
frequency bins were retained. At the network input, only the
lowest 50 frequency bins were retained (i.e., 0 Hz to 765.6 Hz
(inclusive)). The model employedK= 10progressive layers,
with the first eight layers expanding twenty bins each, followed
by expansions of fifteen and twelve bins in the final two lay-
ers. Each temporal attention module employsN q = 8queries
to generate diverse attention maps, while the hidden dimension
in the frequency-fusion block was set tod k = 64. Training
was performed with the Adam optimizer at a learning rate of
1×10 −4 for up to 50 epochs, and the best-performing model
on the validation set was subsequently evaluated on an unseen
test set comprising both simulated and radar-recorded data un-
der through-wall conditions.
We compare the proposed model against established base-
lines, including Wave-V oice Net [12], RANet [11], TF-
Locoformer [17], and EBENet [18]. Consistent with prevalent
practices in time-frequency domain speech enhancement [27],
we maintain the noisy STFT phase of the input signals for wave-
form reconstruction. This design choice enables the model to
focus its capacity entirely on spectral magnitude restoration,
which serves as one of the primary determinants of speech in-
telligibility. To optimize this target, we adopted the log-spectral
amplitude distance as our loss function.

4. Conclusion
We introduced the CAF-Former, specifically designed for
the enhancement of speech captured through a concrete bar-
rier using a 5.31 GHz radar. By integrating temporal multi-
query attention with frequency-aware fusion mechanisms, the
model effectively suppresses clutter noise while restoring high-
frequency harmonics essential for intelligibility. Experimental
results on simulated and recorded data show consistent gains
over state-of-the-art baselines, and ablations confirm the com-
plementary roles of its modules. The results demonstrate the ef-
fectiveness of cascaded attention for robust speech recovery and
highlight its potential for broader non-acoustic applications. Fu-
ture studies will investigate performance on human recordings.
