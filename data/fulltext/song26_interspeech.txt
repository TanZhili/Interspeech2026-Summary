flows for voice conversion

Abstract
V oice conversion (VC) aims to transfer the timbre of a tar-
get speaker while preserving the content of a source speech. A
key challenge, especially in non-parallel settings, is the train-
test mismatch: during training, models often learn from utter-
ances where content and timbre are from the same speaker, hin-
dering their ability to disentangle these factors effectively dur-
ing inference. In this work, we propose CFLOW-VC, an un-
supervised cycle training strategy based on normalizing flows,
built upon the end-to-end VITS framework for high-fidelity
waveform generation. We introduce a mel-style encoder to cap-
ture expressive style features and employ data augmentation to
improve robustness in noisy environments. Experimental re-
sults demonstrate that CFLOW-VC outperforms state-of-the-art
baselines in both objective and subjective evaluations. Audio
samples are publicly available1.
Index Terms: speech recognition, human-computer interac-
tion, computational paralinguistics
1. Introduction
V oice conversion [1] is a technique that transforms the voice of
the source to match the timbre of the target while preserving
the content of the original speech. Traditional voice conversion
methods[2][3] typically decompose the speech signal into
multiple parts. These separated parts are then recombined to
generate a new speech waveform. During this process, one
of the functions of the model is to effectively decouple these
features to ensure that the reconstructed speech maintains high
naturalness. FaCodc [4] divides speech into four components:
content, prosody, acoustic details, and timbre. However, all
four components are derived from the same latent variable,
which places high demands on the model’s ability to decouple
them. STREAMVC[5] and Diff-hierVC[6] enhance model
expressiveness by incorporating whitened pitch. However,
pitch and timbre are difficult to separate, the synthesized speech
still faces issues of unnaturalness.
EAD-VC[7] employs data augmentation by adjusting pitch
or rhythm to disentangle content and timbre. This exposes
the model to diverse content-timbre combinations, enhancing
its disentanglement capability. However, significant pitch
or rhythm changes can affect voice authenticity, whereas
minor changes may inadequately differentiate timbres. So
researchers have focused on non-parallel voice conversion,
enabling many-to-many and any-to-any transformation tasks.
Drawing inspiration from image style transfer techniques
in computer vision, various methods leveraging generative

their training instability and mode collapse issues, which can

introduced an unsupervised, non-parallel many-to-many voice

straints and a non-end-to-end pipeline often result in converted
speech with audible artifacts and reduced naturalness.
Our method builds on FreeVC [14] , an end-to-end, text-free
voice conversion framework based on VITS[15] framework,
the end-to-end framework of VITS effectively ensures the
converted audio quality. FreeVC uses WavLM [16] to ex-
tract self-supervised learning(SSL) features from waveform.
NeuCoSVC[17] and KNNVC[18] suggest that the features
from the final layer of WavLM are rich in content information
but lack timbre details. However, FreeVC fails to adequately
sample the range of potential content-timbre combinations
during training, leading to poor generalization when confronted
with novel combinations.
In the FreeVC framework, the flow’s forward pass converts
the timbre-rich posterior distribution into a simpler prior
distribution. Conversely, the flow’s inverse pass infuses timbre
information back into the prior distribution. By utilizing the
flow’s invertibility, we can modify timbre without compromis-
ing the semantic content, avoiding unnecessary distortions.
Based on this principle, we developed a cycle training strategy
(CTS) using the flow model’s reversibility and the consistency
loss from StarGANv2-VC. Additionally, to further improve
timbre similarity and expressiveness, we adopted Mel-Style
encoder[19] to extract style information. The effectiveness of
Mel-Style encoder in extracting global style features has been
validated[20]. Furthermore, we use WavAugmentation[21] to
enhance the model’s noise robustness. The experimental results
demonstrate that we successfully achieved an effective fusion
of the input source audio’s content and style with the target
audio’s timbre.

testing has a significant impact on the outcomes. Our solution
includes four main innovations:
• To the best of our knowledge, this is among the first works to
integrate normalizing flows into a StarGAN-style adversarial
training framework with explicit cycle consistency, enabling
improved disentanglement and stability in voice conversion.
• We explicitly model the prior distribution in our flow-based
architecture to represent content information, while the pos-
terior distribution captures the acoustic features. This explicit

2. MODEL
Fig1(a) illustrates the CFLOW-VC model framework, which
builds upon the widely used FreeVC structure. Similar to
FreeVC, CFLOW-VC uses WavLM as the input for the prior en-
coder and uses a pre-trained speaker encoder to extract timbre
information. We also introduce a style encoder to capture style-
related features. Additionally, we implement a non-parallel
training strategy that takes advantage of Flow’s invertibility
along with the loss function from StarGANv2-VC. To further
enhance noise robustness, we employ Wav-Augmentation[21]
to generate training data with added reverberation and noise,
and we use a gradient reversal layer(GRL)[22] for speaker clas-
sification to decouple the output of the prior encoder from the
timbre.

Style encoder uses Mel-Style encoder[19] to extract style infor-

Star discriminator maintains the same structure as the discrim-

that learn the common features between real and fake samples

2.2. Data augmentation
Traditional voice conversion models predominantly rely on
clean audio data for training. However, in real-world appli-
cation scenarios, audio inputs often inevitably contain noise
and reverberation, which significantly deviate from the ideal-
ized conditions of the training data. To address this problem,
we use data augmentation. First, we use the RIRs 2 to perform
noise augmentation by direct addition. Subsequently, we intro-
duce random reverberation augmentations. All these enhance-
ments are done using the open-source tool WavAugment[21].
3. Experiment
We utilize the VCTK dataset for training, which comprises 109
speakers with each contributing 400 audio samples. All au-
dio samples are downsampled to 16 kHz. Linear spectrograms
and 80-band mel-spectrograms are calculated using short-time
Fourier transform. The FFT, window, and hop size are set to
1280, 1280, and 320.
3.1. Training strategy
First, we obtain data-augmented data by randomly generating 5
augmented audio clips from each original audio. We then train
the model using a mix of the original and augmented audio data.
The training process consists of two steps:
Pre-train:We pre-train the backbone for 500k steps on four
NVIDIA 4090 GPUs using a batch size of 64.Training hyperpa-
rameters align with the FreeVC configuration, specifically with
respect to the learning rate and associated optimization settings.
2http://openslr.org/28/
CTS:We train the model further with CTS for 200k steps us-
ing the pre-trained model as initialization. During this stage, we
freeze the parameters of the posterior encoder and decoder.
3.2. Evaluation Metrics
We use both objective and subjective metrics to evaluate the
voice conversion capability of CFLOW-VC and compare it with
baselines.
Objective Metrics:We evaluate speaker-similarity(SIM),
speech quality (UTMOS), and robustness (WER). For speaker-
similarity, we employ eres2net[27] to extract the voiceprint fea-
tures and calculate the cosine distance as the Similarity Index.
For speech quality, we employ UTMOS 3 which is a surrogate
objective metric of MOS. For robustness, we compute the Word
Error Rate between the text transcriptions of the source and the
converted speech, using an ASR model Paraformer-en[28].
Subjective Metrics: To evaluate the naturalness and expres-
siveness of our comparison models, we conducted Mean Opin-
ion Score (MOS) tests with 30 listeners. MOS was assessed
based on intelligibility, prosody, and sound quality, with higher
scores indicating more human-like speech.
Baselines: We use four baseline models: Diff-HierVC[6],
DiffVC[29], StarGANv2-VC[13] and FreeVC[14].DiffVC and
Diff-HierVC, representing notable diffusion-based voice con-
version models, have been extensively employed in the field.
Meanwhile, StarGANv2-VC and FreeVC serve as the primary
baseline methods for comparative analysis in this research.
These models were all trained using the publicly available
VCTK dataset. Detailed information on the training procedures,
including specifics like training steps and batch size, can be
found in their respective open-source repositories.
For the ablation study, we used three configurations: without
CTS(w.o CTS); without Data-Augmentation(w.o dau); without
Style-Encoder(w.o se). We randomly selected 5 male and 5 fe-
male target speakers from the VCTK dataset, and randomly se-
lected data from 3 male and 3 female speakers in the test-clean
subset of Libritts as input sources to evaluate both subjective
and objective metrics. To simulate a real environment, we addi-
tionally introduced two test sets:
1. Noisy data, generated by adding noise and reverberation to
the test-clean subset to simulate real-world conditions;
2. Accent data, using data from the SAR 4 dataset that con-
tains audio recordings of speakers from different countries and
regions saying the same sentence.

4.2. Subjective metrics
From Table 2,our experimental results indicate that CFLOW-
VC consistently achieves higher MOS compared to the base-
line model. Specifically, the MOS for CFLOW-VC surpassed
that of the baseline by a statistically significant margin, un-
derscoring its superior ability to enhance speech naturalness.
This improvement can be attributed to CFLOW-VC’s advanced
flow-based architecture, which effectively models the complex
distribution of natural speech, leading to more realistic and
fluid voice conversions.Furthermore, our proposed CFLOW-
VC demonstrated exceptional performance on both theNoise
andAccentdatasets. In theNoisedataset, which includes
various real-world background noises, CFLOW-VC maintained
high speech quality and intelligibility. This robustness in noisy
environments highlights CFLOW-VC’s potential for practical
applications where background interference is commonplace.
Similarly, on theAccentdataset, which encompasses a di-
verse range of regional accents, CFLOW-VC effectively pre-
serves the speaker’s unique vocal characteristics while ensuring
high naturalness. The ability to handle diverse accents with-
out compromising quality demonstrates CFLOW-VC’s versa-
tility and adaptability to different linguistic variations. These
findings collectively illustrate that CFLOW-VC not only en-
hances speech naturalness but also exhibits strong generaliza-
tion capabilities across various challenging real-world scenar-
ios. By addressing both environmental noise and accent vari-
ability, CFLOW-VC demonstrates strong performance com-
pared to existing methods, paving the way for more reliable and
high-quality applications.
4.3. Ablation Study
From Table 1, it is evident that without CTS, simply adding
Style-Encoder to FreeVC results in poorer performance com-
pared to FreeVC itself. The performance drop without CTS un-
derscores its critical role in facilitating the disentanglement of
style and timbre, which the baseline architecture alone cannot
achieve effectively. Without Style-Encoder, although The WER
has decreased, UTMOS and SIM have also seen a noticeable
decline. This indicates that combining the Style-Encoder with
CTS can effectively enhance timbre similarity and speech ex-
pressiveness. Furthermore, the data augmentation scheme im-
proves the model’s robustness against noise and significantly
enhances audio quality.
5. Conclusion
This paper introduces CFLOW-VC, a novel unsupervised cy-
cle training strategy leveraging normalizing flows for the task
of voice conversion. Unlike traditional supervised approaches,
CFLOW-VC eliminates the need for parallel datasets by uti-
lizing a cycle consistency framework, which ensures that the
converted voice can be accurately mapped back to the origi-
nal source. The integration of normalizing flows enhances the
model’s ability to capture complex data distributions, thereby
facilitating high-fidelity voice transformation.
Comprehensive experimental evaluations demonstrate that
the proposed CFLOW-VC model significantly enhances audio
expressiveness and maintains high speaker similarity compared
to existing state-of-the-art methods. Quantitative metrics cor-
roborate the superior performance of CFLOW-VC in producing
natural and speaker-consistent voice conversions.
Future work will explore scaling the model to larger
datasets, extending the framework to singing voice conversion,
and investigating the integration of more powerful pre-trained
models for content and style encoding.
