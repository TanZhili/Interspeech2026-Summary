Bridging the Distribution Gap in Real-World Far-Field Speech Enhancement
via Lightweight Latent Representation Alignment
Biao Liu1,2, Haoyuan Xie1,2, Zengqiang Shang1,∗∗, Mou Wang3, Xin Liu3, Pengyuan Zhang1,2
1 Laboratory of Speech and Intelligent Information Processing, Institute of Acoustics, CAS, China

Abstract
Although speech enhancement models have achieved notable
progress, their performance degrades in real-world far-field sce-
narios due to the distribution gap between simulated and real
data. To address this problem, we introduce a high-quality
real-world dataset. We further propose a lightweight two-stage
framework that bridges the distribution gap through latent rep-
resentation alignment. In the first stage, a pretrained denoising
model performs initial noise and reverberation reduction. In
the second stage, a distribution mapping module aligns first-
stage features to the latent space learned from clean near-field
speech, and the decoder reconstructs high-fidelity speech. Ex-
periments on real-world test sets demonstrate that our method
effectively suppresses noise while preserving speech structure
with only 0.8 GMAC/s computational cost. It achieves 2.94 in
P.835 OVRL and 3.35 in P.808, outperforming strong baselines
in real-world far-field conditions.
Index Terms: real-world speech enhancement, far-field speech,
latent representation alignment, lightweight model
1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) aims to extract clean speech from
noisy observations by effectively suppressing background noise
and reverberation [1, 2], thereby improving speech quality and
intelligibility. It plays a crucial role in a wide range of speech-
driven applications, including voice interaction, teleconferenc-
ing, and smart devices [3, 4]. In recent years, deep learn-
ing–based approaches have been widely adopted for SE and
have demonstrated substantial performance gains over tradi-
tional signal processing techniques [5, 6, 7].
In recent years, numerous impressive performance DNN-
based SE methods have emerged [8, 9]. Early deep learning
approaches primarily focused on refining time–frequency rep-
resentations through full-band and sub-band modeling. For
example, FullSubNet [10] adopts a cascade of full-band and
sub-band networks, enabling the model to capture global con-
textual dependencies while preserving the ability to model lo-
cal spectral stationarity. Building upon increasingly sophis-
ticated spectro-temporal interactions, TF-GridNet [11] intro-
duces stacked multi-path processing blocks that integrate intra-
frame spectral modeling, sub-band temporal modeling, and full-
band self-attention mechanisms, thereby jointly exploiting local
and global time–frequency information. More recently, genera-
tive modeling paradigms have been introduced to tackle speech
enhancement from a substantially different perspective. In par-
ticular, diffusion-based approaches [12, 13] decompose the en-
hancement process into multi-step iterative refinement, progres-

sively reconstructing clean speech from noisy observations to
address highly complex degradation patterns. While these ad-
vanced designs significantly improve modeling capacity, they
typically rely on large-scale architectures to capture intricate
spectro-temporal dependencies, leading to computational re-
quirements ranging from several to tens of GMACs per sec-
ond. To facilitate deployment on resource-constrained devices,
lightweight SE models have also been proposed. For instance,
RNNoise [14] adopts a hybrid DSP and deep learning frame-
work, where a compact recurrent network estimates critical-
band gains while a pitch filter suppresses inter-harmonic noise,
enabling real-time processing on low-power CPUs. LiSenNet
[15] introduces sub-band downsampling and upsampling blocks
together with a dual-path recurrent module to capture band-
aware and temporal features, and further incorporates a noise
detector for adaptive low-cost processing. GTCRN [16] simpli-
fies the DPCRN [7] architecture through grouped strategies and
integrates sub-band feature extraction with temporal recurrent
attention, achieving competitive performance with only tens of
thousands of parameters and ultra-low computational complex-
ity in practice.
However, real-world far-field speech enhancement remains
particularly challenging and important [17, 18, 19]. In practical
acoustic environments, speech signals suffer from severe mid-
frequency and high-frequency attenuation, and the actual sound
propagation characteristics differ substantially from simulated
room impulse responses commonly used in training. A straight-
forward strategy is to incorporate real recordings into model
training. Nevertheless, due to the significant distribution gap be-
tween far-field degraded speech and clean near-field speech, di-
rectly learning this complex mapping with a lightweight single-
stage network is often insufficient, resulting in noticeable degra-
dation.
To address this challenge [20, 21], we first construct a
high-quality real-world dataset comprising 70 hours of paired
near-field and far-field recordings across diverse acoustic sce-
narios. We then propose a lightweight latent representation
alignment framework to bridge the distribution gap without re-
lying on large-capacity models. Specifically, an autoencoder
is trained to learn a compact latent space of clean near-field
speech. Given an input far-field signal, a pretrained denois-
ing model first performs preliminary noise and reverberation
reduction. A lightweight distribution mapping module sub-
sequently projects the preliminarily enhanced speech into the
learned clean latent space, and the decoder reconstructs the en-
hanced speech waveform. Experimental results demonstrate
that the proposed framework consistently improves perceptual
quality under a computational budget of only 0.8 GMAC/s, out-
performing baselines in real-world far-field conditions.

2. Methodology
The overview of the proposed framework is illustrated in Fig-
ure 1. The model follows a lightweight two-stage design to
bridge the distribution gap between real-world far-field speech
and clean near-field speech.
Given a far-field noisy input signal, we first compute its
short-time Fourier transform (STFT) as input features. A pre-
trained denoising model then performs preliminary noise and
reverberation reduction, producing a preliminarily enhanced
speech. Instead of directly mapping this representation to clean
speech in the spectral domain, the second stage aligns it to a
latent space learned from clean near-field speech. Specifically,
an autoencoder is introduced to learn a compact latent repre-
sentation of clean near-field speech during training. During in-
ference, a lightweight distribution mapping module projects the
first-stage output into this latent space. The decoder then recon-
structs the enhanced speech waveform from the aligned latent
representation. By decomposing the enhancement process into
coarse denoising and latent alignment, the framework avoids re-
lying on a single high-capacity network while maintaining com-
putational efficiency suitable for real-time applications. The de-
tailed optimization strategy is described in Section 2.3
2.1. Latent space learning

we introduce an autoencoder that maps waveform signals into
a compact latent space and reconstructs the waveform from the
latent representatation.

2.3. Training Strategy
The proposed framework is trained in a staged manner to ensure
stable latent space learning and effective distribution alignment.
The GTCRN denoising model is pretrained in advance and
remains frozen throughout the entire training process. Dur-
ing the first training stage, the encoder, decoder, and distribu-
tion mapping module are jointly optimized. The objective is
7081
twofold: (1) the autoencoder learns to reconstruct clean near-
field speech with high fidelity, and (2) the distribution mapping
module learns to project preliminarily enhanced far-field speech
into the clean latent space under the latent alignment constraint.
At convergence, the autoencoder is capable of accurately recon-
structing clean speech, and the mapped far-field representations
approach the clean latent distribution.
After this stage converges, we proceed to a second train-
ing stage to further improve enhancement performance. In this
stage, the encoder is frozen, and only the distribution map-
ping module and the decoder are jointly fine-tuned. By fix-
ing the encoder, the latent space structure remains stable, and
the optimization focuses on refining the transformation from
far-field speech to clean latent representations and their wave-
form reconstruction. This strategy reduces optimization diffi-
culty and prevents unnecessary distortion of the learned clean
latent space. Through this staged training procedure, the frame-
work achieves effective distribution alignment and high-quality
speech enhancement.
3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
We use the DNS Challenge 3 dataset [25] to construct simulated
training data. The dataset provides clean speech, diverse noise
recordings, and a large collection of room impulse responses
(RIRs). The clean subset includes Mandarin speech, singing
voice, and emotional speech, covering diverse speaking styles.
The noise set contains over 40,000 real-world noise clips, and
more than 112,000 RIRs are available for reverberation simu-
lation. To generate training pairs, each clean utterance is con-
volved with a randomly selected RIR and mixed with a noise
clip at an SNR randomly sampled from -5 dB to 15 dB. In total,
approximately 600 hours of noisy-clean pairs are constructed.
Following standard practice, only the direct sound and the first
100 ms of early reflections are retained when generating the
training targets. The simulated data are used exclusively for
model training.
In addition to simulated training data, we construct a real-
world extreme far-field dataset for model training and evalu-
ation. The dataset contains 70 hours of paired near-field and
far-field speech recordings collected in 10 diverse real acoustic
environments. Speech content is derived from the AISHELL-3
corpus [26]. To ensure speaker independence, the AISHELL-3
training set is re-partitioned into new training and testing sub-
sets according to speaker identity. For each scenario and each
distance, approximately 2 hours of speech from the training
subset are recorded to construct the paired training data, re-
sulting in 70 hours in total. In addition, about 20 minutes of
speech per scenario are recorded from the testing subset, form-
ing a dedicated real-world test set containing 900 far-field utter-
ances. The speakers used for testing are strictly disjoint from
those in the training set.
Recordings are conducted using a synchronized dual-
channel system. The near-field speech is captured by an Audio-
Technica AT4050 condenser microphone placed 5 cm from the
speaker, while the far-field speech is recorded using a DPA
4060-OC-C-B00 omnidirectional miniature microphone posi-
tioned at distances of 4 m, 6 m, and 8 m. The microphones
are aligned at the same height to maintain spatial consistency.
All recordings are sampled at 48 kHz, and resampled to 16 kHz
in the experiment. The 10 recording environments include in-
door, semi-outdoor, and outdoor scenarios with varying spatial

Due to acoustic propagation delay, the far-field recordings
exhibit relative time shifts with respect to the near-field refer-
ence. To obtain accurate supervision pairs, we perform cross-
correlation-based delay estimation for temporal alignment. The
leading frames of the far-field signal are trimmed according to
the estimated delay, and the corresponding frames at the end of
the near-field signal are removed to preserve temporal consis-
tency.
3.2. Implementation details
We perform STFT using a Hanning window with a length of 512
(32ms) and a hop size of 256 (16ms), and an FFT length of 512.
We adopt GTCRN as the denoising model. The overall archi-
tecture remains consistent with the original design. To improve
the performance within our framework, we increase the chan-
nel dimension of all convolutional and GT-Conv blocks from
16 to 48. Specifically, the encoder consists of two convolutional
blocks with kernel size (1, 5) and stride (1, 2), where the second
convolution uses group convolution with group size 2, followed
by three GT-Conv blocks with kernel size (3, 3) and time dila-
tion factors of 1, 2, and 5, respectively. The decoder mirrors the
encoder structure using corresponding transposed convolution
layers for reconstruction. The encoder of the codec is adapted
from the DAC encoder architecture. It follows a multi-stage 1D
convolutional design with residual units and progressive tempo-
ral downsampling. In our implementation, the encoder is con-
figured with an initial channel dimension of 96, downsampling
rates of (4, 4, 4, 4), and a latent dimension of 257.
Both the distribution mapping module and the decoder are
implemented using a 1D adaptation of ConvNeXt. The archi-
tecture consists of stacked ConvNeXt blocks with depthwise
convolution, LayerNorm, and pointwise feed-forward layers,
combined with residual connections. In our implementation,
the number of channels is set to 256 and the number of Con-
vNeXt blocks is 4. Causal convolution is adopted by applying
left padding before depthwise convolution to ensure temporal
causality. Separate branches are used for magnitude and phase
modeling, followed by linear projection layers for spectral re-
construction.

with a Noam learning rate schedule, where the warm-up steps

3.3. Comparison with the baselines
We evaluate the proposed method with GTCRN, LiSenNet, and
causal TF-GridNet as baseline models. All models are assessed
using DNSMOS metrics based on ITU-T Recommendations
P.808 and P.835 [27, 28], providing a comprehensive evaluation
of perceptual speech quality. To analyze the impact of training
data, the baseline models are trained under two settings. In the
first setting, they are trained on the simulated DNS Challenge
3 dataset, which follows the common training procedure used
in previous work. In the second setting, the same models are
trained on our recorded real-world dataset to ensure a fair com-
parison under identical data conditions. The proposed method
is trained only on the real-world dataset since it is designed for
real far-field speech enhancement. All models are evaluated on
the real-world far-field test set described in Section 3.1.
Table 2 reports the objective evaluation results of the pro-
posed method and the baseline models using DNSMOS P.835
(SIG, BAK, OVRL) and P.808 metrics. As shown in the ta-
ble, TF-GridNet shows noticeable improvements after being
trained on the real-world dataset. In contrast, the lightweight
models GTCRN and LiSenNet do not benefit from training
on real data. Instead, several metrics slightly decrease com-
pared with the models trained on simulated data. This observa-
tion suggests that, for lightweight architectures, directly learn-
ing the mapping from complex real-world far-field speech to
clean speech remains challenging. In comparison, the proposed
method achieves the best performance across all metrics while
maintaining lower computational cost. These results indicate
that the proposed framework effectively reduces the difficulty
of the enhancement task through latent distribution alignment.
Figure 2 shows the STFT spectrograms of enhanced speech
for a representative test sample. Compared with baseline mod-
els, the proposed method produces clearer harmonic structures
and more complete spectral details while suppressing back-
ground noise, resulting in improved speech clarity and overall
quality.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a solution for real-world far-field
speech enhancement. To this end, we construct a high-quality
real-world dataset with paired near-field and far-field recordings
across diverse acoustic environments. We propose a lightweight
two-stage framework that bridges this gap through latent repre-
sentation alignment, where a pretrained denoising model first
performs initial noise and reverberation reduction, a distribu-
tion mapping module then projects the intermediate features
into the latent space learned from clean near-field speech, and a
decoder reconstructs the high-fidelity speech. Experiments on
the real-world far-field test set show that the proposed method
consistently outperforms strong baselines on DNSMOS P.835
and P.808 metrics while maintaining low computational cost,
demonstrating that latent distribution alignment is an effective
and efficient solution for practical far-field speech enhance-
ment.
