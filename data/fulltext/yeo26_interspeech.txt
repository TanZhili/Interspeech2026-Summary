Abstract
While Conformer-Transducers offer state-of-the-art ASR per-
formance, their excessive memory footprints create a bottle-
neck for on-device deployment. Conventional integer quanti-
zation is fundamentally constrained by a discrete grid, impos-
ing a structural lower bound of 1 bit per parameter. To address
this challenge, we propose LittleASR, a mixed-precision frame-
work based on variable-rank binary decomposition. Guided
by a gradient-aware sensitivity metric, LittleASR identifies and
compresses non-critical layers to the sub-1-bit regime, pushing
the limits of compression beyond the boundaries of standard
quantization while maintaining a favorable trade-off between
extreme memory reduction and recognition accuracy.
Index Terms: speech recognition, mixed-precision quantiza-
tion, sub-1-bit quantization
1. Introduction
End-to-end Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), particularly
the Conformer-Transducer [1, 2], has established new bench-
marks in accuracy by combining the local feature extraction
of CNNs with the global context modeling of Transformers
[3, 4, 5, 6]. However, these models typically contain over 100
million parameters, creating a bottleneck for on-device infer-
ence where memory bandwidth and power consumption are
strict constraints [7].
A critical challenge in Transducer compression is the un-
even sensitivity of its components. While the Encoder acts as
a robust feature extractor, the Predictor (Decoder) is autore-
gressive and highly susceptible to approximation errors. Con-
sequently, prior works [8, 9, 10] typically adopt a conserva-
tive mixed-precision strategy: aggressively compressing the En-
coder (e.g., 1–4 bits) while maintaining the sensitive Decoder at
high precision (e.g., 4–16 bits) to prevent context collapse.
However, this strategy faces a fundamental compression
deadlock in extreme on-device scenarios. Since the Decoder
requires a high-precision budget to maintain stability, the only
mathematical path to further reduce the total model footprint
is to compress the Encoder beyond standard limits. Never-
theless, existing integer-based quantization methods are strictly
bounded by a discrete grid, imposing a structural hard floor at
1-bit per weight. This makes the sub-1-bit regime inaccessi-
ble and leaves the redundancy of the Conformer Encoder un-
exploited, so even state-of-the-art 1-bit techniques [8] cannot
compress further once this limit is reached.
To resolve this deadlock, we propose LittleASR, a mixed-
precision framework utilizing LittleBit [11], a binary low-rank

1.Fine-Grained Mixed-Precision Framework:We demon-
strate that rank-adaptive binary decomposition allows for
quasi-continuous bit allocation, enabling fine-grained control
unavailable in discrete quantization schemes.
2.Gradient-Aware Allocation:By leveraging gradient-guided

parameter-heavy pointwise convolutions, LSTM projections,
and linear layers for extreme compression, while Conv2d lay-
ers are quantized to 4-bit via Round-to-Nearest (RTN) to fur-

3.2. Main Results
Table 1 compares the performance of LittleASR against stan-
dard quantization baselines. To rigorously evaluate our frame-
work, we investigate two allocation strategies: uniform rank
(fixed effective bit-width across all target layers) and gradient-
aware mixed rank (sensitivity-guided optimization).
Standard methods such as RTN and AbsMean perform ro-
bustly within their operational range (e.g., 4-bit or 1-bit) un-
der QAT, but are strictly bounded by the discrete integer grid
1741
(w∈ {−1,+1}). This 1-bit hard floor makes it impossible
to reach extreme compression scenarios (e.g.,<10MB) where
fractional bit-widths are required.
In contrast, LittleASR overcomes this discretization bot-
tleneck by reparameterizing quantization as a continuous rank
optimization problem. For instance, the uniform rank config-
uration at 0.8 bpw achieves 6.93% WER on dev-other, outper-
forming the INT2 RTN baseline (7.08%) despite a 2×reduc-
tion in model size (33.0MB→15.8MB). This demonstrates
the intrinsic efficiency of our binary decomposition into the
sub-1-bit regime. Furthermore, the gradient-aware mixed rank
strategy significantly extends the Pareto frontier by aggressively
diverting the bit budget from the redundant Encoder to rein-
force the sensitive Decoder. Notably, at 1.0 bpw, the mixed
strategy achieves a WER of 6.01% on dev-other, outperform-
ing the Tensor-wise AbsMean baseline (6.30%). Crucially,
our variable-rank approach extends compression to 7.1MB (0.2
bpw), a regime where integer baselines physically cannot oper-
ate.
3.3. Analysis of Bit Allocation Strategy
Figure 4 visualizes the layer-wise sensitivity and correspond-
ing bit-width allocation derived by LittleASR, revealing a clear
structural hierarchy exploited by our optimizer. Globally, a
sharp contrast exists between the acoustic encoder and predic-
tion network. Early Encoder layers are aggressively compressed
(0.3–0.4 bpw) due to their low sensitivity to quantization noise.
Conversely, the prediction network retains high precision: the
Joint Network receives≈2.0 bpw, and the Decoder LSTM is al-
located 1.25 bpw. Because we concatenate the four gate weights
(Wii,W if,W ig,W io) for decomposition, this 1.25 bpw effec-
tively represents the structural full-rank limit. This full-capacity
assignment confirms the autoregressive core allows minimal
compression compared to the robust encoder.
Within the Encoder, a depth-wise progression is evident: al-
located rank gradually increases as the network deepens (Layer
0:∼0.3 bpw→Layer 16:∼1.5 bpw). This correlates with the
speech processing hierarchy, where deeper layers encoding ab-
stract semantic information necessitate higher fidelity than shal-
low layers processing raw spectra.
Finally, our method captures micro-level granularity. In
Self-Attention layers (e.g., Layer 10), the Value projection (Wv)
consistently receives significantly higher precision (≈1.9 bpw)
than Key/Query projections (W k,W q≈0.1–0.8 bpw). Func-
tionally, this suggests that while routing logic (W k,W q) is
robust to approximation, the content payload (W v) demands
higher precision to maintain representation quality.
3.4. Ablation Study: Impact of Decoder Fidelity
To isolate the impact of component-wise precision, we con-
ducted a controlled experiment fixing the total model size to
10MB (0.4 bpw). We compare three allocation strategies with
varying precision distributions between the Encoder and De-
coder. As shown in Table 2, the inverse priority configuration
consistently underperforms the uniform baseline, exhibiting a
noticeable degradation in WER. This performance gap confirms
a strong sensitivity asymmetry: the Conformer-Transducer is
critically dependent on the Decoder’s fidelity. Consequently,
effective compression requires exploiting the encoder’s redun-
dancy to allocate sufficient budget to the decoder, whereas the
reverse strategy proves to be structurally inefficient.

To analyze the hardware efficiency of LittleASR, we compare
the theoretical arithmetic cost of a Conformer-Large FFN layer

plications with lightweight sign-bit operations (BOPs). Since
BOPs incur negligible hardware cost [23], the primary effi-
ciency gain comes from the drastic reduction in FP16 operations
(2.10M→0.52M). We note that these figures reflect theoret-
ical operation counts rather than measured on-device latency,
throughput, or power; a full hardware-level evaluation is left to
future work.

4. Conclusion
We proposed LittleASR to overcome the structural 1-bit com-
pression limit of Conformer-Transducers. By leveraging contin-
uous binary low-rank decomposition guided by gradient-aware
sensitivity, our method unlocks the sub-1-bit regime inacces-
sible to rigid integer quantization, financing the sensitive De-
coder’s fidelity through aggressive Encoder compression. Lit-
tleASR maintains stable recognition on LibriSpeech even at ex-
treme sub-1-bit levels, offering a compact solution for memory-
constrained edge devices.
