Abstract
We present an efficient visual knowledge distillation (VKD)
strategy to address the causal audiovisual speaker separation
(A VSS) problem. Causal A VSS systems suffer from significant
performance degradation due to the absence of the future con-
text and the reliance on heavily parameterized visual embedding
models originally developed non-causally. VKD progressively
transfers knowledge from a causally inferred pre-trained visual
front-end to a lightweight, task-oriented student model via a dy-
namic weighting mechanism, preserving speaker-discriminative
embeddings while reducing overall system complexity. VKD
compresses the visual front-end by more than 48 times in model
size and 7.5 times in computational complexity. Through ex-
tensive comparisons, evaluations, and ablation studies on mul-
tiple datasets, we demonstrate that VKD combined with a top-
performing online A VSS separator achieves the state-of-the-art
causal A VSS performance.
Index Terms: audiovisual, speaker separation, causal separa-
tion problem, knowledge distillation
1. Introduction
In real-world acoustic environments, target speech is usually
corrupted by interfering sources, such as competing speakers.
The challenge of removing or attenuating interfering speech,
commonly referred to as the “cocktail party problem” [1], has
motivated extensive research in speaker separation (SS). Re-
cent advances in deep learning have significantly improved SS
systems, providing more effective solutions under challenging
acoustic conditions [2]. Although these systems can separate
mixed speech signals, they often rely on additional steps to re-
solve speaker permutation ambiguity [3]. To address this and
other limitations, researchers have incorporated auxiliary cues
into SS systems, including speaker embeddings [4] and visual
embeddings [5]. In particular, visual cues are inherently robust
to acoustic noise and directly associated with active speakers.
Using the complementary nature of auditory and visual modal-
ities, audiovisual speaker separation (A VSS) improves speech
quality and intelligibility while naturally resolving permutation
ambiguity [6].
Despite substantial advances in SS systems, most algo-
rithms are not suitable for online deployment, as real-time
applications require strict algorithmic causality. To address
this limitation, several causal methods have been proposed
[7–13]. However, empirical evidence indicates substantial per-
formance gap between online and offline variants of SS systems
in both audio-only (AO) and audiovisual (A V) settings [13, 14].
This gap originates from the fundamental properties of SS ap-
proaches, which rely on continuous grouping of speech fea-
tures into the underlying speakers [2]. Unlike non-causal sys-
tems, which can leverage the context of a full utterance for
grouping [3], causal SS systems are limited to past and present
speech information. This constraint makes the grouping task
more challenging and often leads to degraded separation quality
[9, 13, 14]. In particular, the causal A VSS problem arises from
two factors. First, the effectiveness of A V fusion is reduced
when future audio and visual context are unavailable [13]. Sec-
ond, most existing systems rely on pre-trained visual front-ends
to enable A VSS training. However, these front-end models are
typically non-causal and heavily parameterized, making them
difficult to be integrated into an online framework [15]. Despite
its practical relevance, research on lightweight visual front-ends
remains limited [16–18].
We make two key contributions in this study. First, we ex-
tend the online A V-CrossNet [19] by applying causal inference
to a pre-trained visual embedding network, DeepA VSR [20],
achieving strong separation performance with full algorithmic
causality. Second, we propose a simple and efficient teacher-
student strategy for visual front-end module, referred to as vi-
sual knowledge distillation (VKD), which progressively trans-
fers the representation capacity of DeepA VSR to a lightweight
visual front-end. Inspired by [21], the proposed VKD is de-
signed to learn jointly from the teahcer embeddings and the
A VSS task. Unlike the lightweight visual front-ends in [18],
which leverage a teacher–student training with A V-HuBERT
[22] and an auxiliary reconstruction objective, our approach
adopts a task-oriented strategy that preserves training simplicity
while achieving competitive performance relative to the teacher
model. Extensive experiments demonstrate VKD’s superior re-
sults in causal A VSS on multiple datasets, while preserving
computational efficiency. We also present comparisons under
both non-causal and causal settings to quantify the causal sepa-
ration problem, and highlight the effectiveness of our proposed
approach. Ablation studies further show that our approach sur-
passes strong baselines and oracle frame-level permutation in-
variant training (tPIT) [8].

2.1. Causal Visual Embeddings
To extract reliable speaker embeddings from visual cues, we
adopt the pre-trained DeepA VSR model [20], which is widely
used for its strong representation capability. However, its
original architecture is non-causal and therefore unsuitable for
strictly causal A VSS systems. DeepA VSR consists of a 3D
convolutional front-end followed by multiple residual blocks,
where the 3D convolutional kernels span both 2D image di-
mensions and the temporal axis. The temporal receptive field
of these kernels incorporates future frames, thereby violat-
ing causality. To address this issue while retaining the pre-
trained weights, we modify the inference procedure by intro-
ducing asymmetric temporal padding to the input video se-
quence, ensuring that each output embedding depends only on
past and current frames. This adaptation preserves the speaker-
discriminative representations learned during pre-training while
eliminating reliance on future visual information, enabling fully
causal operation without degrading embedding quality.
2.2. Online A V-CrossNet
To achieve both effectiveness and computational efficiency in
A VSS, we adopt online A V-CrossNet [19] as our baseline ar-
chitecture. Online A V-CrossNet separator block consists of a
masked global multi-head self-attention (GMHSA) module, a
cross-band module, and a narrow-band module, enabling ef-
ficient modeling of temporal and spectral dependencies under
causal constraints. An optional Mamba [23] narrow-band mod-
ule further enhances computational efficiency while providing
strong performance. The original online A V-CrossNet is de-
signed to address both audiovisual speech enhancement (A VSE)
and audiovisual target speaker extraction (A VTSE) tasks and
employs a causal visual front-end trained from scratch. How-
ever, directly applying this framework to the A VSS task leads
to unstable speaker assignment and training divergence, as
A VSS critically depends on discriminative and reliable speaker
embeddings for consistent separation. To extend online A V-
CrossNet to the A VSS setting, we replace its original visual
front-end with the causal visual embedding module described
in subsection 2.1. Audio and visual features are then aligned
by speakers along the channel dimension, enabling parallel pro-
cessing of each speaker stream while maintaining accurate cor-
respondence. As shown in Fig. 1, the overall operation can be
formulated as

2.3. Visual Knowledge Distillation
The performance of A VSS systems heavily relies on the visual
front-end, which typically involves a large number of parame-
ters and incurs substantial computational cost. To leverage the
strong representation capabilities while maintaining computa-
tional efficiency, we propose a VKD strategy. This approach
progressively transfers the visual representation learning from
a high-capacity DeepA VSR teacher model to a lightweight stu-
dent model via a dynamic weighting mechanism during train-
ing. The teacher model comprises a 3D convolutional front-end
that captures temporal visual dynamics, followed by 18 resid-
ual layers, which account for the majority of the computational
overhead. To reduce complexity while preserving modeling ca-
pacity, the student model retains the same architectural structure
but reduces the channel dimension of each residual layer by a
factor of 16, resulting in a significantly more efficient design.
The proposed VKD is illustrated in the gray box in Fig. 1.
During the initial training stage, both the pre-trained teacher
and the student networks contribute to the visual stream through
a weighted summation. The teacher is assigned a dominant
weightw 1, while the student is assigned a minor weightw 2,
subject to the constraintw 1 +w 2 = 1. As training proceeds,
w1 is linearly decreased whilew 2 is correspondingly increased,
progressively shifting the contribution to the student and gradu-
ally reducing the teacher’s influence. By a predefined epochK,
the teacher’s contribution becomes zero, and the visual stream
is entirely handled by the student model. Then, the efficient
student model is subsequently trained with the A VSS system
until convergence. The student model is trained entirely on
A VSS, learning to approximate the teacher’s representations
while being jointly optimized for the downstream A VSS objec-
tive. Meanwhile, the teacher provides a stable optimization tra-
jectory since the initial training phase, guiding both the student
and the separator toward reliable A VSS.

training hyperparameters used in our experiments.

3.2. Evaluation Metrics
To evaluate the performance of A VSS systems, we adopt three
objective metrics. The perceptual evaluation of speech qual-
ity (PESQ) [30] assesses perceptual signal quality and typically
ranges from -0.5 to 4.5. In addition, scale-invariant signal-to-
distortion ratio improvement (SI-SDRi) and signal-to-distortion
ratio improvement (SDRi) measure the improvements in SI-
SDR and SDR, respectively, relative to the unprocessed mix-
ture. These two metrics reflect signal fidelity, with higher values
indicating better separation performance. To evaluate computa-
tional complexity, we report the number of multiply-accumulate
operations (MACs). A lower MACs count indicates higher
computational efficiency.
3.3. Hyperparameters
To train the A VSS system, we use a combination of SI-SDR
and spectral magnitude losses, where SI-SDR improves sepa-
ration performance and the spectral magnitude loss improves
overall signal fidelity. Training is conducted on 4 NVIDIA
H100 GPUs, with each epoch comprising 5,000 mini-batches
per GPU. Each batch contains 6 mixtures, where each mixture
includes 3 seconds of audio and 75 frames of visual input from
2 competing speakers. For stable knowledge distillation, we set
the predefined VKD epoch toK= 50, with initial teacher and
student weights of99%and1%, respectively, as the model typ-
ically converges within approximately 100 epochs. Each pro-
posed system is trained using the Adam optimizer with an initial
learning rate of 0.001. We employ the PyTorch ReduceLROn-
Plateau scheduler to adapt the learning rate, configured with a
patience of three epochs and a decay factor of 0.9. The system
is considered to have converged when no improvement in the
loss is observed for 20 consecutive epochs.
4. Experimental Results
In this section, we present the A VSS results on three major
datasets, quantify the impact of causality on separation per-
formance, investigate various efficient visual front-end models,
and evaluate VKD against competitive baselines.
4.1. Audiovisual Speaker Separation
Table 1 reports the performance of representative A VSS systems
on V oxCeleb2-2mix, LRS2-2mix, and LRS3-2mix datasets. For
fair comparison, we include competitive non-causal and causal
A VSS methods, along with a strong non-causal AO baseline
TF-CrossNet. Among offline models, A V-CrossNet achieves
the highest PESQ across all datasets and delivers the best or
comparable SI-SDRi and SDRi to Dolphin, demonstrating its
advantage as a baseline system.
Under causality constraints, A V-ConvTasNet and RTFS-
Net-12 exhibit a significant performance degradation compared
to their offline counterparts. CTCNet exhibits a more sub-
stantial gap, with SI-SDRi degradation over 6 dB, quanti-
fying the causal A VSS problem. Swift-Net-12 narrows the
gap from RTFS-Net-12 through a more efficient fusion en-
coder, while utilizing a full-sized visual front-end. Our pro-
posed systems consistently outperform all existing online base-
lines while effectively closing the gap with offline counterparts.
On LRS2-2mix and LRS3-2mix, online A V-CrossNet-Mamba-
VKD (oA V-CrossNet-Mamba-VKD) surpasses all other base-
lines by over 0.3 in PESQ, 0.8 dB in SI-SDRi, and 0.3 dB in
SDRi. On V oxCeleb2-2mix, oA V-CrossNet-Mamba-VKD out-
performs the most competitive baseline, Swift-Net-12, by 0.2
in PESQ, while maintaining competitive SI-SDRi and SDRi re-
sults. Notably, oA V-CrossNet-Mamba-VKD maintains compa-
rable performance to the uncompressed oA V-CrossNet-Mamba
on V oxCeleb2-2mix and LRS3-2mix, and improves results on
LRS2-2mix. Overall, oA V-CrossNet-Mamba-VKD achieves a
strong balance between strict causality, separation quality, and
computational efficiency, establishing state-of-the-art perfor-
mance among online A VSS systems while remaining compet-
itive with non-causal approaches.

4.3. Online Visual Front-end
We have investigated several online visual front-ends on the
V oxCeleb2-2mix dataset. The DeepA VSR-mid extracts fea-
tures from the first residual block of the baseline front-end,
whereas the one-frame approach relies on a single visual fea-
ture per speaker for the entire utterance. Although DeepA VSR-
mid has the minimal size and nearly three times fewer MACs
than the baseline, it performs worst, suggesting early-layer
features carry limited speaker identity information. The one-
frame approach achieves minimal MACs with reasonable sep-
aration quality but does not reduce parameter count. In con-
trast, the proposed VKD balances model size and computa-
tional cost while maintaining performance comparable to the
full baseline, validating its effectiveness. We additionally eval-
uated two strategies: training the student front-end model from
scratch with the A VSS objective and fine-tuning the knowledge-
distilled student model for A VSS. In both cases, the systems
did not converge and produced poor separation. Consequently,
the results are omitted from Table 2. These findings indicate
that naive training or direct teacher–student training alone may
be insufficient, and that task-oriented, progressive knowledge
transfer through VKD is essential for effective causal A VSS.

4.4. Visual Front-end Efficiency
Table 4 compares the visual front-ends in different A VSS sys-
tems. To our knowledge, several A VSS models perform visual
front-end compression, but none of them are strictly causal.
Therefore, we compare with Dolphin, the latest top-performing
non-causal A VSS system that compresses the visual front-end.
Similar to VKD, Dolphin employs DP-LipCoder [18], which
is trained with a teacher–student framework. It distills knowl-
edge from A V-HuBERT [22] via semantic supervision and an
auxiliary image reconstruction objective. As a causal refer-
ence, Swift-Net-12 adopts the CTCNet-Lip [25] front-end and
achieves 13.9 dB in SI-SDRi and 3.07 in PESQ. Compared to
the CTCNet-Lip, DP-LipCoder achieves compression rates of
14.3 times in parameters and 3.3 times in MACs, with a mi-
nor performance loss (0.2 dB in SI-SDRi and 0.01 in PESQ).
In contrast, VKD directly distills knowledge from the Deep-
A VSR while jointly optimizing the A VSS objective. It achieves
much higher compression rates, with 48.6 times in parameters
and 7.5 times in MACs. Meanwhile, it improves separation per-
formance by 0.6 dB in SI-SDRi and 0.07 in PESQ. These gains
confirmed that VKD enables the lightweight visual front-end to
inherit the teacher’s representation capacity while being explic-
itly optimized for the A VSS task.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we address the causal audiovisual speaker sep-
aration problem by proposing visual knowledge distillation,
a simple yet effective strategy for building a lightweight and
fully causal visual front-end model. Through progressively
transferring knowledge from a causally inferred pre-trained
model to a compact student network, VKD preserves speaker-
discriminative embeddings while substantially reducing model
complexity. Integrated with a strong online separator, the pro-
posed system achieves the state-of-the-art performance for on-
line A VSS, significantly narrows the gap from non-causal mod-
els, and delivers substantial compression in both parameter
count and computational cost.
