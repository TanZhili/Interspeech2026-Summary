Abstract
Deep learning-based speech enhancement (SE) models are typ-
ically trained on synthetic pairs of clean speech and synthe-
sized noisy speech, which often generalize poorly to real-world
acoustic environments. In practical target environments, how-
ever, paired clean target speech aligned with noisy recordings
is usually unavailable, making it difficult to directly use real
data for supervised SE training. To address these limitations,
we propose SwitchSE — a target-domain clean-free fine-tuning
framework with a switch-controlled mechanism that leverages
transcription-noisy speech pairs to upgrade a pre-trained SE
model in realistic environments. Experiments show that with
only 2.9 h of CHiME-3 real noisy speech, SwitchSE sub-
stantially improves target-domain performance while retaining
strong performance on the original synthetic dataset, providing
a data-efficient framework to bridge the gap between synthe-
sized training data and real-world application demands.
Index Terms: Speech enhancement, clean-free fine-tuning, do-

1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) supports applications such as mo-
bile communications, hearing aids, and forensic audio analysis.
Most deep SE models are trained on synthetic noisy-clean pairs
[1, 2], but such models often generalize poorly to real acous-
tic conditions because target-domain clean references are un-
available and synthetic mixtures cannot fully capture real noise,
room, device, and speaker-adaptation effects [3, 4, 5, 6, 7].
In contrast, real noisy speech with text annotations is increas-
ingly available from ASR-style corpora such as GigaSpeech [8]
and WenetSpeech [9]. This motivates clean-free target-domain
adaptation from noisy-transcript pairs.
Several lines of work also relax paired-clean supervision.
Noise2Noise-style denoising removes clean targets but assumes
paired noisy observations of the same utterance [10]; domain-
adversarial training and self-supervised representations target
domain adaptation without paired target clean speech [11, 12];
and real-speech or perceptual-proxy methods exploit real data
or non-intrusive objectives for noise suppression [13, 14, 15].
Very recent SelfSE [16] constructs noisier-to-noisy pairs and
iteratively refines noisy speech, avoiding both clean refer-
ences and transcriptions under a different supervision setting.
SwitchSE instead focuses on target-domain noisy-transcript
data, enabling ASR-guided supervision and switch-controlled
enhancement/ASR operating modes.
To address these challenges, we proposeSwitchSE, a
switch-conditioned dual-mode framework that alternates be-
tween clean-noisy pairs and noisy-transcript pairs through a
simple stochastic switching mechanism, where each batch con-
tains only one data type and activates only its mode-specific
objective. By concatenating a switch Embedding along the tem-
poral dimension to indicate the operational mode (enhancement
or ASR), the model dynamically optimizes task-specific losses.
During inference, the switch token enables controllable output
biasing, allowing the model to produce enhancement results
with different task preferences.
Key contributions: Target-Domain Clean-Free Adapta-
tion:We leverage real noisy-transcript data (e.g., from CHiME-
3 [17]) to fine-tune an SE model for target environments where
paired clean targets are unavailable.Switch-Conditioned
Dual-Mode Learning:The switch-controlled mechanism con-
ditions the same model on two supervision types (clean-noisy
vs. noisy-transcript) and activates only the corresponding ob-
jective per batch, mitigating the trade-offs of single-objective
fine-tuning.Target-Domain Model Updating:With lim-
ited real-world data (2.9 h), SwitchSE improves target-domain
acoustic quality and provides controllable enhancement/ASR-
oriented modes while retaining competitive performance on the
original synthetic benchmark (VCTK).
2. Methodology
We introduce a dual-mode fine-tuning strategy that augments
the SE training pipeline with noisy-transcript pairs. A learn-
able mode embedding is prepended to the input spectrogram to
condition the model on the current supervision type, with mode-
specific losses applied accordingly. At inference time, the mode
embedding enables controllable output biasing toward either en-
hancement quality or ASR-friendliness. The specific details are
shown in the Figure 1.
2.1. Input Feature
First, we mix the two different data types (D Enh andD ASR)
together to ensure that each batch contains only one data type.
• For speech enhancement batches: Noisy-clean speech pairs
are used, where each training data pair is represented as
{xnoisy,y clean}.
• For ASR batches: Noisy-transcription pairs are used, where
each training data pair is represented as{x noisy,y txt}.
ForD ASR (e.g., CHiME-3 real data), paired clean targets
are unavailable; therefore SwitchSE activates only the ASR-
guided objective on these batches. Unlike standard multi-task
learning, only one mode-specific loss is active per batch.
During training, we randomly select one of the batches and
set a mode indicators∈{0,1}based on the data type to guide
the subsequent training process, wheres=0 denotes enhance-
ment mode (SEnh)ands=1 indicates ASR mode (S ASR).

3. Experiment setup
3.1. Enhancement model configuration
Our baseline adopts the published GCRN architecture [18] for
monaural speech enhancement, comprising a grouped LSTM
encoder-decoder (group size 2, hidden size 1024) with hierar-
chical convolutions and dual decoders for real/imaginary spec-
tral reconstruction. We refer readers to [18] for full architectural
details.

[20] pre-trained model from WeNet [21]. In our implementa-

3.3. Datasets
To comprehensively evaluate the proposed framework, we con-
duct cross-dataset validation using two speech corpora (VCTK
and CHiME-3), whose detailed configurations are presented as
follows:
Noise corpus: Training noise uses 10,000 types (126 h)
from Sound Ideas (https://www.sound-ideas.com);
evaluation uses isolated Auditec babble/cafeteria noise (http:
//www.auditec.com).
VCTK provides clean-noisy pairs. We use 99 speakers for
training and 10 gender-balanced speakers for validation/test.
Training mixtures are generated online with SNR uniformly
sampled from -10 to 10 dB, and test mixtures use SNRs in{-6,
-3, 0, 3, 6, 9}dB.
CHiME-3 provides real noisy speech from bus, cafe, pedes-
trian, and street-junction scenarios. We follow the official split:
1600 utterances (2.9 h) for training, 1640 for validation, and
1320 for testing.
3.4. Training configuration
All utterances are sampled at 16 kHz and represented by com-
plex STFT features using a 20 ms Hann window, 10 ms frame
shift, and 320-point DFT. Models are trained with Adam. The
GCRN baseline is pre-trained on VCTK clean-noisy pairs with
learning rate 1e-3, halved after 4 stagnant epochs. During
SwitchSE fine-tuning, VCTK clean-noisy batches and CHiME
noisy-transcript batches are alternated by switch mode; the
learning rate is 4e-4 with U2-style warmup over 10,000 steps.
We select checkpoints by validation loss.
For the U2++ model, 80-dimensional log Mel-filter banks
(FBank) were computed on-the-fly, with a 25 ms window and a
10 ms shift as features. In contrast to pre-trained models, during
the fine-tuning process, the CTC loss utilizes a ‘mean’ reduction
strategy.
3.5. Metrics
Acoustic quality is evaluated with PESQ [22], STOI [23], and
DNSMOS P.808 [24]; ASR performance is evaluated with
WER. Unless otherwise stated, WER uses the same U2++
ASR model that provides the ASR-mode training signal with
ctc greedy search, so these results reflect recognizer-specific
ASR-friendliness rather than universal ASR generalization.

5. Conclusion
We proposed SwitchSE, a clean-free target-domain fine-tuning
framework that uses real noisy-transcript pairs via switch-
controlled enhancement/ASR modes. Experiments on VCTK
and CHiME-3 show improved target-domain acoustic quality
while retaining source-domain performance. Future work will
validate SwitchSE with stronger enhancement backbones and
external ASR models.
