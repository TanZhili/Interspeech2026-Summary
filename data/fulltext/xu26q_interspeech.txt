Abstract
Acoustic perception systems must continually adapt to emerg-
ing sound categories, yet real-world annotation is often un-
available or costly. Continual Generalized Category Discov-
ery (C-GCD) addresses this challenge by retaining previously
learned classes while discovering novel categories from unla-
beled streaming data. However, directly transferring vision-
based C-GCD methods to audio leads to marked performance
degradation because acoustic signals exhibit complex spectro-
temporal structures, strong class overlap, and severe base-class
bias. We therefore propose an audio-oriented C-GCD frame-
work with instance-adaptive regularization to balance consoli-
dation and exploration, GMM-based adaptive thresholding, and
an EMA-updated dynamic teacher for stable pseudo-label gen-
eration. Experiments on LibriSpeech and ShipsEar show con-
sistent gains in novel-class discovery and old-class retention.
On ShipsEar, our method achieves 74.14% cumulative average
accuracy, surpassing Happy by 4.84 percentage points.
Index Terms: generalized category discovery, continual learn-
ing, pseudo-label stabilization, acoustic representation learning
1. Introduction
Acoustic perception serves as a fundamental pillar for au-
tonomous systems to interpret the physical world, underpinning
applications ranging from speech communication [1] and ur-
ban monitoring [2] to underwater surveillance [3]. While deep
learning has catalyzed significant breakthroughs in audio clas-
sification, conventional models predominantly operate under a
“closed-set” paradigm, assuming all categories are predefined
and fully labeled. However, real-world acoustic environments
are inherently non-stationary and open-ended [4, 5, 6]. New
sound events emerge continuously, characterized by weak su-
pervision, disparate durations, and substantial distribution shifts
induced by ambient noise or reverberation [7, 8, 9].
This landscape motivatesContinual Generalized Category
Discovery(C-GCD), where a model must incrementally dis-
cover novel classes from unlabeled data streams while maintain-
ing its ability to recognize previously learned ones [10, 11]. Un-
like standard continual learning, C-GCD is distinctively chal-
lenging as it requires the model to provide its own supervision
through pseudo-labeling in a rehearsal-free setting, where his-
torical raw data is inaccessible [12].
Although C-GCD has progressed in computer vision [13,
14, 15], its extension to the acoustic field is hindered by the fun-
damental nature of sound. Acoustic events are often polyphonic

pseudo-labeling in noisy, non-stationary streams. Furthermore,
a dynamic teacher guidance scheme, utilizing EMA updates
and feature-level distillation, is developed to alleviate semantic
conflicts and enhance category separation under strong acous-
tic overlap. Experiments on LibriSpeech and ShipsEar demon-
strate consistent gains, notably achieving 74.14% accuracy on
ShipsEar, with 4.84% improvement over the strongest baseline.

2.2. Framework Overview
The proposed acoustic C-GCD framework (Figure 2) is engi-
neered for incremental discovery in non-stationary soundscapes
while suppressing catastrophic forgetting underrehearsal-free
constraints. We leverage an AudioMAE backbone to extract ro-
bust spectro-temporal representations. To stabilize feature evo-
lution, we adopt a teacher-student paradigm where a student
model is guided by an Exponential Moving Average (EMA)
updated teacher. Our core mechanism, Instance-Adaptive En-
tropy Regularization (IAER), dynamically partitions unlabeled
streams intopotential-seenandpotential-unseensubsets using
a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM). This GMM-driven thresh-
olding remains resilient to evolving ambient noise and spectral
overlap. The global objective integrates self-distillation, un-
supervised contrastive learning, and hierarchical entropy min-
imization to ensure discriminative boundaries for both legacy
and emerging sound classes.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics
We evaluate the proposed framework on two diverse bench-
marks to verify its robustness. Specifically, we utilize a sub-
set of ShipsEar [22] to test performance under low-SNR under-
water conditions, and LibriSpeech [23] to simulate incremental
discovery in large-scale speech streams. To measure the effec-
tiveness of novel class discovery alongside knowledge reten-
tion, we report overall accuracy (Acc), old/novel class accu-
racy (OldAcc/NewAcc), and their cumulative average accuracy
(CAA) across incremental sessions.
3.2. Experimental Setup
We follow the incremental C-GCD protocol of Happy [19].
ShipsEar uses a 5/1 split with 5 base classes and 3 incre-
ments, while LibriSpeech is evaluated under 50/10 and 80/10
settings. The model recognizes seen classes and discovers
novel ones without accessing historical data. We compare
with SimGCD [20], MetaGCD [10], PromptCCD [21], and
Happy [19]. All experiments use AudioMAE with batch size
64 and 0.1 time/frequency masking. We fine-tune only the 12th
Transformer block and later layers, and optimize with SGD us-
ing momentum 0.9, weight decay5×10 −5, and learning rate
0.1. EMA momentumα e is cosine-annealed from 0.999 to 0.8,
withλ= [0.5,2,1,1]andt= [0.2,0.8].
3.3. Main Results and Discussion
Underwater Acoustic Discovery:On the ShipsEar dataset
(Table 1), our framework achieves the highest Cumulative
Average Accuracy (CAA-All) of 74.14%, outperforming the
vision-centric Happy [19] by +4.84%. This overall gain is
mainly attributed to stronger old-class knowledge retention, as
reflected by the improvement in CAA-Old from 66.27% to
75.00%. Although our method obtains a competitive CAA-
New of 63.92%, it remains lower than Happy’s 71.56%, indi-
cating that the improvement is not uniformly distributed across
old and novel classes. These results suggest that GMM-driven
adaptive thresholding and EMA improve the overall balance be-
tween category consolidation and discovery under low-SNR un-
derwater acoustic conditions, while still leaving room for fur-
ther enhancing novel-class discovery. In contrast, SimGCD and
MetaGCD exhibit substantially lower CAA-New, suggesting
difficulty in maintaining effective exploration under rehearsal-
free incremental settings.
Large-scale Speech Streams:Results on LibriSpeech fur-
ther demonstrate the scalability of our framework under both
the 50/10 and 80/10 incremental protocols. Under the more
fine-grained 50/10 setting (Table 2), the w/GMM+EMA variant
achieves the best CAA-All of 76.94%, outperforming Happy
[19] by +8.34%. This improvement is mainly driven by stronger
old-class retention, where CAA-Old increases from 69.82%
to 82.93%, while CAA-New remains comparable to Happy,
with a marginal gain from 59.42% to 59.43%. Under the
80/10 setting (Table 3), our method shows a more balanced
improvement, achieving the highest CAA-All, CAA-Old, and
CAA-New scores of 86.51%, 87.64%, and 74.73%, respec-
tively. Compared with Happy, this corresponds to gains of
+6.63%, +6.41%, and +10.98%. Notably, in the final incremen-
tal stage S2(C90–C99), w/GMM+EMA improves over Happy
by +15.14% in AllAcc, +14.37% in OldAcc, and +18.25%
in NewAcc. These results suggest that EMA-guided teacher
updates stabilize the feature space and improve the balance
between category consolidation and novel-class discovery in
large-scale speech streams, where MetaGCD and PromptCCD
exhibit weaker overall performance and less consistent adapta-
tion across incremental stages.
3.4. Ablation on EMA-based Dynamic Teacher
The efficacy of adaptive regularization relies on threshold pre-
cision and teacher quality. We evaluate the EMA-driven dy-
namic teacher against a static baseline on LibriSpeech (80/10
split). As shown in Figure 3, the dynamic teacher co-evolves
with the student, refining decision boundaries for novel sam-
ples that semantically resemble known classes. Without EMA
(Figure 3(b)), significant overlap between known (blue) and un-
known (orange) distributions causes high categorical ambiguity.
In contrast, the EMA mechanism (Figure 3(a)) effectively po-
larizes the confidences: known classes cluster in high-score re-
gions while novel classes are suppressed, reducing overlap and
boosting partitioning accuracy from 80.32% to 90.41%. This
confirms that dynamic updates provide a more reliable prior for
instance-level partitioning.

3.5. Algorithm Stability Analysis
To assess algorithm stability, we compare our method with
Happy [19] on LibriSpeech under the 80/10 and 50/10 config-
urations, as shown in Figure 4. The overall accuracy curves in
Figure 4(a) and (d) reveal that Happy is unstable across incre-
mental stages. Although it performs well initially, its accuracy
collapses from above 60% to below 20% after new sessions
are introduced, indicating that it cannot preserve reliable rep-
resentations as the category distribution evolves. The OldAcc
and NovAcc curves in Figure 4(b)-(f)) further clarify this fail-
ure. The sharp decline in OldAcc reflects severe forgetting,
while the deterioration in NovAcc reveals a reduced ability to
discover emerging classes. In contrast, our method maintains
high accuracy across all stages, with only minor fluctuations
under both settings. Its stable OldAcc and NovAcc demon-
strate that instance-adaptive regularization effectively balances
old-knowledge retention and novel-category exploration. More-
over, the gap between the dynamic and static variants, shown by
the green and red curves, confirms the importance of the EMA-
updated dynamic teacher. By providing temporally smoothed
supervision, the teacher stabilizes pseudo-label generation and
consolidates both known and novel category representations,
demonstrating the robustness of our framework in evolving
acoustic environments.

4. Conclusion
This work investigates Continual Generalized Category Dis-
covery for acoustic signals, focusing on incrementally discov-
ering novel categories from unlabeled streams without forget-
ting legacy classes. We show that directly adapting vision-
centric frameworks to acoustic domains often fails due to se-
vere spectral overlap and amplified base-class bias. To address
these issues, we propose an acoustic-tailored framework with
instance-adaptive regularization and dynamic teacher guidance.
By leveraging GMM-driven thresholding and EMA-based up-
dates, our method balances exploration and consolidation, im-
proving class separability in non-stationary environments. Ex-
periments on LibriSpeech and ShipsEar demonstrate consistent
gains in cumulative accuracy and old-class retention. In par-
ticular, the 74.14% cumulative average accuracy on ShipsEar
highlights the robustness of our method for real-world open-
world acoustic learning, supporting reliable deployment under
evolving, weakly supervised, data-scarce acoustic conditions.
