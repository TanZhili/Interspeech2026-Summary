Abstract
Deep learning for pathological voice detection suffers from se-
vere data scarcity. Traditional augmentation often fails to cap-
ture the non-linear glottal instabilities of voice disorders. We
propose a flow-based generative framework to synthesize patho-
logical and healthy vowel phonations using clinical status to-
ken conditioning. We investigate scaling laws by training su-
pervised classifiers on synthetic datasets ranging from 100 to
100,000 samples. Experiments across four languages show
that classifiers trained solely on 100k synthetic samples sur-
pass real-only baselines by +3.9% accuracy and +13.3% sen-
sitivity on held-out real data. Synthetic augmentation further
improves zero-shot transfer across multiple datasets on multi-
class pathology classification (+7.4%/+10.0% F1) and Parkin-
son’s detection from vowels (+6.8% accuracy). Our findings
demonstrate that flow-based generative scaling effectively ad-
dresses data scarcity while improving cross-domain robustness.
Index Terms: pathological speech, voice disorders, text-to-
speech, flow matching, scaling laws
1. Introduction
V oice disorders affect nearly 30% of adults during their life-
time [1]. These conditions, ranging from functional dyspho-
nia to symptoms of neurodegenerative diseases like Parkinson’s
and ALS, heavily impair communication and reduce quality of
life [2, 3]. While clinical diagnosis typically requires invasive,
specialized equipment, Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) frame-
works like HuBERT [4] and WavLM [5] now enable objec-
tive, non-invasive assessment by extracting pathology-sensitive
representations directly from raw audio [6]. Recent studies
have optimized these discriminative models via multi-source
task fusion [7], interpretable concept-based frameworks [8], and
Speech Large Language Models [9].
However, the field remains constrained by a fundamen-
tal data bottleneck. Pathological datasets are inherently small,
imbalanced, and often lack the diversity required for models
to generalize across different environments or disease severi-
ties [6]. Traditional data augmentation methods, such as addi-
tive noise, pitch shifting, or spectral warping, have been shown
to provide negligible performance gains for pathology detec-
tion from sustained vowels [10]. This is largely because linear
transformations fail to capture the complex, non-linear glottal
dynamics and aperiodicity that characterize vocal fold dysfunc-
tion [11]. While recent advances in Text-to-Speech (TTS) have
explored generative synthesis for clinical applications, most fo-
cus on single conditions or prioritize naturalness over preserv-

ing the acoustic irregularities that signify pathology [12–15].
Concurrent work has investigated severity-controllable TTS us-
ing diffusion models for oral cancer speech [16], but focuses on
continuous severity control for a single pathology type rather
than binary classification across multiple disorders.
In this paper, we propose a flow-based generative augmen-
tation framework for pathological voice detection, leveraging
its deterministic generation and precise duration control. We
train F5-TTS [17], a recent Conditional Flow Matching model,
on a diverse corpus of 7,211 sustained vowel recordings across
four datasets and languages, encompassing both healthy and
pathological phonations. By conditioning generation on clini-
cal status tokens (⟨healthy⟩or⟨pathological⟩), the model learns
to synthesize vowels with controllable pathological characteris-
tics. We focus on sustained vowels as they provide language-
agnostic clinical biomarkers; unlike connected speech, which
is influenced by linguistic and prosodic variations, sustained
phonation is primarily governed by laryngeal biomechanics,
making it suitable for cross-linguistic diagnostic applications.
We investigate the scaling laws of synthetic data by generating
datasets ranging from 100 to 100,000 samples and measuring
their impact on downstream classifier performance. Our ex-
perimental results demonstrate that flow-based generative scal-
ing provides substantial performance improvements. Classi-
fiers trained solely on 100k synthetic samples surpass real-only
baselines by +3.9% accuracy and +13.3% sensitivity on held-
out real data. Synthetic augmentation further enables effec-
tive cross-domain transfer across languages and pathologies,
achieving +7.4%/+10.0% F1 on multi-class pathology classi-
fication and +6.8% accuracy on zero-shot Parkinson’s disease
detection. These findings demonstrate that flow-based gener-
ative scaling effectively compensates for clinical data scarcity
while improving robustness across diverse diagnostic tasks.
The primary contributions of this work are as follows. First,
we adapt F5-TTS for clinical voice synthesis, enabling control-
lable generation of pathological and healthy vowel phonations
through clinical status token conditioning. Second, we pro-
vide the first systematic study of synthetic data scaling laws
for pathology detection, demonstrating that classifiers trained
on synthetic samples can surpass real-data baselines. Finally,
we show that our approach exhibits strong cross-domain gen-
eralization across multiple datasets and tasks, showing that
synthetic augmentation improves zero-shot transfer to unseen
pathologies, languages, and diagnostic scenarios.

F5-TTS for pathological speech generation.We employ F5-
TTS [17], a non-autoregressive generative model based on Con-
ditional Flow Matching (CFM) [18]. CFM defines a continuous
transformation between a Gaussian priorp 0 =N(0, I)and
a target data distributionq(x 1)through a learned vector field,
wherex 0 ∼p 0 denotes a noise sample andx 1 ∼q(x 1)de-
notes the target mel-spectrogram features. The model learns a
time-dependent vector fieldv t(xt) :R d×[0,1]→R d that
generates speech via an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE):

advantages for clinical speech synthesis. First, the deterministic
ODE trajectory avoids stochastic sampling, enabling faster and
more stable generation than diffusion models. Second, straight-
path parameterization prevents the recursive error accumula-
tion observed in autoregressive TTS systems [19–21], where
sequential token generation can lead to drift in prosodic fea-
tures. This global trajectory stability is particularly important
for sustained vowels, where even minor fundamental frequency
deviations are highly perceptible. Third, the continuous-time
formulation allows flexible trade-offs between synthesis speed
and quality by adjusting the ODE solver steps at inference.
Architecture and feature representation.F5-TTS employs
a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture [22] operating on
80-bin mel-spectrograms extracted at a 24 kHz sampling rate,
1024-point FFT, 256-sample hop length, and a Hann window.
The DiT blocks utilize long-skip connections to preserve fine-
grained spectral details, Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE)
for temporal encoding, and cross-attention over phoneme en-
coder embeddings for text conditioning. During inference, we
integrate the learned vector field using an adaptive ODE solver
with Sway sampling, which concentrates more function evalu-
ations neart= 1where the distribution is close to real speech,
allowing finer refinement of high-frequency details.
Clinical status conditioning via text tokens.To enable con-
trollable generation of healthy versus pathological speech, we
prepend each vowel’s phonetic transcription with a clinical sta-
tus token during training. The input text sequence takes the
form“⟨status⟩[vowel]”, where[vowel]is the phonetic repre-
sentation (e.g., /a/, /e/, /o/). The phonetic token specifies which
vowel to generate, while the status token controls its acoustic
characteristics. Both tokens are processed as regular text by
the F5-TTS text encoder, and the resulting embeddings con-
dition the DiT via cross-attention. This allows the model to
learn implicit acoustic mappings:⟨pathological⟩tokens are as-
sociated with increased jitter, shimmer, and aperiodicity, while
⟨healthy⟩tokens correspond to stable periodicity and clear har-
monic structure. At inference, we control the health status of
generated samples by selecting the appropriate status token to
generate both classes for balanced classifier training.
Reference-guided synthetic data generation.After training,
we generate synthetic vowels via F5-TTS’s reference-guided
procedure. For each sample, a reference recording matching the
target health status and vowel type is randomly selected from
the training set to provide prosody and speaker identity. The text
prompt, formatted as “⟨status⟩vowel”, specifies phonetic con-
tent and clinical status. This pairing ensures the flow-matching
trajectory originates from an acoustically relevant point, en-
abling the model to modulate fine-grained pathological features
(e.g., jitter, shimmer) while preserving laryngeal biomechanics
of the target phoneme. Generation proceeds by sampling ini-
tial noisex 0∼N(0, I)and integrating the learned vector field
via the ODE solver. While the reference provides speaker iden-
tity, diversity arises from stochastic sampling and variation in
reference selection. To ensure synthetic samples are not near-
duplicates of training data, we verified novel acoustic character-
istics by measuring cosine similarity between synthetic and ref-
erence mel-spectrograms (mean similarity<0.80). We gener-
ate synthetic samples at four scales:N∈{10 2,10 3,10 4,10 5},
preserving the training corpus’s vowel distribution.

3. Experimental Setup
Data selection and preprocessing.We train the flow match-
ing TTS model on four corpora: the German Saarbr¨ucken V oice
Database (SVD) [23], the Portuguese Advanced V oice Function
Assessment Database (A VFAD) [24], the Italian VOice ICar
fEDerico II dataset (VOICED) [25], and the English Perceptual
V oice Qualities Database (PVQD) [26]. Recordings are resam-
pled to 24 kHz, filtered below a 15 dB SNR threshold, and win-
dowed to 5.0 seconds with periodic padding for shorter samples.
The final corpus comprises 7,211 recordings (3,230 healthy,
3,981 pathological). A table summarizing the cross-lingual and
clinical distribution can be found in the project repository.1
Generative and discriminative backbones.The generative
model builds on F5-TTS, fine-tuned for 150K steps on a single
NVIDIA A100 using AdamW, with 1e-5 learning rate, 1,500
warmup steps, and 200-frame batch size. For the discrimina-
tive task, we benchmarked HuBERT [4], WavLM [5], wav2vec
2.0 [27], and voc2vec [28], finding that HuBERT pre-trained on
AudioSet (HuBERT-AS) [29] consistently outperformed alter-
natives. We therefore adoptHuBERT-ASas the discriminative
backbone for all the subsequent experiments.

4. Results and Discussion
SSL model selection.We first compare SSL models trained ex-
clusively on real data via 10-fold CV for pathology detection in
Table 1 to identify the most effective encoder. HuBERT pre-
trained on AudioSet (HuBERT-AS) consistently outperforms
alternatives, likely due to its pre-training on diverse audio be-
yond clean speech, which better captures acoustic variability in
pathological phonations. Models pre-trained on clean speech
(e.g., LibriSpeech [33]) and voc2vec variants show systemati-
cally lower performance. We therefore adoptHuBERT-ASas
the discriminative backbone for all subsequent experiments.

Fig. 1 shows classifier performance as synthetic volume in-
creases: combining real and synthetic data yields monotonic
improvements. At 100 synthetic samples, performance remains
statistically equivalent to the real-only baseline. Standard data
augmentation (Real++), which applies traditional transforma-
tions to the real training set, provides negligible improvements
over the real-only baseline (dashed lines in Fig. 1), confirming
that linear transformations fail to capture the complex acoustic
variability of pathological phonation. In contrast, flow-based
synthetic augmentation surpassesReal++at just 1k samples
and continues to scale monotonically. The most substantial
gains emerge at 10k (+3.4% relative gain in accuracy) and 100k
scales (+7.7%). Reduced standard deviations at 100k (±0.007
vs. ±0.013 baseline) further indicate that synthetic data stabi-
lizes training by exposing the classifier to broader pathological
patterns, reducing sensitivity to fold composition.
Table 2 presents results for models trained exclusively on
synthetic data at four scales and evaluated on held-out real
recordings. Performance is poor at small scales (0.55-0.57 ac-
curacy at 100-1k samples) but improves substantially at 10k
(0.744), approaching the real-data baseline (0.804) even at com-
parable dataset sizes. At 100k, synthetic-only training surpasses
the baseline by +3.9% accuracy and +13.3% sensitivity. In-
terestingly, the sensitivity-specificity balance shifts with scale.
At 1k samples, the classifier exhibits high specificity (0.847)
but low sensitivity (0.354), indicating a conservative decision
boundary that only detects extreme pathological cases. At 100k,
this reverses: high sensitivity (0.907) with moderate specificity
(0.748). This suggests that large-scale synthetic training ex-
poses the classifier to a dense manifold of pathological varia-
tions, improving detection of subtle dysphonic features at the
cost of increased false positives. The real-data baseline main-
tains better balance (0.800/0.810), though the 100k model’s su-
perior sensitivity may be clinically preferable for screening ap-
plications where false negatives are costlier than false positives.

that synthetic samples preserve pathological acoustic character-
istics, we extracted jitter, shimmer, and harmonics-to-noise ra-

rapid pitch and amplitude perturbations. Pathological samples

than healthy samples, consistent with irregular glottal vibra-
tion. HNR values for synthetic pathological samples (15.0 dB)
closely match real pathological recordings (16.7 dB), both sub-
stantially lower than healthy samples (19.6-20.7 dB). This 4-5
dB HNR reduction indicates preserved aperiodicity in glottal
vibration, a typical trait of vocal pathology. Interestingly, syn-

OOD pathology classification.We investigate whether the bi-
nary pathology manifold learned through synthetic augmenta-
tion provides a useful prior for fine-grained clinical diagnosis.
Table 4 presents multi-class classification results on FEMH (20
classes) and IPV (15). Synthetic augmentation yields consis-
tent improvements: +7.4% F1 on IPV (0.531→0.571) and
+10.0% on FEMH (0.286→0.315) at 100k samples. This
confirms that the healthy/pathological manifold provides useful
initialization, teaching fundamental differences between stable
and aperiodic phonation before fine-tuning on specific patholo-
gies. Practically, binary synthetic augmentation can serve as
general-purpose pre-training, reducing the need for large la-
beled datasets per pathology type. However, narrowing F1 gains
between 10k and 100k (IPV: +0.1%; FEMH: +0.5%) suggest di-
minishing returns. We hypothesize that while binary synthetic
data captures general pathological characteristics (jitter, shim-
mer, aperiodicity), it lacks fine-grained spectral variations dis-
tinguishing specific pathology types (e.g., nodules vs. polyps),
causing saturation when real fine-tuning data is limited.
OOD zero-shot transfer.A critical test of synthetic data qual-
ity is whether it improves generalization to unseen domains.
We evaluate zero-shot transfer on PC-GITA for Parkinson’s de-
tection from Spanish vowel recordings. Table 5 shows that
synthetic augmentation improves over the real-only baseline
(0.606 accuracy) despite no exposure to Spanish or Parkinso-
nian speech during training, confirming that sustained vowels
capture universal laryngeal instabilities that transcend linguis-
tic boundaries. Performance peaks at 10k (0.647, +6.8%) but
degrades at 100k (0.607, +0.2%). We hypothesize that this
non-monotonic behavior arises from a mismatch between the
binary pathological manifold and Parkinson’s specific motor-
speech characteristics. At 10k, synthetic data provides regu-
larization without overwhelming adaptation to PD-specific fea-
tures (monotone pitch, reduced loudness), while at 100k, the
classifier over-fits to general pathological patterns, reducing
sensitivity to PD’s tremor-related acoustic signatures. This sug-
gests moderate synthetic augmentation generalizes better than
excessive scaling for distant OOD tasks.
