Rhythm and Context-Aware Alignment

Abstract
Dance-to-music (D2M) generation aims to compose music that
is rhythmically and temporally aligned with dance. Prior meth-
ods rely on coarse rhythm embeddings (e.g.,global motion fea-
tures or binarized joint rhythms), which discard fine-grained
motion cues and weaken alignment. In addition, temporal
mismatches introduced by feature downsampling further im-
pair synchronization. We proposeGACA-DiT, a diffusion
transformer-based framework for rhythm-consistent and tem-
porally aligned music generation. It contains two key mod-
ules: (1) a Genre-Adaptive rhythm extraction module that inte-
grates multi-scale temporal–spatial analysis with adaptive joint
weighting to capture fine-grained, genre-aware rhythm patterns;
and (2) a Context-Aware temporal alignment module that lever-
ages learnable queries to align music latents with relevant dance
rhythms. Experiments on AIST++ and TikTok demonstrate
consistent improvements over state-of-the-art methods in both
objective metrics and human evaluation 1.

1. Introduction
Recent advances in multimodal learning have enabled signifi-
cant progress in generating images [1, 2, 3], videos [4, 5, 6],
and audios [7, 8, 9]. Among these tasks, music generation
has emerged as a key area of interest. In particular, dance-
to-music (D2M) generation has gained increasing attention on
short video platforms, where it can automatically produce mu-
sical accompaniments for user-uploaded dance videos.
D2M aims to generate music that is both temporally syn-
chronized and rhythmically coherent with dance videos. This
task is challenging due to two key issues: (1) the difficulty of
modelingfine-grained rhythmic patternsacross diverse dance
genres and (2) the need for precise frame-leveltemporal align-
ment. To tackle thefirst issue, as illustrated in Figure 1(a),
early studies [10, 11, 12, 13, 14] typically employ motion en-
coders such as ST-GCNs [15] to extract global motion fea-
tures as rhythm representations. While effective at capturing
overall motion semantics, these methods encode rhythm only
implicitly, often resulting in coarse-grained tempo correspon-
dence and limited rhythmic consistency. To obtain more explicit
rhythmic cues, subsequent works such as RhythmicNet [16],
LORIS [17], Text-Inv [18], and MotionComposer [19] compute
first-order differences of keypoints to model motion velocities.

2. Method
2.1. Overview
Figure 2 illustrates the pipeline of GACA-DiT. Given a dance
video and corresponding pose sequences, a genre-adaptive
rhythm extraction module encodes fine-grained rhythmic pat-
terns intoR∈R T×D , while an I3D-based semantic encoder
extracts video featuresV∈R T×D v . The rhythm featuresR
are then temporally aligned to the music latent via a context-
aware temporal alignment module, producing ˜R∈R Tm×D.
Both ˜RandVare then used as conditioning inputs to a diffu-
sion transformer, which generates a music latent that is finally
decoded into a waveform rhythmically consistent and semanti-
cally aligned with the input dance.

3. Experiment
3.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets.We evaluate GACA-DiT on two music–dance
datasets: AIST++ [25] and TikTok [10], following the standard
protocols of prior works [20, 12] for a fair comparison.
Implementation Details.We employ a pre-trained I3D model
[26] (trained on ImageNet [27]) as the semantic encoder to ex-
tract video features from video frames, and a pre-trained V AE
from DiffRhythm [28] to encode and reconstruct audio wave-
forms. The conditional DiT comprises 8 transformer blocks,
each with a 512-dimensional hidden size and 10-head self-
attention. Experiments use 5-second music clips at 44,100 Hz,
and 2D pose skeletons are obtained via DWpose [29]. We train
with a batch size of 4 for 100 epochs using the Adam optimizer
(β1 = 0.9,β 2 = 0.95) with a learning rate of1×10−4. The dif-
fusion process uses a 32-step Euler ODE solver, and classifier-
free guidance [30] with a scale of 4 is applied during inference.

Evaluation Metrics.Forobjective evaluation, following [17],
we assess the alignment between musical rhythms and dance
patterns using Beats Coverage Score (BCS), Beats Hit Score
(BHS), their F1 score, and the standard deviations of BCS
and BHS (denoted as CSD and HSD). To evaluate the aes-
thetic quality of the generated music, we adopt Meta Audiobox-
aesthetics [31], which measures four dimensions: Production
Quality (PQ), Production Complexity (PC), Content Enjoyment
(CE), and Content Usefulness (CU). We further employ the
Fr´echet Audio Distance (FAD) [32] to quantify the distribu-
tional similarity between generated music and ground-truth au-
dio. Forsubjective evaluation, we conduct a Mean Opinion
Score (MOS) study to assess both the overall quality of the gen-
erated music and its rhythmic consistency with the dance video.
Participants rate each sample on a 5-point scale, ranging from 1
(poor) to 5 (excellent).
3.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods
We evaluate the effectiveness of GACA-DiT by comparing
it with several representative dance-to-music (D2M) methods:
D2M-GAN [10], CDCD [20], LORIS [17], and MotionCom-
poser [19]. Table 1 reports the quantitative results on AIST++
and TikTok datasets. GACA-DiT consistently outperforms all
baselines across both rhythm alignment and music quality met-
rics. In particular, it achieves the highest BCS and BHS scores,
reflecting superior beat consistency and harmonic synchroniza-
tion, while attaining the lowest CSD and HSD values, indicat-
ing improved temporal alignment and reduced rhythmic devi-
ation. Moreover, GACA-DiT demonstrates better performance
on aesthetic metrics (CE, CU, PC, PQ) and lower FAD, suggest-
ing that it generates music that is both perceptually pleasing and
closer to ground truth.
We also provide a qualitative comparison between the mu-
sic generated by our method, GACA-DiT, and the MotionCom-
poser [19] in Figure 3. The results demonstrate that our gener-
ated music exhibits better rhythmic alignment with the ground
truth. Additional comparisons can be explored on our demo
page, illustrating the effectiveness of our method.
3.3. Ablation Analysis
Model Components.We conduct ablation studies to evaluate
the effectiveness of each component in GACA-DiT, as listed
in Table 2. Comparing the baseline using only video features,
incorporating the multi-scale wavelet features (M-Wav) signif-
icantly improves beat consistency and reduces temporal devia-
tion. Adding multi-scale phase histograms (M-His) further en-
hances both beat and harmonic synchronization, indicating that
harmonic distribution modeling complements rhythmic cues.
The joint adaptive weighting mechanism (A-W) brings consis-
tent gains across all metrics, highlighting the benefit of dynami-
cally balancing rhythm and harmony information. Finally, inte-
grating the CATA module achieves the best performance on all
metrics, confirming its effectiveness in refining temporal align-
ment and improving overall cross-modal coherence.

3.4. User Study
We further conduct a user study to evaluate the subjective qual-
ity of the generated music. Twenty volunteers were invited to
rate 30 test samples from the AIST++ dataset generated by the
five D2M methods. As illustrated in the violin plots in Figure 4,
GACA-DiT consistently achieves higher median scores and ex-
hibits more concentrated distributions for both rhythmic consis-
tency and overall quality. This indicates that our method not
only generates music that is well-aligned with the input dance
motions but also maintains stable and high-quality audio output
across samples.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced GACA-DiT, a diffusion-based
framework for dance-to-music generation that improves both
rhythmic consistency and temporal alignment. Our approach
integrates a genre-adaptive rhythm extraction module to cap-
ture fine-grained rhythmic patterns and a context-aware tem-
poral alignment module to mitigate cross-modal temporal mis-
matches. Extensive experiments on the AIST++ and TikTok
datasets show that GACA-DiT consistently surpasses SOTAs,
achieving superior performance in both objective metrics and
perceptual quality.
