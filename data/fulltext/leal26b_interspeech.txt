Abstract
Monitoring treatment response during Cognitive Behavioral
Therapy (CBT) is challenging due to the lack of objective, tem-
porally grounded markers. This study investigates longitudi-
nal vocal dynamics in hikikomori patients undergoing CBT us-
ing session-level speech recordings. First, stable Mel-frequency
cepstral coefficients (MFCCs) that exhibited consistent tempo-
ral variation and low early inter-subject variability were iden-
tified. Trajectory analyses then revealed distinct speech evo-
lution patterns between patients showing clinical improvement
or deterioration. Finally, Wav2vec 2.0 embeddings were com-
pared with traditional acoustic features. The fusion of Wav2vec
2.0 with MFCCs and fundamental frequency (F0) achieved
the highest F1-score across age and gender groups, indicating
the complementary value of deep and hand-crafted descriptors.
Overall, treatment outcomes were captured more accurately by
trajectory evolution than by static pre-post differences.
Index Terms: speech signal processing, longitudinal vocal dy-
namics, speech depression recognition, machine learning
1. Introduction
Telepsychology, defined as the use of digital technologies to
provide psychological services, has been widely recognized as
a way to expand access to mental healthcare among individuals
with limited resources [1]. It is usually delivered through web-
based platforms that provide cognitive-behavioral strategies tar-
geting specific conditions, including depressive disorders [2].
Depression remains one of the leading causes of disabil-
ity worldwide and a major public health concern [3]. Rising
prevalence among youth has fostered psychosocial conditions
like hikikomori, defined by prolonged social isolation [4]. Con-
sequently, young individuals experiencing depression alongside
sustained social withdrawal face substantial barriers to access-
ing traditional in-person care.
Within this context, certain telepsychology tools, often
termed self-guided interventions or behavioral intervention
technologies (BITs), enable individuals to engage with psycho-
logical support without direct therapist involvement [1]. Al-
though these approaches show potential in improving accessi-
bility, they are generally not considered equivalent to clinician-
delivered psychotherapy, and current evidence remains incon-
clusive regarding their effectiveness as a low-cost substitute for
conventional treatment across clinical contexts [2].

Addressing these limitations, a promising direction lies
in integrating telepsychology tools with structured, therapist-
guided interventions such as Internet-based Cognitive Behav-
ioral Therapy (ICBT) [5]. ICBT has been shown to achieve
outcomes comparable to face-to-face CBT while offering ad-
vantages in accessibility, flexibility, and continuity of care [6].
This may provide a particular advantage for individuals expe-
riencing hikikomori, who may be reluctant or unable to seek
in-person treatment, as professionally guided ICBT may offer a
feasible and scalable pathway to receive evidence-based mental
healthcare within the home environment.
Moreover, data generated during ICBT enable advanced
computational analyses. Machine learning (ML) models can
leverage longitudinal behavioral and clinical data to identify
early indicators of treatment response, symptom progression,
and potential changes in therapeutic trajectory [7]. This tempo-
ral perspective supports earlier identification of individuals at
risk of poor outcomes and can inform timely adjustments to in-
terventions. Among the data employed in this context, speech
signals are particularly relevant due to their low intrusiveness,
cost-effectiveness, and adaptability across platforms [8].
However, most studies rely on time-independent ML for-
mulations based on global summaries, limiting the modeling
of within-individual temporal dynamics [5]. Many studies also
focus on static classification tasks (e.g., depressed vs. non-
depressed) based on cross-sectional datasets such as DAIC-
WoZ, thereby lacking the longitudinal perspective needed to
track symptom progression and treatment trajectories [9].
Given this context, this paper presents a longitudinal analy-
sis of vocal changes in hikikomori patients undergoing ICBT
within a real-world clinical trial. Rather than only focusing
on depression detection, we investigate how speech evolves
throughout therapy. Specifically, we examine which Mel-
Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs) exhibit systematic
temporal trajectories associated with clinical improvement or
worsening, prioritizing the MFCCs that show meaningful lon-
gitudinal variation while maintaining low inter-subject variabil-
ity in early sessions. The contribution of this work is structured
around three research questions (RQs):
•RQ1 - Marker stability: Which acoustic features exhibit
consistent longitudinal patterns across therapy sessions?
•RQ2 - Outcome dynamics: How do acoustic trajectories dif-
fer between improving vs. worsening patients during CBT?
•RQ3 - Representation comparison: Do pretrained deep
speech representations provide complementary or superior

2. Related works
Depression is characterized by a heterogeneous and dynamic
clinical profile that complicates symptom assessment, particu-
larly due to intra-individual fluctuations that static analyses of-
ten fail to capture [10]. Yamamoto et al. (2020) [11] showed
that timing-related speech features tracked longitudinal varia-
tions in depression severity during repeated clinical interviews.
Related work has also suggested that vocal patterns may reflect
short-term changes in depressive symptoms [10]. However,
these studies mainly relied on correlational analyses and did
not model therapy-related, outcome-oriented speech dynamics.
In contrast, our work examines within-patient acoustic changes
across therapy sessions and relates them to treatment response.
Wang et al. (2023) [12] predicted depression severity
from acoustic features and reported voice markers associated
with symptom scores and pre–post ICBT changes. However,
their longitudinal analysis was limited to pre–post comparisons,
leaving session-by-session vocal dynamics largely unexplored.
Isacsson et al. (2024) [5] modeled symptom trajectories during
ICBT using longitudinal self-reported data and found compa-
rable performance between time-independent models, mixed-
effects models, and LSTM-based temporal approaches. The
limited benefit of explicitly time-dependent formulations sug-
gests that symptom scores alone may not fully capture treat-
ment dynamics, motivating the exploration of complementary
behavioral signals such as longitudinal vocal patterns.
3. Methodology
Fig. 1 presents the proposed pipeline for modeling within-
patient longitudinal speech trajectories and their association
with treatment outcomes. It includes the following steps:
1.Data Collection: Data were collected during the first
phase of the ongoing SOLITAIRE clinical trial (ID:
NCT06138301), with informed consent obtained under the
approved protocol. A total of35patients completed the CBT
protocol and pre-/post-treatment assessments and were in-
cluded in this study (24young adults (Y A) and11adoles-
cents (AD)). Participants were eligible if they scored above
42on the25-item Hikikomori Questionnaire (HQ-25) [4].
The intervention comprised eight CBT sessions conducted
via the COD20 telemedicine platform [13]. Audio was cap-
tured via the AWS Chime SDK under a standardized protocol
(48kHz, mono) to ensure acoustic comparability across ses-
sions. In compliance with ethical requirements, only acoustic
features were analyzed.
Acoustic feature extraction was performed in a container-
ized environment on a HPC server. Recordings were en-
crypted and raw audio was deleted after feature extraction.
The dataset comprises276.7hours of speech (approximately
59.3 minutes per session) segmented into 5-s windows to
balance temporal resolution and acoustic stability, consistent
with “thin slices” theory [14].
Mental health speech datasets are typically small and rarely
longitudinal (e.g., DAIC-WOZ, MODMA) [15]. In con-
trast, this dataset comprises real-world clinical recordings
collected across therapy sessions, and non-identifiable fea-
tures are available upon request to support reproducibility.
2.Signal Preprocessing: This procedure has two steps. i) Sig-
nal Cleansing: RMS normalization and pre-emphasis filter-
ing were applied to reduce recording variability and enhance
acoustic consistency [16]; and ii) Diarization: Patient speech
was isolated using clinician-annotated reference segments
andk-means clustering (k=2), with RMS-based filtering to
reduce silence-driven bias; labels were assigned via cosine
similarity to a patient prototype. Temporal smoothing and
minimum-duration constraints improved segmentation [17],
and diarization quality was evaluated using Diarization Error
Rate (DER) and Jaccard Error Rate (JER).
3.Feature Extraction and Session Summaries: Segment-
level acoustic features are extracted and aggregated into
session summaries, forming short patient-level time series
(S1–S8). For RQ1–RQ2, we focus on MFCCs to capture
compact spectral-envelope information while limiting feature
dimensionality given the cohort size [18]. RQ3 additionally
incorporates fundamental frequency (F0) and deep speech
representations (wav2vec 2.0). Following [9], we employ the
facebook/wav2vec2-base-960hmodel.
4.Intra-patient Normalization: To isolate within-subject tem-
poral variation, each patient trajectory is baseline-corrected
relative to the first session, as shown in equation 1:

4. Experimental results
Experiments were structured around three RQs linking longitu-
dinal vocal changes to therapy outcomes. RQ2 and RQ3 were
stratified by age (Y A/AD) and gender (FE/MA). The cohort
comprised 57.14% female and 42.86% male participants.
For RQ1, after signal cleansing, diarization was optimized
using an RMS percentile of40, a smoothing window of0.50s,
and a minimum segment duration of0.20s, achieving a DER
of0.1698and a JER of0.1698, indicating improved speaker
attribution accuracy. Temporal scores were then computed for
each feature using the entire dataset, and the top MFCCs are re-
ported in Table 1. Results show that lower-order coefficients
(MFCC1–MFCC3) exhibit the strongest longitudinal trends,
while MFCC4 and MFCC6 present lower early inter-subject
variability, suggesting greater stability as longitudinal markers.
In RQ2, we contrasted the mean longitudinal trajectories
of the predominant MFCC (RQ1) between theBetterand
Worseclasses. MFCC1, which was ranked as the most sta-
ble longitudinal marker, was used as a representative example.
Under age stratification (Figure 2), Y A cohort showed clearer
trajectory divergence emerging in mid-to-late sessions, with the
Bettergroup presenting more stable temporal patterns and
lower variability. In contrast, AD trajectories were noisier and
more heterogeneous, indicating greater inter-subject variability.

For RQ3, experiments were implemented in Python 3.10
using scikit-learn (v1.2) for MLP and PyTorch (v2.0) for the
wav2vec2-based GRU. MLP hyperparameters were optimized
via GridSearchCV with early stopping. For temporal modeling,
wav2vec2 embeddings were stored as.npytensors and trun-
cated or padded to a fixed length (T= 256). Fusion combined
GRU outputs from wav2vec2 with session-level MFCC+F0 de-
scriptors. Given the limited dataset size, the GRU used a sin-
gle recurrent layer (h= 128) followed by a two-layer MLP
head (dropout = 0.2), trained for 10 epochs per LOPO fold.
All models were optimized withBCEWithLogitsLossus-
ing per-fold class weights to address imbalance.

4.1. Discussion
Our findings extend prior work on speech-based depression
monitoring, where longitudinal analyses have often been lim-
ited to pre–post comparisons [12], by suggesting that session-
level speech trajectories may provide more informative mark-
ers of treatment outcome. They also complement longitudi-
nal ICBT studies based on symptom reports alone [5], sup-
porting the potential value of vocal signals as behavioral mark-
ers of therapy-related change. The improved performance of
fusion suggests that MFCC/F0 descriptors and self-supervised
speech representations capture complementary aspects of vo-
cal change, while univariate MFCC trajectories should be in-
terpreted as descriptive and hypothesis-generating rather than
standalone clinical markers. Nevertheless, this study has lim-
itations. First, clinical severity scores were available only at
pre- and post-treatment, limiting direct supervision of interme-
diate trajectory changes. Second, the dataset size was relatively
small, constraining model generalization.
5. Conclusion
This work suggests that therapy-related information is bet-
ter captured by longitudinal speech trajectories than by static
acoustic features. Stable MFCC markers showed structured
session-wise changes and differentiated outcome groups, while
wav2vec2 representations provided complementary predictive
information. Future work will focus on larger datasets, contin-
uous symptom monitoring, and broader feature sets.
