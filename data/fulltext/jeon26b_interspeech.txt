Abstract
This paper presents a speech enhancement framework for un-
manned aerial vehicle (UA V) audition systems operating un-
der severe ego-noise. UA V ego-noise exhibits three struc-
tural properties: strong temporal correlation, frequency-varying
noise composition, and amplitude stationarity, which respec-
tively destabilize direction-of-arrival (DoA) estimation, corrupt
noise covariance matrix (NCM) estimation, and cause residual
ego-noise to persist after beamforming. We utilize these proper-
ties to construct a drone-noise-aware speech enhancement sys-
tem. We obtain reliable DoA via phase-consistency–guided
time-frequency (TF) bin selection and use it to parameterize
both a crossover-based hybrid NCM estimation and post-filter
with a data-driven null direction and variance modulation for
suppressing residual noise. Experiments demonstrate98.12%
DoA accuracy at−25dBSNR with consistent SI-SDR and
speech quality improvements over baselines across anechoic
and reverberant conditions.
Index Terms: drone noise suppression, microphone array,
adaptive beamforming, DoA estimation
1. Introduction
The demand for UA Vs has increased rapidly, with applications
in entertainment, public safety, agriculture, and courier ser-
vices. In human-centered deployments, UA Vs can serve as mo-
bile acoustic platforms for spoken communication when people
are dispersed or hard to reach, motivating multimodal sensing
that complements vision with reliable audio. However, strong,
temporally correlated ego-noise often dominates UA V micro-
phones, limiting audio-based modalities under low input SNR
conditions; accordingly, various filtering techniques for UA V
audition have been proposed [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9].
Several beamforming techniques have been developed for
speech enhancement, including the delay-and-sum (DS) beam-
former, the minimum variance distortionless response (MVDR)
beamformer [10, 11], the superdirective beamformer [12, 13],
and the minimum mean square error (MMSE) filter beam-
former [4, 14, 15]. In challenging scenarios such as drone audi-
tion systems, adaptive beamformers have therefore been widely
adopted to enhance speech quality [3, 6].
This work explores a signal processing approach for UA V
audition that operates reliably in both anechoic and reverberant
conditions (Fig. 1). The novelty of this work lies in an ego-noise
informed spatial filtering that jointly designs DoA estimation,
NCM estimation, and residual noise suppression around the ob-
served properties of UA V noise. The proposed framework re-

4. Experimental Results
4.1. Dataset Preparation and Experiment Setup
Drone ego-noise was recorded using a Syma Z4W drone with
four Boya BY-M1 microphones with circular array of radius 0.1
m, positioned 15 cm below the drone.
For speech data, we selected 160 samples from the TIMIT
corpus [20], with a speech source at 1.0m from the array center,
synthesizing the multichannel speech signals using Pyrooma-
coustics [21] under anechoic and reverberant (T 60 = 0.3s)
conditions. Target DoAs were set to{−45,0,45} ◦, and input
SNRs to{−25,−20,−15,−10,−5}dB, resulting in 480 sam-
ples per SNR level. The0◦ direction is defined as the forward-
facing direction of the drone.
The noisy signals were sampled at 16 kHz and transformed
into the TF domain using STFT with a window length of 512
samples, with 75% overlap, and a Hanning window. We setν
via the spatial scale-matching criterion in Sec. 3.2, restricting
candidates to an SII-dominant band that accounts for 80.7% of
the SII-weighted speech energy [22]. This results inν= 3
kHz for our setup. We useσ= 10 ◦ andµ= 0.05as fixed
representative values across all SNRs and acoustic conditions.

4.2. Test Results
4.2.1. DoA Estimation
First, we evaluated DoA estimation performance under noisy
conditions. We compared the DoA estimation performance of
the proposed method with MUSIC [23], SRP-PHAT [17], and
wHisK, a drone noise–aware method [24]. We discretized the
target DoA at5 ◦ intervals. We usek= 2.5, following the
selection criterion described in Sec. 3.1.
Fig. 3 presents the DoA estimation accuracy and mean ab-
solute error (MAE) under both anechoic and reverberant condi-
tions. We regard estimates within±5◦ as correct when comput-
ing accuracy. Across all input SNR levels, the proposed method
outperforms other methods in both accuracy and MAE mea-
sures. Although all methods experience performance degrada-
tion as the input SNR decreases, the proposed method demon-
strates significantly improved robustness. At an input SNR of
−25dB, the proposed approach attains an accuracy of 98.12%
and 94.79% in the anechoic and reverberant case, with corre-
sponding MAE values of1.219◦ and3.052◦, respectively.
4.2.2. Speech Enhancement
We compared the speech enhancement performance of the pro-
posed algorithm with several beamforming baselines. Specif-
ically, we evaluated the proposed method against the MPDR,
MVDR with directional [11] and closeness gain, drone-tailored
MMSE beamformer [5], and a variant of the proposed method
without post-filtering. To assess speech quality, we employed
the SI-SDR [25], PESQ [26], ESTOI [27] as a main metric, and
the three components of DNSMOS P.835, namely SIG, BAK,
and OVRL [28], for auxiliary metric.
From the comparison results, the proposed method consis-
tently outperforms the existing approaches across intrusive ob-
jective metrics, SI-SDR, PESQ, and ESTOI, while achieving
consistent improvements in DNSMOS scores as provided in
Table 1. The performance gap is most pronounced at -25 dB,
where the proposed method improves SI-SDR by up to 7.7 dB
and 7.5 dB over the best-performing baseline in anechoic and
reverberant conditions, respectively.
We conduct an ablation study to evaluate the contribution of
each component in the proposed framework. We use MPDR as
the baseline, and the comparison with two MVDR approaches
isolates the effect of NCM estimation. Our method without
post-filtering reveals the impact of the hybrid masking strategy,
while the complete system quantifies the benefit of the post-
filtering stage.
Specifically, compared to the MPDR beamformer, MVDR
beamformer improves speech preservation, as evidenced by
higher ESTOI and SIG scores regardless of gain type. MVDR
with closeness gain outperforms directional gain, as its spatial
filtering better preserves speech intelligibility concentrated at
lower frequencies. The proposed method without post-filtering
further boosts performance by switching to directional gain
aboveν, where per-bin DoA estimates lose reliability and di-
rectional gain compensates with its narrowing mainlobe.
MMSE beamformer achieves strong background noise sup-
pression, reflected in higher PESQ and BAK scores. The
proposed post-filter complements MVDR by incorporating the
strength of MMSE, the residual noise reduction, while main-
taining speech preservation. Consequently, the full system im-
proves PESQ and BAK without degrading ESTOI, resulting in
consistent gains in SI-SDR and DNSMOS-OVRL.
5. Conclusion
This work addresses UA V speech enhancement under extreme
ego-noise by introducing an ego-noise-informed spatial filtering
framework. Its novelty lies in jointly organizing DoA estima-
tion, NCM estimation, and residual-noise suppression around
the observed structure of hovering UA V ego-noise. TF reliabil-
ity is inferred from temporal phase consistency to obtain robust
DoA estimates, which are reused as a shared spatial reference
for hybrid masking and null-reference post-filtering. The pro-
posed beamforming framework outperforms conventional base-
lines across both anechoic and reverberant input conditions, al-
though spatial filtering in non-hovering state of UA V remains as
a future work.
