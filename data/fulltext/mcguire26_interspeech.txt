Lexical stress-conditioned spatiotemporal gestural coordination in L2 English

Abstract
This study examines the articulatory realisation of lexical stress
in L2 English produced by speakers of Taiwanese Mandarin, a
language without a perceptually salient lexical stress contrast.
Electromagnetic articulography was used to record articulatory
data, and multivariate functional principal component analy-
sis with landmark-based temporal registration was applied to
analyse the spatiotemporal coordination of articulatory gestures
across a syllable. Accentedness ratings were used to contextu-
alise inter-speaker differences in stress-condition separation in
FPCA score space. Four of the six speakers showed evidence of
separation between stress conditions, whereas the two speakers
judged to have the strongest perceived foreign accents did not.
Index Terms: articulatory phonetics, lexical stress, second lan-
guage speech, electromagnetic articulography, gestural coordi-
nation
1. Introduction
Lexical stress is realised through multiple phonetic parameters
rather than a single cue. In English, stressed syllables are acous-
tically characterised by longer vowel duration and higher inten-
sity [1, 2]. Higher fundamental frequency (F0) has also been
associated with stress [3], though F0 is additionally conditioned
by higher-level prosodic structure, making its relationship to
lexical stress alone less straightforward [4]. In the articulatory
domain, stressed syllables have been described as involving hy-
perarticulated gestures, including greater spatial displacement
and longer gestural durations [5, 6]. Jaw movement has also
been discussed as an articulatory correlate of prominence more
generally [7]. Similar effects have been reported for Greek,
where stressed syllables show “longer, larger, and faster ges-
tures” relative to their unstressed counterparts [8]. Together,
this suggests that lexical stress contrasts involve coordinated
spatial and temporal reorganisation of gestural activity across
the syllable. This raises the question of how speakers whose L1
lacks lexical stress realise stress contrasts through supralaryn-
geal articulatory coordination in L2.
Taiwanese Mandarin (TWM) is a syllable-timed tonal lan-
guage that lacks a perceptually salient lexical stress contrast
[9, 10, 11]. Previous research shows that Mandarin-speaking
learners rely heavily on F0 to signal lexical stress in L2 En-
glish, producing larger F0 contrasts than native speakers [12].
Their use of vowel duration and intensity is broadly compara-
ble, but vowel reduction is inconsistently realised [12]. Kim et
al. [13] report broadly similar acoustic patterns and further ex-
amine the articulatory realisation of English lexical stress using
electromagnetic articulography (EMA). They found that both
L1 and L2 speakers showed greater jaw displacement and lip
aperture displacement in stressed vowels, although these effects
were larger in the L1 group, and only L1 speakers exhibited
consistent stress-conditioned differences in tongue dorsum dis-
placement. The analysis focused on vowel midpoint positions
and did not examine consonantal gestures or intergestural co-
ordination across the syllable; because the data were corpus-
based, sentence-level prosodic effects and differences in per-
ceived foreign accentedness were not taken into consideration.
The present study provides a preliminary investigation of
how L1 TWM speakers produce the lexical stress contrast in
L2 English by examining the spatial and temporal organisation
of gestures across the syllable. We examine the syllable/flIkt/
from the noun–verb pairCONflict–conFLICT, which provides
a complex onset and coda involving distinct articulators, allow-
ing constriction targets to be clearly delineated in the articula-
tory record. The syllable is defined articulatorily, from the onset
of movement of the initial gesture to the offset of movement of
the final gesture, rather than by acoustic landmarks. This struc-
ture enables detailed examination of stress-conditioned differ-
ences in spatial displacement and temporal coordination across
the syllable.
To analyse the organisation of gestures, we use functional
principal component analysis (FPCA) with landmark-based
temporal registration. This aligns key constriction events across
tokens while capturing both spatial and temporal variation in
the articulatory trajectories. We then test whether stressed and
unstressed tokens form separable patterns in this multidimen-
sional space, without using acoustic information. Accentedness
ratings provide descriptive context for interpreting variation in
stress-condition separation across speakers.
2. Methods
2.1. Participants, stimuli and recording procedures
Ten native speakers of Taiwanese Mandarin were originally re-
cruited for this experiment. All participants were university stu-
dents in their twenties. The materials consisted of disyllabic
minimal pairs differing only in stress location. Target words
were embedded in the carrier phrase “Say again” and
read from a screen in pseudorandomised order. Participants
completed 8–10 repetitions of each item. The sensors rele-
vant to this study are TT (tongue tip), TD (tongue dorsum), LL
(lower lip), and JAW (lower incisor).
Articulatory data were recorded using electromagnetic ar-
ticulography (Carstens AG501) at a sampling rate of 1,250 Hz
and later down-sampled to 250 Hz. Sensors were attached to
the lips, tongue, and lower incisor (for tracking jaw movement),
as well as to the right and left mastoid processes and the upper
incisor (for head-movement correction).

this original dataset. For the present analysis, we focus solely

2.2. Accentedness rating
Speakers’ pronunciation was evaluated using listener judgments
of perceived foreign accentedness. Two experienced English
language instructors served as raters. Both are L1 English
speakers with extensive experience teaching English in Taiwan;
one is a member of the research team, and the other was, at the
time of data collection, a PhD student in a phonetics laboratory.
Prior to analysis, the raters evaluated recordings of each partic-
ipant reading a stimulus paragraph and assigned a score on a
9-point accentedness-rating scale, where 1 indicated no or min-
imal perceived foreign accent and 9 indicated a very strong per-
ceived foreign accent [14]. Ratings were based on overall pro-
nunciation, including segmental realisation and prosody. Inter-
rater agreement was high (ICC(2,2) = 0.947, absolute agree-
ment). The two ratings were averaged to yield a single accent-
edness rating (AR) for each speaker (see Table 1). Given the
small number of speakers, AR was used descriptively to con-
textualise speaker-level patterns rather than as a predictor in a
statistical model.
2.3. Articulatory measurements
Articulatory measurements were made in MATLAB using
MVIEW [15]. Thefindgestalgorithm was used to delineate
the four primary gestures in the syllable/flIkt/. Gestures were
identified in the vertical (z) dimension of the relevant sensors:
LL for/f/, TT for/l/, TD for/k/, and TT for/t/.
Thefindgesttimestamps were used to identify the four con-
sonantal gestures in the syllable/flIkt/and to define tempo-
ral landmarks for analysis. The onset of movement toward the
initial/f/(GONS) was taken as the beginning of the syllable
and is referred to below as Landmark 1. Landmark 2 corre-
sponds to the achievement of the/f/constriction (NONS), fol-

2.4. FPCA
Functional principal component analysis (FPCA) has been ap-
plied to a range of dynamic phonetic data, including acoustic
and articulatory trajectories [16, 17, 18, 19]. In the present
study, multivariate FPCA with landmark-based temporal reg-
istration was applied to the articulatory trajectories following
the framework described by Gubian and colleagues, with im-
plementation based on the accompanying tutorial materials and
code examples [20, 21]. Landmark registration aligned key con-
striction events across tokens prior to FPCA. In cases where ad-
jacent landmarks occurred in an unexpected order, monotonic
ordering was enforced by shifting the earlier-than-expected
landmark 1 ms past its predecessor. This minimum perturba-
tion was applied solely to satisfy the mathematical requirements
of landmark registration and affected only 6 out of 98 tokens.
Landmarks were defined for the consonantal gestures (LL, TT,
and TD), but not for the JAW trajectory, because jaw movement
substantially overlapped with the tongue dorsum gesture. The
JAW trajectory was therefore included only as a spatial dimen-
sion constrained by the global syllable onset and offset land-
marks.

3.2. FPCA analysis
The FPCA analysis combines spatial variation in the articula-
tory trajectories with temporal variation captured by landmark
registration. Spatial dimensions comprised the vertical trajecto-
ries of LL, TT, TD, and JAW, while temporal variation was rep-
resented by the log time-warping function. This allowed stress-
conditioned differences to be examined as joint spatiotemporal
patterns across the syllable, rather than as either landmark tim-
ing or articulatory displacement alone.
For each speaker, separation between stressed and un-
stressed tokens in the two-dimensional FPCA score space
(PC1–PC2) was assessed using PERMANOV A (999 permuta-
tions; Table 2). A permutation-based test was used because
the small and unequal per-speaker token counts made the dis-
tributional and covariance assumptions of MANOV A difficult
to justify. Table 2 reports both the permutationp-values and the
associatedR 2 (variance explained by stress condition) for each
speaker; given the limited data, these statistics are treated as de-
scriptive and should be interpreted alongside the score plots in
Fig. 3.

4. Discussion
The present study asked whether Taiwanese Mandarin speakers’
production of English lexical stress is reflected in the suprala-
ryngeal articulatory record alone. This question is motivated
by previous work showing that Mandarin-speaking learners rely
heavily on F0 when producing lexical stress in English [12].
The aim was therefore to examine whether stressed and un-
stressed tokens show separable, interpretable patterns of artic-
ulatory coordination when spatial trajectories and landmark-
based timing information are analysed together without acous-
tic information. Rather than testing whether stress condition
could be predicted from the articulatory record using a clas-
sifier, FPCA with landmark-based registration was used be-
cause it allows stress-conditioned differences to be examined in
a reduced-dimensional space while still providing interpretable
spatial and temporal perturbation patterns.
Stressed and unstressed productions of/flIkt/showed sepa-
ration in FPCA score space for four of the six speakers, whereas
the two speakers with the highest AR values did not show such
separation. This indicates that, for these speakers, supralaryn-
geal articulatory patterns carried enough stress-related informa-
tion to distinguish the two conditions in FPCA score space,
without using acoustic information in the analysis. The speaker-
level pattern is also consistent with the possibility that speakers
with lower AR values use articulatory strategies more similar to
those reported for L1 stress production.
The perturbation plots suggest that, for the speakers show-
ing separation, the stress contrast involved joint spatiotemporal
reorganisation across the syllable. Stressed tokens were gen-
erally associated with expansion of the registered time axis,
greater jaw lowering during the vocalic interval, and a higher
lower-lip position around achievement of the onset/f/con-
striction. These patterns are broadly consistent with previous
descriptions of stress-related gestural modulation in L1 speech
[5, 6, 8]. Importantly, the landmark-timing results showed
longer stressed syllables for all speakers, but syllable dura-
tion alone did not determine separation in FPCA score space:
01M showed one of the largest stressed–unstressed differences
in overall syllable duration, yet did not show clear stress-
conditioned separation in FPCA score space. This suggests that
the observed separation reflects coordinated spatial and tempo-
ral differences, rather than syllable duration alone.
The two speakers with the highest AR values also pro-
duced target-achievement reversals in the onset or coda clus-
ters. Speaker 07F produced cases in which L3 preceded L2, and
speaker 01M produced cases in which L5 preceded L4. These
reversals suggest less stable temporal sequencing of consonan-
tal constrictions for these speakers and may contribute to the
absence of clear stress-conditioned separation in FPCA score
space. However, given the small number of speakers and to-
kens, the relationship between AR, gestural sequencing, and
stress-conditioned separation should be treated as exploratory.
Overall, the results show that FPCA with landmark-based
temporal registration provides a useful framework for exam-
ining stress-conditioned spatiotemporal coordination across an
entire syllable. Future work should extend this approach to
larger datasets with more speakers, more target items, and more
varied syllable structures, allowing the relationship between
AR, acoustic cue use, and articulatory coordination to be mod-
elled more directly.
