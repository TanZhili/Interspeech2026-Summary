Abstract
This paper proposes a Weakly Masked Residual Reliabil-
ity Learning (WMR 2L) framework to enhance the robust-
ness of speech models in cross-domain scenarios. WMR 2L
jointly models prediction confidence and residual dispersion
for pseudo-label weighting, enabling the separation of reli-
able predictions from overconfident ones. To mitigate supervi-
sion bias introduced by selective pseudo-labeling, we propose
a weak confidence masking strategy that partially masks high-
confidence regions, thereby promoting learning from less reli-
able regions. In addition, a multi-perturbation consistency reg-
ularization is designed to further improve pseudo-label quality.
Experimental results show that WMR 2L consistently outper-
forms existing baselines across multiple cross-domain speech
recognition tasks, achieving relative WER reductions of 13.8%
on CHiME-4 (noisy), 25.0% on SLURP (human–machine in-
teraction), and 15.7% on CORAAL (accented), and also yields
notable improvements on speech translation tasks. Our code

1. Introduction
End-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) mod-
els rely on large-scale parameters and training data, and have
achieved strong performance across a wide range of scenar-
ios [1, 2]. However, when confronted with unseen domain data,
such as acoustic conditions involving noise or accents, their per-
formance still degrades significantly [3, 4]. In such scenarios,
conventional approaches typically depend on fully supervised
training with labeled data from the target domain [5–7]. Never-
theless, acquiring high-quality annotations is extremely costly
and often constrained by privacy protection and data-sharing
limitations [8, 9], which restricts the applicability of traditional
domain adaptation methods. Under the setting where only a pre-
trained speech model and a limited amount of unlabeled target-
domain speech are available, enabling models to acquire robust-
ness to the target domain through self-train has become an ac-
tive research topic [10, 11].
To address this problem, numerous methods have been ex-
plored, including pseudo-labeling [12, 13] and consistency reg-

sign labels to unlabeled samples [16], while consistency regu-
larization methods [17, 18] follow the smoothness assumption,
encouraging the model to produce consistent predictions un-
der different perturbations of the same input [19]. However,
pseudo-labels often contain substantial noise, and directly using
them may mislead the model or even degrade performance [20].
Traditional pseudo-label selection strategies rely on fixed confi-
dence thresholds or simple confidence ranking. However, under
domain shift, model overconfidence makes these strategies un-
reliable, as predictions tend to concentrate on overly confident
tokens [21]. Moreover, although down-weighting unreliable to-
kens can reduce the introduction of erroneous supervision, it
also leads to insufficient training signals near decision bound-
aries and in complex acoustic regions [22, 23].
To address these challenges, we propose Weakly Masked
Residual Reliability Learning (WMR 2L) for pseudo-labeling.
WMR2L exploits the observation that reliable predictions ex-
hibit both high confidence and sufficient dispersion among
non-maximum class probabilities, enabling it to distinguish
trustworthy predictions from overconfident ones and stabilize
pseudo-label reweighting. To further mitigate supervision bias
from selective pseudo-labeling, we introduce a weak confidence
masking strategy that encourages semantic recovery from unre-
liable regions while preserving informative content.
For consistency regularization in speech labels, prior work
has constructed perturbations using Monte Carlo sampling or
Gaussian noise injection [19, 24]. However, such perturbations
mainly operate at the model level and lack sufficient explo-
ration of speech-level perturbations, resulting in limited capa-
bility to filter low-quality pseudo-labels. We propose a multi-
perturbation consistency regularization that explores a variety
of speech perturbations, including spectral masking, temporal
cropping and segment substitution, reverberation, and filtering.
This allows us to comprehensively evaluate the reliability of
pseudo-labels under diverse perturbations.
Experimental results demonstrate that our method signifi-
cantly outperforms existing baselines on multiple cross-domain
ASR tasks. Specifically, it reduces WER by 13.8%, 25.0%, and
15.7% on the CHiME-4 dataset (noisy), the SLURP dataset (hu-
man–machine interaction scenarios), and the CORAAL dataset
(accented), respectively. Our approach also achieves strong per-
formance on speech translation tasks. The main contributions of
this paper are summarized as follows:

2. Related Work
Pseudo Labeling.Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) has
become a key approach for cross-domain speech recognition,
enabling adaptation without manual annotation via pseudo-
labeling [24–26]. In this setting, pseudo-label quality is crit-
ical to domain adaptation performance [27, 28]. To improve
reliability, prior work has adopted information maximization
strategies [29] to estimate confidence and uncertainty, includ-
ing entropy- and energy-based metrics [30, 31], prompt depen-
dency [32], and attention-based methods [24]. In addition, Pu-
lakurthiet al.indirectly assess pseudo-label reliability by con-
straining the sharpness of output distributions [26]. While these
approaches reduce pseudo-label noise, they are limited by the
low separability of confidence scores. This limitation often
leads to biased supervision signals [33].
Consistency Regularization.One line of work exploits un-
certainty derived from different decoding strategies to impose
consistency constraints [34–36]. Another line of research lever-
ages confidence estimation systems, training separate neural
networks [37, 38] to assess the reliability of pseudo-labels. In
the image domain, consistency is enforced through multiple
perturbations [39, 40]. Inspired by these approaches, we extend
multi-perturbation consistency to speech, using diverse speech
perturbations for more reliable consistency regularization and
pseudo-label filtering.
3. Method

els. To this end, we propose WMR 2L for reliable pseudo-
label weighting, together with multi-perturbation consistency
regularization for effective pseudo-label filtering. The overall

4. Experiments

We evaluate WMR 2L across diverse domains with unlabeled
speech data. OnCHiME-4[41], we train on thetrain-realchan-
nel 1 subset and evaluate on the real and simulateddev/testsets.
ForCORAAL[42], we use 3000 utterances from African Amer-
ican English speakers, split into 2000 training, 500 validation,
and 500 test samples.SLURP[43] is a dataset for real-world hu-
man–machine interaction, containing natural spoken commands
between users and voice assistants. For multilingual speech
translation, we useCoVoST2[44], a Common V oice–based cor-
pus, and experiment with Estonian, Indonesian, and Welsh.

4.3. Effectiveness of Our Method
Comparison with Baseline and Existing Methods.As shown
in Table 1, the baseline method (Base: Whisper) shows im-
provement after self-training, but error rates remain high.STAR
introduces consistency constraints but struggles with effectively
filtering reliable pseudo-labels. Methods likeConfidence+MP,
Entropy+MP, andMargin+MPuse token-level indicators com-
bined with multi-perturbation, achieving some improvement,
but their WERs remain suboptimal due to their insensitivity to
non-maximum classes under overconfident predictions.
In contrast,WMR 2L+MPachieves the best performance
across all datasets, reducing WER by up to 25.0% on SLURP
and 15.7% on CORAAL compared to the baseline. These re-
sults show that WMR 2L is an effective token-level weight-
ing strategy. Furthermore, compared toWMR 2L+Beam,
WMR2L+Sample, andWMR 2L+No,WMR 2L+MPconsistently
achieves the best performance, showing that model-only un-
certainty is insufficient to characterize prediction reliability.
WMR2L+UAis suboptimal, whereas data-driven consistency
regularization is more effective for pseudo-label filtering.
Speech Translation Performance.As shown in Table 2, self-
training improves performance on most languages (e.g., Indone-
sian from 38.8 to 41.6). WMR 2L further boosts performance,
achieving BLEU scores of 10.5, 41.8, and 13.6 on Estonian, In-
donesian, and Welsh, respectively, narrowing the gap to fully
supervised training.
Scalability to Larger Models.As shown in Table 3, WMR 2L
achieves consistent WER reductions over both zero-shot and
self-train on Whisper Large-v3.
4.4. Ablation Study
Ablation Analysis of Masking Variants.As shown in Table 4,
applying strong masking (SMR2L) degrades performance, with
WER increasing on CHiME-4. In contrast, the weak mask-
ing variant (WMR2L) yields consistent improvements, achiev-
ing better results on both CHiME-4 and CORAAL. When com-
bined with multi-perturbation (WMR2L+MP), the lowest WER
is achieved on both datasets. These results highlight the effec-
tiveness of weak confidence masking and the consistent benefits
of multi-perturbation across reliability learning variants.

5. Conclusion
This paper proposes a Weakly Masked Residual Reliability
Learning framework that enhances cross-domain performance
of speech models by improving pseudo-label reliability. The
proposed weak confidence masking strategy, together with
multi-perturbation consistency regularization, further improves
model generalization under diverse acoustic conditions. Exper-
imental results show that our approach consistently outperforms
existing baselines and offers a practical solution for domain
adaptation in both ASR and speech translation tasks.
