Abstract
On-device keyword spotting (KWS) requires strictly causal
models for zero-lookahead streaming. Distilling from non-
causal teachers introduces causality mismatch: teacher features
encode future context. Point-wise regression to these absolute
features creates capacity conflicts that severely hinder continu-
ous inference. To mitigate this, we propose Causal Temporal
Relation Distillation, transferring intra-sample temporal topol-
ogy instead of point-wise features. Applying a lower-triangular
mask to relation matrices establishes a strictly realizable histor-
ical anchor. Our offline bidirectional variant (Ours-Bi) utilizes
the teacher’s full topology as an auxiliary prior without altering
the zero-lookahead inference graph. On Google Speech Com-
mands V2, Ours-Bi improves causal 1D-CNN accuracy from
95.12% to 96.91%. Under a continuous-stream protocol, it
halves the False Rejection Rate (8.52% to 4.15% at 1.0 FA/h),
demonstrating vastly superior long-context robustness.
Index Terms: keyword spotting, knowledge distillation,
streaming inference, causality mismatch, temporal relation
1. Introduction
Keyword spotting (KWS) serves as the primary entry point for
on-device spoken interaction. Constrained by battery and com-
pute resources, edge-deployed KWS models must maintain low
parameter counts and multiply-accumulate operations (MACs).
To ensure real-time responses without introducing algorithmic
lookahead latency, these lightweight networks are typically de-
signed as strictly causal streaming architectures, where predic-
tions depend only on current and past acoustic inputs [1, 2, 3].
Transformer-based sequence models (e.g., AST [4]) pro-
vide global temporal modeling capabilities and are often used as
high-capacity teachers in cross-architecture knowledge distilla-
tion (KD) [5, 6, 7, 8]. In this paradigm, a non-causal sequence
model provides offline supervision, while a lightweight causal
model is retained for student-only streaming inference.
Although practically meaningful for deployment,
intermediate-level knowledge transfer faces two challenges.
The first is the mismatch in temporal resolution and hidden
dimensionality, commonly addressed via feature projection
or temporal pooling. The second is a deployment-relevant
physical constraint:causality mismatch[9, 10]. A non-causal
teacher’s per-step features implicitly encode future context.
Forcing a causal student to point-wise regress these absolute
features under a limited historical receptive field creates an
inconsistency between the supervision signal and the student’s
realizable capacity. In practice, this mismatch can reduce
transfer effectiveness [11, 12].

To address this issue, we propose a Causal Temporal Re-
lation Distillation framework. Unlike conventional mini-batch
relational KD, our method aligns the relative evolution topol-
ogy along the temporal dimension within each sample [13]. We
first map heterogeneous features to a unified temporal abstrac-
tion granularity via parameter-free pooling to construct com-
parable relation structures. Crucially, we introduce alower-
triangular causal maskto remove future-dependent entries
from the teacher’s relation matrix. This aligns the relation distil-
lation objective with streaming causality constraints, mitigating
supervision conflicts.
Furthermore, motivated by the concept of training-time
privileged information [14], we investigate an offline bidirec-
tional variant (Ours-Bi). Rather than discarding the lower-
triangular mask, this variant builds upon the strictly realiz-
able historical anchor and additionally utilizes the teacher’s full
topology matrix as an anticipatory regularization signal during
offline training. By encouraging the student to implicitly align
its internal manifold with a global topology under a limited
historical receptive field, Ours-Bi safely transforms the once-
harmful causality mismatch into beneficial guidance. As this
full-matrix supervision participates only in backpropagation, it
introduces no additional inference cost and perfectly preserves
the zero-lookahead on-device forward graph. Evaluated on the
Google Speech Commands V2 (12-class) benchmark [15], our
main contributions are:
• We identify the capacity conflicts caused by causality mis-
match in cross-architecture streaming distillation, particu-
larly how point-wise regression to non-causal absolute fea-
tures severely hinders continuous long-horizon inference.
• We propose Causal Temporal Relation Distillation, utiliz-
ing a lower-triangular mask to transfer intra-sample historical
topology under strict zero-lookahead constraints.
• We introduce an offline bidirectional variant (Ours-Bi) that
securely grounds full-matrix topology as an anticipatory priv-
ileged prior, incorporating global context awareness without
compromising causality or deployment cost.
• Beyond closed-set accuracy and per-step latency, we estab-
lish a rigorous continuous-stream event-level evaluation pro-
tocol (FRR @ FA/h), demonstrating vastly improved long-
context noise robustness.

2.3. Offline Bidirectional Relation Distillation Variant
(Ours-Bi)
While forcing a strictly causal student to regress point-wise ab-
solute future features introduces severe capacity conflicts (as
discussed in Sec. 2.1), the relative temporal topology of the fu-
ture encapsulates valuable macroscopic evolution rather than
exact acoustic values. Building upon the strictly realizable
causal foundation established in Sec. 2.2, we further introduce
an offline bidirectional variant (Ours-Bi) to safely harness this
future context.
Since training is performed offline, the full input sequence
and the non-causal teacher features are globally accessible.
Ours-Bi introduces an additional supervision term that utilizes
the teacher’s full temporal relation matrix (including future-
context associations):

Crucially, in our implementation, Ours-Bi is optimized by
addingL bi on top ofL rel. The causal mask (M) is not
discarded; rather, it serves as a stable, conflict-free historical
anchor. Grounded firmly on this realizable anchor, the full-
matrix topology (L bi) functions as a soft privileged prior [14].
It encourages the student, under a limited historical receptive
field, to implicitly align its internal manifold with a global full-
context topology. This gracefully transforms the once-harmful
causality mismatch into beneficial anticipatory regularization.
Ours-Bi only modifies the offline training loss terms; it strictly
preserves the student’s causal forward architecture, input cache
length, and zero-lookahead temporal access pattern during in-
ference.
2.4. Training Objective and Deployment Properties
The unified joint training objective is defined as
L=L ce +λ kdLkd +λ relLrel +λ biLbi,(8)
whereL ce denotes the classification cross-entropy loss andL kd
denotes the output-level logits distillation loss.
For the compared settings,λ rel >0andλ bi = 0corre-
spond to the unidirectional temporal relation distillation (Ours),
whileλ bi >0corresponds to the offline variant with additional
bidirectional supervision (Ours-Bi).
Mathematically, optimizing the joint objective (λ relLrel +
λbiLbi) is elegantly equivalent to applying anasymmetric tem-
poral relation constraint. The strictly realizable causal histor-
ical dependencies (the lower triangle of the relation matrix) are
heavily weighted by (λ rel +λ bi) to ensure absolute fidelity to
the streaming receptive field. Conversely, the non-causal future
dependencies (the upper triangle) are decayed into a softer aux-
iliary regularization penalized only byλ bi. This mathematical
asymmetry perfectly mirrors the physical causality constraints
of zero-lookahead on-device inference.
Because the computation of relation matrices and the full-
size relation matching operations are entirely decoupled from
edge-side inference, the actual deployment cost is determined
solely by the selected 1D-CNN backbone, preserving strict
causality and ultra-low per-step latency without any algorithmic
overhead.
3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol
Datasets and Input Features.We evaluate on Google
Speech Commands V2 (GSC v2) following the standard 12-
class protocol (10 commands +Silence/Unknown) and the of-
ficial train/val/test split. Audio is resampled to 16 kHz and
padded/cropped to 1.0 s. Inputs are 64-d log-Mel spectro-
grams (25 ms window, 10 ms hop;98×64). A unified aug-
mentation pipeline (time shift±0.1 s, noise mixing with SNR∼
U[−5,10]dB, and frequency masking) is applied to all meth-
ods. Closed-set accuracy is reported as mean±standard devia-
tion over three training seeds.
Network Architectures and Training Details.The
teacher is AST [4] (AudioSet-pretrained, fine-tuned on GSC
v2;∼85M params; frozen for distillation). To strictly isolate
the effectiveness of distillation mechanisms from architectural
engineering tricks, we intentionally adopt an extremely mini-
malist, pure causal 1D-CNN baseline (only 5.2M MACs) with-
out advanced gating or complex self-attention. This highly con-
strained capacity provides an unsaturated testbed to clearly ob-
serve the relative gains of mitigating causality mismatch. All
student-based methods share this identical backbone and causal
forward graph (0 ms lookahead). Training uses AdamW for
120 epochs (5 warm-up), batch size 128, LR10 −3 with cosine
annealing; Vanilla KD uses a temperature ofτ KD = 4. For
feature-level distillation, we use a single distilled layer: the last
temporal encoding layer. For AST, we remove the classifica-
tion ([CLS]) token and average the time-frequency (TF) patches
along the frequency dimension to obtain a temporal represen-
tationH T . Feature KD uses a training-only linear projection
to match the teacher and student hidden dimensions. We set
L= 24,λ kd = 0.5,λ rel = 1.0, andλ bi = 0.3; the latter two
values were selected on the validation set and were not tuned on
the test stream.
Edge-Deployment Evaluation Metrics.Deployment cost
is reported by Params and MACs. Streaming latency is mea-
suredper inference stepon Raspberry Pi 4B (ARM Cortex-
A72) with ONNX Runtime (CPU): we update the causal cache
using 20 ms chunks and time only the single-step forward pass
(500 steps after warm-up; variance<0.1ms 2). Except for loss
definitions, all methods use identical student backbones, data,
optimizer, and post-processing.

3.4. Continuous Streaming Noise Robustness
We evaluate always-on robustness using continuous streams
dynamically constructed by embedding more than 4K
class-balanced GSC v2 test keywords and inserting im-
poster/Unknown events at∼2×the target count (minimum
onset spacing 1.5s). Background audio mixes non-stationary
environmental noises (e.g., MUSAN) with test-setUnknown
words. Following the event-level streaming metric FRR @
FA/h [20, 6, 21, 22], all strictly causal models share identical de-
tection post-processing: 50 ms moving-average smoothing and
a 500 ms refractory window. Thresholds are calibrated on a val-
idation stream to meet a target FA/h (1.0 or 2.0) and then locked
for evaluation on an unseen test stream; hits are matched within
±200ms and FA/FRR are computed at the event level. The
non-causal AST teacher is excluded.
As shown in Table 4, Feature KD underperforms Vanilla
KD at 1.0 FA/h (7.60% vs. 7.15%), while Ours-Bi achieves
the best robustness, reducing FRR from 8.52% to 4.15% under
the same constraint. Because all student variants use the same
causal forward graph and identical post-processing, Ours-Bi in-
troduces no additional algorithmic lookahead or per-step infer-
ence cost. This supports that relation-based distillation trans-
fers a more stable long-horizon structure than point-wise fea-
ture regression, and that streaming robustness is not necessarily
reflected by closed-set accuracy alone.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we investigated causality mismatch in cross-
architecture distillation for streaming KWS. We identified that
forcing causal students to point-wise regress non-causal abso-
lute features introduces capacity conflicts that severely degrade
continuous robustness. To mitigate this, we proposed Causal
Temporal Relation Distillation. By replacing point-wise re-
gression with intra-sample temporal topology alignment via a
lower-triangular mask, our method establishes a strictly real-
izable historical anchor. Furthermore, our offline bidirectional
variant (Ours-Bi) integrates the teacher’s full-context topology
as an auxiliary prior safely grounded on this causal founda-
tion. Evaluations on Google Speech Commands V2 demon-
strate that our approach achieves the best closed-set accuracy
among the compared methods and nearly halves the false re-
jection rate in rigorous continuous-stream detection. Crucially,
these substantial robustness gains introduce zero additional pa-
rameters, MACs, or algorithmic latency to the edge-deployed
causal student. These conclusions are limited to the evalu-
ated GSC v2, AST-to-1D-CNN, strict zero-lookahead setting;
broader datasets, teacher-student pairs, and causal architectures
remain for future work.
