Abstract
Assistive technology requires understanding complex audio
which involves jointly modeling speech content, emotional
cues, and environmental context. We propose a Gated Multi-
Task model that performs automatic speech recognition, speech
emotion recognition, and acoustic scene classification, often
treated as independent tasks, within a unified framework. Built
on the Whisper-small encoder, the model employs task-specific
heads and a gating mechanism that classifies input audio as
clean speech, noisy speech, or non-speech to conditionally ac-
tivate downstream modules during inference. We introduce
ESAS-32K, a simulated dataset combining emotional speech
with acoustic scenes under varying signal-to-noise conditions.
Experiments show that Whisper-based gated multi-task learn-
ing improves task-specific metrics compared to ungated and
single-task baselines, and mitigates speech-to-text hallucina-
tions, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method
for context-aware audio analysis.

1. Introduction
Human speech conveys not only linguistic content but also rich
paralinguistic and contextual information, including emotional
state, health, age, gender, cultural background, and environmen-
tal cues [1]. This information is important for a sensitive assis-
tive technology based on audio. Traditional audio processing
systems have largely focused on single tasks such as voice activ-
ity detection (V AD) [2], keyword spotting [3], speaker recogni-
tion [4], language identification [5], speech emotion recognition
(SER) [6], automatic speech recognition (ASR) [1], sound event
detection (SED) [7], and acoustic scene classification (ASC)
[8]. These tasks analyze speech and non-speech acoustic sig-
nals, and have critical applications in human–computer interac-
tion, mental health assessment [9, 10], emergency alerts [11],
elder-care monitoring [12], and environmental monitoring [13].
In real-world scenarios, intelligent auditory systems must op-
erate effectively across diverse and dynamic acoustic environ-
ments such as homes, parks, or crowded public spaces. For
instance, a smart assistant supporting elderly people may need
to simultaneously transcribe speech for issue comprehension
(ASR), detect emotional distress (SER), and recognize environ-
mental context (ASC). Similarly, an auditory agent deployed
in public spaces could transcribe calls for help, assess urgency
from vocal cues, and infer scene context to trigger timely re-
sponses. In such cases, selectively activating only relevant tasks

is crucial for both resource efficiency and quick response. These
use cases motivate unified audio models that exhibit situational
awareness by jointly modeling speech, emotion, and environ-
mental context.
Recent transformer-based foundation models such as Whis-
per [14], Wav2Vec 2.0 [15], HuBERT [16], and WavLM
[17] have demonstrated strong generalization across speech
tasks due to large-scale pre-training on diverse and noisy data.
Beyond ASR, these models have achieved competitive per-
formance in V AD, SER, speech translation, SED, and ASC
[18, 19, 20, 7]. However, deploying independent models for
each task is often inefficient under real-time and memory con-
straints. Multi-task learning (MTL) with shared representations
has therefore emerged as a promising alternative. MTL involves
performing two or more tasks within a single model. Several
MTL frameworks have been proposed in recent years. Chen
et al. [21] introduced MTLSER, a Wav2Vec 2.0-based model
jointly performing ASR, SER, and speaker identification. Whis-
per is augmented with auxiliary heads for noise or background
classification to improve robustness in specific speech applica-
tions [22, 13]. Another study developed a transformer-based
dual-decoder architecture for simultaneous speech and back-
ground sound classification [23], showing improved robustness
under low SNR conditions. These studies indicate the potential
of shared encoders for noisy speech understanding.
Whisper [14] is particularly attractive for MTL as it retains
noise-variant representations that implicitly encode contextual
sound information in noisy environments [24, 22, 25]. How-
ever, despite its strong performance in noisy ASR, Whisper is
prone to hallucinations during inference, generating spurious
or repetitive transcriptions on silent or non-speech segments
[26, 27]. Existing mitigation strategies, such as external V AD-
based filtering [28] or decoding constraints [26], are typically
applied post hoc and do not address intrinsic task routing. Ad-
ditionally, Whisper does not explicitly leverage scene context in
spite of adequately representing it. These limitations motivate
our proposedGated Multi-Task Whisperframework. We jointly
model ASR, SER, and ASC using a learnable V AD-like gate
that dynamically activates task heads based on input type [29],
enabling efficient inference while suppressing hallucinations in
Whisper on non-speech segments.
Our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose a gated MTL architecture built on Whisper that
conditionally activates ASR, SER, and ASC heads based on
the predicted input type.
• We simulate a mixed-corpus, mixed-SNR dataset named
ESAS-32K comprising over 32,000 samples by combining
emotional speech and acoustic scene datasets.

novation is athree-wayV AD-inspired gate that conditionally

2.5. Inference and Conditional Routing
During inference, task execution is conditioned onˆygate as fol-
lows:
•ˆygate == 0: ASR + SER
•ˆygate == 1: ASC only
•ˆygate == 2: ASR + SER + ASC
This gate-controlled routing reduces computation and sup-
presses ASR hallucinations on non-speech inputs by avoiding
unnecessary decoding [26].

The model is initialized from theWhisper-small.en1 variant pre-
trained on 680,000 hours of multilingual and noise-rich audio.
It consists of a 12-layer Transformer encoder and 12-layer de-
coder with a hidden size of 768. Optimization is performed

Most notably, the integrated gating mechanism sup-

4.2. Importance of Gating Mechanism
Table 2 compares the proposed model with and without the gat-
ing mechanism. While SER and ASC accuracy and F1-scores
show only marginal degradation in the ungated setting, the ASR
hallucination rate increases drastically. More than 96% of non-
speech samples in the ungated model produce meaningless or
inappropriate transcriptions, including repetitions and profani-
ties [26]. This behavior persists even when repetition penalties
are applied, confirming that decoding constraints alone are in-
sufficient. The gate-controlled architecture is therefore essential
for reliable ASR in mixed speech and non-speech environments.
4.3. Comparison with single task baseline models
The proposed model consistently outperforms or matches
single-task and state-of-the-art baselines across all evaluated
tasks (see Table 3). For SER, the proposed multi-task model
surpasses both Whisper-SER and the 2D-CNN baseline de-
rived from [35], indicating that shared representation learning
improves emotion classification in noisy environments when
trained jointly with ASR and gating. It is important to note that
SER models are trained and evaluated exclusively on speech
samples (i.e., clean and noisy speech segments). For ASC, per-
formance is slightly lower than the Inception-ResNet baseline
but remains consistent, even under reduced supervision. The
individual ASC task is applied only to non-speech and noisy
speech samples to determine the model’s ability to general-
ize scene understanding within a unified framework. The pro-
posed V AD gate achieves superior performance compared to
both feature-based and Whisper-based single-task baselines, ef-
fectively performing three-class discrimination between clean
speech, noisy speech, and non-speech. Most notably, the gate-
controlled architecture nearly eliminates ASR hallucinations.
Among all evaluated models, only the single-task ASR base-
line is trained and tested on the full ESAS-32K dataset (in-
cluding non-speech segments) to assess susceptibility to hal-
lucinations in noisy audio, where it exhibits severe degrada-
tion. While state-of-the-art baselines are trained independently
for each task, the proposed method leverages pre-trained Whis-
per weights with joint optimization across four tasks and still
achieves comparable or superior performance, demonstrating
the effectiveness of gated MTL.
4.4. Key Takeaways
•Integrated gating reduces hallucinations:The gated multi-
task Whisper model explicitly suppresses hallucinated tran-
scriptions during silent or non-speech inputs (as observed in
native Whisper models), reducing hallucination rates from
>96% to<1% via the novel V AD-inspired gate trained as
an auxiliary task that performs dynamic routing at inference.
•Joint training preserves task performance:Jointly opti-
mizing ASR, SER, ASC, and the gating yields performance
comparable to (or better than) task-specific baselines. The
mechanism itself is architectural and modular, and can be ex-
tended to different multi-task audio settings.
•Robustness under limited supervision:The model main-
tains reasonable performance even with as little as 10%
labeled training data, demonstrating suitability for low-
resource scenarios.
•Decoding constraints improve ASR quality:
no repeat ngram sizereduces WER by prevent-
ing repetition loops without affecting classification accuracy.
•Whisper as a shared backbone:The pre-trained Whisper
encoder generalizes effectively across speech and ambient
sound tasks. The proposed design is independent of model
size and can be applied to all Whisper variants.
•Generalization beyond current settings:The current evalu-
ation focused on fixed-length (10 seconds) English audio and
a limited set of acoustic scenes; extending the framework to
variable-length, multilingual, and cross-corpus settings, and
improving gate robustness under severe noise, are key direc-
tions.
5. Conclusion and Future Scope
The Gated Multi-Task Whisper framework jointly models ASR,
SER, and ASC with a shared encoder for robust, unified audio
understanding. A V AD-inspired gate enables conditional task
activation during inference, significantly reducing ASR halluci-
nations on non-speech inputs while maintaining strong perfor-
mance across tasks, validated on the ESAS-32K dataset with
noisy and mixed acoustic conditions. The proposed framework
is especially useful for audio monitoring and voice-based as-
sistants. Future work includes variable-length and multilingual
evaluation, generalization to other domains, improved routing
under varied noise, and examining ethics and privacy in contin-
uous monitoring deployments.
