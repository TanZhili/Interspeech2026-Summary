Abstract
This paper investigates the quality of impersonated (mimicked)
speech by extracting speaker embeddings from both spectral
and prosodic features. Prosodic features analyzed include loud-
ness, pitch, speaking rate, shimmer, and tempogram ratio, while
spectral features cover MFCCs (Mel Frequency Cepstral Coef-
ficeints), chroma, tonnetz, spectral roll-off, spectral bandwidth,
spectral flux, and spectral centroid. The evaluation begins with
a perception test to identify the best mimicked artist. The study
then explores whether a Deep Neural Network (DNN) can pre-
dict this artist based on the Mean Opinion Score (MOS) test.
A correct prediction, called a ”hit”, occurs when the model’s
output matches the top-rated artist. Model performance is
measured using a top-1 ranking on the MIMICz dataset. Re-
sults show that augmented speaker embeddings derived from
Emphasized Channel Attention, Propagation and Aggregation
(ECAPA) embeddings outperform those derived from other em-
bedding methods.

1. Introduction
Mimicry is the ability to imitate diverse subjects, including pub-
lic figures, accents, and even animal sounds. V oice mimicry, or
vocal impersonation, focuses on reproducing another individ-
ual’s vocal traits [1], requiring fine control of articulation, pitch,
tempo, and loudness [2]. It draws on both speech production
knowledge and the target voice’s unique characteristics. Inte-
grating spectral and prosodic features further enhances speech
emotion recognition by providing complementary cues for ac-
curate emotion identification.
Research on automatic speaker recognition for voice imi-
tation has employed prosodic features such as pitch and dura-
tion [3], with DTW achieving performance comparable to hu-
man judgment [4]. Hybrid methods combining MFCC-based
i-vectors, phonation features, DNN scoring [5], and DTW have
shown effectiveness in mimicry assessment [6]. Other ap-
proaches include formant modification, GAN-based conver-
sion, and style transfer, although challenges remain in pre-
serving naturalness and supporting cross-gender imitation [7].
SoundVerify employs a two-stage Transformer and wav2vec2-
based framework for Hindi voice impersonation, effectively
distinguishing genuine and spoofed speech while maintaining
speaker identification performance [8].
Recent advances in speaker recognition have been driven
by deep neural embedding architectures and improved loss
formulations. The x-vector framework established a robust
TDNN-based embedding paradigm, significantly outperform-
ing traditional i-vector approaches in text-independent tasks
[9]. Residual learning through ResNet architectures further
improved representation learning by enabling deeper networks
without degradation [10]. Large-scale datasets such as V ox-
Celeb accelerated progress by providing diverse speech un-
der unconstrained conditions [11]. Building on these ad-
vances, the ECAPA-TDNN architecture introduced channel at-
tention, multi-layer feature aggregation, and enhanced prop-
agation strategies, establishing a strong baseline for modern
speaker verification [12, 13, 14, 15].
The effectiveness of ECAPA-TDNN in modeling discrim-
inative speaker characteristics and capturing subtle spectral–
temporal variations makes it well suited for voice mimicry
recognition. These properties are essential for distinguishing
genuine speech from well-executed imitations, where differ-
ences are often subtle and distributed across prosodic and spec-
tral dimensions. In this work, speaker embeddings derived
from prosodic and spectral features [16, 17] are represented
using ECAPA embeddings and evaluated using a deep neural
network–based scoring mechanism [18], with performance val-
idated through perception tests.
2. Proposed System
In the proposed framework, illustrated in Fig.1, spectral and
prosodic features are extracted on a frame-by-frame basis and
utilized to generate ECAPA embeddings. These vectors are
further refined by an attention mechanism integrated within a
sparse autoencoder, resulting in attention-augmented ECAPA
embeddings. The augmented vectors are then fed into a
DNN classifier, with each output node representing a particu-
lar celebrity. To evaluate mimicry artists, we analysed the target
probability scores associated with the relevant celebrity node,
ranking artists by their scores. The top mimicry artist is iden-
tified based on the highest score, derived by fusing augmented
ECAPA embedding from both prosodic and spectral features.
2.1. Feature Extraction
Prosodic features, such as loudness, pitch, speaking rate, shim-
mer, and tempogram ratio [19], along with spectral features in-
cluding MFCCs [20], chroma [21], tonnetz, spectral roll-off,
spectral bandwidth, spectral flux, and spectral centroid [22],
are extracted from audio signals. These features are then con-
verted into embeddings, with ECAPA representations denoted
as E-vector, generated using the architecture in [23].ECAPA
was trained from scratch on the MIMICz dataset (20 celebri-
ties), enabled by the studio-quality recordings, relative ranking
objective, and strong regularization.

2.2. Evaluation System
The DNN model is designed for a classification task with 20
classes, using a sequential architecture that begins with an in-
put layer for a 32-dimensional vector, followed by a series of
dense layers with ReLU activations and dropout for regulariza-
tion. The model consists of five dense layers with decreasing
units (512, 512, 256, 128, and 64) to balance complexity and
capacity, each followed by a dropout layers (0.3,0.2,0.2,0.1,0.1)
to prevent overfitting. The final output layer uses sigmoid ac-
tivation of 20 units, optimized with the Adam optimizer and
categorical cross-entropy loss.
3. Performance Evaluation
3.1. Dataset
The proposed system is assessed using the MIMICz dataset
[28]. Audio recordings of twenty well-known Malayalam 1
movie actors (targets) were collected from films and entertain-
ment programs, and five mimicry artists (Artist-1 to Artist-5) re-
produced these voices in a studio environment. The recordings
were captured at a sampling rate of 44.1 kHz and comprise 400
training utterances (25 per artist, ∼15 s each) and 100 test utter-
ances (5 per artist, ∼10 s each), totaling 500 files (∼116.6 min).
This study focuses on the text-independent setting to investigate
the role of vocal characteristics in voice mimicry assessment.
A subjective evaluation was conducted with fifteen native

Malayalam listeners balanced across age and gender to assess
mimicry artists. Participants first listened to a celebrity’s origi-
nal voice, followed by imitations, and rated the similarity on a
5-point scale (1 = lowest, 5 = highest). Mean Opinion Scores
(MOS) were obtained by averaging listener ratings, with the
highest MOS indicating the most skilled artist. Inter-annotator
agreement analysis showed strong internal consistency ( α =
0.86), while Kendall’s W = 0.34 and Fleiss’ κ = 0.31 in-
dicated fair agreement, which is common in subjective voice
similarity evaluations.

The prosodic features and the spectral features are extracted us-
ing Librosa toolkit, with a frame size of 30ms and a frame shift
of 10ms. ECAPA embeddings are derived from these features
and refined using a 1-D CNN. The resulting output is then pro-
cessed through an attention layer and a sparse autoencoder. The
augmented speaker embeddings (E∗
l -vector) obtained from this
process are combined using score fusion technique. The final

The DNN was trained for 256 epochs with a batch size of
32 and a learning rate of 0.001, using 10% of the data for valida-
tion. System performance is measured by the top-K hit rate, i.e.,
the percentage of cases where the best human-identified mimic
appears within the top K of five ranked artists. The primary
focus is on the top-1 hit rate, which identifies the single most
accurate mimic for each of the 20 celebrities.

5. Conclusion
This study demonstrates that combining prosodic and spectral
cues through augmented ECAPA embeddings improves the re-
liability of voice mimicry assessment. Among the evaluated
methods, augmented E-vectors achieved the strongest perfor-
mance, with fusion strategies providing additional gains. Be-
yond recognition accuracy, the results show the ability of these
approaches to capture the subtle temporal and prosodic charac-
teristics of successful mimicry. Future work will explore real-
time detection, cross-lingual adaptation, and larger training cor-
pora to improve generalization.
