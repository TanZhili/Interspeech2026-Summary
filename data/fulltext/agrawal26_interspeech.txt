Abstract
We present Amadea: an interactive speech learning platform
for pitch-sensitive languages, including pitch accent languages
such as Japanese and lexical tonal languages such as Mandarin.
The system pairs an AI companion interface with real-time pro-
nunciation feedback. When a learner speaks, the system tran-
scribes the input and generates a native reference equivalent au-
dio of the phrase. The system aligns the two audio files and
compares their normalised fundamental-frequency (F0) con-
tours to produce a pitch-pattern score. A graph visualising any
pitch divergence throughout the phrase is then presented to the
user. Amadea supports structured lessons as well as open-ended
companion dialogue, with real-time feedback on both.
Index Terms: prosody, pitch accent, speech feedback, conver-
sational AI, prosodic visualisation
1. Introduction
Second-language learners often have limited access to suitable
instructors to practise with, and current language-learning plat-
forms offer limited acoustic feedback. For pitch-sensitive lan-
guages such as Japanese and Mandarin, this gap significantly
undermines the clarity and fluency of the learner.
Research in computer-assisted pronunciation training
(CAPT) has explored a range of approaches to automating pitch
scoring and corrective feedback, and visualisation of speech
patterns [1]. Prior work on Japanese pitch accent learning
has also examined fundamental frequency analysis [2, 3]. De-
spite these advances, existing systems are centered on fixed
prompts and rote repetition exercises, offering little spontane-
ity. Amadea addresses this limitation by embedding pronuncia-
tion feedback directly within both structured lessons and open-
ended conversation with an AI companion.
Crucially, when a learner speaks, Amadea transcribes their
utterance and generates a native-like version of the intended ut-
terance. The system then aligns and compares the normalised
fundamental-frequency (F0) contours of the two signals to pro-
duce a pitch-pattern score. The companion interface lowers in-
hibition, while visual feedback and audio replay guide learners
towards improved intonation. Figure 1 presents a screenshot of
the user interface. Overall, Amadea’s contribution is a system
design enabling pitch-contour feedback during free-form con-
versation by generating a reference utterance from the learner’s
input, allowing pronunciation feedback to be applied to learner-
selected phrases rather than only to pre-selected fixed prompts.

2. System overview
Amadea has two practice modes for speech. Inlesson mode,
learners work through pre-scripted phrases while role-playing
likely everyday scenarios. Inconversation mode, learners en-
gage in unscripted conversation with an AI companion, encour-
aging spontaneity within scenarios freely configurable by the
learner while still receiving realtime prosody feedback.
Learner audio is captured in the browser and passed to ASR.
Though the expected text is already known for scripted lessons,
this is not the case for free-form conversation mode. A central
design feature to note here is that the system reliably infers the
user’s intended phrase, and uses it to derive a pronunciation ref-
erence. Here, the intended phrase is approximated by the ASR
transcription, which captures the most likely interpretation of
the learner’s utterance. This allows the system to correctly infer
the communicative intent of the speaker even when prosodic de-
viations, common in early or even intermediate learners, would
lead to an unintended or context-inappropriate verbalisation.
Amadea synthesises a reference audio of the intended phrase
while the AI companion continues the exchange uninterrupted,
and graphic visualisation of the reference audio provides cor-
rective feedback on how a native speaker would have produced
their intended meaning.

4. Real-time conversational practice
The purpose of the AI companion interface is to encourage user
engagement and sustained speech production. One persistent
limitation of existing platforms is their reliance on massed rep-
etition, which has been shown to be less effective for long-term
retention than distributed practice [6]. Research on relational
agents shows that conversational systems with a social presence
can bolster user engagement over extended periods of time [7],
and link chatbot-based approaches to improvements in user mo-
tivation and confidence in language learning contexts [8].
Amadea builds on this by presenting the learner with a re-
sponsive, human-like conversational partner. The companion’s
real-time speech and consistent personality encourage interac-
tion that more closely resembles ordinary dialogue. Crucially,
corrective feedback is embedded alongside this interaction in
an unobtrusive manner, using visualisations and tooltips to in-
dicate pitch divergence without interrupting the exchange. The
result is an immersive setting in which learners are drawn into
speaking with reduced social inhibition, while still receiving
continuous guidance on how to refine their pronunciation to-
wards native-level fluency.
5. Limitations and future work
Current limitations include ASR errors and consequent dis-
tortions in inferred targets, noisy F0 extraction, and temporal
alignment failures. Additionally, native prosody is not uniquely
defined, and where multiple intonations might be acceptable,
the system contrasts deviations against a single reference tra-
jectory.
Future work will focus on validation and robustness. We
will first assess external validity by quantifying the relationship
between Amadea’s pitch-pattern score and expert human judge-
ments of prosodic accuracy. Longitudinal analyses will then
examine if repeated interaction produces measurable improve-
ment. Finally, we will compare pronunciation gains between
lesson modeandconversation modeto evaluate fluency gains.
6. Conclusion
Amadea demonstrates how conversational AI can support pitch-
sensitive language learners by integrating effortless dialogue
with real-time prosodic feedback. The system creates a clear
practice loop: the learner hears a fluently intonated reference,
attempts to reproduce it, and immediately sees where their pitch
contour diverges together with a score, making pitch-learning
immersive and engaging.
The design supports both structured lessons and open-
ended dialogue, allowing learners to receive continuous feed-
back in the course of natural communication. In doing so,
Amadea reframes pronunciation training from isolated practice
into an ongoing, interaction-driven process, offering a practi-
cal step towards addressing long-standing limitations in existing
pitch-sensitive language learning systems.
