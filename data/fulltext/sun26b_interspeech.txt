PAN-Mask: Pathology-Aware Neurological Masking with End-to-End
Learnable Weights for Neurological Disorder Detection from Speech

Abstract
Self-supervised models like WavLM advance pathologi-
cal speech classification but rely on content-agnostic random
masking, which overlooks sparse, localized clinical biomarkers.
We propose PAN-Mask (Pathology-Aware Neurological Mask-
ing), a task-aware regularization framework. A lightweight de-
tector computes and aggregates six interpretable acoustic de-
scriptors via learnable attention to estimate frame-level pathol-
ogy salience. By selectively masking high-salience segments,
PAN-Mask discourages shortcut learning and compels the en-
coder to capture robust, distributed representations. Joint opti-
mization enables the model to learn task-discriminative patterns
aligned with clinical biomarkers without manual design of fea-
ture importance. Evaluated on six datasets across three disor-
ders and five languages using identical hyperparameters, PAN-
Mask yields 8.31–22.72% accuracy gains (avg. 13.82%) over
random masking and provides interpretable clinical insights.
Index Terms: Pathological Speech Classification, Self-
supervised Speech Models, Alzheimer’s disease detection,
Parkinson’s disease detection, speech biomarkers
1. Introduction
Early detection of neurological and psychiatric conditions
through speech has become a central objective in digital health
research. Alzheimer’s disease (AD) affects over 55 million peo-
ple worldwide and is projected to nearly triple by 2050 [1];
Parkinson’s disease (PD) affects approximately 10 million in-
dividuals with prevalence expected to double [2]; and depres-
sion remains widely underdetected [3]. A meta-analysis of 96
studies reports diagnostic sensitivities above 0.80 for AI-driven
speech biomarkers, while highlighting heterogeneity arising
from recording conditions and language [4]. Explainable AI
studies further demonstrate Area Under the Curve (AUC) val-
ues of 0.76–0.94 for detecting cognitive decline from acous-
tic and linguistic markers [5]. In PD, speech impairments af-
fect up to 90% of patients and may precede motor diagno-
sis by a decade [2], motivating increasing adoption of self-
supervised representation learning [6]. Multimodal systems and
voiceprint-based analyses further support speech as a viable
multilingual digital biomarker [7, 8, 9].
Self-supervised speech models have substantially advanced
clinical speech classification. Models such as wav2vec 2.0 [10]
and WavLM [11] learn rich representations from unlabeled
speech and consistently achieve state-of-the-art results on clin-
ical benchmarks [12]. However, these models inherit auni-

objectives. In pathological speech, diagnostically relevant cues
are often sparse and temporally localized. Although highly
informative, a disproportionate reliance on a small number of
these salient frames may encourageshortcut learning, limiting
the model’s ability to capture the distributed and complemen-
tary biomarkers necessary for robust and generalizable clinical
assessment.
To address this limitation, we proposePAN-Mask, a task-
aware masking framework that replaces content-agnostic ran-
dom masking withdisease-aware masking. PAN-Mask intro-
duces a lightweight pathology detector that extracts six inter-
pretable acoustic descriptors: rhythm irregularity, pause like-
lihood, pitch monotony, energy drop, voice quality anomaly,
and periodicity, and aggregates them via end-to-end learnable
attention to estimate frame-levelpathology salience. During
training, frames with high salience are preferentially suppressed
through a differentiable masking mechanism. By attenuating
dominant pathological segments, the downstream classifier is
encouraged to construct more distributed, condition-relevant,
and robust representations from the remaining signal. Because
the detector and classifier are jointly optimized, the masking
distribution adapts automatically to various diseases and lan-
guages without the need for manual, per-dataset feature engi-
neering.
Our contributions are as follows:
1. We introduce PAN-Mask, a pathology-aware training frame-
work that formulates disease-aware masking as a task-aware
regularization strategy for clinical speech modeling.
2. We design an end-to-end learnable pathology detector that
leverages frame-level attention over six interpretable features
to automatically identify disease-specific biomarkers, facili-
tating robust cross-disease and cross-lingual adaptation.
3. We conduct extensive evaluation on six datasets spanning
three disorders (AD, PD, depression) and five languages,
demonstrating consistent accuracy improvements of 8.31–
22.72 percentage points over conventional random masking,
with an average gain of 13.82 percentage points.

2.3. Disease Aware Masking
During training, a fractionp= 0.4of all waveform samples is
masked. This fraction is chosen to suppress dominant patho-
logical cues, forcing the model to discover subtler, distributed
biomarkers in the remaining signal. We compare two placement
strategies that use the same total masking budget.
Random masking (control).Waveform samples are
masked at uniformly random positions. This isolates the effect
of salience-conditioned placement from masking itself.
Pathology aware masking.Masking is concentrated on
the frames most indicative of disease. Frames whose salience
exceeds the 85th percentile are prioritized: 90% of the mask-
ing budget targets these regions, while 10% remains random to
preserve stochastic regularization.

Crucially, the soft mask (Section 2.4) attenuates rather than
eliminates high-salience frames, acting as targeted regulariza-
tion analogous to Dropout that encourages the encoder to ex-
ploit distributed biomarkers. This explains why random mask-
ing degrades performance (Section 4.2): it indiscriminately dis-
rupts sparse clinical cues, whereas pathology-aware masking at-
tenuates selectively.
Because this discrete, span based allocation is not differ-
entiable, the end to end model replaces it with the continuous
soft mask described next. Validation data is never masked under
either condition.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate on six publicly available clinical speech datasets
spanning three conditions. ADReSS [24] and ADReSSo [25]
provide English picture description recordings for AD versus
healthy control classification. NCMMSC2021 contains Man-
darin speech from the same clinical task. ADReSS-M [26] is
a multilingual corpus for AD detection. NeuroV oz [27] pro-
vides Spanish recordings from PD patients and matched con-
trols. The Androids Reading Corpus [28] includes Italian read-
ing task recordings for depression screening. We follow official
splits when available. Otherwise, we construct speaker-disjoint
80/20 train–validation splits with a fixed random seed (42).
3.2. Implementation Details
We finetune WavLM Base (pretrained checkpoint from Hug-
gingFace) with a two class classification head. Waveforms
are truncated or zero padded to 10 seconds (160 000 samples).
Training proceeds for 20 epochs with batch size 4, the AdamW
optimizer (learning rate2×10 −5, weight decay 0.01), a cosine
learning rate schedule with 10% linear warmup, and automatic
mixed precision. Gradients are clipped at norm 1.0. Mask-
ing hyperparameters (probability 0.4, pathology ratio 0.9, per-
centile 85) are fixed across datasets without task-specific tun-
ing. Additional runs with different random seeds indicate that
the relative advantage of pathology-aware masking over random
masking remains consistent.

provement of13.82 percentage points (pp).

Two observations stand out. First, pathology-aware mask-
ing consistently outperforms both No mask and Random mask-
ing. Random masking degrades accuracy by 4.2% to 13.3%
below No mask across all six datasets, confirming that mask-
ing without pathological guidance disrupts clinically relevant
signals. In contrast, both P. (fixed) and P. (E2E) recover or
surpass No mask on most datasets, demonstrating that target-
ing pathology-informative frames is essential for effective reg-
ularization. Second, P. (E2E) is consistently superior to P.
(fixed). While P. (fixed) suffers from instability (e.g., 58.8%
on ADReSSo vs. 73.5% for No mask) because uniform fea-
ture weighting cannot adapt across disorders, P. (E2E) achieves
the best accuracy on five of six datasets, improving over Ran-
dom by an average of 13.8%. This confirms that jointly training
the pathology detector with the downstream classifier enables
the model to discover disease-specific biomarker patterns, vali-
dating the core design of our learnable pathology-aware frame-
work.

5. Conclusion
We presented PAN-Mask, a framework that improves ran-
dom masking with disease-aware masking guided by six inter-
pretable acoustic features and end-to-end learnable attention.
By selectively suppressing salient pathological frames during
training, the model learns more distributed and robust represen-
tations that generalize across conditions and languages with-
out per-dataset tuning. Experiments on six datasets in five lan-
guages demonstrate consistent accuracy gains of 8.31 to 22.72
percentage points over random masking. Our findings align
with established clinical literature, validating both the diag-
nostic relevance of the selected biomarkers and the efficacy of
pathology-aware regularization for neurological disorder detec-
tion from speech.
