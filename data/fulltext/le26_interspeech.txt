Abstract
Effective speaker anonymization faces a critical bottleneck: bal-
ancing robust privacy protection with the preservation of down-
stream utility. To resolve this trade-off, we introduce VerAno, a
novel framework that leverages a Vector Quantized Variational
Autoencoder (VQ-V AE) to tokenize Self-Supervised Learning
(SSL) speech features. By strategically constraining codebook
size, VerAno achieves the self-supervised disentanglement of
speaker-specific features from speaker-independent features.
These discrete tokens are fused with pretrained speaker embed-
dings and processed by a flow-matching transformer to synthe-
size high-fidelity acoustic representations. Experimental eval-
uations on the V oicePrivacy Challenge datasets demonstrate
that VerAno substantially outperforms baseline models. Fur-
thermore, the system generalizes favorably across languages,
demonstrating promising cross-lingual capabilities when ap-
plied to unseen data.

1. Introduction
The rapid integration of speech capabilities into Large Lan-
guage Models (LLMs) enables remarkable context-aware inter-
actions [1]. However, relying on centralized servers for these in-
teractions introduces significant privacy risks, as speech signals
could reveal sensitive attributes including identity, gender, and
health status. Consequently, speaker anonymization conceal-
ing identity while preserving linguistic and paralinguistic util-
ity has become essential. To standardize this task, the V oicePri-
vacy Challenge (VPC) was established, providing benchmarks
to evaluate trade-offs between security and utility [2, 3, 4].
Existing approaches generally fall into signal-
processing [5] or neural voice conversion (VC) do-
mains [6, 7, 8]. While signal-processing methods are
often vulnerable to re-identification and content distortion [9],
neural VC approaches typically offer superior privacy and
content preservation [8]. These approaches often dissociate
speech into speaker-specific attributes (timbre) and speaker-
invariant features (linguistic content, prosody). To achieve
this, prior works have utilized bottleneck features from Au-
tomatic Speech Recognition (ASR) [7] systems or using SSL
representations [10].
Current disentanglement strategies face a critical deadlock.
ASR-based bottlenecks are language-dependent and often de-
grade paralinguistic fidelity [4, 11], while continuous SSL fea-
tures inadvertently encode speaker timbre, leading to identity

ally been polarized between the error propagation of autore-

tion that recent flow-matching approaches actively seek to re-
solve [14, 15].
To resolve these challenges, we introduceV erAno, a
versatileanonymization framework that leverages a self-
supervised VQ-V AE tokenizer as a tunable information fil-
ter. By quantizing continuous WavLM [16] features, we ef-
fectively filter out speaker-specific traits, such as low-level
timbre, while retaining high-level speaker-independent seman-
tic and emotional content. These discrete tokens condition a
non-autoregressive Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) mod-
ule, which generates high-fidelity acoustic features via Ordi-
nary Differential Equations (ODEs). Experimental evaluations
on VPC 2024 datasets demonstrate that VerAno outperforms
baselines in both average and weighted rankings, while exhibit-
ing cross-lingual generalization to unseen languages. 1
2. Anonymization System
2.1. Overview
As illustrated in Figure 1, VerAno first processes raw speech
to extract two distinct representations: an utterance-level em-
bedding that captures global speaker identity, and continuous
frame-level WavLM features. These WavLM features pos-
sess high informational capacity, capturing fine-grained de-
tails regarding acoustic timbre, expressive style, and seman-
tic meaning [16]. To decouple speaker-specific features from
the speaker-independent traits, we apply an information bottle-
neck strategy using a VQ-V AE tokenizer with a restricted code-
book size. This forced discretization acts as a filter, discard-
ing fine-grained, speaker-specific acoustic details while pre-
serving essential linguistic and prosodic structures. To syn-
thesize the anonymized speech, these disentangled discrete to-
kens are combined with a target speaker embedding to con-
dition a non-autoregressive flow-matching transformer. The
transformer maps a prior noise distribution to a target Mel spec-
trogram via an ODE solver, which is finally converted into a
waveform by a vocoder. The following sections detail these
components and our anonymization strategy.

The core mechanism of our disentanglement relies on the
codebook size,K. Because the network is optimized to min-

most dominant variances in the signal, such as phonetic con-
tent and broad prosodic structure. WhenKis constrained,
the codebook lacks the representational bandwidth to encode
the high-frequency, fine-grained acoustic nuances that define
speaker identity. Conversely, asKincreases, the bottleneck re-
laxes, allowing more speaker-specific traits to leak into the to-
kens, which improves audio fidelity but compromises privacy.
By replacing ASR bottlenecks with self-supervised SSL tok-
enization, VerAno avoids rigid language dependencies. Fur-
thermore, by constraining the codebook size, the system mit-
igates the timbre leakage associated with continuous SSL fea-
tures [12], allowingKto serve as a tunable knob for navigating
the privacy-utility trade-off.
2.3. Flow Matching Acoustic Model
To generate high-fidelity Mel spectrograms from discrete to-
kens, we employ a CFM framework with a Transformer back-
bone [15]. CFM learns a deterministic Continuous Normalizing
Flow that transforms a simple prior distributiony 0∼N(0,I)
into the complex data distributiony 1 (Mel spectrograms) via an
ODE.
We define the probability density path using an Optimal
Transport interpolation. Given a noise sampley 0, a target Mel
spectrogramy 1 ∈R T×D mel (Tacoustic frames,D mel Mel
bands), and a noise parameterσ, the intermediate flow statey t
is constructed via linear interpolation at a uniformly sampled
time stept∼U(0,1):
yt = (1−(1−σ)t)·y 0 +t·y 1 (8)
The derivative of this path yields a constant target velocity
ofy 1−(1−σ)·y 0. Given a sequence of aligned discrete tokens
s∈{1,...,K} T , and a speaker embeddingespk∈R Dspk, we
train a neural velocity fieldv t(yt,t,s,e spk;θ)to approximate
this target constant-velocity trajectory.
We employ a Transformer architecture to parameterizev t.

2.4. Anonymization strategy
To conceal speaker identity during inference, we substitute the
source speaker’s embedding with a target vector randomly sam-
pled from a pre-computed pool of external speakers. For each
utterance, a distinct target identity is selected from this exter-
nal pool. This strategy is more likely to prevent the overestima-
tion of protection capabilities often associated with manipulated
speaker embeddings [21].
3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets and Metrics
Our models (tokenizer, acoustic model, and vocoder) are
trained on the standard training splits of LibriSpeech (LS) [22]
and CREMA-D [23]. We adhere to the V oicePrivacy Chal-
lenge (VPC) 2024 protocols [4], utilizingLibriSpeech-dev/test-
cleanfor privacy and linguistic assessment, andIEMOCAP-
dev/test[24] for emotion preservation.
Performance is quantified using three key metrics:
•Privacy (EER):The Equal Error Rate (↑better) computed
by the semi-informed ASV system (ASV LS
anon) trained on
anonymizedtrain-clean-360subset of LS.
•Content (WER):Word Error Rate (↓better) to measure lin-
guistic intelligibility, evaluated using the official VPC ASR
model.
•Emotion (UAR):Unweighted Average Recall (↑better) to
assess affective retention, obtained via the official VPC emo-
tion recognition system.
To test cross-lingual generalization, we follow the evalua-
tion plan in [11] which uses Multilingual LibriSpeech (MLS)
dataset [25] (German, Portuguese, Italian, Spanish). Here, pri-
vacy is benchmarked against both the semi-informedASV LS
anon
and a pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN [26] on original V oxCeleb 1
and 2 datasets[27, 28] (ASV V ox
orig ). Linguistic utility in this set-
ting is evaluated using Whisper-large-v3 [29].
3.2. Implementation Details
Speech Tokenizer:We adopt the RepCodec implementa-
tion [30], replacing its base convolutional encoder and decoder
blocks with V ocos backbones [31]. We extract features from
the 18th layer of WavLM Large. 2 The tokenizer is trained for
152k steps with a batch size of 32 using the AdamW optimizer
(lr= 2·10 −4,β 1 = 0.6,β 2 = 0.9). The loss weights are set
toα= 30,β= 0.2, andθ= 0.9. To analyze the impact of
information discretization, we evaluate various codebook sizes
Kranging from 32 to 12,888, denoting the respective models
asCB K.
Acoustic Model:The flow-matching transformer com-
prises 16 layers, 16 attention heads, an hidden dimension of
768, and an FFN dimension of 3092. It is trained for 400k
updates (batch size 32) using AdamW (lr= 8·10 −5,β 1 =
0.6,β 2 = 0.9). Forσ, it is set to1·10 −5.
Inference & Anonymization:We utilize the ReDim-
Net [32] checkpoint for speaker encoding. The speaker pool is
constructed from LStrain-clean-100, extracting one embedding
from a random utterance per speaker. During generation, we
solve the ODE using 10 Euler steps and reconstruct the wave-
form via HiFi-GAN based vocoder [33].
3.3. Baseline Systems
We benchmark VerAno against the complete suite of official
VPC 2024 baselines, denoted asB1throughB6. For cross-
lingual evaluation, we specifically employ the multilingual vari-
ant of the B3 baseline, referred to asB3-Mul[11].

4.4. The Privacy-Utility Trade-off
As shown in Figure 2, we analyze the impact ofKas a tunable
information bottleneck. AsKincreases, the tokenizer captures
finer acoustic granularity, significantly improving utility reten-
tion. However, this fidelity comes at a cost: higher-resolution
tokens inadvertently encode speaker-specific features, provid-
ing more leverage for re-identification attacks. Conversely, re-
ducingKstrengthens privacy by filtering out these identity
cues, albeit with some degradation in linguistic naturalness.
This confirms that codebook size is the critical hyperparame-
ter for navigating the privacy-utility trade-off.
Notably, we observe a non-linear relationship: expanding
the codebook from 32 to 128 yields massive utility gains with
minimal privacy loss, whereas increasing from 8192 to 12888
exponentially degrades privacy for diminishing utility returns.
Consequently, the optimal operating point for privacy-sensitive
applications likely lies in the lower range (e.g.,K= 32−128),
creating a bottleneck tight enough to strip speaker discrimina-
tive feats like timbre but loose enough to encode essential pho-
netics and prosodies.
5. Conclusion
This paper introducedV erAno, a unified framework utilizing
self-supervised tokenization and conditional flow matching to
redefine the privacy-utility trade-off in speaker anonymization.
Through extensive evaluation, we demonstrated that constrain-
ing the codebook size acts as an effective privacy knob, allow-
ing our system to surpass official baselines in overall rankings
and perform competitively in cross-lingual generalization. In
future work, we plan to explore distillation techniques to ac-
celerate the flow-matching inference, moving VerAno closer to
real-time deployment for on-device privacy applications.
