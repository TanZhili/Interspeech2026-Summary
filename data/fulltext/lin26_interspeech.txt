Abstract
Counterfactual attention learning mitigates biased attention by
disentangling task-relevant cues from spurious regions. Learn-
able Counterfactual Attention (LCA) introduces a learnable
counterfactual branch to expose misleading yet discriminative
biases during training. However, learning in a single repre-
sentation space often leaves residual biases entangled with the
main attention. We propose Progressive Learnable Counterfac-
tual Attention (P-LCA), a multi-stage framework that refines
attention via stage-wise representation projection. After each
counterfactual training stage, main-branch attention features are
projected into a new latent space, allowing subsequent stages
to examine biases from different perspectives and reveal hid-
den patterns. This design enables thorough and stable debias-
ing. Experiments on Artist20, GTZAN, and EMOPIA datasets
show consistent improvements across music classification tasks,
while attention visualizations indicate focused, stable attention
patterns.
Index Terms: counterfactual attention learning, learnable
counterfactual attention, music classification
1. Introduction
Attention mechanisms have become a fundamental component
in modern deep learning models, enabling networks to se-
lectively emphasize informative regions while suppressing ir-
relevant signals. In music classification tasks, attention has
been widely adopted to capture discriminative time–frequency
patterns and improve robustness against background interfer-
ence [1]. Despite their empirical success, most attention mech-
anisms are learned under weak supervision, relying solely on
the final classification loss without explicit guidance on where
the model should attend. As a result, attention is not guaranteed
to align with task-relevant cues and is easily misled by spurious
correlations or dataset-specific biases [2, 3].
To address this limitation, Counterfactual Attention Learn-
ing (CAL) [3] was proposed as a causal-inspired framework
that introduces random counterfactual attention maps as inter-
ventions during training. By contrasting predictions obtained
from factual and counterfactual attentions, CAL encourages the
model to learn attention patterns that have a stronger causal ef-
fect on the final prediction, thereby alleviating the shortcomings
of purely weakly supervised attention learning. However, em-
ploying random attention as a counterfactual intervention intro-
duces inherent limitations, particularly in audio domains where
task-relevant cues, such as vocal content or background music
characteristics, are temporally distributed and persist through-
out the recording. In such cases, random attentions are unlikely

to correspond to semantically meaningful non-target regions,
limiting their ability to reliably expose and suppress mislead-
ing cues [2].
Motivated by this observation, Learnable Counterfactual
Attention (LCA) [2] was introduced to replace random atten-
tion interventions with a learnable counterfactual branch that
explicitly captures bias-related attention patterns during train-
ing. By guiding the main (factual) branch to deviate from these
misleading but discriminative bias-related regions, LCA signif-
icantly improves attention robustness. Nevertheless, LCA oper-
ates within a single representation space, which limits its ability
to fully disentangle complex and residual biases that may re-
main entangled with the main attention. In practice, certain bias
patterns may only become separable when viewed from alter-
native representation perspectives.
To overcome this limitation, we propose Progressive Learn-
able Counterfactual Attention (P-LCA), which extends LCA
into a multi-stage framework through stage-wise representation
projection. Stage-wise representation projection explicitly re-
shapes the attention-conditioned feature space, thereby alter-
ing the relative separability of bias-related and task-relevant
cues across stages. By reprojecting the main-branch attention-
conditioned features after each counterfactual training stage, P-
LCA enables subsequent stages to re-examine attention bias
from transformed representation perspectives, exposing resid-
ual or entangled bias patterns that remain indistinguishable un-
der earlier representations. By progressively exploring atten-
tion across multiple representation perspectives, P-LCA enables
more thorough and stable debiasing than single-stage counter-
factual learning.
Our contributions are threefold:
•We propose P-LCA, a multi-stage extension of LCA that pro-
gressively refines attention via stage-wise representation pro-
jection, enabling progressive debiasing across multiple rep-
resentation perspectives.
•We introduce stage-wise representation projection as a
perspective-shifting mechanism, which reshapes the feature
space underlying LCA learning, allowing residual and entan-
gled bias patterns to become separable across stages.
•We validate the effectiveness of P-LCA on diverse music clas-
sification tasks, including artist, genre, and emotion recogni-
tion, demonstrating consistent improvements on Artist20 [4],
GTZAN [5], and EMOPIA [6], along with more focused and
stable attention patterns.

Other attention variants, such as channel-wise attention mech-

attention mechanisms are typically learned under weak supervi-
sion, relying solely on the final classification loss without aux-
iliary guidance. As a consequence, the causal link between at-
tention and prediction is often overlooked, which limits perfor-
mance gains [3]. To address this, Raoet al.[3] introduced
CAL, which incorporates random attention maps as counterfac-
tual interventions (auxiliary signals) to guide attention learning.
By contrasting predictions obtained from learned and random
attentions, CAL promotes the main-branch attention to focus
on more discriminative regions. Nevertheless, the stochastic na-
ture of such interventions hampers their ability to consistently
localize harmful regions, thereby constraining overall perfor-
mance [2]. To overcome this limitation, Linet al.[2] intro-
duced LCA, which equips attention-based models with a learn-
able counterfactual branch to explicitly identify bias-related
cues. However, because LCA performs counterfactual learn-
ing in a single representation space, it can miss bias patterns
that only become separable under further projections, leaving
residual biases entangled with the main attention.
Building upon these insights, we observe that while LCA
effectively mitigates attention bias, it remains constrained by
operating within a single representation space. Consequently,
certain bias patterns may remain entangled with the main at-
tention when examined from a fixed representation perspective.
This motivates a flexible framework that iteratively re-examines
attention under different representation perspectives to progres-
sively uncover and suppress residual biases. To this end, we
propose P-LCA, which extends LCA via stage-wise representa-
tion projection for multi-stage counterfactual refinement.
3. Approach
3.1. Revisiting learnable counterfactual attention
LCA [2] formulates attention learning from a causal perspec-
tive by explicitly modeling the effect of attention on prediction.
In LCA, the attention-based model is described using a causal
graph consisting of feature representationsX, attention maps
A, and the final predictiony(see Figure 1). The main atten-
tion branch produces attention mapsAconditioned on the in-
put featuresX, which, together withX, determine the factual
predictiony.
To perform counterfactual intervention on the attention
mechanism, LCA introduces a learnable counterfactual branch
that generates counterfactual attention maps ˜A, also condi-
tioned on the same feature representationX. As illustrated
in Figure 1, the counterfactual prediction˜yis obtained by re-
placing the original attention mapsAwith the counterfactual
attention maps ˜A, while keeping the feature representationX
fixed. This replacement serves as an intervention on the atten-
tion mechanism of the main branch. Based on this formula-
tion, LCA defines the effect of attention on prediction as the
difference between the factual and counterfactual outcomes,
i.e.,y ef f ect =y−˜y. By optimizing this attention effect
jointly with the standard classification objective, LCA encour-
ages the main attention branch to provide stronger discrim-
inative evidence for prediction than the counterfactual atten-
tion branch. Intuitively, the counterfactual attention branch is
guided, through its training objectives, to focus on plausible yet
bias-related cues that may lead to misleading predictions, while
being constrained not to outperform the main attention branch.
This competitive formulation forces the main attention branch
to move away from such biased regions and instead attend to
more task-relevant and causally meaningful patterns.
Despite its effectiveness, LCA performs counterfactual
learning within a single stage and under a shared representa-
tion space defined byX. Since both the factual attentionA
and the counterfactual attention ˜Aare learned and compared
under the same feature representation, the underlying represen-
tation perspective remains unchanged. As a result, bias patterns
that are entangled with task-relevant cues in this space may not
be fully exposed or suppressed. This limitation motivates ex-
tending LCA toward a multi-stage framework that re-examines
attention under transformed representation perspectives.
3.2. Our progressive learnable counterfactual attention
Figure 2 illustrates the proposed P-LCA, which extends the
originalgenreMERTarchitecture equipped with LCA into
a multi-stage refinement framework. While the original
genreMERT(with LCA) performs counterfactual learning
within a single representation space, P-LCA introduces a pro-
gressive formulation that repeatedly re-examines attention be-
havior under transformed representation perspectives. Com-
pared to the originalgenreMERT(with LCA) [2], P-LCA in-
troduces only two additional components—a shared stage-wise
representation projection module and fixed stage embeddings.
All attention formulations and training objectives follow the
original LCA design and are applied at each refinement stage,
with the overall training objective formed by aggregating the
stage-wise losses.
Specifically, P-LCA consists ofKrefinement stages. We
denote the input to stagekas the stage representationH (k).
The refinement process is initialized from the MERT fea-
tures [22] after an initial convolutional projection, denoted as
X. The first refinement stage operates onX, and subsequent
stages operate on progressively refined representationsH (k+1)
(see Figure 2). At each stage, the model follows the standard
LCA formulation, including a main (factual) attention branch
and a counterfactual attention branch operating on the cur-
rent stage representation. Given the stage input, both branches
compute temporal attention weights over the current stage rep-
resentation. The main attention branch produces attention-
conditioned representations for prediction, while the counter-
factual attention branch captures plausible yet bias-related tem-
poral patterns and provides counterfactual outcomes for com-
puting the attention effect. Accordingly, the two branches at
stagekproduce the factual and counterfactual predictionsy (k)

The key extension of P-LCA lies in how successive refine-
ment stages interact. Following the counterfactual supervision
at stagek, the attention-conditioned representation produced by
the main attention branch is reprojected through a shared stage-
wise representation projection module (see the green block in
Figure 2) and used as the input to the subsequent stage. While
this projection preserves the feature dimensionality, it explic-
itly transforms the latent representation and reshapes the fea-
ture distribution on which attention is computed. Consequently,
bias patterns that remain entangled with task-relevant cues in
one representation space may become more separable when ex-
amined under a transformed representation perspective.
To ensure that the model can distinguish different refine-
ment stages while preserving shared attention parameters, P-
LCA further incorporates fixed stage embeddings based on si-
nusoidal positional encoding (see the blue circles in Figure 2).
Each refinement stage is associated with a unique embedding
that is injected into the attention computation as contextual con-
ditioning. This design enables the attention mechanism to be
stage-aware without introducing stage-specific attention param-
eters. As a result, attention can adapt its behavior across succes-
sive stages while maintaining a consistent learning formulation,
preventing ambiguity between refinement steps and facilitating
stable progressive learning.
By alternating between counterfactual supervision and
stage-wise representation projection, P-LCA enables succes-
sive stages to progressively expose and suppress residual at-
tention biases. This design allows main-branch attention to be
refined across multiple representation perspectives, leading to
more thorough and stable debiasing than single-stage counter-
factual learning, while retaining the originalgenreMERTar-
chitecture and training formulation.

4. Experiments

the original architecture with a multi-stage refinement frame-

fixed stage embeddings. This results ingenreMERT(with P-
LCA), in which the main-branch attention is progressively re-

parison results—except for ours—based on the values reported

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed Progressive Learnable Counterfac-
tual Attention (P-LCA), a multi-stage extension of Learnable
Counterfactual Attention (LCA) that refines attention through
stage-wise representation projection. By reprojecting attention-
conditioned features across refinement stages, P-LCA enables
attention to be re-examined under transformed representation
perspectives, effectively alleviating residual biases that remain
entangled with the main-branch attention in a single represen-
tation space. Extensive experiments on artist, genre, and emo-
tion classification tasks demonstrate consistent performance im-
provements over LCA-based baselines. Attention visualizations
