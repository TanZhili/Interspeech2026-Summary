Abstract
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative
disorder, and early detection is critical for intervention. Speech
offers a non-invasive, accessible biomarker for AD detection,
but suffers from severe data scarcity. Existing augmentation
methods in text or speech enrich training data but often ne-
glect cognitively relevant patterns. We propose a cognitive-
heuristic guided multimodal generative augmentation frame-
work that explicitly incorporates AD-related cognitive declines.
Our method integrates retrieval-augmented generation with
large language models for cognitively informed text generation,
and an attribute-guided text-to-speech system to reproduce AD-
related acoustic features. This design captures both linguistic
and acoustic alterations associated with cognitive decline. Ex-
periments on multiple benchmarks show that our augmented
data consistently enhances performance, highlighting the effi-
cacy of cognition-driven multimodal synthesis for speech-based
AD detection.
Index Terms: Alzheimer’s Disease, RAG, TTS, LLM
1. Introduction
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [1] is a neurodegenerative disorder
that poses a serious threat to public health, and its early identi-
fication is of great importance for clinical intervention and de-
laying disease progression. Speech, as a non-invasive, low-cost,
and easily accessible signal, provides insights into an individ-
ual’s cognitive status, including speech comprehension and pro-
duction abilities. [2, 3, 4] However, speech-based AD detection
is severely hindered by the bottleneck of data scarcity. Patient
speech collection is constrained by privacy and ethical consid-
erations, resulting in publicly available corpora that are limited
in both scale and annotation quality.
To mitigate this, researchers have explored various data
augmentation methods. In the text modality, strategies include
synonym replacement [5], back-translation [6], and GPT-based
generation [7, 8]. In the speech modality, acoustic perturbations
[9], voice conversion (VC) [6, 10], and text-to-speech (TTS)
[11, 12], are commonly employed. While these methods enrich
training sets to some extent, they suffer from two critical lim-
itations: (i) lack of multimodal jointly, as most rely on single-
modality perturbations that fail to maintain the intricate align-
ment between speech and text; and (ii) loss of cognitive con-
straints, where generated samples often neglect cognitively rel-
evant linguistic and acoustic signatures, such as specific pause

Cognitive decline in AD manifests as a set of distinct yet
interdependent markers [13], such as abnormal silences, speech
rate fluctuations, and reduced lexical diversity (as illustrated

hinges on incorporating cognitive knowledge that reflects im-

space with statistical indicators observed in real patient speech
(e.g., pause frequency and lexical diversity), we synthesize sam-
ples that reflect AD-style behavioral patterns and improve the
robustness of detection models.
Motivated by these considerations, we propose a cognitive-
heuristic-guided multimodal generative augmentation frame-
work. Our approach leverages Retrieval-Augmented Genera-
tion (RAG) [15] to provide external knowledge to Large Lan-
guage Models (LLMs) for cognitively informed text generation.
The generated texts are then paired with an attribute-guided
text-to-speech (TTS) system that reproduces AD-related acous-
tic characteristics based on specific cognitive labels. This de-
sign ensures linguistic–acoustic consistency, where the synthe-
sized speech mirrors the disfluencies present in the correspond-
ing text. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
1. Multimodal Cognitive Alignment. We propose a framework
that anchors multimodal generation in cognitive heuristics to
ensure linguistic–acoustic consistency.
2. Knowledge-Constrained Text Generation. We design a RAG-
enhanced pipeline that constrains LLMs to produce cogni-
tively grounded linguistic manifestations, preventing uncon-
strained hallucinations during text generation.
3. Attribute-Guided Speech Synthesis. We develop an attribute-
guided text-to-speech (TTS) system to generate AD-style
acoustic variants, enriching training data and improving de-
tection model robustness.
Experimental results show that incorporating our augmented
data into training improves model recognition performance.

2. METHODOLOGY
The proposed cognitive-heuristic data-augmentation framework
consists of three stages: 1) Data annotation to construct an
AD-specific feature knowledge base that captures characteris-
tics associated with cognitive decline; 2) AD-style text genera-
tion based on the extracted knowledge, leveraging a LLM with
RAG; 3) Speech synthesis to generate audio exhibiting AD-
related speech characteristics. The overall architecture is shown
in Fig. 2.
2.1. Cognitive-Heuristic Data Annotation
The core of our heuristic approach lies in grounding the gen-
erative process in the statistical distribution of real-world cog-
nitive manifestations. In real-world AD datasets, cognitive de-
cline typically manifests as systematic impairments in patients’
speech, including increased linguistic disfluency, reduced lexi-
cal diversity, and pronounced acoustic-level irregularities.
For linguistic fluency (f flu), we quantify the frequency of
non-lexical fillers (e.g., “uh”, “um”) occurring within each ut-
terance. For lexical complexity (f lex), we compute metrics in-
cluding the Moving-Average Type-Token Ratio (MATTR) [16]
capturing the reduced lexical diversity commonly observed in
AD-style speech. For acoustic fluency (f aco), we extract tem-
poral features such as the pause-to-speech ratio (derived from
character-level timestamps) [17] and fluctuations in speech rate.
These extracted features are aggregated into a cognitive at-
tribute vectorc= [f flu, flex, faco], such thatc∈[0,1] 3. By
modeling these heuristics, we construct a behavioral blueprint
that constrains the generation process, promoting task relevance
and distributional consistency with authentic cognitive profiles.
2.2. Knowledge-Constrained Text Generation
To prevent Large Language Models (LLMs) from generating
overly fluent or ”hallucinated” content, we implement a Dual-
Source Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) strategy[18,
19]. This approach anchors the synthesis process in both clini-
cal theory and real-world behavioral patterns, ensuring the gen-
erated scripts are statistically consistent with authentic cognitive
profiles.
For a given task promptPwhich defines the semantic task,
the RAG module performs semantic search across two indexing
structures: (i) an Expert Knowledge Base (Kexp) providing clin-
ical boundaries for linguistic impairment [13], and (ii) Exem-
plary Corpora (K exe) containing authentic transcriptions from
AD patients to serve as behavioral templates.
To bridge the gap between numerical features and linguis-
tic execution, we propose a Hierarchical Instruction Mapping
mechanism. This mechanism translates the full attribute vector
cinto a Behavioral Constraint Prompt (P cons) through three
targeted mapping functions:
• Semantic Constrainment (Φlex): Maps lexical scores (flex) to
constraints that limit vocabulary richness, forcing the LLM
to substitute specific nouns with generic deictic expressions
(e.g., ”that cold thing”).
• Rhythmic Perturbation (Φ flu ): Maps disfluency scores (f flu)
to structural mandates, instructing the LLM to insert non-
lexical fillers (e.g., ”uh”, ”um”) and self-repair cycles.
• Temporal Anchoring (Φ aco): Maps acoustic scores (f aco) to
the density and placement of temporal markers. This in-
structs the LLM where to embed<pause>tags based on
the prosodic breakdown patterns observed in real AD speech,
ensuring that the text provides a ”rhythmic skeleton” for sub-
sequent speech synthesis.
By integrating these constraints, the LLM functions as a con-
trolled generator:
Tsyn =LLM(P,k exp,k exe,Pcons)(1)
This design ensures thatT syn not only maintains seman-
tic integrity but also explicitly embeds cognitive markers that
strictly follow the cognitive blueprint defined byc.
2.3. Attribute-Guided Speech Synthesis
A critical innovation of our framework is the achievement
of multimodal cognitive alignment, ensuring that the acoustic
properties of the synthesized speech strictly correspond to the
linguistic markers in the text. We utilize an attribute-guided
TTS system based on a CosyV oice2 [20] architecture that is
fine-tuned to respond to cognitive labels.
In this stage, the synthesis process is conditioned on the
generated scriptT syn, which already contains explicit mark-
ers (e.g.,<pause>,<filler>). To transform these textual

3. Experiment Setups
3.1. Data and Preprocessing
We utilize two primary datasets in our experiments: the
ADReSSo dataset [21](comprising 237 samples) and the De-
mentiaBank Pitt Corpus. The DementiaBank Pitt Corpus is
used to fine-tune our attribute-guided TTS model, leveraging
its extensive speech–text pairs to capture cognitively consistent
characteristics. The ADReSSo dataset serves as the primary
corpus for AD detection training and provides seed samples for
multimodal generative augmentation. To ensure a fair evalu-
ation, all samples from the ADReSSo test set are strictly ex-
cluded from the training and fine-tuning stages.
The raw audio signals undergo a systematic preprocessing
pipeline to ensure high-quality multimodal alignment. First,
Demucs [22] is applied to separate speech from background
noise, producing clean speaker tracks. WhisperX [23] is then
used for sentence-level segmentation, followed by a 30-second
slicing strategy to standardize input duration. This process
yields approximately 80,000 high-quality training samples.
To capture subtle cognitive–linguistic features essential for
AD detection, we perform cognitive-heuristic annotation on the
processed corpus. WhisperX timestamps are used to extract
pause segments, which are categorized into short, medium, and
long durations [24]. Qwen2.5-Omni [25] is further employed
to identify instances of unclear pronunciation. Unlike standard
preprocessing pipelines, fillers such as “uh” and “um” are ex-
plicitly preserved in the transcriptions. These elements are en-
coded as cognitive labels, allowing the synthesized data to re-
flect AD-style speech rhythms while maintaining multimodal
consistency between text and audio.

3.3. Evaluation Metrics
For AD multimodal detection and audio augmentation exper-
iments, the primary evaluation metrics include Accuracy, F1-
score, Recall, and Precision, which are employed to quantify the
performance improvements of downstream AD detection mod-
els under different augmentation strategies.
In addition to detection performance, the evaluation of text
augmentation methods also incorporates linguistic quality anal-
ysis. Specifically, it considers: text fluency (Perplexity, PPL
[29]), diversity (MATTR [16], Distinct-2, D-2 [30]), semantic
fidelity (SBERT similarity [31]), and classification consistency
(the consistency of predictions made by a classifier trained on
original data when applied to augmented text, measured by Ac-
curacy and F1).
3.4. Implementation Details
For cognitively-consistent speech synthesis, we adopted
CosyV oice2 and performed full fine-tuning for 10 epochs using
the cognitively annotated speech data described in Section 3.1.
Training was conducted on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. The
remaining hyperparameters were set according to the training
configuration reported in the CosyV oice2 framework [20].

4. Results and Discussion
4.1. Multimodal Detection Performance
As shown in Table 2, incorporating cognitive-driven multi-
modal augmentation consistently improves the performance of
all models. The augmented samples are used to expand the
training data for the detection models, allowing them to learn
richer cognitive–linguistic and acoustic patterns. For exam-
ple, CogniAlign achieves higher Accuracy, F1, Precision, and
Recall after augmentation. These results suggest that the aug-
mented data help alleviate the limitations of the original train-
ing samples in capturing semantic, cognitive, and AD-related
acoustic manifestations.
Different models exhibit varying sensitivities to augmenta-
tion. For example, the Yuan model shows a notable improve-
ment in Recall, whereas CogniAlign achieves more balanced
improvements across all metrics. These results suggest that
the effectiveness of augmentation depends on the feature fu-
sion strategies of different architectures, highlighting the need
for model-specific adaptation
4.2. Effectiveness of Text-only Augmentation
From Table 3, our method outperforms traditional strategies
(deletion/insertion, back-translation) and fine-tuned GPT-2 in
Accuracy, F1, and Precision. This demonstrates that the gen-
erated texts more accurately preserve AD-related linguistic fea-
tures, thereby enhancing the classifier’s ability to identify ab-
normal language patterns.
As shown in Table 4, our augmented texts achieve good
fluency (PPL = 30.65) and semantic fidelity (SBERT =
0.955), while maintaining moderate diversity in Distinct-2 and
MATTR. This indicates that the generated texts preserve both
naturalness and semantic variation. Regarding classifier consis-
tency, our method achieves higher Accuracy and F1 than back-
translation and GPT-2 fine-tuning, and performs competitively
with deletion-based augmentation. In contrast, GPT-2 fine-
tuning lacks cognition-driven constraints, resulting in trade-offs
between the preservation of AD-related cognitive markers and
diversity enhancement.
Overall, cognition-driven LLM-based text augmentation
effectively balances completeness, semantic fidelity, and di-
versity, providing a reliable and robust strategy for AD text

4.3. Effectiveness of Speech-only Augmentation
From Table 5, cognition-driven augmentation combining LLM
and TTS achieves the best performance across Accuracy, F1,
and Recall. Compared with acoustic perturbation and VC
augmentation, our method provides significant improvements,
demonstrating the importance of cognitive label conditioning.
While perturbation and VC introduce some gains, they often re-
sult in an imbalance between Precision and Recall. Similarly,
raw-data TTS improves Recall but lacks control over the consis-
tency of AD-related cognitive patterns. In contrast, our method
achieves a better balance between naturalness and the controlla-
bility of AD-related behavioral manifestations, offering a more
targeted and effective audio augmentation strategy for multi-
modal AD detection.

5. CONCLUSION
We presented a cognitive-heuristic multimodal augmentation
framework that integrates RAG-enhanced text synthesis with
attribute-guided TTS to capture clinical AD signatures. Our
experiments suggest that grounding generative models in cog-
nitive heuristics can help alleviate data scarcity and potentially
improve AD detection performance. While a preliminary ex-
ploration, this work indicates the potential of modeling fine-
grained cognitive changes in multimodal AI, providing initial
insights for future speech-based cognitive health monitoring.
