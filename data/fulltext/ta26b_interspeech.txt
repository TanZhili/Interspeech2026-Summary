Abstract
Speech emotion recognition (SER) faces challenges due to
label ambiguity and high model uncertainty during early train-
ing. Standard training with hard one-hot targets ignores these is-
sues, forcing models to commit to a single class before learning
meaningful representations. We propose Progressive Weak Su-
pervision (PWS), a strategy that relaxes supervision in the early
stages and gradually tightens it as the model matures. During
the initial epochs, a sample is considered correctly predicted if
the true label is among the top-kpredictions, with a soft proba-
bility distribution assigned across them. As training progresses,
kdecays linearly to 1, thereby recovering standard supervision.
We evaluate PWS using a WavLM-Base encoder on IEMOCAP
(English, 4-class) and ViSEC (Vietnamese, 4-class). Withk=3,
PWS achieves 78.08% unweighted accuracy on IEMOCAP and
85.70% on ViSEC, surpassing several strong baseline methods.
Code is available at https://github.com/skyemo47/PWS.

1. Introduction
Speech emotion recognition (SER) is a foundational task in af-
fective computing, supporting applications ranging from men-
tal health monitoring and dialogue systems to call-center an-
alytics [1]. Despite steady progress driven by self-supervised
representations [2, 3, 4], SER remains considerably more chal-
lenging than most classification tasks for a structural rather than
incidental reason: emotions are inherently continuous, overlap-
ping, and subjectively perceived [5]. A single utterance may be
plausibly annotated assadby one listener andneutralby an-
other; both interpretations are acoustically defensible. Conven-
tional training, however, encodes only one. The ground-truth
label is therefore a compressed and partial representation of the
true emotional signal, and forcing a model to treat it as absolute
truth introduces a systematic mismatch between the training ob-
jective and the underlying data distribution.
A second challenge compounds the first. Although self-
supervised encoders have become the de facto standard in SER,
they are pre-trained primarily for automatic speech recognition
(ASR) and are not designed with emotional representation in
mind [6]. Their learned features capture phonetic and linguistic
structure well, but the paralinguistic cues—such as pitch dy-
namics, breathiness, and prosodic contour—that are central to
emotion are less explicitly represented. As a result, early in
training the model operates in a high-uncertainty regime [4]:
predicted distributions are diffuse, class boundaries are poorly
defined, and confidence is largely uncalibrated. The softmax

Prior work addresses only part of this problem. No existing
method simultaneously accounts for the model’s current predic-
tive state, the ambiguous nature of emotion labels, and the stage
of training—the three factors that jointly determine how super-
vision should be calibrated. Curriculum learning [7] reorders
training examples by difficulty but does not adapt the supervi-
sion signal itself. Label smoothing [8] replaces one-hot targets
with a uniform mixture over all classes, softening supervision
globally while ignoring the model’s current predictions and the
training stage; it distributes equal relaxation to clearly incorrect
and near-correct predictions alike.
We argue that the appropriate solution is toadapt the super-
vision signal to the model’s current state of knowledge. Early in
training, when uncertainty is high and learned representations
are immature, supervision should be permissive: partial credit
should be awarded when the model identifies the true class as
a plausible candidate. As training progresses and the model
develops confidence and discriminability, supervision should
progressively sharpen until it recovers full one-hot precision.
This is the core principle behind Progressive Weak Supervision
(PWS).
Concretely, PWS defines a dynamic correctness criterion.
At each epoch, a prediction is considered weakly correct if the
true label appears among the model’s top-koutputs. When this
condition holds, soft targets concentrate probability mass on
the true class while distributing the residual mass over the re-
maining top-kcandidates, acknowledging that these predictions
may reflect genuine acoustic similarity rather than model error.
When the true label is absent from the top-k, a hard corrective
signal is applied unconditionally. The thresholdkis held con-
stant during an initial warm-up phase and then decayed linearly
to 1, at which point PWS reduces exactly to standard cross-
entropy. The method requires no architectural changes and adds
negligible computational overhead.
Our contributions are as follows:
• We propose PWS, a principled training strategy that aligns
supervision strength with model maturity, directly addressing
the compounded problem of label ambiguity and early-stage
uncertainty in SER.
• We provide a systematic ablation overk init∈1,2,3and the
concentration parameterα, demonstrating consistent mono-
tonic improvement with increasingkfor 4-class emotion
recognition and confirming that the three-phase decay sched-
ule is essential for peak performance.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets and Evaluation Protocol
IEMOCAP[9] is the most widely used English SER bench-
mark, comprising approximately 12 hours of scripted and im-
provised dyadic interactions from 10 actors across 5 sessions.
Following standard practice, we merge happy and excited into
a single happy class, retain four emotion categories (neutral,
happy, sad, angry) containing 5,531 utterances.
ViSEC[10] is a Vietnamese SER corpus comprising 5,280 ut-
terances from 147 speakers across four matched emotion cate-
gories (neutral, happy, sad, angry). Vietnamese is a tonal lan-
guage whose prosodic structure differs fundamentally from En-
glish, making ViSEC a meaningful probe of cross-lingual gen-
eralization.
Cross-validation.We apply session-based 5-fold cross-
validation on IEMOCAP—one session per fold—to prevent
speaker leakage across train and test splits. For ViSEC we use
speaker-stratified 5-fold cross-validation. All results are aver-
ages over five folds.
Metric.Following standard SER practice [11], we reportUn-

We use WavLM-Base [4] as the acoustic encoder, initial-

3.4. Main Results
Table 1 reports UA on both datasets.
PWS outperforms all baselines on both datasets.PWS
(kinit=3) achieves 78.08% UA on IEMOCAP and 85.70% on
ViSEC. Within the controlled comparison, it surpasses CE
by 4.66 and 11.90 points, LS by 3.93 and 10.40 points, and
LS+CL by 3.19 points (IEMOCAP only, as ViSEC results are
unavailable). Against external results, it exceeds EmoDim—
the strongest prior WavLM-Base method—by 1.10 points on
IEMOCAP.
Gains grow withk init.Increasingk init from 2 to 3 yields con-
sistent further improvements on both datasets (+1.50 on IEMO-
CAP, +3.60 on ViSEC). This can be explained as follows. For
a 4-class problem,k init = 3covers nearly the full label space.
During early training, this provides the richest constructive sig-
nal: the model receives credit whenever the true class appears
among any of its three strongest predictions. Because emotions
such asneutral/sadandhappy/angryare frequently confused
due to acoustic overlap, rank-2 or rank-3 predictions often re-
flect genuine perceptual ambiguity rather than model failure.
PWS converts these near-misses into a useful learning signal
rather than issuing a destabilizing penalty.
Gains are amplified on the lower-resource corpus.The im-
provement on ViSEC (+11.90 over CE) is more than twice that
on IEMOCAP (+4.66). ViSEC combines limited per-speaker
data with a tonal language that is prosodically unfamiliar to a
model pre-trained on English ASR, both of which prolong the
resolution of model uncertainty. This makes the relaxed early
supervision of PWS especially effective, and confirms that PWS
scales to more challenging recognition conditions.

3.7. PWS vs. Label Smoothing
The results collectively support a consistent interpretation. The
central advantage of PWS over label smoothing—its clos-
est conceptual relative—lies in two properties that LS lacks:
model-awarenessandtemporal adaptation. LS distributes a
fixed residual uniformly to allCclasses at every epoch, includ-
ing classes the model already ranks last and at the final epoch
when the model is fully converged. PWS directs residual mass
only to the model’s current top-kcandidates, so credit flows to
acoustically similar classes rather than to unrelated ones, elim-
inating this relaxation entirely once representations have con-
verged. Together, these two properties account for the 3.93-
point UA gap between LS and PWS (k init=3) on IEMOCAP,
despite using the same encoder and training data.
4. Conclusion
We introducedProgressive Weak Supervision(PWS), a training
strategy that adapts supervision strength to the model’s learn-
ing stage. Instead of enforcing strict one-hot targets throughout
training, PWS temporarily relaxes the correctness criterion by
allowing the true label to appear among the model’s top-kpre-
dictions and assigning a soft target distribution over these can-
didates. The tolerance parameterkthen decays during training,
gradually recovering standard cross-entropy supervision.
Experiments with a WavLM-Base encoder on IEMOCAP
and VISEC demonstrate consistent improvements over cross-
entropy and label smoothing baselines, with particularly strong
gains in the lower-resource setting. Per-class analysis confirms
that PWS disproportionately improves recall on acoustically
confusable classes.
