Consistency-Regularized Dual-Branch Network with Performance-Aware
Mean Teacher for Sound Event Detection
Lipeng Dai1, Qing Wang ID 1,∗, Wu Guo1, Peng Gao1, Zhijun Zhang1, Kuiliang Li1, Jinjie Fu1
1 NERC-SLIP, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei, China

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a method to enhance pre-trained
model-based sound event detection. The model architecture
consists of a pre-trained Audio Teacher Student Transformer
(ATST) front-end and a cascaded dual-branch back-end that
models shallow and deep features of ATST, respectively. To
capture more discriminative cues, we propose a temporal topo-
logical consistency loss to align the temporal structural similari-
ties of the dual-branch features. For model training, we propose
a performance-aware mean teacher framework, which features
an adaptive exponential moving average strategy to adjust the
teacher model’s update magnitude based on the student model’s
performance, thereby ensuring the stability of model updates.
Furthermore, we employ a cross-stage fusion strategy to com-
bine the strengths of different training stages. Experimental re-
sults on the DCASE 2024 Challenge Task 4 dataset demonstrate
the superiority of the proposed method.
Index Terms: sound event detection, dual-branch network,
temporal topology, mean teacher
1. Introduction
Sound event detection (SED) aims to identify the categories
and temporal boundaries of sound events within audio record-
ings, supporting a broad range of applications such as smart
homes [1], security monitoring [2], and industrial fault detec-
tion [3]. Over the past decade, deep neural networks (DNNs),
including convolutional neural networks (CNNs) [4], recurrent
neural networks (RNNs) [5], Transformers [6], and their vari-
ants [7,8], have been successfully applied to SED. Recently, the
paradigm of using pre-trained models (PTMs) such as ATST
[9], PaSST [10], and BEATs [11] to replace traditional CNNs
as feature extractors has become increasingly prevalent, further
driving the development of SED.
Following this trend, we introduced a dual-branch Con-
former network (ATST-DBC) [12] to effectively leverage the
multi-layer features of ATST for SED. It explicitly separates
shallow and deep representations to model their respective tem-
poral dependencies. While this method has achieved promising
results, there remains room for further improvement. In ATST-
DBC, the dual branches operate independently, which poten-
tially leads to scale inconsistency between their feature sets and
results in a local optimum during model training. The Kullback-
Leibler (KL) divergence, which is widely applied in knowledge
distillation [13, 14], provides an intuitive approach to address-
ing this problem. However, KL divergence is designed to mea-
sure the discrepancy between probability distributions, making
it inappropriate for non-probabilistic intermediate representa-

tions. Additionally, its frame-wise operation overlooks tempo-
ral structures, which is suboptimal for tasks with strong tempo-
ral dependencies like SED. Therefore, it is necessary to explore
a temporal-aware constraint for intermediate representations to
exploit the synergy between the dual branches.
Beyond model architecture, the quantity and quality of
training data significantly influence system performance. In the
field of SED, due to the high cost of precise annotation, it is dif-
ficult to obtain large-scale labeled training data, which has led to
the widespread use of semi-supervised learning (SSL) [15, 16].
Among these, the mean teacher (MT) framework has emerged
as the dominant SSL approach. It maintains the teacher model
as an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student model’s
weights to generate reliable pseudo-labels. In previous stud-
ies, Zheng et al. [17] and Koh et al. [18] essentially focused on
enhancing the knowledge alignment between the student and
teacher models by introducing diverse perturbations. However,
these methods often overlook the non-monotonicity of the stu-
dent model’s training process and typically employ a fixed rate
to update the teacher model. In [19], Brotons et al. intro-
duced a strategy that maintains multiple teacher models updated
at different rates and selects the optimal one to guide the stu-
dent model, achieving performance improvements. Inspired by
this, we incorporate an adaptive EMA mechanism into the MT
framework to enhance its robustness.
The contributions of this work are as follows.
• We propose a temporal topology consistency (TTC) loss, en-
forcing the consistency of the dual-branch features by mini-
mizing the mean squared error (MSE) between their tempo-
ral self-similarity patterns. This approach can synchronize
the evolutionary trajectories of sound events across branches
while preserving the inherent feature complementarity of the
dual-branch network.
• We propose a performance-aware mean teacher (PA-MT)
framework that adaptively updates the teacher model at vary-
ing rates based on the student’s performance. Without requir-
ing additional teacher models, it ensures stable knowledge
transfer during the student-teacher learning process.
• We employ a cross-stage fusion (CSF) strategy to mitigate the
performance trade-off identified by Nam et al. [20] during the
fine-tuning of PTMs. On the DCASE 2024 Challenge Task
4 dataset, the proposed method achieves 0.541 PSDS1, 0.768
mpAUC, and 1.309 Score, establishing a new state-of-the-art
(SOTA) performance to the best of our knowledge.

primary improvements: (1) imposing a specifically designed

(2) upgrading the standard MT to PA-MT based on adaptive
EMA. Notably, there are four distinct loss functions. In the fol-
lowing sections, we elaborate on each approach and its associ-
ated loss sequentially.

During training, the framework minimizes a composite
loss, comprising a supervised loss and an alignment loss. The
alignment loss requires the teacher and student models to pro-
duce consistent outputs for the same sample under different per-
turbations. By employing the EMA-updated teacher model as a
stable target generator, MT provides more reliable supervision,
further exploiting the potential value of unlabeled data.
3.2.2. The proposed framework
Due to training fluctuations, parameter updates of the student
model are not consistently effective, revealing the limitations of
traditional EMA with a fixed decay factor. To address this, we
propose the performance-aware mean teacher (PA-MT) frame-
work, which employs an adaptive EMA to dynamically ad-
just the teacher model’s update magnitude based on student
performance. As depicted in Figure 1, the original, weakly-
augmented (via time masking), and strongly-augmented (via
time masking and mixup [24]) data views are used to calculate
the losses.L sup denotes the supervised loss for labeled data.
LP A-MT denotes the alignment loss for both labeled and unla-
beled data.L TTC is the TTC loss. With loss weightsα,βand
γ, the total optimization objective is defined as
Ltotal =α·L sup +β·L P A-MT+γ·L TTC.(5)
Notably,L ref is excluded from the optimization objective,
acting instead as a reference signal to drive the adaptive EMA.
As illustrated in Algorithm 1, for the sake of training efficiency,
we utilize the original labeled data within each mini-batch to
compute the reference lossL ref, which serves to evaluate the
newly updated student model’s learning status on the current
samples. IfL ref reaches a new minimum, the small decay fac-
torλ fast is adopted to allow the teacher model to more aggres-
sively incorporate the student model’s parameters; otherwise,
the large decay factorλ slow is adopted to ensure the stability of
the teacher model. This performance-aware mechanism miti-
gates the negative impact of training fluctuations, thereby pro-
viding more reliable pseudo-labels for the student model.
3.3. Cross-stage fusion strategy
We follow the conventional training procedure [12]. In the first
stage (Stage1), the ATST encoder remains frozen, and only the
context network is trained. In the second stage (Stage2), the
ATST and the context network are jointly updated, where the
fine-tuning of the ATST is adapter-based. Since fine-tuning
PTMs often involves a performance trade-off, we employ the
cross-stage fusion (CSF) strategy after the two-stage training
to combine the unique strengths of both general-purpose and
specialized models. Specifically, the final prediction is ob-
tained by averaging the predictions of the models before and
after fine-tuning. This simple fusion strategy enhances the sys-
tem’s overall performance and robustness without incurring ad-
ditional training costs.
4. Experimental Setup
Dataset and evaluation metrics:Experiments are conducted
on the DESED [25] and MAESTRO Real [26] datasets provided
by DCASE 2024 Challenge Task 4. The training set contains
10000 synthetic strongly-labeled clips, 1578 weakly-labeled
clips, 14412 unlabeled clips, 3470 extra strongly-labeled clips,
and 7503 softly-labeled clips. Additionally, we augment the
dataset with≈7000 strongly-labeled clips from AudioSet [27].
The test set includes a total of 1184 clips from both DESED
and MAESTRO Real, comprising strongly-labeled and softly-
labeled clips. Each clip is 10 s long. System performance is
evaluated using two metrics [28]: the polyphonic sound event
detection score 1 (PSDS1), which focuses on the precision of
temporal localization, and the mean partial area under the curve
(mpAUC), which focuses on the classification performance un-
der low false positive rates (FPR). The Score is PSDS1+
mpAUC, serving as the official ranking metric to reflect over-
all performance. Experimental results in this paper are the best
from three independent runs based on these datasets.
Configurations:All input clips are uniformly sampled at
16 kHz. Training is performed using the AdamW [29] opti-
mizer. In Stage1, the context network is trained for 200 epochs
with a learning rate of10 −3, a weight decay of10 −2, and a
mini-batch size of 36. In Stage2, the model is fine-tuned for
250 epochs; the learning rates for the ATST adapters and the
context network are set to10−4 and10−3, respectively, with a
weight decay of10−3 and a mini-batch size of 28. The type of
supervised loss is binary cross-entropy (BCE), while the type
of alignment loss is MSE. We fix the loss weights atα= 0.5
andβ= 0.5, but tune the hyperparameterγspecifically for our
model. For post-processing, a median filter is applied to smooth
the model’s output.
5. Experimental Results
In this section, we conduct a multi-faceted evaluation to validate
the proposed method. First, we compare our method with SOTA
methods to assess its overall performance, complemented by
ablation studies to analyze the contribution of each design el-
ement. Furthermore, we explore the impact of parameter con-
figurations for TTC loss and PA-MT, comparing them with the
respective counterparts to demonstrate their advantages.

5.1. Comparison with SOTA methods
Table 1 (top) compares the performance of our proposed method
with other SOTA methods on the dataset described in Section 4,
where “CP-JKU (Iter.2)” employs three PTMs for ensemble and
iterative training. In Stage1, the proposed method achieves the
second-best results in both PSDS1 (0.532) and mpAUC (0.748),
consequently attaining the highest Score (1.280). In Stage2, the
proposed method achieves the optimal mpAUC (0.768). De-
spite a slight decrease in PSDS1 (0.525), the Score (1.293) still
reaches the best single-system performance, approaching that
of the ensemble system with full fine-tuning. After applying
the CSF strategy, the proposed method achieves 0.541 PSDS1,
0.768 mpAUC, and 1.309 Score, setting a new performance
record on this dataset. Collectively, the results at each stage are
competitive, indicating that constraining the temporal topology
of dual-branch features, coupled with stable training and fusion
strategies, is beneficial for SED.
5.2. Ablation study
To verify the effectiveness of each design element in the pro-
posed method, we conduct several ablation studies, and the re-
sults are shown in Table 1 (bottom). It is evident that removing
any design element results in overall performance degradation.
Specifically, the TTC loss primarily improves PSDS1, indicat-
ing that synchronizing the evolution trajectories between the
dual branches enhances the system’s sound event localization
capability. Replacing PA-MT with standard MT leads to a de-
cline in both PSDS1 and mpAUC, suggesting that the teacher
model updated via adaptive EMA provides superior learning
targets for the student model. However, in Stage2, the simul-
taneous application of TTC loss and PA-MT leads to a marginal
decrease in PSDS1. This performance trade-off is addressed
by a straightforward CSF strategy, which also benefits from the
robust performance established in Stage1. These findings re-
veal the complementary gains among the design elements and
demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

stage helps preserve feature diversity. Table 2 compares TTC
loss with conventional MSE loss and KL divergence, demon-

dual-branch features is better suited for SED.

For PA-MT, we fixλslow = 0.999and explore the optimalλ fast
with a step size of 0.002, as depicted in Figure 3. Withλ fast
increasing from 0.895 to 0.997, the system performance gener-

updates. As shown in Table 3, PA-MT outperforms standard
