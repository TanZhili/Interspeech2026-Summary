Continuous Time-Varying Emotion Control Zero-Shot Text-To-Speech With
Emotion Orthogonal LoRA

1 Shanghai Jiao Tong University, China ,2 Ping An Technology, China

Abstract
High-quality emotional text-to-speech (TTS) often depends on
large scale corpora and coarse discrete emotion labels, which
limits fine-grained and time-varying control in practice. We
address this by integrating continuous Valence, Arousal, and
Dominance (V AD) control into a pretrained model via low-rank
updates inserted into the backbone transformations. Our Emo-
tion Orthogonal LoRA (EO-LoRA) uses three separate low-
rank branches aligned to valence, arousal, and dominance, and
an orthogonality regularizer encourages the branches to cap-
ture distinct effects. We further introduce Flow-DGPO, a rein-
forcement learning alignment stage for flow matching TTS that
improves emotion controllability while preserving intelligibil-
ity and speaker similarity. Experiments show that our method
improves continuous and time-varying emotion control while
maintaining synthesis quality with limited training data.
Index Terms: emotional text-to-speech, Emotion Orthogonal
LoRA, reinforcement learning, time-varying emotion control
1. Introduction
Emotional expressiveness is an important part of speech com-
munication. Beyond the words, emotion is conveyed through
prosody, including pitch movement, energy, speaking rate, and
subtle voice quality changes. With modern generative text-
to-speech (TTS) backbones, especially models built on flow
matching frameworks [1], zero-shot TTS can reach high nat-
uralness and preserve speaker identity well. However, reli-
able emotion control is still difficult in practice. Many sys-
tems [2, 3, 4, 5, 6] rely on discrete emotion categories, which
are simple but too coarse for describing intensity differences
and gradual transitions. Reference based prompting is widely
used in zero-shot TTS [7, 8, 9, 10]. However, the reference
signal often entangles emotion with speaker traits and content
prosody, which makes it difficult to adjust emotion without af-
fecting other attributes. In addition to utterance-level control,
real speech can show emotion shifts within a single sentence,
which calls for time-varying emotion control. This setting is
challenging because the intended emotion changes can be weak-
ened or excessively smoothed, and the speech can gradually
lean toward the emotion carried by the prompt speech.
Recent work explores different choices along these trade-
offs. For discrete emotion control, IndexTTS2 [11] improves
expressiveness and studies separating emotion from speaker
traits, but the control signal is still restricted to a small set
of emotion categories. Prompt based approaches such as
EmoV oice [12] use free-form text to describe emotion, which
increases flexibility, while the results can vary across prompts
and often require careful data design to stay consistent. To sup-
port finer control over emotion strength and smooth transitions,
some methods adopt dimensional emotion representations. In
particular, Valence, Arousal, and Dominance (V AD) [13] de-
scribes emotion with three continuous values that reflect pos-
itivity, activation, and dominance. EmoSphere-TTS [2] pro-
poses a spherical emotion representation derived from arousal,
valence, and dominance signals, and uses it to control both emo-
tion style and intensity without relying on human emotion an-
notation. For time-varying emotion control, EmoCtrl-TTS [14]
aligns frame-level V AD trajectories in a zero-shot setting, but it
relies on large-scale emotional resources. Reinforcement learn-
ing has recently been introduced to speech generation to better
balance control and quality. EmoDPO [15] uses direct prefer-
ence optimization (DPO) [16] to strengthen emotional expres-
sion, and F5R-TTS [17] applies a GRPO [18] objective to im-
prove naturalness and speaker similarity in flow matching TTS.
Despite recent progress, there is still limited work that system-
atically applies reinforcement learning to enhance emotion con-
trollability, particularly for time-varying emotion control.
In this paper, we study continuous and time-varying emo-
tion control for zero-shot TTS built on a pretrained flow match-
ing backbone. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) [19] is a widely
used way to steer large pretrained models, but it typically ap-
plies a single low-rank update to each adapted layer and does
not offer an explicit mechanism to separate different emotion at-
tributes. In contrast, we inject valence, arousal, and dominance
signals into the backbone transformations through three aligned
low-rank update branches, each scaled by its corresponding
control value at every time step. This design provides a direct
and interpretable interface for emotional control, and it reduces
coupling across emotion dimensions by construction. Further,
we propose Flow-DGPO, a preference optimization alignment
stage for flow matching TTS. It improves emotion expression
by comparing multiple sampled candidates and optimizing the
model toward candidates that better match the target emotion,
while still preserving intelligibility and speaker similarity. Our
contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose EO-LoRA, which injects three valence, arousal,
and dominance scaled low-rank update branches into selected
linear layers of a pretrained flow matching TTS backbone,
together with an orthogonality regularizer that encourages the
branches to learn distinct updates and reduces interference
across emotion dimensions.
• We propose Flow-DGPO, a preference based alignment stage
for flow matching TTS that improves emotion controllability
through candidate comparison and preference optimization,
while maintaining intelligibility and speaker similarity.
• Experiments in time-varying and cross-lingual settings show
consistent gains in emotion controllability with competitive
speech quality under a limited training data.

2. Proposed Methods
In this section, we present three components. I) EO-LoRA in-
jects a continuous V AD sequence into selected linear layers us-
ing three low-rank branches scaled by Valence, Arousal, and
Dominance at each time step. II) Flow-DGPO is a reinforce-
ment learning based alignment stage that improves emotion
controllability while keeping speech quality stable. III) We de-
scribe the overall training and inference procedure.

from prompts. In this work, we incorporate emotion control into
the backbone transformations. We allocate three separate low-

each dimension modulates generation through its own learnable
update and provides a direct and interpretable control signal.

3. Experiment
3.1. Experimental setup
Data.We train on two emotional corpora, EmoV oice-DB
[12] and the English subset of ESD [23]. EmoV oice-DB con-
tains over 20,000 English emotional speech samples (about 40
hours). ESD provides 10 English speakers with 5 emotion cat-
egories (Angry, Happy, Sad, Surprise, Neutral), with 350 ut-
terances per speaker per emotion (about 1,750 utterances and
1.2 hours per speaker). We evaluate (i) time-varying emo-
tional transitions on EMO-Change, and (ii) cross-lingual emo-
tion transfer on JVNV S2ST [24]. For EMO-Change, we follow
[14] and build prompts from RA VDESS [25] by concatenating
two utterances that share the same transcript but have differ-
ent emotions, forming a single prompt with an explicit emotion
shift. We then synthesize speech from a fixed text and evalu-
ate whether the output follows the prompt’s emotion transition
while keeping content and speaker identity. For JVNV S2ST,
we follow the Japanese-to-English protocol in [14] on JVNV
[24], where Japanese speech provides the audio prompt and
the translated English text is used as input, testing cross-lingual
preservation of emotion and speaker traits.
Implementation details.We extract frame-level V AD using a
pretrained wav2vec2-based predictor [26] fine-tuned on MSP-
PODCAST [27] with a 0.5 s window and 0.30 s hop size, and
linearly interpolate V AD sequence to match mel frames. Ad-
ditionally, we linearly normalize the predicted V AD values to
the range[−0.5,0.5]. We build on the pretrained F5-TTS [22]
Base checkpoint. EO-LoRA usesr= 16andα= 32and is in-
serted into attentionValue/Outputprojections and feed-forward
linear layers, adding about 10M trainable parameters. Training
has two stages, supervised fine-tuning and Flow-DGPO align-
ment. Supervised fine-tuning useλ orth = 0.05. Flow-DGPO
usesβ= 20, group sizeG= 8, 32 sampling steps, and reward
weightsλ emo = 3,λ spk = 1,λ wer = 1.
Model for comparison.We compare EO-LoRA with flow
matching TTS models, including EmoCtrl-TTS [14] and the F5-
TTS backbone [22]. Since EmoCtrl-TTS is not publicly avail-
able, we report the results as provided in EmoCtrl-TTS [14]. As
a baseline, we fine-tune F5-TTS with the V AD sequence embed-
ded as an additional conditioning stream and fused with stan-
dard inputs before entering the DiT backbone. For experimen-
tal consistency, we follow the evaluation protocols in EmoCtrl-
TTS to construct JVNV S2ST and EMO-Change.
3.2. Metrics
Objective metrics.I) WER: computed by Whisper-large-
v3 [28]; II) speaker similarity (SIM-o): defined as the co-
sine similarity between WavLM-large speaker embeddings [29]
of the generated speech and the reference prompt; III) Au-
toPCP: utterance-level prosody similarity computed by Au-
toPCP multilingual v2 [30]; IV) Emo SIM: time-varying emo-
tion similarity as the average frame-level cosine similarity be-
tween aligned emotion embedding sequences of the generated
speech and the prompt, where embeddings are extracted by
emotion2vec [31]; V) Aro-Val SIM: the similarity of arousal
and valence trajectories as the average frame-level cosine simi-
larity, where trajectories are extracted by predictor [26].
Subjective metrics.We conduct MOS tests on naturalness
(NMOS), speaker similarity (SMOS), and emotion similarity
(EMOS) to the target. Ratings use a 5-point Likert scale, and
we report mean scores with95%confidence intervals. We re-
cruited 20 native English listeners and evaluated 20 utterances.

3.3. Results and analysis
Objective evaluation.Table 1 reports objective results on
JVNV S2ST and EMO-change. Here we only analyze EO-
LoRA, F5-TTS baseline and EmoCtrl-TTS. On JVNV S2ST,
EO-LoRA improves over F5-TTS baseline on controllability,
with AutoPCP increasing from 2.83 to 3.27, Emo SIM from
0.632 to 0.688, and Aro-Val SIM from 0.601 to 0.679, while
speaker similarity increases from 0.426 to 0.442 and WER de-
creases from 8.4% to 4.9%. Compared with EmoCtrl-TTS, EO-
LoRA is competitive on emotion similarity despite using less
emotion training data, while a remaining gap in speaker simi-
larity and WER is expected since the prompt speech is Japanese
but the backbone is not trained on Japanese data. On EMO-
change, EO-LoRA improves over F5-TTS baseline more sub-
stantially, with AutoPCP increasing from 3.17 to 3.51, Aro-Val
SIM from 0.837 to 0.905, and Emo SIM from 0.693 to 0.753,
while WER drops from 7.1% to 0.6%. EO-LoRA also com-
pares favorably to EmoCtrl-TTS on this benchmark: it achieves
higher AutoPCP, Emo SIM, and Aro-Val SIM, suggesting that
injecting V AD through internal low-rank modulation is particu-
larly effective for emotion transitions within an utterance.
Subjective evaluation.Table 2 shows that listeners report the
same trends as the objective metrics. Across both JVNV S2ST
and EMO-change, EO-LoRA improves naturalness, speaker
similarity, and emotion similarity over F5-TTS, and adding
Flow-DGPO brings further gains. Overall, EO-LoRA + Flow-
DGPO achieves the strongest listener preference. Audio sam-

Effect of the orthogonality regularizer .RemovingL orth
consistently weakens controllability. For example, on EMO-
change, Emo SIM drops from 0.753 to 0.708 and Aro-Val SIM
drops from 0.905 to 0.843, indicating that the orthogonality
term reduces interference across the V AD branches and stabi-
lizes per dimension control.
Effect of reinforcement learning alignment.Flow-DGPO
consistently improves both controllability and quality without
an obvious tradeoff. On JVNV S2ST, it improves speaker sim-
ilarity and reduces WER, while further increasing AutoPCP,
Emo SIM, and Aro-Val SIM over EO-LoRA. On EMO-change,
it yields the same pattern, bringing additional gains in speaker
similarity, intelligibility, and all controllability metrics. Overall,
the alignment stage strengthens emotion controllability while
preserving intelligibility and speaker identity.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed EO-LoRA combined with Flow-
DGPO to enable robust, time-varying emotion control in zero-
shot TTS. Our approach injects valence, arousal, and dom-
inance through three aligned low-rank branches and reduces
interference across them, while Flow-DGPO further improves
controllability while preserving intelligibility and speaker simi-
larity. Experiments show consistent gains over strong baselines
in both objective and subjective metrics. Moving forward, we
plan to extend this approach to other generative backbones.
