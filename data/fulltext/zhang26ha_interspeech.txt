Abstract
Edge keyword spotting (KWS) in continuous streaming must
satisfy strict false-alarm (FA) budgets while maintaining high
detection sensitivity. At stringent points (≤0.5FA/h), CE-
trained lightweight causal models suffer severe false rejec-
tion rate (FRR) degradation against phonetically similar im-
posters. We proposeMargin-Aware Contrastive Regularization
(MACR), an offline auxiliary objective that applies intra-class
attraction only to target keywords to avoid globally cluster-
ing the heterogeneousUnknowncategory, and enforces margin-
based repulsion emphasizing offline-mined hard negatives. On
Google Speech Commands V2 under an event-level streaming
protocol, MACR achieves>40%relative FRR reduction over
CE at strict points (0.5–0.2 FA/h) with minor changes in closed-
set accuracy. As a training-only regularizer, MACR introduces
zero additional parameters, MACs, or algorithmic latency to the
deployed causal inference graph.

1. Introduction
Keyword spotting (KWS) serves as the primary acoustic entry
point for always-on voice interfaces on edge devices. Con-
strained by strict battery and memory budgets, these systems
typically rely on lightweight, strictly causal acoustic models to
ensure zero-lookahead streaming inference [1, 2, 3, 4]. While
contemporary architectures optimized via the standard Cross-
Entropy (CE) objective often achieve strong performance on
isolated-utterance closed-set benchmarks, real-world deploy-
ment presents a fundamentally different challenge. In practical
environments, maintaining high detection sensitivity in contin-
uous, unsegmented audio streams while adhering to stringent
false alarm (FA) constraints (e.g.,<0.5FA per hour) remains
a critical bottleneck [5].
Empirical observations indicate that the standard CE ob-
jective primarily establishes functional decision boundaries for
classification but does not explicitly enforce a geometric safety
margin in the latent representation space. Under continuous-
stream conditions, this structural limitation leaves the model
vulnerable to phonetically similar non-target speech (imposters)
and complex environmental noises. To maintain strict false
alarm rates against these confusing samples, deployment typ-
ically requires conservative threshold calibration [6]. Conse-
quently, this threshold tightening often leads to severe degra-
dation in the False Rejection Rate (FRR), rendering the system
overly conservative in practice.

Recent literature has explored various strategies to suppress
false alarms, including hard-example probability weighting [7],
cross-layer discriminative consistency [8], and synthetic im-
poster augmentations [9]. Alternatively, supervised contrastive
learning offers a mechanism to explicitly shape the latent geo-
metric space [10]. However, a direct application of global con-
trastive clustering to KWS introduces a specific representational
risk. The Unknown category in standard KWS protocols is not
a compact acoustic class; rather, it aggregates diverse non-target
speech patterns that, during real-world deployment, inherently
interleave with nuisance conditions such as environmental noise
and silence [11]. Forcibly clustering such disparate acoustic
patterns into a single latent centroid induces representational
distortion, which we empirically observe to degrade, rather than
improve, baseline discriminative capacity.
To address these continuous-stream vulnerabilities with-
out inflating deployment overhead, we propose Margin-Aware
Contrastive Regularization (MACR). Functioning exclusively
as an offline auxiliary training objective, MACR is designed
to reshape the latent acoustic geometry for streaming robust-
ness. It restricts its intra-class pull optimization strictly to
structurally consistent target keywords. Simultaneously, it em-
ploys a margin-aware repulsion penalty heavily weighted to-
ward offline-mined hard negatives, explicitly separating them
from target embeddings without over-penalizing already well-
separated non-confusing non-target/background samples. Un-
like standard supervised contrastive formulations, MACR does
not impose global class-wise clustering over the heterogeneous
Unknowncategory, and instead explicitly decouples target com-
pactness from imposter-margin enforcement. Importantly, the
gains come from the training objective rather than inference-
time heuristics: decoding, smoothing, and thresholding remain
identical across methods.
Unlike generic supervised contrastive objectives that treat
the non-target pool as a homogeneous class, we explicitly model
the heterogeneity of Unknown audio and optimize the embed-
ding geometry for ultra-low false-alarm operating points, which
is where standard objectives often degrade.
Evaluated on the Google Speech Commands V2 (12-class)
benchmark under a rigorous continuous-stream event-level pro-
tocol, the primary contributions of this work are:
• We empirically show that standard CE training can exhibit
a representation-margin limitation, explicitly demonstrating
its FRR vulnerability under strict continuous-stream FA con-
straints.
• We introduce MACR, an offline regularization strategy that
selectively pulls target classes while maintaining a geometric
margin against hard imposters, avoiding distortion of the het-
erogeneous Unknown manifold and substantially mitigating

2. Methodology
Standard lightweight keyword spotting models are typically op-
timized using the Cross-Entropy (CE) objective. While CE ef-
fectively establishes functional decision boundaries for isolated
utterance classification, it does not explicitly enforce a geomet-
ric margin between classes in the latent representation space.
Under continuous-stream detection, insufficient representation
margin can make the model more vulnerable to phonetically
similar non-target speech (imposters), especially when thresh-
olds are calibrated for strict false alarm (FA) operating points.
To mitigate this vulnerability, we introduce Margin-Aware Con-
trastive Regularization (MACR), an offline auxiliary objective
designed to reshape the latent acoustic geometry. The overall
framework is illustrated in Fig. 1.

LetBdenote a training mini-batch. For each samplei∈B,

provide targeted gradient signals during representation shaping,
hard negative samples (e.g., confusing non-target speech im-
posters) are mined asynchronously from non-target audio avail-
able in the training split [12]. Specifically, hard negatives are
defined by the top-Khighest target posteriors among non-target
windows, dynamically tracked via the exponential moving aver-
age (EMA) of the training network to reflect evolving decision
boundaries. We use a sample-level binary indicatorhi∈{0,1}
for these mined hard negatives; in the repulsion term, the pair
weight depends on whether the negative samplenis marked as
hard (i.e.,h n = 1).
2.2. The MACR Objective
The MACR objective comprises two constrained compo-
nents: target-restricted intra-class pull and margin-aware hard-
negative repulsion. This design intentionally prioritizes ro-
bust target-vs-imposter separation under strict FA constraints,
while preserving compatibility with CE supervision to maintain
closed-set discrimination.

induces severe representational distortion. Therefore, the pull

2.3. Joint Optimization and Zero-Overhead Inference
During offline training, the network is optimized via the joint
objectiveL total =L CE +λL M ACR. Empirically, directly
combining unnormalized CE logits with theℓ 2-normalized
MACR branch can lead to optimization instability. We use a

weight decay of10 −4, which stabilizes joint optimization and
prevents the CE loss from overshadowing the contrastive gradi-
ents during early training. At deployment, the MACR branch
is entirely discarded. The classifier forward path remains unal-
tered, retaining an identical causal inference graph, parameter
count, algorithmic lookahead (0 ms), and computational foot-
print (MACs) as the standard CE baseline.
3. Experiments
To objectively assess the deployment viability of the pro-
posed offline training regularization, we prioritize a continuous-
stream evaluation protocol over isolated closed-set classifica-
tion. The objective is to evaluate false alarm (FA) suppres-
sion under realistic constraints without introducing architectural
confounders.
3.1. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol
Dataset and Architecture Constraints.Empirical evaluations
are conducted on the Google Speech Commands V2 (GSC v2)
dataset using the standard 12-class keyword spotting proto-
col [15]. Closed-set accuracy is reported strictly on the stan-
dard isolated-utterance test split. To isolate the specific impact
of the optimization objectives from model capacity, all evalu-
ated configurations utilize an identical causal 1D-CNN back-
bone (approximately 150K parameters). The temporal convo-
lution layers employ causal padding to ensure zero-lookahead
streaming inference. Acoustic features are 64-dimensional log-
Mel spectrograms. We employ a stratified mini-batch sampler
to ensure multiple distinct target keywords are present in ev-
ery batch, strictly preventing empty denominators in Eq. 1. To
ensure strict baseline fairness, Std. SupCon is trained with its
canonical 2-layer MLP projection head (which is discarded at
inference to maintain zero deployment overhead), treating Un-
known as a standard labeled class. We also benchmark Arc-
Face [14] to evaluate a widely adopted zero-overhead angular
margin alternative. All methods use AdamW for 120 epochs (5
warm-up; batch size 128; learning rate10−3 cosine-decayed to
10−5; weight decay10 −4) with identical augmentation:±0.1
s time shifting, noise mixing atSNR∼U[−5,10]dB, and
frequency masking. Checkpoints are selected by validation-
stream FRR at 0.5 FA/h after threshold calibration. MACR uses
m= 0.4,α= 2.0,τ= 0.1, andλ= 0.5.
Continuous-Stream Protocol.For stable evaluation at 0.2
FA/h, the test set comprises 50 independently synthesized 2-
hour streams (100 hours total), with 2,000 class-balanced target
events per stream. Validation and test streams use split-specific
GSC v2 target, Unknown, and Silence samples with disjoint
background-noise segments mixed atSNR∼U[−5,10]dB.
Hard negatives are the top-5000 training non-target windows
ranked by target posterior from an EMA model (decay 0.999)
and refreshed every 5 epochs. Operating thresholds (θ) are inde-
pendently calibrated on a validation stream and strictly locked
for event-level test evaluation using a 1.0 s causal sliding win-
dow.
Strict Threshold Calibration and Metrics.Event-level
detection relies on a causal sliding window. We use a 1.0 s
causal analysis window with a 10 ms hop size for all methods.
System target posteriors undergo identical post-processing: a
50 ms moving-average smoothing and a 500 ms refractory win-
dow. Keyword hits are registered within a±200ms tolerance
window, and metrics (FRR/FA) are evaluated at the event level
over the 10 target keywords (withSilenceandUnknowntreated
as non-target/background classes in detection). To ensure un-
biased comparisons, the operating detection threshold (θ) for
each model is independently calibrated using an isolatedvali-
dation streamto meet predefined False Alarm per hour (FA/h)
constraints. Once calibrated, thresholds are strictly locked and
applied to the unseentest streamto measure the False Rejection
Rate (FRR).
While the continuous stream is constructed by embed-
ding events into background mixtures for controlled false-alarm
statistics, we follow established always-on KWS evaluation
practice and report results over long-duration streams to obtain
stable FRR@FA/h estimates. We view this protocol as comple-
mentary to fully naturalistic recordings.

Baseline Vulnerabilities & Manifold Distortion.While
the CE baseline achieves 95.82% closed-set accuracy, its FRR
degrades severely to 29.85% at 0.2 FA/h, indicating limited
geometric margin against continuous-stream imposters. Alter-
native mitigations (Focal Loss, CLDC [8], GraphemeAug [9])
also retain high FRR at extreme thresholds. Crucially, apply-
ing global geometric constraints fails: Std. SupCon degrades
closed-set accuracy (95.50%), and ArcFace—despite achieving
peak accuracy (96.10%) via uniform angular margins—suffers
severe streaming degradation (23.50% FRR at 0.2 FA/h). This
confirms our core hypothesis: forcing the highly heterogeneous
Unknownclass into a compact centroid or uniform angular sec-
tor topologically distorts the natural acoustic manifold. Fur-
thermore, even if one heuristically masks the Unknown class
in standard SupCon to create a ”Target-Only” variant, its un-
bounded global repulsion lacks the explicit geometric safety
margin required to precisely separate boundary-confusing im-
posters without over-penalizing diverse background noises.
The MACR Advantage.MACR’s asymmetric, target-only
pull strictly avoids this global distortion. As FA constraints
tighten to 0.2 FA/h, MACR yields a 15.05-percentage-point
FRR reduction (29.85%→14.80%), corresponding to a 50.4%
relative reduction over CE, while keeping closed-set accuracy
highly competitive (95.68% vs. 95.82%). By explicitly priori-
tizing localized margin separation from hard imposters rather
than solely optimizing isolated boundaries, MACR provides
a highly robust, zero-overhead operating point for always-on
streaming.

4. Conclusion
We addressed a practical failure mode of lightweight causal
KWS under continuous-stream deployment: when thresholds
are calibrated to meet strict false-alarm budgets, CE-trained
models can incur large FRR increases against phonetically sim-
ilar imposters. We proposed Margin-Aware Contrastive Regu-
larization (MACR), an offline objective that enforces an explicit
margin between target embeddings and hard negatives while
avoiding undesirable global clustering of the heterogeneous Un-
known category. On Google Speech Commands V2 with an
event-level streaming protocol, MACR improves robustness at
stringent operating points—reducing FRR from 16.32 to 7.64 at
0.5 FA/h and from 29.85 to 14.80 at 0.2 FA/h—with only minor
changes in closed-set accuracy. Because MACR affects training
only, it adds no parameters, MACs, or algorithmic latency to
the deployed causal inference graph, making it a practical drop-
in regularizer across the tested lightweight causal KWS back-
bones. The current evidence is limited to the evaluated English
multi-keyword strict-FA streaming setting; broader languages,
custom wake words, and far-field conditions remain for future
work.
