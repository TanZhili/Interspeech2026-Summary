Abstract
This paper presents SayCheck, an AI-powered speech therapy
platform designed to support children’s speech practice through
engaging gameplay and automated speech analysis. The plat-
form combines two integrated components: Say Bananas, a
Mario-style speech therapy game that motivates home prac-
tice, and PhoneAid, an automatic speech analysis system that
evaluates children’s speech productions at both the phoneme
and phonological attribute levels. The system enables thera-
pists and caregivers to configure targeted speech exercises while
providing detailed diagnostic feedback about pronunciation per-
formance. PhoneAid extends traditional phoneme-level assess-
ment by analysing articulatory and phonological speech at-
tributes, allowing more informative feedback about how speech
sounds differ from target productions. A demonstration of the
system highlights the workflow from gameplay-based speech
elicitation to detailed automatic speech assessment and sum-
mary reporting.
Index Terms: Gamified Speech Therapy, Mispronunciation
Detection, Phonological Attribute Analysis
1. Introduction
Speech therapy for children often requires consistent and repet-
itive home practice to reinforce clinical intervention. However,
maintaining engagement and motivation during repeated speech
exercises can be challenging, particularly for younger children.
Digital speech therapy tools have increasingly explored the use
of gamification and automatic speech assessment to improve
participation and provide scalable support outside the clinic.
SayCheck was developed to address these challenges by
integrating interactive gameplay with automatic speech analy-
sis. The platform combines a child-friendly game environment
with AI-based pronunciation assessment to encourage regular
speech practice while providing detailed diagnostic information
for therapists and caregivers. The system is composed of two
major components: Say Bananas [], a gamified speech practice
environment, and PhoneAid, an automatic speech analysis and
feedback tool.
The demonstration will showcase how speech exercises
can be seamlessly integrated into gameplay and how detailed
phonological analysis can provide insight into children’s speech
production beyond traditional correctness scoring.

3. Say Bananas
Say Bananas [1] is a side-scrolling adventure game designed
to encourage repeated speech practice in a motivating environ-
ment. The game adopts a Mario-style format in which children
navigate themed islands, collect coins and stars, and unlock new
characters, costumes, and power-ups.
Before gameplay begins, the therapist or caregiver config-
ures the speech exercises. Parameters include the number of
stars required to complete a level and the number of target
words spoken for each star collected. Target words can be
selected according to phoneme, phoneme position within the
word, and word length (see Figure 2). The system also al-
lows filtering and selection of target words based on the speech
sounds being practised.

4. PhoneAid
PhoneAid is the automatic speech analysis component of Say-
Check. The system analyses recorded speech productions and
provides detailed diagnostic feedback at both phoneme and
phonological attribute levels.
Traditional mispronunciation detection systems typically
operate at the phoneme level [2], where detected phoneme se-
quences are compared with expected canonical pronunciations.
While effective for identifying substitutions, insertions, and
deletions, phoneme-level systems provide limited information
about how the pronunciation differs from the target sound.
PhoneAid extends this approach using an attribute-based
speech analysis framework derived from recent phonological-
level mispronunciation detection research [3]. The system is
based on a wav2vec2 speech representation model [4] com-
bined with a phonological feature detection layer. Instead of
representing speech only as phoneme symbols, each phoneme
is represented as a combination of articulatory and phonological
attributes. For example, the phoneme /m/ may be represented as
voiced, nasal, bilabial, and sonorant.
The attribute model performs multi-label sequence pre-
diction using a separable connectionist temporal classification
(SCTC-SB) framework [5]. Speech representations extracted
from wav2vec2 are mapped into multiple binary attribute se-
quences representing the presence or absence of each phonolog-
ical feature. The recognised attribute sequences are then aligned
with reference attribute sequences derived from the canonical
pronunciation.

5. Conclusion
This demonstration presents SayCheck, a speech therapy plat-
form that combines gamified speech practice with detailed auto-
matic pronunciation analysis. By integrating Say Bananas and
PhoneAid, the platform supports engaging home-based speech
practice while providing clinically relevant diagnostic informa-
tion.
The use of phonological attribute modelling enables richer
speech analysis than conventional phoneme-level approaches
and offers greater insight into the nature of pronunciation er-
rors. The system demonstrates how AI-based speech analysis
and game-based interaction can be combined to support scal-
able and engaging speech therapy applications for children.
