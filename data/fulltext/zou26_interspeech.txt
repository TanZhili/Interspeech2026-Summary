Less is More: Boosting Bimodal Music Emotion Recognition with Adaptive
Audio Sequence Compression
Dinghao Zou ID 1,∗∗
Wuhan University of Science and Technology, China
zoudh@wust.edu.cn
Abstract
Fusing audio and symbolic MIDI modalities delivers compre-
hensive affective cues for music emotion classification, yet au-
dio features suffer from severe temporal redundancy compared
with compact MIDI representations, leading to prominent in-
formation density imbalance that may hinder effective bimodal
fusion. To address this, we exploit music’s inherent tempo-
ral dynamics—preserving salient features in high-variation re-
gions and averaging low-variation regions—we propose Pool-
ingVQ. It leverages Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder
(VQ-V AE) to quantize local audio features into index sequences
indicating local variation intensity, which guides dynamic pool-
ing (e.g., max pooling for high-variation segments) to filter re-
dundancy while retaining emotional dynamics. Experiments on
EMOPIA and VGMIDI show that audio sequence compression
via PoolingVQ facilitates effective bimodal fusion, achieving
state-of-the-art performance, thus offering a practical scheme
for audio multimodal fusion.
Index Terms: music emotion classification, multimodal fusion,
audio sequence compression, VQ-V AE, adaptive pooling
1. Introduction
Music Emotion Recognition (MER) is a core task in music in-
formation retrieval that aims to infer affective states, typically
characterized by arousal and valence in the widely adopted Rus-
sell’s circumplex model [1]. Since music conveys emotion
through heterogeneous cues, recent research increasingly fo-
cuses on bimodal fusion. Specifically, audio signals provide im-
mediate perceptual information—such as timbre, loudness, and
spectral shape—whereas symbolic representations like MIDI
encode the structural ”grammar” of a composition, including
melody, rhythm, and harmony [2, 3].
Early MER systems predominantly relied on single-
modality approaches. For audio, hand-crafted features such
as MFCCs and spectral centroids were fed into SVMs or ran-
dom forests [4], while for MIDI, researchers modeled melodic
contours and chord progressions [5, 6]. More recently, deep
learning has advanced the field, with models like MusicBERT
[7] (symbolic) and MERT [8] (acoustic) providing robust rep-
resentations. However, single-modality systems face inherent
limitations: audio lacks explicit structural parsing, while MIDI
omits fine-grained timbral nuances. Consequently, fusing audio
and symbolic representations has become a dominant strategy to
exploit complementary cues [9]. Typical pipelines either con-
catenate CNN-based audio features with LSTM-encoded MIDI
sequences [10] or employ attention mechanisms [11, 12].

Despite these advancements, we identify a critical chal-
lenge in bimodal fusion: severe temporal redundancy (sparse
musical information density) in the audio modality [13]. A
60-second excerpt yields millions of samples, resulting in an
overly long token sequence even after feature extraction, which
dilutes musical information density. In contrast, MIDI bene-
fits from compact compound-word encoding [14]; for the same
musical segment, the resulting token sequence is both concise
and information-rich. As illustrated in Figure 1, there is a stark
disparity in the feature sequence lengths extracted by the pre-
trained models used in this work. The redundancy introduced
by the audio modality not only enlarges the fused representation
and causes feature dilution, but also hampers fusion efficiency,
ultimately limiting classification performance.

To mitigate this, conventional remedies such as global av-
erage or max pooling [15] are often applied but remain sub-
optimal. Both methods enforce indiscriminate aggregation
across temporal signals, ignoring the intrinsic difference be-
tween emotion-critical transient regions and redundant station-
ary passages. Alternatively, in the era of large language mod-
els, training a low-frame-rate tokenizer [16, 17] can dramati-
cally increase information density, but this approach demands
prohibitive computational resources for re-training. This raises
two critical questions: How can we effectively compress pre-
trained audio feature sequences to enhance information density
without re-training the backbone models? And does such com-
pression significantly improve bimodal fusion performance for
emotion classification?
To address these questions, we propose PoolingVQ, a
codebook-guided local aggregation module inspired by VQ-
V AE quantization [18]. It employs a dynamics-adaptive tem-
poral pooling scheme [19, 20] that preserves salient transients
via max pooling while summarizing stationary passages via av-
erage pooling. This enables us to compress token sequences
to achieve an effect comparable to a 25Hz low-frame-rate tok-
enizer, with the entire process built directly upon off-the-shelf
pre-trained models. Our framework integrates PoolingVQ into
a “pre-training + compression + fusion” pipeline.
First, frozen pre-trained backbones (MERT-95M for audio,
MIDIBERT [21] for MIDI) extract features from long-form in-

Second, PoolingVQ adaptively compresses the audio se-

segments activate max pooling to capture transients. This adap-
tive scheme reduces sequence length by approximately two-
thirds, significantly boosting information density.
Finally, the compressed audio and MIDI sequences are
fused for emotion classification. We evaluate PoolingVQ across
two fusion paradigms—a sophisticated cross-attention network
and a simple concatenation baseline—to robustly verify its ef-
fectiveness in enhancing downstream performance.
In summary, our contributions are: (i) Proposing Pool-
ingVQ for adaptive mitigation of audio temporal redundancy
via codebook-guided dynamic pooling; (ii) Validating that au-
dio redundancy reduction enhances bimodal performance, thus
achieving state-of-the-art music emotion classification on the
EMOPIA and VGMIDI datasets; (iii) Releasing the implemen-
tation code of our method at an anonymous GitHub repository1.
2. Method
As shown in Figure 2, our model comprises three modules: pre-
trained extractor, Audio feature Compression, and multi-modal
fusion.
2.1. Pre-trained extractor
This module employs frozen pre-trained models MERT-95M
and MIDI-BERT to extract feature representations for audio and
MIDI modalities, respectively. To prevent excessive truncation
of music information segments during batch learning, maxi-
mum music durations are established. Based on the lengths of
the two datasets, these are set to 60 seconds (EMOPIA) and 130
seconds (VGMIDI), respectively. In the audio branch, input au-
dio is resampled to 24000Hz according to MERT-95M’s spec-
ifications, while the MIDI branch retains MIDI-BERT’s pre-
trained parameters unchanged.
2.2. Audio feature Compression module

extracted from the frozen pre-trained model MERT-95M. In

2.3. Multi-modal fusion Module
In this module, we designed and implemented two fusion strate-
gies with different levels of complexity to systematically verify
the impact mechanism of audio feature compression on bimodal
fusion performance. By comparing the performance differ-
ences between simple concatenation fusion and complex cross-
attention fusion, we deeply analyze the role of feature compres-
sion in multimodal interactions.
(1) Simple Concatenation Fusion: Due to the significant
temporal dimension differences between audio and MIDI fea-
ture sequences extracted by pre-trained models, direct feature
concatenation faces the challenge of dimension mismatch. To
effectively solve this temporal alignment problem, we adopted
a temporal alignment method suitable for this scenario [26]. We
upsampled the shorter MIDI feature sequence to the same tem-
poral resolution as the audio features through linear interpola-
tion, ensuring consistency in the temporal dimension between
the two modalities.

methods have stronger modal interaction capabilities compared
to simple concatenation fusion, enabling deep-level feature se-
lection and weighted fusion. In this paper, based on litera-

module for bimodal processing. Stage 1: Audio→MIDI Cross-

Datasets.We evaluate on EMOPIA and VGMIDI. EMOPIA is
a piano-only dataset comprising 1,087 clips (≈13 h) recorded
in clean conditions; the single-instrument setting and short av-
erage duration reduce acoustic noise and ease optimization, but
can also encourage overfitting. VGMIDI is a multitrack sym-
bolic corpus of video-game music spanning diverse styles and
tempi; in our experiments we use a labeled subset of 200 pieces
(≈5 h). Compared with EMOPIA, the higher track count and
longer average duration make VGMIDI more challenging for
modeling and fusion. For consistency, we map the valence-
arousal (V A) annotations of VGMIDI to the same four-quadrant
(4Q) categories used in EMOPIA, as detailed in Table 1. And
we follow the official splits: EMOPIA 70/20/10 and VGMIDI
60/20/20 (train/validation/test).
Training Details.We initialize the codebook via k-means
on features randomly sampled from 50% of the training data.
Training is optimized by Adam (β 1=0.9,β 2=0.99,ϵ=10 −6)
with grouped hyperparameters: non-codebook layers adopt a
learning rate of10 −4 and weight decay of5×10 −5, while code-
book parameters use a learning rate of5×10 −5 and weight de-
cay of10 −5. Such separate learning schedules stabilize code-
book training by mitigating centroid drift while enabling faster
adaptation of other layers. We adopt early stopping if validation
performance plateaus for five consecutive epochs.
3.2. Results analysis
Table 2 summarizes accuracy and macro-F1 on EMOPIA and
VGMIDI, covering representative methods across three stages:
early single-modal neural networks, symbolic pretraining, and
modern multimodal fusion. A clear upward trajectory is evi-

We conduct ablation studies to investigate the impact of au-
dio sequence compression on bimodal fusion, focusing on three
key aspects: the effectiveness of PoolingVQ, Comparison of
Pooling Strategies, and the influence of codebook size.
Effectiveness of PoolingVQ.Figure 3(a) illustrates the impact
of PoolingVQ on fusion performance. On the EMOPIA dataset,
simple concatenation benefits from PoolingVQ (Acc≈0.88),
suggesting that the task relies on robust global cues (e.g., tempo,
dynamics) that are well-preserved after compression, effec-
tively filtering noise. In contrast, on the more challenging VG-
MIDI dataset (Acc≈0.50), simple fusion struggles with com-
pressed features, likely because fine-grained discriminative de-
tails are smoothed out. However, when combined with cross-
attention fusion, PoolingVQ consistently improves performance
on both datasets. This indicates that the cross-modal attention
mechanism can re-contextualize the compressed audio features
using symbolic MIDI cues, recovering lost details while bene-
fiting from reduced redundancy.
Comparison of Pooling Strategies.Figure 3(b) compares
our adaptive PoolingVQ with standard global average and max
pooling. As introduced, global pooling methods often discard
critical temporal variations. In contrast, our variance-aware
approach, which adaptively selects between max and average
pooling based on feature stability, achieves superior perfor-
mance. This confirms that preserving transient information in
high-variance regions while summarizing stable regions is cru-
cial for maintaining the expressiveness of audio representations.
Impact of Codebook Size.Figure 3(c) explores the effect of
codebook sizeP, which determines the granularity of the la-
tent audio feature space. A largerPprovides a finer partition,
making the model more sensitive to subtle temporal variations.
However, excessively large codebooks may introduce noise or
lead to overfitting. Results on both datasets suggest an optimal
range forPwhere the model balances the granularity of feature
representation with the robustness of temporal aggregation, ef-
fectively capturing significant temporal dynamics without being
overwhelmed by minor fluctuations.

4. Conclusion
This study focuses on the temporal redundancy of audio se-
quences and explores sequence compression to improve the
bimodal fusion performance for music emotion classification.
We propose a novel audio sequence compression framework
termed PoolingVQ, which adopts VQ-V AE quantization to cap-
ture temporal variations and employs adaptive pooling strate-
gies according to local feature dynamics. Experimental results
on the EMOPIA and VGMIDI datasets demonstrate that the
proposed method enhances audio feature information density
and achieves robust bimodal fusion. As the current pooling de-
cision strategy is rule-based, future work will develop learnable
networks to dynamically optimize pooling schemes for better
preservation of salient audio features during compression.
