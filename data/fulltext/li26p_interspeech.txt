4. Discussion
Thestudyexaminedtheeffectsofdeicticandiconicgestureson
focusprosodyinCantonese-speakingchildrenwithandwithout
ASD. The two groups exhibited distinct gesture-prosody pat-
terns. Fordeicticgestures,gestureeffectsintheTDgroupwere
limitedandlocalizedtopost-focusduration,withnoreliableon-
targeteffects,consistentwithpriorfindingsthatmanualdeictic
gesturesdonotaffecttheacousticorarticulatorymarkingoffo-
cused words [2]. However, the ASD group produced shorter
syllablesatbothon-andpost-targetpositionsinthepresenceof
a deictic gesture. This pattern may reflect reduced multimodal
integrationorincreasedmotor-cognitiveload,whichalignswith
Weak Central Coherence theory [15] and Enhanced Perceptual
FunctioningModelofASD[ 16]. Whencognitiveandmotorre-

sourcesarelimited,autisticchildrenmayallocateefforttogestu-
ralprominencewhileinvestinglessattheprosodiclevel,thereby
formingatrade-offbetweengestureandprosody.
With regard to iconic gestures, we found an overall boost-
ing effect at the on-target position: syllable duration increased
when an iconic gesture was present. This finding is consistent
with the cumulative-cue hypothesis [20], which posits a pos-
itive correlation between acoustic and kinematic correlates of
prominence,andourfindingsuggeststhatthiscorrelationispri-
marily realized with iconic gestures. Previous studies have in-
dicatedthatgesturescanenhanceprosodicprominence[ 5,21],
and the present study specifies that this effect holds for iconic
gesturesamongCantonese-speakingchildreninparticular. Asa
plausiblebiomechanicalaccount,iconicgesturestypicallyhave
larger movement amplitudes than deictic gestures and involve
twohandsratherthanone,whichgeneratesstrongerupper-limb
impulses; these impulses couple with respiration and affect the
vocal drive, thereby increasing the acoustic prominence [22].
FortheTDgroup,suchaboostingeffectalsoextendedtomean
f0 onpost-targetsyllables. Theyshowedhighermean f0 when
theiconicgesturewaspresent. Onepossibleexplanationisthat
when the gesture still remains active, such as during retraction
oraslightovershoot,anelevatedrespiratorydriveissustained,
leadingtoariseinpost-focus f0.
Notably, the TD group showed duration shortening at the
post-focus position with both deictic and iconic gestures. Prior
work has demonstrated that Cantonese speakers use post-focus
durationcompressiontoenhancetheprominenceoftheon-focus
syllable[23]. Inthepresentstudy,TDchildrenadoptedasimilar
durationcontraststrategywhendoinggestures. Bycompressing
thetemporalextentofthepost-focussyllable,theystrengthened
thedurationalcontrastbetweentheon-andpost-focuspositions,
therebyreinforcingtheprosodicprominenceoftheon-focussyl-
lable.
5. Conclusion
The current study investigated how co-speech gesture shapes
theprosodicrealizationoffocusinCantonese-speakingchildren
withandwithoutASD.Resultsshowedthatgesturesmodulated
prosodiccharacteristicsinbothgroups. Inaddition,distinctpat-
terns observed between the ASD and TD groups suggest diffi-
cultieswithmultimodalintegrationinautisticchildren.
