Abstract
Speech tokenizers serve as the critical interface for integrating
continuous audio into large language models. However, most
existing speech tokenizers require massive pretrained models
or complex multi-stage training pipelines. To address this, we
propose ZipCodec, a single-stage-trained, pretrained-model-
free tokenizer that bypasses the need for heavy encoder ar-
chitectures. By offloading acoustic reconstruction to a pow-
erful Zipformer-based flow-matching decoder, ZipCodec en-
ables an ultra-lightweight encoder of just 4.4M parameters.
Furthermore, we introduce an Encoder Consistency Regular-
ization (ECR) loss to enhance the encoder’s semantic capac-
ity in a purely self-supervised manner, without relying on ex-
ternal teachers or labels. Experimental results demonstrate
that ZipCodec achieves competitive acoustic and semantic per-
formance, outperforming models with encoders more than 20
times larger and delivering improved zero-shot TTS generation.

1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has
established discrete speech tokens as a foundational paradigm
for unifying speech understanding and generation [1]. By
converting continuous audio waveforms into compact discrete
units, speech tokenizers serve as the critical interface that en-
ables speech to be modeled using the same text-based models
that have revolutionized natural language processing.
However, early speech codecs such as EnCodec [2] and
SoundStream [3] were primarily designed for signal compres-
sion and high-fidelity transmission. Because these models pri-
oritize acoustic-level reconstruction, their tokens often lack
the semantic richness required for effective language model-
ing. To better serve the downstream tasks, recent advancements
have focused on introducing semantic information into dis-
crete representations, essentially rebalancing the semantic and
acoustic modeling within the discrete latent space. Currently,
methodologies achieving this can be broadly categorized into
two paradigms. The first isSemantic Distillation/Guidance,
which forces the encoder to learn semantic representations by
distilling knowledge from pretrained models [4, 5] or intro-
ducing semantic-related tasks (e.g., supervised ASR training)
[6, 7, 8]. The second is the use ofSemantic Side-Models
[9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14], which directly incorporates pretrained
models (e.g., HuBERT [15] or Whisper [16]) as additional se-
mantic encoders.

While these approaches successfully narrow the “seman-
tic gap”, they inevitably introduce significant drawbacks: re-
liance on extra pretrained models, demands for labeled train-
ing data, or increasingly complex multi-stage training pipelines.
Meanwhile, the overall parameter count of these tokenizers has
surged, particularly on the encoder side, which is burdened by
massive pretrained backbones. This trend prompts a fundamen-
tal question:Are such convoluted, multi-stage pipelines and
increasingly massive encoder architectures truly indispensable
for driving semantic-rich speech tokens?
Meanwhile, recent advances in flow-matching (FM) [17]
and Zipformer [18] provide a promising alternative for speech
tokenizer design. FM learns a vector field that transports noise
to the target speech distribution [19, 20, 21], making it well
suited to recovering speech from highly compressed represen-
tations under quantization-induced uncertainty. In parallel, Zip-
former, a U-Net-style architecture with multi-scale temporal
modeling, has been widely validated as a strong and efficient
speech encoder. Using Zipformer as the FM decoder backbone
further enhances acoustic modeling while preserving efficiency.
Together, they motivate a powerful-decoder design that substan-
tially simplifies the tokenization pipeline, enabling an extremely
lightweight encoder to produce high-quality and semantically
informative tokens.
Building upon these insights, we proposeZipCodec 1, a
fast, single-stage, and pretrained-model-free speech tokenizer.
To enhance semantic capacity without external pretrained mod-
els or supervised labels, we further introduce Encoder Consis-
tency Regularization (ECR), a purely self-supervised loss that
enforces representation consistency across augmented views of
raw audio, encouraging robust semantic extraction while pre-
serving overall simplicity.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We proposeZipCodec, demonstrating that semantic-rich and
high-quality speech tokenizer can be achieved by an ultra-
lightweight encoder (only 4.4M parameters) and a simple
single-stage training pipeline through flow-matching based
autoencoder. This drastically simplified architecture main-
tains superior generative performance while potentially ac-
celerating inference for downstream understanding tasks.
• We introduce the ECR loss, an effective self-supervised train-
ing mechanism that substantially enhances the semantic ex-
traction capability of the encoder without any external pre-
trained teachers or labeled data.
• We conduct comprehensive comparative evaluations on
downstream LLM-based zero-shot TTS tasks, proving that
tokens extracted by our lightweight ZipCodec directly trans-

2. ZipCodec
2.1. Overview
ZipCodec is built upon the flow-matching-based diffusion au-
toencoder architecture, with a quantization bottleneck. It con-
sists of a speech encoder, a Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)
[22] module, an FM decoder, and down-/up-sampling modules.
The encoder and FM decoder both adopt the Zipformer back-
bone. Figure 1a gives an overview of the ZipCodec model.
We choose mel-spectrogram as the input/output domain of
the model instead of the short-time Fourier transform (STFT)
or the waveform, since the mel-spectrogram removes phase re-
dundancy and compresses irrelevant spectral details, thereby
significantly reducing the modeling difficulty. The V ocos [23]
vocoder is used to reconstruct the waveform from the mel-
spectrogram.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets
We trained both ZipCodec and ZipCodec-distill on the follow-
ing datasets. a) Emilia [27]: an in-the-wild multilingual speech
dataset containing over 101k hours of recordings. For our ex-
periments, we use the Chinese and English subsets, which to-
gether comprise approximately 96.7k hours of speech. b) Lib-
riTTS [28]: a clean, read English speech corpus with about 585
hours of audio. This dataset is used for model development.
The model is evaluated on Librispeech PC [29] test clean
set and the SeedTTS en/zh testsets [30], we only use the ground
truth audios to evaluate the reconstruction ability of speech to-
kenizers, and use both the prompt audio and ground truth audio
to evaluate the down-stream TTS task.
3.2. Model Configuration
ZipCodec model operates on the mel-spectrogram of 24kHz
speech. We follow the mel-spectrogram setting in V ocos [23]
vocoder and the window length and the overlap are 1024 and
256. The CNN downsampling module contains two convolution
layers with strides= 2and kernel sizek= 2, each followed
by a GELU activation [31]. For the encoder, the Zipformer
backbone uses 4 layers with a feature dimensiond enc = 192
and a feedforward dimension of 512. For the flow-matching
decoder, the embedding and feedforward dimensions are set to
512 and 1536. The FSQ module serves as the quantization bot-
tleneck, which has 11 groups and each group has 3 channels
with quantization levels of[8,5,5]. The frame rate and the
bit rate at the bottleneck are 23.4375 Hz and∼1.97 kbps re-
spectively. The decoder operates with downsampling rates of
[1×,2×,4×,2×,1×]and layer numbers[2,2,4,4,4]for the
five stacks.
For the TTS model used for evaluating the down-stream
task performance, we build a discrete token-based non-
autoregressive (NAR) zero-shot TTS model[32, 33].
3.3. Training and Inference Details
ZipCodec is trained using the ScaledAdam [18] optimizer. On
the Emilia dataset, the model is trained for 400k iterations (ap-
proximately 2.5 epochs). The final model is obtained by averag-
ing 11 checkpoints from iterations 350k to 400k with an interval
of 5k steps. ZipCodec-distill is initialized from the pretrained
ZipCodec model and further trained for 60k steps, where 11
checkpoints between 50k and 60k steps are averaged to produce
the final model. On the LibriTTS dataset, ZipCodec is trained
for 30 epochs, and the final model is obtained by averaging the
checkpoints from epochs 26 to 30. All models were trained us-
ing NVIDIA H20 GPUs. We sampleβto match a target SNR
uniformly chosen from 10,20,30 dB.λis set to 0.25 and will be
discussed in Section 3.6.
During inference, we use the Euler solver for flow-
matching. For ZipCodec, the NFE is set to 10 withγ= 0.5.
We further apply a logit-normal sampling time schedule [34]
withη= 0.5. For ZipCodec-distill, the NFE is set to 3 with
other parameters the same.
3.4. Metrics
For speech tokenizer evaluation, we adopt four objective met-
rics to measure the quality of reconstructed speech, including
word error rate (WER), speaker similarity (SIM-o), perceptual
evaluation of speech quality (PESQ) [35], and ViSQOL [36].
All the metrics are computed between the ground truth speech
and the reconstructed speech. For WER, we use Hubert [15]-
based ASR model, Whisper-large-v3 [16] and Paraformer-zh
[37] respectively for Librispeech PC test-clean, SeedTTS en
and zh. For SIM-o, we extract speaker embeddings using a
WavLM-based [38] ECAPA-TDNN [39] model and compute
the cosine similarity between the reconstructed speech and the
original speech.
For downstream TTS evaluation, we report WER, SIM-o,
and UTMOS [40]. The evaluation models for WER and SIM-o
are the same as speech tokenizer evaluation. SIM-o is measured
between the prompt speech and generated speech.

3.6. Ablation: Influence of the ECR loss
We verify the impact of the ECR loss proposed in Section 2.2 by
gradually increasing the weightλin Equation 12. Models are
trained on LibriTTS corpus. The results presented in Table 2
demonstrate that ECR loss significantly enhances the semantic
modeling capabilities of the encoder. In particular, atλ= 0.25,
the model achieves a relative reduction of 5.4% in WER com-
pared with the baseline without ECR (λ= 0.00).
In addition, ECR also slightly benefits acoustic recon-
struction, which yields improvements in the metrics SIM-o,
PESQ, and ViSQOL. Crucially, ECR operates in a purely self-
supervised manner. It effectively enhances the semantic extrac-
tion ability without relying on external pretrained speech mod-
els or labeled data, thereby strictly maintaining the simplicity
and parameter efficiency of the ZipCodec architecture.

We report the results of the evaluation on the Librispeech PC
test clean. For fair comparison, all models share the same NAR
TTS backbone and are trained under identical configurations,
solely swapping the speech codec.
The results, presented in Table 3, demonstrate the substan-
tial superiority of ZipCodec in downstream speech generation.
Specifically, ZipCodec achieves a significantly lower WER of
2.16%, highlighting its strong ability to preserve semantic in-
formation for high intelligibility. Furthermore, ZipCodec excels
in acoustic fidelity and speaker similarity, delivering the highest
SIM-o and the best overall UTMOS. This indicates that despite
its lightweight and pretrained-model-free design, ZipCodec ex-
tracts semantic-rich tokens that directly facilitate high-quality
zero-shot speech generation.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we presented ZipCodec, a simple, pretrained-
model-free speech tokenizer that delivers high-quality and
semantic-rich tokenization. By introducing a Zipformer-based
flow-matching autoencoder into the tokenization paradigm,
ZipCodec achieves strong semantic and acoustic performance
with an ultra-lightweight 4.4M-parameter encoder and a dras-
tically simplified training pipeline. Furthermore, the proposed
self-supervised ECR loss effectively captures robust semantic
representations without relying on external teachers or labeled
data. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that ZipCodec outper-
forms models with encoders larger than 20 times, both in recon-
struction and in downstream zero-shot TTS tasks.
