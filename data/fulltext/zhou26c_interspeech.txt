Abstract
Evaluating Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) is chal-
lenging due to the diverse speech and audio tasks. Existing
benchmarks often lack a unified framework, focusing either on
understanding or generation. We introduce UG-Bench, a com-
prehensive benchmark systematically assessing LALMs across
four competencies: speech perception, audio perception, speech
generation, and spoken language understanding. Its decou-
pled design enables standardized comparisons and supports new
tasks. Evaluating 11 LALMs and 5 specialized speech genera-
tion models, we find significant gaps in instruction following
and generation quality, underscoring the need for better seman-
tic understanding. UG-Bench offers a unified, scalable evalua-
tion framework to advance LALM research.
Index Terms: speech and audio understanding, speech genera-
tion, large audio-language models, benchmark evaluation
1. Introduction
The rapid development of large language models (LLMs) has
significantly impacted the field of artificial intelligence, en-
abling breakthroughs in natural language processing, computer
vision, and other related areas. Recently, there has been a grow-
ing interest in extending the capabilities of LLMs to encom-
pass the audio domain, particularly speech. This has led to the
emergence of large audio-language models (LALMs) that can
process and understand spoken language, opening up new pos-
sibilities for human-computer interaction and multimodal appli-
cations.
However, evaluating LALMs remains challenging due to
the complexity and diversity of speech and audio tasks. Several
benchmarks have been established to assess the performance of
speech models on specific tasks [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]. These
benchmarks are typically static, providing predefined datasets
and task configurations for evaluation. For example, some
benchmarks focus on tasks like automatic speech recognition,
and speech emotion recognition, offering task-specific datasets
and metrics to measure performance. While these benchmarks
are effective for evaluating specialized capabilities, they often
lack flexibility in accommodating new tasks or evolving re-
quirements. Existing benchmarks often focus on specific as-
pects of speech processing, and may not provide a comprehen-
sive assessment of the overall capabilities of LALMs. More-
over, many current benchmarks primarily focus on evaluating
speech and audio understanding, neglecting the critical aspect
of speech generation. This can be attributed to the fact that a

Recently, some efforts have been made to address these lim-
itations. Dynamic-SUPERB [9], to the best of our knowledge, is
the first benchmark designed for building universal speech mod-
els capable of leveraging instruction tuning to perform multiple
tasks in a zero-shot fashion. AIR-Bench [10] stands out as the
first generative evaluation benchmark for large audio-language
models. It includes a diverse set of tasks covering speech, nat-
ural audio, and music, making it a comprehensive evaluation
tool. Another significant benchmark is SD-Eval [11], which ad-
dresses spoken dialogue understanding and generation by incor-
porating paralinguistic and environmental information. While
these benchmarks represent important advancements, a need re-
mains for a more unified and comprehensive evaluation frame-
work encompassing both understanding and generation capabil-
ities across diverse speech and audio tasks.
To address these limitations, we introduceUG-Bench, a
comprehensive and unified evaluation framework for LALMs.
UG-Bench is designed to assess model performance across
four key competencies:speech perception, audio percep-
tion, speech generation, and spoken language understand-
ing. Each competency is further divided into specific tasks, en-
abling a granular and multidimensional analysis of a model’s
abilities. As far as we know, UG-Bench is the first benchmark
that simultaneously evaluates both the understanding and gen-
eration capabilities of LALMs.
Our framework offers several advantages over existing
benchmarks:
1.Comprehensive Coverage: UG-Bench covers a wide range
of speech and audio tasks, including speech recognition,
speech translation, emotion recognition, music information
retrieval, and speech synthesis. Unlike many existing bench-
marks that primarily focus on speech understanding, UG-
Bench also evaluates the speech generation capabilities of
LALMs, providing a more complete picture of their perfor-
mance.
2.Unified Evaluation: We have developed a unified interface
for evaluating various LALMs, simplifying the process of
comparing different models and promoting reproducibility.
3.Extensive Datasets: We have curated a large and diverse col-
lection of datasets for evaluating LALMs, with a volume sig-
nificantly larger than that used in previous benchmarks. This
ensures a more robust and reliable evaluation. For example,
our evaluation encompasses a total of 19 tasks and utilizes 36
datasets. The evaluation results are derived from processing
153,485 test samples.

2. Evaluation Framework Design
To systematically evaluate LALMs across diverse speech and
audio tasks, we design a decoupled evaluation framework that
separates model implementation from task-specific evaluation
and provide a detailed ranking of the evaluated models.
2.1. Framework Overview
The UG-Bench evaluation framework is structured around four
core components, designed for modularity and consistency:
•Input Standardization Module:This module standardizes
the input format across different models. To ensure fairness,
a unified prompting strategy is employed. Given the consid-
erable variability in supported functionalities and prompting
mechanisms across existing LALMs, we adopt the following
approach: if a model requires a specific prompt format, its
designated template is used; otherwise, a default prompt en-
sures consistency. This strategy ensures that models are eval-
uated equitably, accommodating their unique characteristics
while mitigating inconsistencies arising from model-specific
prompt variations.
•Model Interface Module:This module encompasses a
range of large-scale speech and audio-language models. It is
responsible for processing input data (speech, text, or audio
signals) and generating intermediate results.
•Output Unification Module:This module standardizes the
output formats across different models, ensuring compatibil-
ity with downstream tasks. By enforcing a unified input-
output format, model selection is effectively decoupled from
task evaluation, facilitating the seamless integration of di-
verse models.
•Task Evaluation Module:This module is responsible for
evaluating model performance across a variety of speech and
audio tasks. Because the Intermediate Module provides a
consistent output format, tasks can be defined and modified
independently, without necessitating alterations to the up-
stream model components.
This modular design offers enhanced flexibility for incorpo-
rating new models and tasks. It simplifies benchmark expansion
by eliminating dependencies between model implementations
and task evaluations.
2.2. Tasks and Datasets
To comprehensively evaluate the performance of LALMs, UG-
Bench encompasses four key competencies: speech perception,
audio perception, speech generation, and spoken language un-
derstanding. Each competency is further decomposed into a set
of specific tasks, allowing for a fine-grained and multidimen-
sional analysis of a model’s capabilities. A diverse collection of
datasets, covering a broad spectrum of speech and audio tasks,
was curated to ensure a thorough evaluation. These datasets are
categorized under the four aforementioned competencies.

2.3. Ranking Methodology
Due to diverse evaluation metrics across tasks, a single measure
is insufficient for ranking. We use a weighted ranking strategy
based on relative performance within each task. The ranking
process is as follows:
1.Sub-rank calculation:A sub-rank is computed for each
competency by averaging the ranks obtained by a model
across all tasks within that competency.
2.Weighted score calculation:A final score for each model
(M) is calculated as a weighted average of its sub-ranks. The
weights are heuristically assigned based on factors such as
the number of tasks and the size of the datasets associated
with each competency: speech perception (0.4), audio per-
ception (0.3), speech generation (0.2), and spoken language
understanding (0.1). The formula is:

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Models
Eleven open-source LALMs were evaluated: Qwen-Audio [27],
Qwen2-Audio [28], BLSP [29], Speech-GPT [30], Salmoon
[31], AnyGPT [32], LTU [33], SenseV oice [34], WavLLM
[35], Audio-Flamingo [36], and PandaGPT [37]. Because
most open-source LALMs currently lack speech generation
capabilities, five state-of-the-art, open-source or faithfully re-
produced, specialized speech generation models were also in-
cluded: MaskGCT [38], F5TTS [39], CosyV oice [40], Bark 1,
and ChatTTS2. Models were evaluated using their official im-
plementations and checkpoints without fine-tuning. All experi-
ments were conducted on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU.
3.2. Evaluation Metrics
A range of task-specific metrics were employed to assess model
performance. For Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Word
Error Rate (WER) was used for English, and Character Er-
ror Rate (CER) for Chinese. For Speech Emotion Recogni-
tion (SER), V ocal Sound Classification (VSC), Music Instru-
ment Classification (MIC), Music Genre Classification (MGC),
and Intent Classification (IC), Accuracy (Acc) was used. For
Speech-to-Text Translation (S2TT), BLEU was used. For Au-
dio Captioning (AC) and Music Captioning (MC), the average
of FENSE and SPIDEr were used. For Text-to-Speech (TTS),
WER and Mean Opinion Score (MOS) were used. Ten native
English speakers were recruited to provide MOS annotations
for each synthesized speech sample.

models, hallucination also manifests in LALMs. For exam-
ple, in ASR tasks, models often generated repetitive charac-
ters or irrelevant text at the end of sentences, in addition to
the actual text content from the speech.
•Overfitting concerns:Current LALMs showed signs of
overfitting. For instance, in ASR tasks, some models per-
formed well on mainstream datasets like Librispeech but ex-
perienced a significant performance drop on other datasets.

task’s complexity and the early development stage of rele-
vant technologies. To comprehensively evaluate LALMs in
this area, we included expert speech synthesis models (F5-
TTS, MaskGCT, Bark, Chat TTS, and CosyV oice). These ex-
pert models, designed specifically for speech synthesis without
any other tasks, represent the state-of-the-art. Figure 1 com-
pares speech generation capabilities by MOS. The figure shows
that LALMs generally achieve lower scores than expert mod-
els. This performance gap highlights the shortcomings of cur-
rent LALMs in speech generation and suggests that special-
ized models remain preferred for high-quality speech genera-
tion. Future work may focus on improving LALMs’ support
and optimization for these tasks, particularly regarding natural-
ness and audio quality, to narrow the gap with expert models.

4.5. Spoken Language Understanding
As shown in Figure 2, the performance of current LALMs on

ineffectiveness. Initial experiments with slot filling proved ex-

need for substantial improvement in semantic understanding ca-

5. Conclusion
This paper introduced UG-Bench, a comprehensive bench-
mark for evaluating LALMs across diverse speech and audio
tasks. UG-Bench’s decoupled design enables flexible integra-
tion of models and tasks. Our evaluation of eleven LALMs and
five specialized speech generation models revealed significant
performance variations, especially in spoken language under-
standing and speech generation. While some LALMs showed
strong perception capabilities, a substantial gap exists between
LALMs and specialized models in speech generation. Many
LALMs also struggle with semantic understanding, a critical
area for future research. UG-Bench is a valuable tool to track
progress and drive LALM advancements.
