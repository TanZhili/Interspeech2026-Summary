Energy Redistribution in the Spectro-Temporal Modulation Domain for
Near-End Listening Enhancement
Amin Edraki1, Amirhossein Hajavi1, Irina Kezele1, Yuanhao Yu1
1 Huawei CBG, Canada
[amin.edraki, amirhossein.hajavi, irina.kezele, yuanhao.yu]@huawei.com
Abstract
Near-end listening enhancement (NELE) improves speech in-
telligibility by modifying speech prior to playback in noisy
environments. We propose a NELE algorithm that redis-
tributes speech energy in the spectro-temporal modulation do-
main rather than the conventional time–frequency domain. A
global modulation mask is optimized using an objective mea-
sure of speech intelligibility and applied under an energy con-
straint. An ablation study shows that jointly modifying spectral
and temporal modulations results in more consistent gains than
single-dimension masking. Evaluation on several noise types
demonstrates consistent improvements over established NELE
baselines across multiple objective and subjective measures of
speech intelligibility.
Index Terms: Near-end Listening Enhancement, Intelligibility,
Spectro-Temporal Modulation
1. Introduction
Near-End Listening Enhancement (NELE) is a speech enhance-
ment approach designed to improve intelligibility during play-
back in noisy environments [1, 2]. It addresses scenarios where
the clean speech signal is available prior to playback, such as in
public announcement systems. NELE algorithms operate under
an energy constraint [3], redistributing speech energy without
increasing total signal energy.
Existing NELE approaches can be broadly divided into
heuristic algorithms and intelligibility-driven optimization
methods. Heuristic approaches modify speech signals us-
ing established perceptual principles [4], for example through
spectral shaping and dynamic range compression [5], whereas
optimization-based approaches modify speech to maximize an
intelligibility metric [6, 7]. Despite these differences, most
NELE methods operate by reallocating a fixed energy budget
across perceptually important time–frequency regions, typically
emphasizing mid- and high-frequency bands.
A large body of research shows that Spectro-Temporal
Modulation (STM) patterns play a central role in speech per-
ception [8, 9, 10, 11]. STM patterns highlight important speech
structures, such as formant transitions [12, 13], that are diffi-
cult to represent and selectively manipulate in the conventional
time–frequency domain. However, most NELE methods oper-
ate in the time–frequency domain and do not explicitly modify
STM patterns.
In this work, we propose an STM-domain NELE frame-
work that redistributes speech energy using a learned mask ap-
plied to an STM representation of speech. We perform an ab-
lation study comparing temporal-only, spectral-only, and joint
modulation masking to quantify the contribution of each mod-
ulation dimension and determine the most effective parame-
terization. Based on this analysis, we derive a modulation-
domain NELE method and evaluate it against established
time–frequency-domain NELE baselines. Results show consis-
tent improvements across objective intelligibility metrics, Au-
tomatic Speech Recognition (ASR)-based recognition perfor-
mance, and subjective intelligibility score.
The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Sec-
tion 2 describes the proposed STM-based NELE framework,
and Section 3 details the STM mask learning procedure. Sec-
tion 4 presents the datasets, evaluation metrics, and baseline
methods. Section 5 reports the ablation study used to select
the mask parameterization. Section 6 compares the proposed
method with baseline NELE algorithms. Finally, Sections 7
and 8 provide discussion and concluding remarks.

3. STM Mask Optimization
The STM maskMis the only trainable component in the pro-
posed framework and determines how speech energy is redis-
tributed across STM frequencies. The objective of the optimiza-
tion is to learn a modulation-domain weighting pattern that re-
allocates a fixed energy budget toward modulation regions that
improve intelligibility under noisy playback conditions. The
mask coefficients are treated as segment-independent free pa-
rameters that are directly optimized from data. The same glob-
ally learned mask is applied to all spectrogram segments.
Mask parameters are estimated using gradient-descent with
a differentiable loss function derived from the Extended Short-
Time Objective Intelligibility index (ESTOI) metric [15, 16]:
l=−ESTOI(x,x NELE +η),(5)
wherexdenotes the clean unprocessed reference signal,x NELE
is the clean signal processed by the proposed framework, and
ηrepresents additive noise. Minimizing this loss corresponds
to maximizing ESTOI-predicted intelligibility of the processed
speechx NELE in the presence of background noiseη.
4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Training Protocol
In all experiments, the STM mask parameters are trained using
5-second speech segments extracted from 4000 utterances from
LibriSpeech train-clean-100 [17]. Each utterance is mixed with
a randomly selected environmental noise signal from AudioSet
[18, 19] at a randomly selected SNR between−15and0dB. A
batch size of 32 utterances is used for a maximum of 50 epochs.
Training is terminated if the validation ESTOI does not improve
for 10 consecutive epochs.
4.2. Objective Evaluation Protocol
For objective evaluation, we use a randomly selected subset of
100 utterances from LibriSpeech dev-clean. Noisy playback
conditions are simulated using three DEMAND noise types
[20] (restaurant, living room, station) and Speech-Shaped Noise
(SSN) generated via linear predictive coding-based spectral en-
velope matching using white noise and 100 samples of the
clean speech dataset. Noise is added to both clean and NELE-
processed signals at SNRs of−10and−5dB.
We report three intelligibility prediction algorithms: ESTOI
[15], weighted Spectro-Temporal Modulation Index (wSTMI)
[21], and Spectro-Temporal modulation Glimpsing Index
(STGI) [22]. The latter two metrics are not used during modula-
tion mask optimization and serve as independent intelligibility
predictors with demonstrated robustness under various acoustic
conditions [21, 22]. In addition, we report Word Error Rate
(WER) [23] obtained from a pretrained Whisper-small ASR
system [24]. WER provides a complementary proxy that re-
flects downstream recognition performance.
4.3. Subjective Evaluation Protocol
To verify the perceptual gains, we conduct a small-scale subjec-
tive closed-set intelligibility test using Mandarin Chinese ma-
trix sentences [25, 26], which have a fixed five-word struc-
ture: Name–Verb–Numeral–Adjective–Noun. A total of 5 nor-
mal hearing native Mandarin listeners participated in the study.
Stimuli were presented diotically via a dedicated external audio
interface and a high-quality headphone in a quiet room.
Each participant completed two lists of 10 sentences per
condition, with trials presented in randomized order. In each
trial, the stimulus could be played once and listeners indi-
cated what they heard by selecting one alternative from each
of the five on-screen word columns. Performance was scored
as sentence-correct and aggregated per condition. Due to the
time-intensive nature of the listening test and the limited num-
ber of participants, the evaluation was restricted to restaurant
noise at -5 dB SNR across all NELE algorithms and is intended
as a preliminary perceptual study.

4.4. Baseline Algorithms
For comparative evaluation (Section 6), we use three NELE
baselines: Spectral Shaping and Dynamic Range Compression
(SSDRC) [5], OptimalSpeech Intelligibility Index (SII) [6], and
iMetricGAN [27]. We use the authors’ implementation for Op-
timalSII and iMetricGAN, while SSDRC is implemented by us
following the published description.
5. Ablation Study: Role of Spectral and
Temporal Modulations
In this ablation study, we evaluate the relative contribution of
spectral and temporal modulation processing within the pro-
posed STM-domain NELE framework. The goal of this study is
to identify how different STM masking parameterizations affect
enhancement performance. Training and objective evaluation
use the procedures described in Sections 4.1 and 4.2.
5.1. Mask Variants
We compare four STM mask parameterizations that differ in
how modulation frequencies are modified:
•Temporal-only mask: masking varies only across tempo-
ral modulation frequencies and is constant across the spectral
modulation axis.
•Spectral-only mask: masking varies only across spectral
modulation frequencies and is constant across temporal mod-
ulation frequencies.
•Separable mask: masking is factorized into independent
temporal and spectral components whose outer product de-
fines the joint mask.
•Joint mask: masking varies jointly across both spectral and
temporal modulation frequencies.

5.2. Results and Analysis
Figure 2 shows performancegainsrelative to the unprocessed
baseline for all mask variants across different noise types. For
brevity, we only report the results for the -5 dB SNR condition.
The results show metric-dependent differences between STM
mask parameterization performance. STGI predicts similar per-
formance across most variants and does not indicate statistically
significant differences except in restaurant noise, where joint
and separable masks outperform the single-dimension masks.
ESTOI and wSTMI show a more consistent trend across
conditions. Both metrics indicate that masks operating across
both spectral and temporal modulation dimensions outperform
temporal-only and spectral-only masks. They also consis-
tently show larger gains for spectral-only masking than for
temporal-only masking. WER results show a different order-
ing: temporal-only masking performs comparably to joint and
separable masks, while spectral-only masking performs signifi-
cantly worse in SSN and restaurant noise conditions.
Across metrics and conditions, mask parameterizations that
allow modification along both modulation dimensions (joint
and separable) show the most consistent improvements over
single-dimension masks. Since the separable mask achieves
performance comparable to the joint mask with substantially
fewer parameters (260 vs. 16K), we select the separable pa-
rameterization for subsequent baseline comparisons. Figure 3
shows the optimized separable STM mask which will be used
for comparison to NELE baselines in Section 6 and is discussed
further in Section 7.

7. Discussion
The learned STM mask in Figure 3 shows reduced gain near
DC in both modulation dimensions, indicating that the opti-
mization shifts energy away from very slow modulation pat-
terns toward higher frequencies. The same trend held across
other mask parameterizations (not shown). Along the spectral
modulation axis, two emphasis regions appear: a peak near 1.5
cyc/kHz, aligning with formant structure, and a second near
6 cyc/kHz, corresponding to finer spectral detail. Along the
temporal axis, the mask trends high-pass, down-weighting very
slow envelope modulations. Since natural speech has a heavily
lowpass MPS, this reflects a reallocation of energy away from
the lowest bands, not a claim that high modulations carry more
energy or matter more.
The ablation study indicates that STM masking is more
effective than restricting masking to a single dimension.
Since the separable mask achieves comparable performance
with far fewer parameters, the results indicate that structured
modulation-domain control is more important than parameter
count.
The subjective evaluation in this work was limited in scale,
including a small number of participants and a single noise type
and SNR condition. Broader perceptual validation remains nec-
essary. Nevertheless, the observed trends are encouraging. No-
tably, the listening test was conducted in Mandarin Chinese, a
tonal language that differs fundamentally from the English ma-
terial used for objective evaluation. Most large-scale NELE
subjective intelligibility tests have focused on non-tonal lan-
guages [2, 3, 28], making it particularly interesting to examine
whether NELE algorithms generalize to tonal speech. The pre-
liminary results suggest that the considered NELE algorithms
extend to Mandarin, although larger and more diverse listening
studies are required to confirm this finding.
8. Conclusion
We presented a NELE method that redistributes speech energy
in the STM domain using a fixed STM mask under an overall
energy constraint. Unlike conventional NELE approaches that
operate in the time–frequency domain, the proposed method
modifies modulation-domain representation. An ablation study
showed that masks controlling both spectral and temporal mod-
ulations provide more consistent gains than single-dimension
masking. Across multiple noise types and SNR conditions, the
proposed approach achieved larger average improvements than
established NELE baselines in several objective intelligibility
metrics and ASR word error rate. A small-scale subjective lis-
tening test also showed perceptual improvements.
