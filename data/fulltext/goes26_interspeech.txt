Abstract
In order to achieve precise pronunciation control, high-quality
TTS systems rely on structured and disentangled speech
representations. We propose PhonemeCV AE, a phoneme-
conditioned variational autoencoder that learns a structured
continuous latent space for phoneme-level representations.
The model introduces class-conditioned Gaussian priors for
each phoneme and employs a contrastive objective to promote
compact intra-class clustering and clear phoneme class dis-
entanglement. The resulting latent space enables controllable
phoneme modifications via interpolation at inference time,
allowing smooth transitions between phonological classes and
semantically meaningful modifications of synthesized speech.
Furthermore, the combination of contrastive regularization
and phoneme-conditioned priors forms a structured latent
topology in the V AE that generalizes across English speech
datasets, supporting consistent phoneme interpolation without
compromising synthesis quality.
Index Terms: Speech Synthesis, Phoneme Disentanglement,
Generative AI, Contrastive Learning, Phoneme Editing
1. Introduction
Neural audio synthesis and text-to-speech (TTS) systems now
generate highly natural-sounding speech [1, 2, 3], but their deep
internal representations remain difficult to interpret and con-
trol [4]. In particular, controlled and interpretable phoneme-
level manipulation remains limited for generating fine-grained
interpolations within existing utterance pairs, such as /s/–/f/,
without full re-synthesis, as needed for mispronunciation de-
tection, dialect compensation, and therapeutic applications.
Several approaches have been proposed to address pho-
netic representation learning. Self-supervised speech models
such as wav2vec [5] have demonstrated that phonetic informa-
tion emerges in the hidden representations of deep neural net-
works [6]. Other approaches explicitly model phonetic structure
using phonetic posteriorgrams (PPGs), which represent proba-
bility distributions over phoneme classes and have been used for
phoneme editing [7]. Additionally, neural phoneme paraphras-
ing methods have been proposed to generate mispronounced
phoneme sequences for data augmentation [8]. However, many
of these approaches rely on discrete phoneme representations,
which limit the ability to perform smooth phonetic transforma-
tions or controlled interpolation between speech sounds.
To address these limitations, we proposePhonemeCVAE,
a phoneme-conditioned variational autoencoder that learns a
structured and continuous latent space for phoneme represen-
tations. Our model is based on aβ-V AE [9, 10] with class-
conditioned Gaussian priors, allowing each phoneme class to
be represented as a distribution in latent space. To further en-
courage phoneme disentanglement, we combine this formula-
tion with supervised contrastive learning [11], which promotes
compact intra-class clustering and separation between phoneme
classes. This structured latent representation enables controlled
phoneme manipulation through interpolation between class pri-
ors. Unlike GANs, autoregressive models, or diffusion-based
approaches, which often lack an explicitly regularized and in-
terpretable latent space, our approach aligns latent geometry
with phoneme classes. In this way, the latent space provides
a potential control interface between phoneme-level representa-
tions and downstream synthesis, supporting smooth phonolog-
ical transitions and targeted edits in synthesized speech. Our
contributions in this study are as follows:
• We introducePhonemeCVAE, aβ-V AE with class-
conditioned Gaussian priors that models phoneme classes as
structured distributions in a continuous latent space.
• We combine class-conditioned priors with supervised con-
trastive learning to encourage phoneme disentanglement and
improve latent space structure.
• We demonstrate that the resulting latent space enables con-
trollable phoneme editing via interpolation while maintaining
high perceptual speech quality.

2.2. Disentanglement in Variational Autoencoders

xi over the compressed latent codes stored inz i [14]. Given

2.3. Contrastive Learning
In contrastive learning (CL), the goal is to find a similarity met-
ric for the input data, in our casex i, that corresponds to the
distances in the latent space [16, 17]. The latent space is struc-
tured to minimize intra-class distance while maximizing inter-
class separation. The combination of contrastive learning with
vector-quantized autoencoders for voice conversion at phoneme
level has been explored previously [18]. In that setting, the gen-
erated latent space is of discrete nature. In contrast, our ap-
proach requires a continuous latent space since we aim to grad-
ually resample phonemes that show fine transitions between the
source and target phoneme classes. We adopt the contrastive
formulation inspired by Prototypical Networks [11] and proxy-
based loss [19]. Given that we know the phoneme classes and
duration of all speech sounds, class prototypes can be formed

2.4. Dataset
We trained our models on the open-source LIBRISPEECHcor-
pus [20], comprising a large collection of English audiobooks
recorded by multiple speakers. The dataset consists of≈1000h
of audio recordings. Due to its extensive amount of data and
quality of recordings, LIBRISPEECHhas become a widely used
benchmark in the audio processing field. For our study, we used
a smaller subsetTRAIN-CLEAN-100, which consists of a100h
subset of the original corpus. To normalize the data, we applied
globalz-score normalization.
For testing our model, we further selected two publicly avail-
able English datasets, LJSPEECH[21] and CMU ARTIC
SLT/BLD [22], both of which are commonly used in speech
synthesis research. For the CMU ARTICdataset, we utilized the
SLT subcategory with a female and BLD with a male speaker.
The audios are first resampled to16kHz. Then, given that
all datasets contain text transcripts, we employed the Montreal
Forced Aligner [23] to generate phonetic alignments. We used
theenglish mfaphonetic dictionary and acoustic model, re-
sulting in71phoneme classes present in the LIBRISPEECH
training set. Log-Mel spectrograms were computed using
nMels = 80, a window size of64ms, a hop size of16ms,
NFFT = 1024, and a frequency range of80–7600Hz, follow-
ing theMICROSOFT/SPEECHT5 HiFi-GANvocoder con-
figuration and corresponding Hugging Face checkpoints2.

for a subset of100k phonetic tokens drawn from the previously
unseen LJSPEECHdataset, as shown in Fig. 2. We found that
the learned priors helped to disentangle the latent space of the
phonemes. Importantly, strong clusters emerge between the left
and middle panels in Fig. 2. However, the middle panel still
contains regions where phoneme classes are not clearly sepa-
rated, such as/t/and/b/(light orange and light yellow-green).
Adding contrastive loss to the prior yields clear separation be-
tween phoneme classes in the latent space (right panel).

word pairs. The results clearly demonstrate the interpolation
capability of our approach: by traversing the centroids of dif-
ferent latent phoneme classes, phonemes are gradually sampled

source classes (α= 0.0) and target class (α= 1.0). Com-

4. Discussion and Conclusion
In this work, we analyzed how the supervised contrastive loss
contributes to the ability of theβ-V AE to learn to separate
phoneme classes, which later enables the model to interpolate
between the phonemes, enhancing the contrast between related
phonemes. Together with our ability to reproduce high-quality
audio samples, we believe that phoneme editing and enhancing
will have a positive impact on TTS systems, especially in op-
timizing speech clarity. Compared to a V AE without learned
priors and contrastive loss, the proposedPhonemeCVAEpro-
duces tighter intra-class clustering, stronger inter-class separa-
tion, and positive silhouette scores across all evaluated datasets
(see Tab. 1). The UMAP visualizations further confirm that con-
trastive regularization in combination with the phoneme priors
transforms a diffuse, entangled latent manifold into compact,
clearly separated phoneme clusters.
Importantly, this improved geometry is not purely vi-
sual. Because each phoneme is modeled as a Gaussian class-
conditional prior, the structured latent space enables controlled
interpolation and extrapolation between phoneme centroids.
This allows targeted enhancement of minimal-pair contrasts
while preserving generative continuity. The combination of
KL-based regularization (encouraging compact class priors)
and supervised CL (explicitly maximizing class separability)
proves to be complementary: the former stabilizes the distribu-
tion, while the latter enforces discriminative structure (Tab. 1).
Despite these promising results, we acknowledge remain-
ing limitations. First, phoneme supervision relies on forced
alignment, which assumes discrete phoneme boundaries and
may introduce alignment noise, while natural speech exhibits
strong coarticulation effects. Second, the evaluation primarily
focused on latent space metrics and structural analysis. Third,
experiments were limited to English datasets, leaving multilin-
gual robustness and low-resource scenarios for future work.
Overall,PhonemeCVAEdemonstrates that supervised con-
trastive learning is an effective and principled mechanism for
imposing phonological structure in generative speech models.
By aligning latent geometry with phoneme classes while main-
taining continuity, the approach contributes toward more in-
terpretable, controllable, and linguistically grounded TTS sys-
tems. Future work will explore multilingual extensions, and
integration with more advanced generative back-ends to further
improve phoneme-level controllability in speech synthesis.
