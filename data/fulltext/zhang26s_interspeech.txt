DASR-CPO: Reference-Free Contrastive Preference Optimization for
Correcting Mandarin Semantic Drift in Low-Resource Chinese Dialect ASR
Tao Zhang1, Haiyang Zhang 1,∗∗
1 Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, China
zhangtao123@bupt.edu.cn, zhhy@bupt.edu.cn
Abstract
Pretrained ASR models such as Whisper-Large-v3 can degrade
on low-resource Chinese dialects because a Mandarin-dominant
prior favors frequent homophones. This causes Mandarin se-
mantic drift: dialect words are transcribed as phonetically simi-
lar but semantically wrong Mandarin forms, often on entity- or
keyword-critical spans. LoRA supervised fine-tuning reduces
character error rate (CER) but does not explicitly suppress these
competitors. We propose DASR-CPO, a reference-free con-
trastive preference optimization method that teaches the model
to prefer dialect-correct spans over mined Mandarin confusions.
Preference pairs come from data-driven confusion mining with-
out manual annotation. We localize drift-prone spans by se-
quence matching and train with a span-level contrastive objec-
tive using hard negatives. On MagicData Sichuanese (4.53 h),
DASR-CPO reduces CER from 24.85% to 22.58% and improves
dialect-entity F1 from 72.82 to 74.15 with zero inference-time
overhead.
Index Terms: dialect speech recognition, preference optimiza-
tion, Whisper adaptation, entity correction
1. Introduction
Large-scale pretrained speech models, exemplified by Whis-
per [ 1], have recently advanced automatic speech recognition
(ASR) via weakly supervised learning on massive multilin-
gual corpora. Despite strong robustness on high-resource va-
rieties such as Standard Mandarin, their performance often de-
grades when deployed on low-resource Chinese dialects (e.g.,
Sichuanese), where phonology and lexicon differ from the
Mandarin-dominated pretraining distribution. While this gap is
often attributed to acoustic and domain mismatch, we observe a
more specific and persistent decoding failure mode that directly
harms semantic fidelity.
We call this failure mode Mandarin Semantic Drift : for di-
alect speech, the model produces a phonetically plausible hy-
pothesis but systematically substitutes dialect lexical items with
high-frequency Mandarin homophones, yielding semantically
incorrect transcripts. For instance, dialect entities such as “ 阆
中” may be decoded as the Mandarin-frequent “ 郎 中”, and
“巴适” may drift to “ 巴士” (Table 2). This behavior suggests
that decoding is dominated by an entrenched Mandarin prior:
dialect-specific words receive insuﬀicient probability mass, and
search prefers Mandarin tokens that are frequent in pretraining
even when they contradict the intended dialect semantics. Such
drift is especially damaging in downstream applications where
entity correctness matters more than minor character-level edits.

Standard adaptation pipelines, including supervised fine-
tuning (SFT) and parameter-eﬀicient fine-tuning (PEFT) with
LoRA [2], optimize maximum likelihood of the reference tran-
script. While effective for reducing overall character error rate
(CER), likelihood training provides no explicit mechanism to
penalize the strongest Mandarin-biased competitors for a given
dialect span. Consequently, the model may increase pθ(y+|x)
without suﬀiciently decreasing pθ(y−|x); under decoding, these
high-probability Mandarin confusions can still dominate even
after adaptation.
Motivated by preference alignment [ 3, 4], we reframe di-
alect adaptation as a preference optimization problem: for the
same audio x, the dialect-correct transcript y+ should be pre-
ferred over a Mandarin-biased confusion y−. We propose
DASR-CPO, a resource-eﬀicient dialect alignment framework
based on reference-free contrastive preference optimization.
First, we construct preference supervision via confusion min-
ing, extracting hard-negative Mandarin confusions from model
hypotheses without manual preference labels. Second, we op-
timize a span-level objective that explicitly enlarges the mar-
gin between dialect-correct spans and their strongest Mandarin
competitors, focusing gradients on drift-prone entities. Finally,
to address byte-pair encoding (BPE) fragmentation of multi-
character dialect words, we introduce a full-sequence dynamic
matching strategy that reliably localizes dialect spans for prefer-
ence optimization.
Our contributions are:
• Problem. We identify and formalize Mandarin Semantic
Drift as a ranking error on dialect-critical spans in low-
resource Chinese dialect ASR.
• Method. We propose DASR-CPO, combining data-driven
confusion mining with reference-free span-level contrastive
preference optimization and hard-negative pooling.
• Span supervision. We introduce full-sequence dynamic
matching to localize dialect spans under BPE tokeniza-
tion, enabling stable span-level training without modifying
inference-time decoding.
• Results. On MagicData Sichuanese with 4.53 hours of la-
beled data, DASR-CPO improves both CER and dialect-
entity metrics over LoRA fine-tuning, with zero inference-
time overhead.

(e.g., SpecAugment [ 5]), multilingual transfer, semi-/weakly-
supervised learning, and lexicon-based constraints, where the
core challenge is the scarcity of labeled speech and the mismatch

ASR, the mismatch is exacerbated by systematic phonological

In contrast, our analysis emphasizes a decoding-level seman-

that can persist even after acoustic adaptation.
2.2. Foundation models for ASR and adaptation
Recent foundation ASR models trained on massive multilin-
gual or web-scale corpora (e.g., Whisper) demonstrate strong
robustness in high-resource languages but may degrade in low-
resource dialect settings due to distributional priors in the pre-
training data [ 1]. A common practice is supervised fine-tuning
(SFT) on the target domain/dialect to improve recognition qual-
ity. However, likelihood-based fine-tuning optimizes the gold
transcript without explicitly penalizing strong competitors (e.g.,
frequent Mandarin homophones), which can leave stubborn se-
mantic confusions uncorrected. Our approach complements
SFT by introducing a contrastive preference signal against
mined Mandarin confusions to explicitly reshape the ranking on
drift-prone spans.
2.3. Parameter-eﬀicient fine-tuning
Parameter-eﬀicient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods such as
adapters [10], prefix/prompt tuning, and LoRA enable adapting
large pretrained models under limited compute and memory
budgets. LoRA injects low-rank updates into selected weight
matrices and is widely adopted for eﬀicient adaptation of large
models [ 2]. In this work, we employ LoRA for the SFT stage
and further show that preference alignment can be layered on
top of a PEFT-adapted checkpoint, providing targeted semantic
rectification without increasing inference-time cost.
2.4. Contextual biasing and entity correction
A practical line of work addresses entity errors through con-
textual biasing or decoding-time constraints. Representative
approaches include beam-search score boosting and contex-
tual language models [ 11, 12], deep contextualization with
contextual-entity encoders [ 13, 14, 15], pronunciation-aware
contextualization [ 16], and more recent eﬀicient matching or
bias-control techniques [ 17, 18, 19]. These techniques can be
effective but often introduce additional decoding complexity, re-
quire test-time context, or trade general accuracy for entity recall
when biasing is too strong. We include a loss-reweighting base-
line (Context Bias) and instead propose a training-time align-
ment objective that directly contrasts the gold dialect span with
its Mandarin homophone competitors, yielding semantic correc-
tion with zero inference-time overhead.
2.5. Preference optimization and discriminative training
Preference optimization has recently emerged as an effective
alignment paradigm, especially in LLMs, where pairwise com-
parisons (chosen vs. rejected) are used to reshape model prefer-
ences [3, 4]. In ASR, related ideas appear in sequence-level or
discriminative criteria that optimize relative hypotheses rather
than token-wise likelihood (e.g., MMI/sMBR and expected-
WER/MWER objectives [ 20, 21, 22, 23, 24]) and in methods
that leverage hard negatives to refine decision boundaries. Mo-
tivated by these directions, we formulate dialect adaptation as
a preference alignment problem and adopt reference-free CPO
to avoid the memory overhead of a frozen reference model
[4]. Crucially, because ASR tokenization can fragment dialect
words under BPE, we introducespan-level scoring with dynamic
matching so that optimization targets the complete semantic unit
rather than a single token.
3. Methodology
We propose DASR-CPO (Dialect Alignment via Semantic Rec-
tification), consisting of two phases: (1) offline confusion min-
ing to construct dialect–Mandarin homophone candidates, and
(2) mixed-objective span-level preference alignment that explic-
itly reshapes rankings on drift-prone spans.
3.1. Mandarin Semantic Drift as a ranking error
Let x ∈ X denote an utterance and y ∈ Y a BPE token se-
quence. For a dialect utterance, we focus on dialect-critical
spans (e.g., entities) and their Mandarin homophone competi-
tors. Mandarin Semantic Drift can be viewed as a local rank-
ing error: the model assigns higher probability to a Mandarin
competitor span than to the dialect-correct span under the same
acoustic evidence. Our goal is to reverse this preference locally
while preserving general ASR quality.

3.3. Full-sequence dynamic matching for span localization
BPE tokenization can fragment multi-character dialect words
into variable-length token sequences, and the same surface form
may map to different token boundaries depending on context. To
obtain stable span supervision, we localize each dialect term oc-
currence by full-sequence dynamic matching between the gold
character string and the detokenized reference. Concretely, we
match the term at the string level and map it back to token indices
using the tokenizer’s offsets, producing a set of span locations
{(t, m)} where t is the start token index andm is the span length
in tokens. This matching is performed for every occurrence of
each target term in ypos and enables span-level preference opti-
mization without assuming a fixed BPE segmentation.

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Dataset and Protocol
We use ASR-CSICHDIACSC, an open-source Sichuan dialect
conversational speech corpus released on MagicHub by Magic
Data, which contains 4.53 hours of transcribed dyadic conver-
sations from 12 pairs (24 speakers).
Preprocessing. Recordings are segmented into utterances us-
ing provided timestamps. We resample audio to 16 kHz, dis-
card segments shorter than 0.5s, remove non-speech tags (e.g.,
[LAUGHTER]), strip punctuation, and convert Traditional Chi-
nese to Simplified Chinese. All metrics are computed after ap-
plying the same normalization to both hypotheses and refer-
ences.
Data partitioning. We perform utterance-level random shuf-
fling with a fixed seed (42) and split the corpus into train/dev/test
with a ratio of 0.8:0.1:0.1 (approx. 3.62h / 0.45h / 0.45h).
Speaker-disjointness is not enforced due to the limited number
of sessions; all methods share the identical split.
Dialect lexicon. We construct a Sichuanese dialect lexiconLdia
from the training transcripts by extracting distinct dialect entity
types (e.g., region-specific lexical variants). The lexicon is used
for entity-centric evaluation and confusion-focused sampling in
Stage 2.
4.2. Evaluation Metrics
We report Character Error Rate (CER) as the primary metric.
Using Ldia, we additionally report:
• Dialect Recall: percentage of gold entity mentions exactly
matched in the hypothesis.
• Entity F1: harmonic mean of entity-level precision and re-
call.
• Correction Rate (CR): measured on a hard-negative subset
H defined as the set of entity mentions that are mistranscribed
by the zero-shot baseline; CR is the fraction of these mentions
corrected by an adapted model.
4.3. Model and Training Configuration
Backbone and LoRA. We use Whisper-Large-v3 (∼1.55B pa-
rameters). LoRA is applied to query/value projections ( r=32,
α=64, dropout 0.05). We enable 8-bit loading and gradient
checkpointing to fit consumer-grade GPUs. Dropout is set to
0.09 for regularization.
Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). We train for 3 epochs
with AdamW (LR 1 × 10−3, warmup 100 steps), per-device
batch size 8, gradient accumulation 2, and mixed precision
(fp16).
Stage 2: Preference Alignment (DASR-CPO). Starting from
the SFT checkpoint, we train for 2 epochs with LR 1 × 10−6,
CPO temperature β=0.5, and loss weight λ=1.0. We apply: (i)
confusion-focused sampling (keeping utterances containing tar-
get entities); (ii) duration filtering (2s–25s); (iii) gradient clip-
ping (norm 1.0); and (iv) encoder freezing.
Decoding. All systems use identical decoding with
the HuggingFace pipeline ( task=transcribe,
language=chinese), using the backbone’s default
generation configuration (no external LM, no prompting).
4.4. Baselines
We compare against: (1) Zero-shot Whisper; (2) Pure LoRA
(Stage 1 only); and (3) Context Bias , an SFT variant up-
weighting dialect tokens by γ ∈ { 1.5, 5.0} in cross-entropy,
selected on the dev set.

5. Results and Discussion
5.1. Main results
Table 1 reports overall ASR accuracy and entity-centric seman-
tic metrics. DASR-CPO achieves the lowest CER among our
baselines (22.58%), improving over Pure LoRA by a modest
2.27 absolute (about 9.1% relative; 24.85 →22.58).While the
CER improvement appears moderate, it is crucial to note that
semantic drift often occurs on keywords that carry the cen-
tral meaning of the utterance. As such, the gain in Entity F1
(+1.33) reflects a more significant qualitative improvement in
the model’s ability to convey the speaker’s intent, effectively
mitigating the jarring semantic errors that characterize the Man-
darin baseline. While the absolute reduction in overall CER
appears modest (24.85% → 22.58%), this is expected: dialect-
specific entities and keywords constitute only a small fraction
of the total character count in continuous speech. The stan-
dard CER metric dilutes the impact of targeted semantic cor-
rections. Instead, the substantial gains in Entity F1 ( +1.33) and
the precise reductions in false positive homophones (Table 1)
more accurately reflect the method’s effectiveness in rectifying
Mandarin Semantic Drift. These qualitative improvements are
crucial for downstream semantic fidelity.
5.2. Entity-level effects
Compared with Pure LoRA, DASR-CPO improves Dialect Re-
call (66.39→67.94, +1.55), improves Entity F1 (72.82 →74.15,
+1.33), and reduces false positives (FP 39 →32, -7). In general,
preference strength (e.g., β, λ) can affect the precision–recall
balance, so we recommend tuning on the dev set when transfer-
ring to other dialects or lexicons.
5.3. Hard-negative correction on Mandarin Semantic Drift
To directly measure whether we correct the targeted drift, we
evaluate Correction Rate (CR) on a hard-negative subset H,
defined as entity mentions that are consistently mistranscribed
by the zero-shot baseline (Sec. 4). On this subset, DASR-CPO
improves Correction Rate (CR) compared with likelihood-based
fine-tuning, demonstrating that span-level preference alignment
can flip the ranking between a dialect term and its strongest Man-
darin competitor. Table 2 lists representative mined confusion
pairs that characterize Mandarin Semantic Drift.

5.5. Decoding, latency, and limitations
Decoding. All models use an identical decoding configuration
via the HuggingFace Whisper pipeline ( task=transcribe,
language=chinese) with the same generation settings for
fair comparison. Overhead. DASR-CPO introduces no
inference-time overheadsince it does not add modules or rescor-
ing at test time. Limitations. (i) Our train/dev/test split is not
speaker-disjoint due to the small corpus size, which may overes-
timate generalization to unseen speakers. (ii) The method relies
on mined confusion pairs and a lexicon; performance can be sen-
sitive to lexicon coverage. (iii) The increased FP suggests cali-
bration is needed to improve precision under ultra-low-resource
supervision.
5.6. Conclusion
In this work, we introduced DASR-CPO, a novel parameter-
eﬀicient framework designed to mitigate Mandarin Semantic
Drift in low-resource dialect ASR. By reframing dialect adapta-
tion as a preference optimization problem, we successfully uti-
lized mined confusion pairs to construct hard negatives without
requiring manual preference labeling. Our proposed span-level
objective and full-sequence dynamic matching strategy ensure
that the model learns to prioritize dialect-specific lexical items
over their high-frequency Mandarin homophones. Experimental
results on the Sichuanese corpus demonstrate that DASR-CPO
significantly improves entity recognition and overall transcrip-
tion accuracy compared to standard LoRA fine-tuning and con-
textual biasing baselines. Importantly, our method introduces
zero inference-time overhead, making it highly practical for de-
ployment. Future directions include validating the scalability of
DASR-CPO across diverse Chinese dialects and investigating its
eﬀicacy in multilingual code-switching scenarios.
