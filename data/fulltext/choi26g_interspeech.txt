Speaker Localization

Abstract
Localizing multiple moving speakers in dynamic acoustic en-
vironments requires resolving time-varying spatial cues under
severe spatial-spectral overlap. To address this challenge, we
propose SpkGuideDOA, a framework that enhances a spatial
cue estimator with speaker-wise representation guidance while
maintaining computational efficiency. An auxiliary guidance
generator extracts implicit speaker representations from multi-
channel magnitudes and injects them via residual modulation at
a pooled resolution. Training is driven by a localization objec-
tive with an additional loss that stabilizes the guidance branch.
Joint permutation-invariant training ensures consistent speaker
assignment across modules. Experiments on simulated mixtures
and the LOCATA dataset demonstrate improved localization
performance while maintaining computational efficiency.
Index Terms: moving multi-speaker localization, direct-path
inter-channel phase difference (DP-IPD), guidance representa-
tion, permutation-invariant training.
1. Introduction
Speaker localization estimates the direction of arrival (DOA) of
active speakers from microphone array recordings. These direc-
tional cues make the acoustic scene geometrically interpretable
and offer useful spatial priors for audio processing [1, 2]. They
can complement content cues when speech spectra overlap
across speakers. This becomes increasingly important in com-
plex and time-varying multi-source environments.
While conventional localization methods [3–6] were devel-
oped under quasi-static acoustic assumptions, recent applica-
tions [7–14] have shifted attention towards online localization
of moving speakers. In dynamic scenes, speaker directions vary
continuously, requiring temporally consistent estimation rather
than isolated frame-wise predictions. Moreover, speaker trajec-
tories may intersect or partially overlap in angle, producing
nearly identical spatial cues across sources. Under such condi-
tions, spatial information alone becomes insufficient for stable
speaker-to-track assignment. Several approaches [9–12] address
moving multi-speaker localization through deep neural net-
works. IPDNet [9] refines direct-path inter-channel phase differ-
ences (DP-IPD) with permutation-invariant training (PIT) [15],
while TF-Mamba [13] and IPDNet2 [10] adopt state-space
modeling with Mamba [16] for efficient and causal designs.
However, as they still rely primarily on spatial representations,
they lack an explicit mechanism to enforce speaker-specific dif-
ferentiation when DOAs overlap or become indistinguishable.
To address this limitation, a recent work [14] has ex-
plored multi-speaker tracking strategies. This approach decou-
ples speech separation and DOA estimation and subsequently
associates their outputs to stabilize source identity. While this

In this work, we reinterpret multi-speaker DP-IPD-based
localization as a single-class directional inference problem and
introduce speaker-wise guidance solely to enhance localization
robustness. The proposed framework is not a multi-task formu-
lation but a localization enhancer. A guidance generator (GG)
estimates speaker-wise representations, which are injected into
the spatial cue estimator (SCE) through residual modulation.
Importantly, the voice-activity detection (V AD) loss is confined
to the GG and does not backpropagate gradients to the SCE,
while maintaining localization as the primary objective. To en-
sure consistent speaker-wise alignment between the guidance
and localization streams, we adopt a joint-PIT strategy during
optimization. Through this disentangled and guidance-driven
design, the proposed method mitigates overlap-induced ambi-
guity while preserving computational efficiency.

3. Proposed Method
3.1. Spatial Cue Estimator (SCE)
The lower branch in Figure 1 represents the SCE, which fol-
lows the DP-IPD pipeline as in [9, 10]. The SCE starts with
a point-wise convolution (PConv) and then stacks Localiza-
tion Layer (LL). As shown in Figure 1(c), Each LL includes a
grouped 1D frequency convolution (1D F-GConv) block com-
posed of a depthwise 1D frequency convolution (1D F-DConv)
and a grouped point-wise convolution (GPConv) withH 1/12
groups, whereH 1 is a hidden dimension for the SCE. It is
followed by Cross-band and Narrow-band modules adopted
from [10, 19]. Guidance is injected after pooling operation at
resolutionT′×F′, whereF ′=F/Fp with the frequency pool-
ing factorFp, to limit speaker-wise branching overhead.
3.2. Guidance Generator (GG)
The GG produces speaker-wise guidance features that en-
code spectral dominance patterns without explicitly predicting
DOA. Unlike separation networks [14, 20], it extracts speaker-
conditioned representations aligned with pooled SCE features
without reconstructing source signals.

they are pooled intoT′ to temporally align with the SCE.

the GG is aligned with the speakers that the SCE aims to disen-

4. Experimental Setup
4.1. Implementation and Baselines
Signals were sampled at 16 kHz. The STFT was computed us-
ing a 512-point analysis window with a 256-sample hop size,
following [11]. We used pooling factors ofT p=12,F p=16,
hidden dimensions ofH 1=96,H 2=48,H 3=12andH 4=256.
For the band-wise feature extractor in Figure. 1(b), the GG in-
put yieldedG 1=32,G 2=8,G 3=4, andG 4=4sub-bands. The
αin (9) was set to 0.4 to keepL ψ
VAD on a scale comparable
toL ψ
IPD. The auxiliary decoder was a Linear–PReLU–Linear
stack. For DP-IPD template matching, templates were gener-
ated over a uniformly spaced angular grid of 73 azimuths in
[-180◦,180◦]and 37 elevations in[-90 ◦,90◦].
Each epoch used 50,000 generated mixtures (batch size 4)
and training ran for 15 epochs. Adam [33] was used with an ini-
tial learning rate of 0.001 and a decay factor of 0.975 per epoch.
For comparison, we evaluated IPDNet [9], IPDNet2 [10] and
TF-Mamba [13]. Since [13] originally predicted azimuth only,
its training target was modified to DP-IPD for both azimuth and
elevation. For fair comparison, all models, including the pro-
posed method, were trained under the same training scheme. To
assess the effectiveness of the guidance, ablation studies were
conducted, summarized in Table 1. The number of parameters
and floating-point operations (FLOPs) during inference were
measured using the official PyTorch utility [34].
4.2. Data Generation and Evaluation Protocol
We adopted the 12-channel 3D array from [35]. Clean Lib-
riSpeech [36] was divided into training, validation, and test sets,
with each sample having a duration of 5 seconds. Moving mix-
tures were generated with gpuRIR [37] following trajectory-
recipes in [38], with room sizes uniformly sampled from
(3,3,2.5)to(10,8,6)m 3 and reverberationT 60∈[0.2,1.2]s.
Gaussian noise was applied at SNRs in[−5,20]dB. For real-
world evaluation, we used LOCATA [35] that contains moving
sources. The maximum number of speakers was set toK=2,
consistent with the experimental settings in [9,10] and the max-
imum number of speakers in the real-world dataset [35].
To evaluate robustness under spatial overlap, additional test
sets were formed by grouping static mixtures according to the
angular distance between two speakers (Figure 2). For each
separation angle, 500 mixtures were generated in same ran-
domized room configurations above. Performance was evalu-
ated using the miss detection rate (MDR) and mean absolute er-
ror (MAE) based on spherical angular distance with a tolerance
of≤30 ◦, following [11]. MAE was computed with respect to
the ground-truth number of active speakers to properly evalu-
ate multi-speaker localization. To prevent models from benefit-
ing from over-estimating the number of speakers, false alarm
rate (FAR) was additionally reported. For each model, the ac-
tivity threshold was tuned on the validation set to yield the best
MDR/FAR performance and then fixed for testing.

5. Results and Analysis
5.1. Quantitative Evaluation on Simulated and Real Data
Table 1 shows that SpkGuideDOA achieved the best overall per-
formance on both simulated and real datasets while maintain-
ing competitive computational complexity. It consistently ob-
tained the lowest MDR, FAR, and MAE, indicating improved
speaker discrimination rather than over-detection. Comparisons
with baselines, including IPDNet2 which shares a similar back-
bone and capacity-matched ablations further indicate that the
gains arise from the proposed guidance mechanism rather than
increased model size.
5.2. Robustness Across Angular Separation
Figure 2 analyzes the performance as a function of angular sep-
aration. The MAE results (left) reflected overall localization
accuracy and showed that our method maintained controlled
errors even in moderate separations (15–30°), demonstrating
robustness beyond closely spaced scenarios. The MDR–FAR
metric (right) evaluated whether speakers were detected as dis-
tinct sources rather than merged; in the most challenging range
(0–15°), our approach achieved the lowest scores, indicating
superior stability under severe spatial ambiguity. Qualitative
examples in Figure 3 further confirmed that, while compet-
ing methods often collapsed two nearby speakers into a single
peak, our model consistently preserved distinct source identi-
ties. Overall, the proposed method improved both detection re-
liability and directional accuracy across separation ranges.
5.3. Ablation Analysis of Guidance and Training Strategy
The ablation study in Table 1 evaluated the contribution of each
architectural and training component, clarifying the role of the
GG and its supervision in localization performance.
Necessity of the Guidance Generator .Removing the GG
(w/o GG) resulted in a substantial performance drop despite
reduced computational complexity, indicating that the perfor-
mance gain was closely tied to speaker-wise guidance injection
rather than backbone scaling alone.
Guidance and auxiliary supervision.Removing the aux-
iliary V AD loss (w/o V AD Loss) significantly degraded perfor-
mance, suggesting that localization supervision alone was insuf-
ficient to induce stable speaker-wise modulation. Explicit activ-
ity regularization helped the GG form more structured and dis-
criminative guidance representations. Also, this indicated that
the performance drop could not be attributed merely to reduced

into the SCE, mask estimation did not align well with the
compact guidance representation. In practice, effective mask
supervision would require high-resolution interaction before
pooling, which would substantially increase FLOPs due
to speaker-wise feature replication. Instead, the V AD loss
provided a structurally compatible supervision signal at the
pooled resolution, enabling efficient learning of speaker-aware
modulation under our low-complexity design.
6. Conclusion
We proposed SpkGuideDOA, a guidance-driven framework
for moving multi-speaker localization. By injecting pooled
speaker-wise residual guidance into a DP-IPD localizer, it mit-
igated assignment instability while preserving a localization-
focused backbone. Experiments showed consistent gains in ro-
bustness and directional accuracy with minimal overhead, high-
lighting the effectiveness of speaker-aware modulation for ef-
ficient moving-speaker localization. The formulation is general
and can extend to larger speaker counts without altering the core
pipeline, which we consider for future study.
