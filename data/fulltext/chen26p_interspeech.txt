Bridging the Gap: A Hierarchical Framework for Cross-Modal Style
Modeling in Expressive TTS
Jiale Chen, Jiaxun Li, Wei Li, Yuanpeng Wang, Yuehai Wang∗∗
Zhejiang University, China

Abstract
Natural language prompts offer a highly flexible interface for
expressive text-to-speech (TTS), yet cross-modal gaps and one-
to-many mapping problem hinder reliable style control. To ad-
dress this, we propose OTAFlow, a novel hierarchical cross-
modal style modeling framework. First, optimal transport
alignment, contrastive learning, and multi-task supervision are
jointly optimized to construct a unified style space that pre-
serves instance-level correspondence and expressive factor sep-
arability. Second, a conditional flow matching module is intro-
duced to capture the residual modality gap, explicitly address-
ing the one-to-many challenge by generating diverse acoustic
style embeddings from a single prompt. When plugged into a
downstream TTS backbone, evaluations show that our method
substantially outperforms baseline models in fine-grained style
retrieval and synthesized style accuracy, effectively delivering
diverse and controllable expressive speech. Audio samples can
be found in our demo page 1.

1. Introduction
Recent neural text-to-speech (TTS) achieves high natural-
ness [1, 2, 3, 4, 5], but practical applications increasingly
demand flexible control over expressive styles. While con-
ventional methods rely on reference audio or coarse style
tags [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11], suitable references are often unavailable
and tags lack descriptive granularity. This motivates the use of
natural-language style prompts [12, 13, 14]. However, reliable
prompt-based control remains challenging: style descriptions
and speech signals exhibit a substantial cross-modal represen-
tation gap, and a single prompt inherently corresponds to di-
verse valid acoustic realizations, creating a severe one-to-many
mapping problem.
Robust prompt-driven control requires a cross-modal style
space where speech attributes are less entangled. Prior work
explores factorized modeling and multi-level contrastive objec-
tives to disentangle speech factors [3, 10], and incorporates
factor-aware supervision such as content-centric disentangle-
ment and label-aware contrastive learning [15, 16]. Yet these
methods often rely on specialized encoders or point-wise ob-
jectives and do not explicitly address distribution-level cross-
modal mismatch.
Prompt-conditioned expressive speech is inherently one-to-
many, making point-wise alignment unstable. Instruct-TTS [12]
adopts cross-modal metric learning, and PromptTTS2 [13] sam-

explicit generative mechanism that produces diverse yet plausi-

We therefore propose a hierarchical cross-modal style mod-
eling framework. First, optimal transport (OT) is used to align
prompt and speech style embeddings at the distribution level,
which is less sensitive to one-to-many ambiguity than point-to-
point distances [18, 19, 20]. This alignment is combined with
contrastive learning and multi-task supervision to obtain a uni-
fied style embedding space. Second, conditional flow matching
(CFM) [21] is introduced in the embedding space to model the
conditional distributionp(z s|zt)and enable sampling of audio
style embeddings conditioned on text prompts. The resulting
embeddings can be integrated into a standard TTS backbone,
and a DiT-based flow matching is used in this work for down-
stream generation and verification.
Main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
• A unified cross-modal style representation framework is pre-
sented by combining OT-based distribution alignment, con-
trastive learning, and multi-task supervision.
• CFM is introduced to model the conditional distribution in
the unified embedding space, enabling prompt-conditioned
sampling and reducing residual structured modality gap.
• The learned style space is applied to a DiT-based flow match-
ing TTS backbone, for further downstream validation.
2. Methodology
To enable flexible cross-modal control of speech styles, we pro-
pose OTAFlow as illustrated in Fig. 1. The framework consists
of three stages: (1) a multi-task and contrastive learning stage
with optimal-transport based distribution alignment to construct
a unified text–audio style embedding space, (2) a flow-matching
stage that models the residual modality gap by sampling audio-
style embeddings conditioned on text embeddings, and (3) a
flow-matching based speech generation stage conditioned on
the sampled style embedding.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets.We conduct experiments on the expressive English
speech dataset Textrolspeech [22], which contains about 330
hours of speech paired with natural-language style prompts
from multiple speakers. Each prompt describes several style
factors. To mitigate the emotional imbalance in the official split,
we form a balanced test set by randomly sampling 50 utterances
across eight typical emotions, reserving the rest for training. All
waveforms are sampled to 22.05 kHz. STFT is used to extract
Mel spectral features. The Mel spectrum is normalized using
the global mean and standard deviation of the dataset.
Baselines.To evaluate the effectiveness of our unified style em-
bedding space in reducing the modality gap while preserving
instance-level correspondence, we compare four variants built
upon the same pre-trained CLAP backbone. Note that to ensure
basic expressive factor separability, all trained variants incor-
porate the multi-task auxiliary supervision. In all variants, the
CLAP encoders are kept frozen and we compute cross-modal
retrieval using cosine similarity in the resulting embedding
space. To evaluate whether the learned style representations
and the prompt-to-style sampler improve expressive synthesis,
we compare our model with several TTS baselines that sup-
port natural language style control, including EmoV oice [17],
CosyV oice2 [5] and IndexTTS2 [4].
Metrics.We employ a combination of objective and subjective
metrics to evaluate both the alignment quality of the latent space
and the acoustic quality of the synthesized speech. To quan-
tify the alignment between text and audio in our constructed
latent space, we conduct cross-modal retrieval tasks (Text-to-
Audio and Audio-to-Text). We report Recall@k (k=1,5,10) and
Median Rank (MedR) for both text-to-audio and audio-to-text
retrieval in the embedding space. All embeddings are normal-
ized and are computed using cosine similarity. For downstream
TTS, we report both subjective and objective metrics. Sub-
jective evaluation includes naturalness MOS (nMOS) and style
MOS (sMOS). For objective evaluation, we use the open source
UTMOS2 to assess the MOS of the synthesized speech. We use
pre-trained speech emotion representation model 3 to calculate
the emotion similarity (ESIM). To assess timbre control capa-
bility, we use a pre-trained WavLM speaker verification model4
to extract features of the synthesized speech and the real speech
of the target speaker, and then calculated the Speaker Encoder
Cosine Similarity (SECS).
Implementation Details.We use a pre-trained CLAP model
as the frozen backbone for both text and audio, and our TTS
model is constructed upon Matcha-TTS [23]. We replace the
original estimator with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) and in-
ject condition via AdaLN. When we train the TTS model, any
other modules are frozen. The control coefficients for different
modes are randomly selected between [0,1], forcing the TTS
model to learn the robust mapping of semantic and paralinguis-
tic information. Both stages of training use the Adam optimizer
with a cosine learning-rate schedule, where the learning rate is
annealed from1×10 −4 to1×10 −5 and then kept constant.
Training is performed with a batch size of 8 using 16-bit mixed
precision.

3.2. Main Results
Cross-Modal Retrieval.Table 1 presents the cross-modal re-
trieval performance. Off-the-shelf frozen CLAP features show
severe cross-modal mismatch. Since all trained variants share
the multi-task supervision to maintain basic factor separabil-
ity, the performance gaps directly reflect the effectiveness of the
alignment strategies. Relying solely on point-wise contrastive
learning or distribution-level alignment yields suboptimal re-
sults. By synergizing both, our joint objective surpasses all
variants, achieving the best bidirectional Recall and MedR to
successfully bridge the modality gap.
Expressive Text-to-Speech.Table 2 presents the downstream
synthesis performance. In Track-1, our method achieves the
best style consistency, reaching the highest sMOS (3.88) and
ESIM (69.19) among all baselines, indicating that our pro-
posed text-to-style modeling effectively captures and transfers
prompt-specified expressive attributes. Furthermore, in Track-
2, where reference audio is used strictly for speaker condition-
ing, our method maintains superior style control accuracy (high
SMOS and ESIM) while achieving highly competitive speaker
similarity. This demonstrates a crucial advantage of our hier-
archical framework: the unified style space successfully dis-
entangles text-driven style from acoustic timbre. It effectively
prevents the inherent style of the reference audio from leaking
into and overriding the text prompt’s instructions. The remain-
ing gap on nMOS/UTMOS is expected given the lightweight
downstream backbone and limited training scale, but it shows a
decisive advantage in accurate expressive synthesis.

4. Conclusion
This paper presents a hierarchical framework for prompt-driven
cross-modal style modeling in expressive TTS. By combining
an OT-based distribution alignment with contrastive learning
and multi-task auxiliary supervision, our model constructs a
unified style space that mitigates the modality gap while pre-
serving instance-level correspondence and improving separa-
bility along key expressive factors. To further address the in-
herent one-to-many nature of prompt-conditioned speech and
the residual structured mismatch left by similarity-based ob-
jectives, we introduce conditional flow matching, enabling di-
verse audio-style embedding sampling under the same prompt.
Extensive experiments on Textrolspeech demonstrate that our
approach significantly improves cross-modal retrieval perfor-
mance and yields stronger style consistency in expressive syn-
thesis.
