Abstract
Electromyography-to-speech (ETS) offers a promising solu-
tion for voicing silent speech, yet conventional methods often
struggle to achieve high semantic intelligibility. We introduce
GETS, a novel framework that establishes a new state-of-the-
art (SOTA) with a Word Error Rate (WER) of 11.89% on the
silent EMG test set. GETS comprises a diffusion-based genera-
tive network conditioned on EMG signals, integrated with a se-
mantic guidance mechanism derived from silent speech recog-
nition (SSR). Our framework not only demonstrates superior se-
mantic intelligibility by effectively disambiguating underspec-
ified EMG inputs but also faithfully preserves the prosodic
characteristics—such as temporal alignment and energy con-
tours—inherent in the original EMG signals. This synergistic
fusion of acoustic EMG cues and SSR-based semantic guidance
establishes a new benchmark for high-fidelity, naturalistic silent
speech communication.
Index Terms: silent speech interface, electromyography,
speech synthesis, classifier guidance
1. Introduction
Silent speech interfaces (SSIs) aim to reconstruct audible
speech from biosignals recorded during silent articulation, of-
fering a vital communication pathway for individuals with
severe speech or motor impairments [1, 2]. Among vari-
ous modalities, facial electromyography (EMG) is particularly
promising, as it non-invasively captures articulatory muscle ac-
tivity and preserves discriminative information [3].
Despite recent advances, EMG-to-speech (ETS)—the task
of directly synthesizing intelligible speech from EMG sig-
nals—remains challenging. Silent EMG signals are inherently
ambiguous and offer limited access to the underlying linguistic
content of an utterance. To learn the EMG-to-speech mapping,
most ETS models [4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10] rely on paired voiced
acoustic targets as proxy supervision. Nevertheless, synthesized
silent speech continues to exhibit substantially lower intelligi-
bility than voiced speech. This suggests that minimizing acous-
tic reconstruction error does not inherently guarantee semantic
or linguistic consistency in the generated output.
To address this limitation, we propose GETS, a high-fidelity
framework for guiding ETS synthesis via silent speech recog-
nition. Specifically, we employ a pre-trained silent speech
recognition (SSR) model to provide classifier guidance, steer-
ing the generative process toward semantically coherent rep-
resentations derived directly from the EMG input. Our main
contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce GETS, a novel framework that achieves a new
state-of-the-art (SOTA) in EMG-to-speech synthesis, reach-
ing a word error rate (WER) of 11.89% on the silent EMG
test set—a significant leap over existing methodologies.
• To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to inte-
grate diffusion-based classifier guidance with silent speech
recognition for ETS, effectively facilitating the generation of
semantically coherent and intelligible speech from highly un-
derspecified EMG signals.
• Through extensive temporal and energy-based analyses, we
provide empirical evidence that GETS preserves the prosodic
characteristics inherent in EMG signals. This confirms
that our framework performs a synergistic fusion of signal-
derived prosodic cues and SSR-based semantic guidance, dis-
tinguishing it from conventional text-to-speech mapping.

2. Related work
2.1. EMG-to-Speech Synthesis
Early ETS approaches primarily focused on learning a di-
rect mapping from EMG features to acoustic representations.
Gaddy and Klein [4] encoded EMG signals with convolu-
tional and transformer layers, leveraging dynamic time warp-
ing (DTW) [11] to mitigate temporal misalignment between
silent and voiced signals before generating mel-spectrograms.
Subsequently, other studies shifted the supervision from acous-
tic features to content representations. SU-ETS [5] predicted
soft speech units from EMG signals as content representations,
enabling multi-speaker synthesis, and Ullah and Kim [6] im-
proved speech unit modeling by enhancing the EMG encoder
with a Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) module. Recently, Diff-
ETS [7] pioneered the integration of diffusion-based generative
modeling for ETS. Despite these architectural advancements,
existing paradigms remain heavily centered on acoustic recon-
struction rather than semantic fidelity, often resulting in high
WER exceeding 25%. Consequently, generation quality heavily
depends on the representational capacity of the EMG encoder,
which limits intelligibility in prior ETS systems.
2.2. Guided Diffusion for Speech Synthesis
Denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) [12] have
rapidly become a dominant paradigm for high-fidelity speech
synthesis. Models such as DiffWave [13] and Grad-TTS [14]
established high-fidelity neural vocoding and text-to-speech
synthesis. A distinctive property of DDPMs is the use of guid-
ance to steer the reverse diffusion process toward a desired
condition at inference time, without retraining the generative
model. Guided-TTS [15] leveraged classifier guidance [16] for
speech synthesis by employing a separately trained phoneme
classifier to steer an unconditional DDPM toward a target

3. Method
The proposed GETS framework aims to synthesize high-
fidelity, intelligible speech from silent EMG signals. As de-
picted in Figure 1, GETS decomposes the generation process
into two synergistic components: (1) EMG-conditioned mel
generation, which captures signal-level acoustic characteristics
and prosodic cues, and (2) SSR-guided semantic refinement,
which provides high-level linguistic constraints. This dual-path
architecture ensures that the synthesized speech is both prosodi-
cally natural and semantically accurate, effectively bridging the
information gap inherent in EMG signals.

backbone [13], adapted for EMG-conditioned synthesis. A fun-

3.2. SSR-guided Semantic Refinement
While EMG conditioning captures articulatory dynamics and
prosodic information, it does not explicitly enforce semantic
correctness. To address this issue, we incorporate text-based
classifier guidance inspired by [17] for enhanced speech intel-
ligibility. Given an EMG signalE, we first obtain a predicted
text ˆtfrom silent speech recognition (SSR) model [19], which
serves as a linguistic constraint during mel generation. We

t. To encourage semantic consistency between the generated

3.3. Network architecture
EMG-conditioned Mel Generator.The network consists of a
dedicated EMG encoder and a DiffWave-based decoder [13].
The encoder features a series of convolutional layers for local
feature extraction, followed by transformer blocks to capture
long-range temporal dependencies within the EMG signals. To
leverage established representations, the encoder is initialized
with pre-trained weights from [4] and subsequently jointly
fine-tuned with the diffusion backbone.
Silent Speech Recognition Network.We employ the pre-
trained MONA-LISA [19] for EMG-to-text recognition. The
final text predictions are obtained by ensembling multiple
recognition outputs and refining them using GPT-4 [21] LLMs
fine-tuned on the LibriSpeech datasets [22]. MONA-LISA
obtains a WER of 8.93% on silent EMG recognition.
ASR Network.Following the architecture used in recent
multi-modal speech synthesis frameworks [17], our ASR guid-
ance module is based on the audio-only variant of A VEC [20]
model. The network is initially pre-trained on the large-scale
LRS2 [23] and LRS3 [24] datasets and further fine-tuned on
the training set of the Gaddy & Klein audio data.

contains EMG and audio recordings from a single subject dur-
ing both vocalized and silent articulation tasks. It comprises
1,285 silent-voiced parallel pairs and 5,470 non-parallel voiced
utterances for training. It includes 8-channel monopolar EMG
signals sampled at 1 kHz and corresponding audio signals
sampled at 16 kHz. Following the preprocessing protocol of

4.2. Implementation Details
The diffusion-based generation model is trained withT=400
diffusion steps following a linear noise schedule from
β0=0.0001toβ T =0.02. We train the model using the Adam
optimizer with a learning rate of2×10 −4, a batch size of 8
per A6000 GPU, and anℓ 1 loss for 400 epochs. Conditional
dropout (p=0.2) is applied during training, and the diffusion
parameters following standard DDPM formulation. The check-
point with the lowest validation loss is selected for evaluation.
For inference stage, we apply EMG-conditioned classifier-
free guidance with weightω 1=2.5. SSR-guided semantic re-
finement is used with weightω 2=1.5for reverse diffusion
timestepst≤270. The generated mel-spectrograms are con-
verted to waveform using a pretrained HiFi-GAN vocoder [26]
trained on 16 kHz audio setting.

5.2. Prosodic Analysis and Signal Fidelity
Since the GETS framework generates mel-spectrograms con-
ditioned on both raw EMG signals and SSR-derived text, it is
essential to discern the specific contributions of each modal-
ity. To this end, we evaluate the prosodic fidelity of the synthe-
sized speech by analyzing temporal alignment and energy con-
tours relative to the ground-truth (GT). Note that pitch-related
metrics are excluded, as EMG signals inherently lack funda-
mental frequency information. All analyses in this section are
conducted using voiced EMG data to ensure the availability of
time-aligned GT audio for objective comparison.
5.2.1. Temporal Alignment Analysis
To assess temporal alignment, we measure the onset and off-
set timing discrepancies (in ms) for both words and pauses.
To decouple alignment accuracy from recognition performance,
we evaluate these metrics exclusively on the common set of
correctly recognized words for each model. Acoustic bound-
aries are extracted using WhisperX [29] as a reliable proxy for
ground-truth (GT) timestamps, and performance is quantified
using the Mean Absolute Error (MAE). A pause is identified
when the silent interval between valid words exceeds 50 ms. As
summarized in Table 2, our GETS model (ω 1=2.5,ω 2=1.5)
achieves competitive, and in some cases superior, alignment
performance compared to the baselines. Notably, GETS out-
performs SU-ETS [5] in word-level alignment and shows en-
hanced pause-level precision compared to Gaddy’s model [4].
This indicates that our framework maintains high prosodic
consistency despite its significant gains in semantic perfor-
mance. Notably, when the EMG guidance weight is neutral-
ized (ω1=0,ω 2=1.5), the temporal alignment degrades sharply.
This empirical evidence confirms that GETS actively extracts
articulatory timing information from the EMG signal, distin-
guishing our framework from a naive text-to-speech (TTS) sys-
tem that relies solely on linguistic input.
5.2.2. Energy Contour Analysis
To evaluate the preservation of speech intensity patterns, we
compare the frame-level log-mel energy contours of the syn-

As shown in Table 3, GETS exhibits competitive energy con-
sistency, showing a slight gap compared to Gaddy’s model [4]
but outperforming SU-ETS [5]. Combined with our tempo-

EMG-shuff, which pairs a random EMG signal with the correct

5.3. Ablation Study for Guidance Weights
To investigate the impact of the proposed guidance mecha-
nisms, we conducted an ablation study by varying the weights
ω1 (EMG signal-based classifier-free guidance) andω 2 (SSR-
based classifier guidance). Table 4 summarizes the WER results
on the silent EMG test set across various weight configurations.
The results demonstrate that both EMG signal-derived acous-
tic features and SSR-driven semantic guidance are indispens-
able for high-performance synthesis. Specifically, neutralizing
the EMG guidance (ω 1=0) results in a substantial degradation
of performance, underscoring the necessity of raw EMG signal
conditioning for stable speech reconstruction. Furthermore, the
semantic guidance weightω 2 exerts a more pronounced influ-
ence on linguistic accuracy; its absence leads to a sharp increase
in WER, as the model loses the high-level semantic constraints
required to disambiguate EMG signals.
6. Conclusion
In this study, we presented GETS, a diffusion-based ETS frame-
work that successfully bridges the gap between EMG signal fi-
delity and semantic intelligibility. By achieving a state-of-the-
art (SOTA) WER of 11.89%, we demonstrated that silent speech
recognition-guided semantic refinement can effectively steer the
generative process without sacrificing the unique prosodic cues
inherent in EMG signals. Our extensive analysis of tempo-
ral and energy profiles confirms that the synthesized speech is
grounded in actual articulatory dynamics rather than being a
purely linguistic-dependent speech synthesis. We believe that
the GETS framework paves the way toward reliable and intelli-
gible synthesis for silent speech communication, offering a ro-
bust foundation for future assistive technologies.
