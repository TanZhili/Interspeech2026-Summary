A Multimodal Semi-Supervised Framework for Automatic Construction of a 
Cross-Lingual Taigi Speech–Chinese Subtitle Corpus 
Cheng-Hsiu Cho, Chih-Chung Kuo,Yu-Siang Lan, Chao-Shih Huang, Yan-Ming Lin, Yuan-Fu Liao 
Institute of Artificial Intelligence Innovation, National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University 
{Cho312581013.ii12, cck, chipsrinlan.ii13, josephcs, ym01556, yfliao}@nycu.edu.tw 
Abstract 
The lack of labeled data for low -resource languages such as 
Taigi (Taiwanese) limits AI development. This work addresses 
the issue by exploiting online videos containing Taigi speech 
with hard -coded Chinese subtitles. We propose a multimodal 
semi-supervised framework that combines a trimodal Audio -
Visual-Language Model (AVLM) with an iterative pseudo -
labeling process inspired by knowledge distillation. A Vision –
Language Model fuses OCR outputs and AVLM predictions to 
generate refined subtitles, enabling automatic construction of a 
large-scale Taigi speech –Chinese subtitle corpus from 
unlabeled videos.  
Experiments show that the AVLM reduces subtitle 
recognition CER from 36.8% to 9.3%. The resulting 860 -hour 
corpus improves downstream tasks: Whisper’s cross -lingual 
transcription CER decreases from 57.8% to 37.8%, and Taigi –
Chinese translation BLEU doubles from 0.2016 to 0.4033. 
These results demonstrate an effective and transferable 
paradigm for low-resource corpus construction.

multimodal corpus construction, semi -supervised learning, 
cross-lingual 
1. Introduction 
Taigi, an important native language in Taiwan, faces significant 
challenges in its preservation and technological development 
due to the long -term scarcity of digital resources. Although a 
large volume of Taigi audiovisual content exists online, it 
presents a unique challenge: the speech is in Taigi, while the 
subtitles embedded in the video frames are in Traditional 
Chinese (as shown in Fig. 1), and  no independent subtitle files 
are available. 
This cross -lingual speech –subtitle phenomenon 
complicates conventional automation. Automatic Speech 
Recognition (ASR) systems cannot directly address such cross-
lingual transcription tasks. Furthermore, existing Taigi corpora 
generally suffer from insufficient scale, limited domains, 
inconsistent quality, and high construction costs, which remain 
inadequate for training modern AI models. 
To overcome these challenges, this study presents an 
innovative framework that integrates multimodal learning with 
semi-supervised iterative training to automatically construct a 
high-quality corpus from large-scale video data. The core idea 
is to leverage Taigi speech as an auxiliary modality and employ 
a VLM to reconcile recognition outputs from OCR and AVLM 
hypotheses, thereby improving the recognition accuracy of 
hardcoded Chinese subtitles. 
The main contributions of this research can be summarized 
as: 
(1) Framework innovation: A novel framework that integrates  
a robust trimodal AVLM with a knowledge-distillation-like 
iterative process and VLM -based pseudo -label fusion to 
address cross -lingual hardcoded subtitle recognition in 
videos. 
(2) Corpus construction: A large -scale (860 hours), precisely 
aligned Taigi speech –Chinese subtitle corpus is 
automatically built using the proposed framework, reducing 
subtitle-recognition Character Error Rate  (CER) from 
36.8% to 9.3%. 
(3) Downstream impact: The constructed corpus enables two 
applications: (i) Taigi-to-Chinese speech transcription with 
Whisper Large-V2, lowering CER from 5 7.8% to 3 7.8%; 
and (ii) Taigi –Chinese translation with Qwen2.5 -14B, 
where BLEU doubles from 0.2016 to 0.4033. 
2. Related work  
This research aims to tackle the unique challenge of 
automatically constructing a "cross -lingual speech -subtitle" 
corpus from audio -visual media. Our technical framework is 
built upon advances in subtitle extraction, multimodal learning, 
and semi-supervised training.  
The work most closely related to our study is [1], which 
presents a preliminary exploration of  end-to-end multimodal 
subtitle recognition for Taiwanese TV programs using a 
bimodal VLM approach. While this prior study lays important 
groundwork for processing Taigi video subtitles, it is limited to 
the recognition of Taigi subtitles. In contrast, our work focuses 
on the recognition of Chinese subtitles, thereby addressing a 
different and more challenging cross-lingual setting. 
2.1. From OCR to VLM for Subtitle Extraction 
Traditional subtitle extraction in video heavily relies on Optical 
Character Recogniti on (OCR) technology [2]. Although 
modern OCR systems are mature in processing standard, clean 
images, their performance degrades significantly when 
bounding boxes are detected incorrectly, and they cannot 
compensate for semantic context.  
To address this, VLMs [3] have recently been applied to 
this task. While LLMs' contextual knowledge can correct some 
recognition errors, it introduces a new problem: when features 
cannot be extracted from the image, the model may over -rely 
on internal expertise and  generate "hallucinated" content that

2.2. Multimodal Learning for Audio -Visual 
Understanding 
To overcome the limitations of single -modality approaches, 
multimodal learning has proven effective in enhancing 
reliability under complex conditions. This  concept originates 
from the "McGurk Effect" in cognitive science, which reveals 
that human language comprehension is inherently a process of 
integrating auditory and visual information. 
Recent advancements, from CLIP  [5], which performs 
contrastive pre-training on language and images, to ImageBind 
[6], which embeds multiple modalities into a unified semantic 
space. Related work , such as Video -LLaMA [7], has begun 
integrating both audio and visual modalities into LLMs. 
Our research follows this trajectory but with a more focused 
goal: we use the audio modality as an independent source of 
validation to constrain and complement visual subtitle 
recognition, thereby addressing the complex challenges of real-
world video content. 
2.3. Semi-Supervised Learning for Corpus 
Construction 
In scenarios with scarce labeled data, learning from large 
amounts of unlabeled data is an effective strategy for building  
low-resource language corpora. Successful cases of automated 
corpus collection from platforms like YouTube already exist [8-
10]. Semi -supervised learning provides the theoretical 
foundation [11-12], with the core idea of iteratively discovering 
new knowledge from the model's own predictions. 
Our framework is a practical application of the Iterative 
Pseudo-Labeling (IPL) principles: we cross-validate the outputs 
from a vision -centric OCR model and an Audio -Visual-
Language Model (AVLM) that integrates acoustics and 
semantics. High-quality pseudo-labels are generated only when 
a strong consensus is reached  and then used to fine -tune the 
model in the next iteration, creating a bootstrapping 
optimization loop. 
3. Proposed method 
The overall workflow, illustrated in Fig. 2, consists of two parts. 
Part (a), shown on the left, describes the initial video-processing 
stage. Only a small number of videos with subtitle files can be 
collected as test data, while the majority without subtitle files 
undergo preprocessing. For these vi deos, subtitle text is 
generated using a conventional OCR model (PaddleOCR) [2]. 
All videos are then segmented by subtitle timing, producing a 
triplet for each subtitle instance: the  subtitle text, the subtitle -
bearing frame, and the aligned speech segment. 
Subtitle triplets from videos without subtitle files are 
subsequently fed into the iterative refinement framework shown 
in Fig. 2(b). The OCR-generated subtitle text serves as the first 
candidate pseudo-label. At the same time,  the subtitle-bearing 
images and audio segments are provided to the trimodal 
recognition model (AVLM), which generates a second 
candidate pseudo-label. 
Finally, a Vision–Language Model (VLM) is employed to 
ensure that high-quality pseudo-labels are produced only when 
a strong consensus is reached. These pseudo -labels are used to 
fine-tune the model in the next iteration, forming a 
bootstrapping optimization loop. Further methodological 
details are provided in the following sections. 
3.1. AVLM Architecture 
To address the limitations of traditional OCR (susceptible to 
visual noise) and Vision –Language Models (VLMs, prone to 
semantic hallucinations), a trimodal Audio -Visual-Language 
Model (AVLM) is proposed. The audio encoder is not used for 
direct transcription; instead, it provides complementary 
acoustic–semantic cues to the LLM decoder. 
The AVLM architecture, shown in Fig. 3, consists of: 
(1) Vision Encoder: SigLIP [15] is used to extract high -level 
visual features from input images. 
(2) Audio Encoder: Whisper Large-V2 [16], finetuned on Taigi 
speech data, is employed to extract acoustic features from 
audio waveforms. 
(3) LLM: Qwen2.5 -7B [17] is used for text encoding and 
multimodal decoding, as its tokenizer natively supports 
commonly used Taigi characters. 
(4) Multimodal Feature Fusion: An early-fusion strategy [18] 
is adopted by concatenating visual, audio, and text 
embeddings. Before concatenation, visual and audio 
features are projected into the embedding space using two-
layer GELU-MLP connectors following L LaVA [3]. This 
allows the LLM to jointly weigh information from vision, 
audio, and instructions within a unified semantic space, 
yielding more reliable judgments than any single modality. 
The AVLM is fine-tuned using the LoRA [19] technique. Both 
the vision and audio encoders are frozen, while only the vision 
connector, audio connector, and LLM decoder are fine -tuned. 
This strategy avoids the complexity of full -modality training

3.2. Semi-Supervised Iterative Pseudo -Label 
Refinement 
The core framework generates high-quality pseudo-labels from 
unlabeled data and improves model performance through 
knowledge-distillation-like iterative pseudo -label refinement, 
as follows: 
(1) Generation of Candidate Pairs: 
PaddleOCR and AVLM are applied to the same video 
segment to perform subtitle recognition, generating a pair of 
pseudo-labels (Chinese subtitle texts) as candidates for further 
processing. 
(2) CER-Based Filtering: 
The CER between the two candidate texts is computed. 
Only pairs with a CER below a predefined threshold (e.g., 0.25) 
are retained for the next step, indicating strong consensus and 
implying higher pseudo-label quality. 
(3) VLM-Based Fusion:  
Instead of directly selecting one of the two candidates, the 
VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is employed to fuse them into a single 
refined subtitle. Carefully designed prompts inform the VLM 
of the typical error patterns of each candidate (e.g., OCR may 
confuse visually simila r characters, while AVLM may 
hallucinate) and explicitly instruct it to reference the original 
image as the ground truth. This process yields higher -quality 
pseudo-labels and substantially improves the effectiveness of 
iterative training [21]. 
(4) Model Training and Iteration: 
The high -quality pseudo -labels are used to fine -tune the 
AVLM. This improved model can then extract more and better 
pseudo-labels from the same fixed data pool in the next 
iteration, creating a positive cycle of data quality and model 
performance improvement. 
3.3. Speech-Text Alignment 
After iterative refinement, a large number of (audio clip, 
pseudo-label) pairs are obtained. To mitigate boundary 
imprecision in the audio clips, forced speech –text alignment 
[22] is applied. This process yields a large -scale Taigi corpus 
with high-precision audio–text alignment. 
4. Experiments 
This section presents a quantitative evaluation of the proposed 
multimodal semi-supervised framework. The experiments are 
organized into three stages: (i) verification of the baseline 
performance and robustness of the trimodal AVLM, (ii) 
assessment of the effectiveness of the semi-supervised iterative 
optimization framework, and (iii) application of the resulting 
corpus to downstream tasks to demonstrate its practical utility. 
4.1. Datasets 
Two datasets are used for evaluation. The first is the PTS-Taigi 
dataset [1], employed for the initial training and performance 
validation of the AVLM. It contains 134.33 hours of training 
data and 12.32 hours of test data, sourced from Taigi programs 
of Taiwan’s Public Television Service (PTS). The dataset 
provides video frames with Chinese subtitles and their 
corresponding ground-truth text.  
The second dataset, a self-constructed benchmark called the 
Golden Set, is collected from eight Taigi videos from non-PTS 
channels with subtitle files across diverse genres (soap operas, 
historical dramas, and folk stories). It comprises approximately 
7.76 hours of Taigi speech and 13,086 Chinese subtitle 
sentences. Unlike the PTS -Taigi dataset, which focuses on 
controlled broadcast material, the Golden Set  captures more 
diverse and challenging real-world scenarios, making it a more 
reliable benchmark for assessing generalization and 
downstream task performance. 
4.2. Performance and Robustness of AVLM 
The core capabilities of the initial AVLM were first evaluated 
under supervised learning. As shown in Table 1, the proposed 
trimodal AVLM significantly outperforms the bimodal models 
on the PTS-Taigi test set for Chinese subtitle recognition. The 
AVLM achieved a CER of 7.46%, compared with 10.26% for 
the vision-only model (VLM) and 35.29% for the audio -only 
model (ALM), demonstrating the benefit of multimodal fusion.  
Furthermore, Table 2 shows that the AVLM exhibits much 
smaller performance degradation than the bimodal models 
when subjected to visual interference (Gaussian blur) or 
auditory interference (Gaussian noise) on the PTS-Taigi test set, 
confirming its robustness. 
4.3. Semi-Supervised Iterative Refinement 
Before initiating the iterative process, various refinement 
models and prompts were evaluated on the Golden Set  to 
determine how two pseudo -label candidates could be merged 
into an improved one. Different Qwen2.5 models (7B, 14B, 
VL-7B) were compared using  three prompts of increasing 
complexity: 
(1) Prompt #1 (Direct Merging): Instructs the model to merge 
two sentences into a single sentence. 
(2) Prompt #2  (Error Awareness): Extends Prompt 1 by 
informing the model of common error patterns in pseudo -
labels generated by OCR and AVLM. 
(3) Prompt #3  (Visual Judgment): Builds on Prompt 2 by 
explicitly directing the VLM to reference the source image 
when making its final decision. 
As shown in Table 3, prompt design plays a critical role in 
using an LLM (Qwen2.5) to fuse and refine pseudo -label 
candidates. A simple directive , such as Prompt 1, which only 
requests merging two sentences, produces poor results. 
In contrast, Prompt 2, which highlights the weaknesses of 
each candidate, significantly improves refinement, achieving an 
Error Reduction Rate (ERR) of 59% –69% across all models.

When Prompt 3 is applied, explicitly instructing Qwen2.5 -

After selecting the optimal refinement strategy, multiple 
iterations were conducted on a 500 -hour unlabeled video pool, 
with performance evaluated on the Golden Set. As shown in 
Table 4, the framework exhibits a clear positive feedback loop, 
progressively improving both data quality and model 
performance. 
(1) Round #0: Baseline and Fusion Results 
Baseline performance was first established using pseudo -
labels generated solely by PaddleOCR, yielding a CER of 
16.07% and representing the performance of a traditional OCR 
approach. In contrast, the initial AVLM, trained only on the 
PTS-Taigi dataset, per formed poorly on the more diverse 
Golden Set , with a CER of 36.8%, suggesting a  domain 
mismatch. 
The strength of the proposed framework is reflected in the 
Fusion-Refined results: by fusing the outputs of PaddleOCR 
and the initial AVLM, the refinement model produced pseudo-
labels with a CER of 15.8%, surpassing the accuracy of either 
method individually. Through this initial filtering and 
refinement, 212 k qualified sentences (156 hours, 31% of the 
500-hour pool) were extracted to serve as training data for the 
first iteration. 
(2) Round #1: First Iteration 
 Using the 212k high-quality pseudo-labels from the initial 
round, the AVLM was fine-tuned, and the CER on the Golden 
Set was reduced to 15.2% for the AVLM and to 11.9% for the 
Fusion-Refined variant. 
(3) Rounds #2-3: Convergence 
The improved model progressively extracted a larger 
quantity (434 hours, 87% of the unlabeled pool by Round 3) 
and a higher-quality (CER reduced to 9.4% by Round 3) set of 
pseudo-labels from the data pool. This created a continuous ly 
improving cycle, with the AVLM’s CER progressively 
decreasing and saturating at 9.3% in the third round. 
These results demonstrate a positive feedback loop between 
data quality and model performance, confirming the AVLM as 
a strong foundation for semi-supervised refinement. 
4.4. Downstream Applications 
The proposed approach was applied to an additional 500 hours 
of unlabeled video, yielding 426 hours of extracted data. 
Combined with the 434 -hour dataset reported in Table 4, this 
resulted in an 860-hour pseudo -labeled corpus. To assess its 
practical utility, this corpus was evaluated on two downstream 
tasks using the Golden Set described in Section 4.1. 
4.4.1. Cross-Lingual Speech Transcription 
The 860-hour corpus was used to further fine-tune the Whisper 
large-v2 model for Taigi speech -to-Chinese subtitle 
transcription. As shown in Table 5, this resulted in consistent 
and substantial improvements on the Taigi –Chinese 
transcription task: the CER decreased fro m 57.8% to 37.8%, 
and the BLEU score improved [23]. 
4.4.2. Cross-Lingual Machine Translation 
A parallel corpus of approximately 590k aligned sentence-pairs 
was constructed by transcribing Taigi speech from the 860-hour 
corpus using one of our Taigi ASR systems (CER: 15.3%) [24] 
and then aligning the resulting Taigi transcripts with the refined 
Chinese subtitles. This parallel corpus was used to fine-tune the 
Qwen2.5-14B LLM for Taigi–Chinese machine translation. As 
reported in Table 6, fine-tuning increased the BLEU score from 
0.2016 to 0.4033. 
Overall, these results demonstrate the practical value of the 
proposed framework: it enables scalable construct ion of high -
quality supervision for downstream cross -lingual ASR and 
machine translation. 
5. Conclusion 
This work introduces a multimodal semi-supervised framework 
for constructing a large -scale Taigi speech –Chinese subtitle 
corpus. By integrating audio, visual, and textual modalities, the 
framework establishes a robust trimodal AVLM and employs a 
knowledge-distillation-style process with VLM -guided fusion 
to iteratively combine and refine OCR and AVLM outputs into 
reliable pseudo -labels. This process reduced subtitle -
recognition CER from 36.8% to 9.3%, approaching the 7.5% 
achieved with full supervision. 
The iterative refinement process produced an 860 -hour, 
high-precision aligned corpus, providing a valuable resource 
for Taigi AI research. Downstream evaluations show ed 
substantial gains: Whisper’s cross -lingual transcription CER 
decreased from 57.8% to 37.8%, and Qwen2.5 -14B’s Taigi -
Chinese translation BLEU i mproved from 0.2016 to 0.4033. 
These results underscore the robustness of multimodal 
modeling and highlight LLM-driven multimodal fusion as a key 
enabler for scalable and transferable corpus construction for 
Taigi and other low-resource languages.
