Abstract
The Talking-to-Me (TTM) task aims to determine whether a
speaker is talking to the egocentric agent (i.e., the camera
wearer) in egocentric social interaction scenarios, which has
great potential for applications such as assistive robotics and
augmented reality. The task is challenging due to complex
and long-term semantic dependencies among natural conversa-
tions of multiple speakers. In this paper, we propose a mul-
timodal conversational context framework that jointly models
speaker-aware semantic dependencies of multi-round dialogues
and gaze-aware visual cues. The framework employs a speaker-
aware speech embedding module and a conversational context
modeling module to exploit contextual information of past and
current utterance with speaker identities and dialogue rounds.
Gaze-aware visual cues are also extracted from a Looking-
at-Me (LAM)-oriented visual backbone. Experiments on the
Ego4D TTM benchmark show that our approach significantly
outperforms existing baselines.
Index Terms: social interaction detection, speaker diarization,
multimodal analysis, egocentric video
1. Introduction
With the rapid development of wearable devices (e.g., GoPro
and Aria glass), it becomes popular to capture natural interac-
tions between multiple individuals. Enabling intelligent agents
to understand human interaction intentions in real-world social
scenarios has become increasingly important with great poten-
tial for applications such as human–computer interaction, assis-
tive robotics, and augmented reality [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]. Within
this context, the egocentric talking-to-me (TTM) [7] task has
emerged, aiming to determine whether the camera wearer is ad-
dressed by an interaction partner in a social interaction scenario.
Existing approaches to the TTM task can be broadly di-
vided into three categories. Audio-visual methods [8, 9, 10]
directly fuse speech and facial visual cues to infer whether a
speaker is addressing the camera wearer. However, these meth-
ods lack explicit modeling of conversational history, limiting
their ability to capture speaker intent across multi-round dia-
logues. Multi-task learning approaches [11, 12, 13] leverage re-
lated social perception tasks to enhance the TTM performance.
For example, EgoT2 [13] jointly optimizes the TTM task with
Looking-at-Me (LAM) and active speaker detection in a uni-
fied framework. Recently, context-aware methods such as SIC-
Net [14] combine short-term textual conversational context us-
ing pretrained language models with visual social cues for the

TTM task. However, they typically rely on coarse identity rep-
resentations (i.e., camera wearer or not) and limited temporal
scope, making it difficult to model semantic dependencies and
explicit speaker roles across long-range conversations. Further-
more, in unconstrained egocentric social interactions, interac-
tion partners frequently move off-screen or temporarily look
away (e.g., looking down at objects during tasks), causing a se-
vere absence of direct visual behaviors like gaze.
To address these challenges, we propose a multimodal con-
versational context framework for the TTM task which jointly
models speaker-aware semantic dependencies of multi-round
dialogues and gaze-aware visual cues. Natural conversations
in egocentric videos involve complex semantic dependencies
among multiple speakers that are frequently off-screen. Mul-
timodal input with semantic context modeling allows the model
to identify dialogue flow even when visual cues are missing. To
model speaker-aware semantic dependencies, we explicitly em-
bed and store the conversational contextual information com-
posed by utterance-level transcriptions, fine-grained speaker
identities and dialogue rounds. Current and historical contex-
tual information are utilized together with a Transformer en-
coder to exploit the long-range semantic dependencies of multi-
round dialogues. To model the gaze-aware visual cues, we
incorporate latent visual features from a pre-trained backbone
learnt with the LAM [15] task, enabling the model to leverage
gaze-aware visual cues for more robust TTM prediction.
The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
•We propose a multimodal conversational context framework
for the TTM task that effectively integrates conversational
context of multi-round dialogues and gaze-aware visual cues;
•We introduce ID-aware speaker embedding to explicitly
model speaker identities in multi-party conversations;
•The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance
on the public Ego4D TTM.

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset and Experimental Setup
3.1.1. Dataset and Evaluation Metric
Our TTM dataset is derived from the Social Interaction bench-
mark of Ego4D [7]. The Social Interaction subset includes
389 training clips (32.4 hours), 50 validation clips (4.2 hours),
and 133 test clips (11.1 hours). As the test split is not pub-
licly available, we report all results on the validation set. Each
non–camera wearer speech segment is annotated with a binary
TTM label indicating whether it is addressed to the egocentric
agent. After preprocessing, the dataset comprises 26,691 train-
ing samples and 2,469 validation samples. All methods are eval-
uated using the evaluation metric of mean Average Precision
(mAP), following the evaluation protocol of the official TTM
benchmark [9].
3.1.2. Experimental Setup
Model Configuration. Our model jointly leverages audio-
based textual cues and visual information for TTM predic-
tion. For the audio branch, we use Whisper [16] for tran-
scription, CAM++ [19] for speaker embedding extraction, and
RoBERTa [17] for textual representation learning. For the
visual branch, we adopt the LAM [15] baseline model with
ResNet-18 [21] as the visual encoder. On top of these pre-
trained backbones, we employ an utterance-level Transformer
encoder [20] to model contextual dependencies across dialogue
turns, followed by a self-attention layer to integrate gaze-aware
visual features. The fused representation is finally fed into a
two-layer MLP to produce the TTM probability.
Training Configuration. The pretrained components, includ-
ing Whisper, CAM++, and the LAM model, are strictly frozen
during training. We first fine-tune the last two layers, Layer-
Norm layers, and the pooler layer of the pretrained RoBERTa
model for 5 epochs using a newly initialized classification head.
Subsequently, the linear projection layer of speech embedding
fsse, the utterance-level Transformer encoder, the linear pro-
jection layer of visual embeddingf gve, the multimodal fusion
module, and the TTM prediction headf ttm are trained for 10
epochs, using the AdamW optimizer [22] with a learning rate
of10 −5. All fine-tuning and training procedures are conducted
using cross-entropy loss. All experiments were conducted on a
single NVIDIA RTX 4090.

3.4. Qualitative Result
Figure 3 illustrates qualitative results of our TTM prediction
model in a multi-speaker scenario. The three frames are sam-
pled from the same video and depict different interaction states.
In the first frame, the camera wearer initiates the conversation,
establishing dialogues with other participants. In the second
frame, the participant with ID=1responds while clearly direct-
ing the gaze toward the camera wearer, and the model assigns
a high TTM score, correctly predicting that the utterance is ad-
dressed to the camera wearer. In contrast, the third frame shows
the same active speaker who has moved the attention away from
the camera wearer toward another participant, and the model
predicts a low TTM score. The examples demonstrate that ac-
curate TTM prediction is achieved by jointly modeling speaker
identity, conversational context, and gaze-aware visual cues.
However, our model occasionally fails in face-missing and se-
mantically vague scenarios, such as when a player looks down
at cards and talks to themselves, where missing gaze cues and
ambiguous intent together lead to false predictions.
4. Conclusion
This paper investigates the Talking-to-Me (TTM) task in ego-
centric multi-speaker scenarios, aiming to determine whether
a speaker is addressing the egocentric agent. We propose a
multimodal conversational context framework that jointly mod-
els speaker-aware semantic dependencies of multi-round dia-
logues and gaze-aware visual cues from the Looking-at-Me task
for enhanced social perception. Experiments on the Ego4D
TTM benchmark demonstrate that our method consistently out-
performs existing baselines by a large margin. Nevertheless,
there is still space of improvement for the TTM task. Fu-
ture work may explore large-scale multimodal language models
(e.g., LLaV A [24, 25, 26] and GPT-4V [27]) to enhance contex-
tual and intention understanding.
