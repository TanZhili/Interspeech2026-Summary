Abstract
Speech-driven facial generation typically relies on 2D synthe-
sis, limiting realism and applicability. We propose ES-3DF, the
first framework to reconstruct editable, textured 3D faces di-
rectly from speech. ES-3DF employs a Disentangle module that
decouples facial features into 3DMM geometry and UV texture
maps to enable fine-grained editing. To bridge the modality gap,
we introduce a Dual-Branch Alignment module enhanced by a
Class-Aware Multi-Slot Memory Bank. By leveraging a novel
Multi-Slot InfoNCE loss, this mechanism dynamically aligns
speech embeddings with diverse, evolving speaker prototypes,
significantly improving identity preservation in the latent space.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that ES-3DF outperforms
state-of-the-art baselines in geometric accuracy, identity preser-
vation, and visual quality, underscoring its potential for realistic
3D avatar generation.

1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of cross-modal generation has spurred
interest in leveraging speech as a key input for facial synthesis,
addressing emerging needs in interactive and immersive tech-
nologies. At its core, it is a cross-modal generation task, where
faces are generated from raw speech features or aligned mul-
timodal features, either as direct inputs or as generator condi-
tions. Early approaches [1, 2] relied on simple GAN or en-
coder–decoder structures [3, 4], often yielding blurry images.
Recent works leverage advanced face generators or diffusion
models, such as [5], which aligns the latent space of StyleGAN2
[6], and [7, 8, 9], which employ latent diffusion to synthesize
high-quality faces.
Despite recent progress, most existing methods remain con-
fined to 2D synthesis, inherently lacking explicit 3D representa-
tions. As a result, generated faces often fail to maintain geomet-
ric consistency across viewpoints and cannot support precise
control over facial shape or expression. Cascaded approaches
such as [10] attempt to first generate 2D images from speech
and then reconstruct 3D geometry, but errors in the 2D stage
inevitably propagate into the 3D reconstruction. Similarly, edit-
ing methods [11, 5] introduce feature-level controls, yet remain
coarse and incapable of fine-grained manipulation of geometry

tual avatar animation or personalized face modeling.
To address these limitations, we propose ES-3DF, the
first framework to reconstruct textured 3D faces directly from
speech signals, effectively eliminating the noise accumulation
and error propagation inherent in cascaded pipelines. To further
bridge the inherent modality gap and complex identity repre-
sentation in direct mapping, ES-3DF incorporates a novel Align
module enhanced by a Multi-Slot InfoNCE strategy. Instead of
relying on a single prototype or simple regression, we construct
a Class-Aware Memory Bank that stores multiple latent texture
prototypes for each speaker, updated via an Exponential Mov-
ing Average (EMA). This design forces the model to learn a
discriminative joint embedding space, ensuring robust identity
preservation. Simultaneously, a Disentangle module separates
3D facial features into UV maps and 3DMM coefficients, en-
abling geometric accuracy and controllable editability.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows: (1) We
propose ES-3DF, a unified framework for high-quality 3D face
reconstruction directly from speech, eliminating the error prop-
agation of cascaded methods. (2) We introduce a Multi-Slot
InfoNCE strategy with a dynamic memory bank to bridge the
speech-texture modality gap, significantly enhancing speaker
identity preservation. (3) We achieve fine-grained editing via
explicit geometry-texture disentanglement. Extensive experi-
ments demonstrate clear advantages over state-of-the-art base-
lines in geometric accuracy, identity preservation, and editabil-
ity.
2. Methods
Our goal is to reconstruct a 3D face of a speaker, using only
a short speech signal as input, while ensuring both precise ge-
ometry and high-quality textures. To this end, we propose a
framework consisting of two modules (Fig. 1): Disentangle and
Align. The Disentangle module decomposes 3DDFA V3 [12]
into a feature extractorE f and a geometry generatorG α, and

together disentangle geometry and texture from the unified 3D
featuresF 3D. The Align module employs a dual-branch net-
work to map speech featuresF s extracted by ECAPA-TDNN
[13] into disentangled 3DMM shape coefficients and texture
features. Crucially, to bridge the modality gap, we introduce a
Class-Aware Multi-Slot Memory Bank, which leverages multi-
slot contrastive learning to align speech embeddings with di-

2.2. Geometry–Texture Disentanglement
To enable speech-driven 3D face generation with improved ge-
ometric consistency and texture fidelity, we introduce a geom-
etry–texture disentanglement module to replace the 2D gener-
ators commonly used in prior methods. Specifically, the pre-
trained 3DDFA V3 [12] extracts 3D facial features and, through
its geometry branchG α, generates 3DMM coefficients that en-
sure accurate reconstruction and enable fine-grained, control-
lable editing of facial shape and expression. In parallel, the
StyleGAN2-based [6] UV generator focuses on high-quality
texture synthesis, enhancing photorealism in the reconstructed
faces. Crucially, explicitly decoupling the facial geometry (rep-
resented byαid) from texture features facilitates the subsequent
cross-modal alignment phase, allowing for the adoption of dis-
tinct alignment strategies tailored to the inherent complexity of
each generation task. To train the UV generator effectively, we

2.3. Speech-3D Feature Alignment
In the speech-driven reconstruction phase, we decouple the gen-
eration of 3DMM identity coefficients (α id) and facial texture
features (Ftex). Given the low dimensionality ofα id∈R 80,

baselines.

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset
In this work, V oxCeleb1[16] is used for voice, VGGFace[17] is
used for facial images, and the intersection of the two speakers
is taken, with a total of 1225 people. A total of 924 speak-
ers with the initials F-Z were selected as the training set, while
the validation and testing sets consisted of the remaining 301
speakers.
3.2. Data Processing and Training
For speech, 80-dimensional Mel filterbank features are ex-
tracted from 16kHz audio with a 25ms window and 10ms shift.

3DF exhibits superior visual fidelity and preservation of speaker
attributes (e.g., facial shape and age) compared to both base-

3.5. Correlation Between Speech and Facial Anatomy
Biologically, acoustic features are heavily modulated by res-
onant cavities and articulators, as corroborated by our spatial
metrics on neutral geometry (Table 2). The nose’s high IoU
(84.55%) aligns with its role as a stable primary acoustic res-
onator. Conversely, while lip regions show substantial improve-
ments, their relatively lower absolute IoU (54-55%) reflects the
inherent ill-posedness of inferring static, resting lip morphol-
ogy solely from dynamic speech signals. The high skin IoU
(87.94%) captures the underlying stable jaw structure, and up-
per face regions (60-67%) correlate with speech prosody. This
confirms our mechanism captures true biological speech-face
mappings rather than spurious statistical correlations.

3.6. Ablation studies
Ablation on Disentanglement.To evaluate the specific contri-
bution of geometry–texture disentanglement, we compare our
full framework with a variant that shares the same 3D recon-
struction features but bypasses mesh rendering by directly gen-
erating images (denoted as Ours w/o 3D). Qualitative results in
Fig. 2 indicate that lacking explicit disentanglement compro-
mises facial geometry. Quantitatively (Tables 1 and 2), Ours
w/o 3D consistently lags behind ES-3DF and occasionally even
the baseline. This ablation validates the central hypothesis of
our work: the key to accurate speech-driven 3D reconstruction
lies not only in leveraging 3D reconstruction features, but also
in disentangling geometry and texture into separate generation
pathways. By enforcing this separation, our architecture max-
imally exploits the geometric cues encoded in speech-aligned
features, thereby yielding reconstructions that are both struc-
turally faithful and visually coherent.
Ablation on Multi-Slot Contrast.Qualitatively, the vari-
ant without contrastive learning (Ours w/o ctr) fails to capture
fine-grained identity attributes, such as age and ethnicity, as ef-
fectively as the full ES-3DF. Quantitatively, as shown in Ta-
bles 3, the full model achieves superior feature similarity com-
pared to the ablation counterpart. These results confirm that the
multi-slot contrastive strategy is crucial for mining robust iden-
tity cues from speech signals, thereby significantly enhancing
the discriminative power of the generated 3D features.
3.7. Controllability and Applications.
A key advantage of ES-3DF is its support for fine-grained, se-
mantically interpretable editing. By parameterizing geometry
with 3DMM coefficientsα, our framework enables flexible ad-
justments to shape, expression, pose, and translation while pre-
serving texture. Qualitatively, Fig. 2 demonstrates precise geo-
metric modifications without compromising identity, and Fig. 3
illustrates robust multi-view rendering capabilities. Quantita-
tively, ”Ours w/GTα” achieves higher feature consistency than
the standard ”Ours” (Tables 3), confirming the effectiveness of
our editing mechanism. These capabilities stem from the ex-
plicit geometry–texture disentanglement, ensuring stable tex-
ture under geometric edits and offering strong potential for per-
sonalized virtual avatar interactions.
4. Conclusion
In this work, we present ES-3DF, a novel framework for di-
rect speech-driven 3D face reconstruction, comprising a Disen-
tangle module and an Align module. The Disentangle module
separates geometry and texture to ensure geometric accuracy,
texture quality, and controllable editability, while the Align
module leverages multi-slot contrastive learning to fully cap-
ture identity information from speech, mapping it to 3D fa-
cial features for robust identity preservation. ES-3DF outper-
forms state-of-the-art baselines in these metrics. Experiments
validate superior facial shape consistency, feature retention, en-
hanced by fine-grained editing. Multi-view rendering under-
scores its potential for virtual avatar animation. To further en-
hance speaker discriminability, future work will further opti-
mize the cross-modality alignment of high-dimensional identity
features to more comprehensively extract facial detail attributes
from speech features.
