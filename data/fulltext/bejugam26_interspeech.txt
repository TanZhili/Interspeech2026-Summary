Abstract
Most deep learning based speech enhancement methods are
usually trained in a supervised fashion, i.e., they typically rely
on parallel corpora of noisy and clean speech pairs. This is
often difficult to obtain in real-world scenarios, leading to the
use of synthetic data. In this work, we propose a novel un-
supervised speech enhancement method that does not require
paired training data. We introduce a multi-discriminator GAN-
based architecture to capture global (or utterance-level) and lo-
cal (or frame-level) characteristics of the speech signal. Addi-
tionally, we incorporate self-supervised representations from a
pre-trained model to provide auxiliary information to the gen-
erator and thereby enhance the denoising capability. Our exten-
sive experimental results on the V oiceBank+DEMAND dataset
demonstrate that the proposed method achieves comparable or
better performance across both intrusive and non-intrusive qual-
ity measures.
Index Terms: speech enhancement, unsupervised learning,
generative adversarial networks, self-supervised representations
1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) aims to improve the quality and in-
telligibility of speech signals degraded by background noise [1].
It plays a critical role in a wide range of applications, including
robust automatic speech recognition (ASR), hearing aids, mo-
bile communication, teleconferencing, and assistive technolo-
gies [2, 3]. Over the past decade, SE has become an essential
component for building reliable and user-friendly speech-driven
systems.
In recent years, the advent of deep learning has signifi-
cantly advanced the field of SE, with supervised deep neural
network (DNN)-based methods achieving state-of-the-art per-
formance across multiple benchmarks [4, 5, 6, 7]. These ap-
proaches typically rely on noisy and clean speech pairs to learn
nonlinear mappings in the time or time-frequency domain. Al-
though these models have demonstrated remarkable improve-
ments in both perceptual quality and intelligibility, they heavily
rely on the availability of parallel training datasets, which are
often difficult or even impossible to obtain in real-world scenar-
ios. Collecting clean speech in the same acoustic environment
as noisy data is impractical, leading to synthetic data generation.
This mismatch between training assumptions and deployment
limits the scalability and applicability of supervised models.
In the absence of parallel training data, statistical signal
processing techniques such as Wiener filtering [8], spectral sub-
traction [9], and minimum mean-square error (MMSE)-based
estimators [10] can be used as alternatives. These methods ex-
ploit the statistical properties of speech and noise to derive ana-
lytical estimators for the clean signal. For instance, the Wiener
filter computes an optimal linear filter under a mean-square er-
ror criterion between the estimated and desired clean signal, as-
suming stationary power spectral densities for the signal and
additive noise. MMSE-based approaches derive nonlinear gain
functions via Bayesian inference, typically under Gaussian or
conditionally Gaussian priors, to minimize conditional MSE.
Although computationally efficient, relying on simplified dis-
tributional assumptions often results in degraded performance
under nonstationary noise and in complex acoustic environ-
ments [11].
Motivated by the limitations of supervised deep learning
and classical statistical approaches, research has increasingly
focused on self-supervised and unsupervised speech enhance-
ment methods that eliminate the need for parallel training data.
Noisy-target training (NyTT) [12] augments noisy speech with
additional noise to form training pairs and simulate a super-
vised setting, but the effectiveness is restricted to in-domain
noise conditions. RemixIT [13] adopts a teacher–student frame-
work, where a supervised teacher model generates pseudo-clean
targets that are remixed with noise to train a student network.
However, the quality of the student model remains heavily
dependent on the reliability of the teacher’s outputs. QMix-
CAT [14] addresses the limitations of RemixIT and NyTT by
generating three distinct types of supervised training data from
noisy and teacher-predicted enhanced mixtures to learn a di-
verse range of background acoustic characteristics. In addition
to the diverse datasets, it dynamically exchanges the parameters
of teacher and student models during training to improve the
mixing process and final performance.
Recently, Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)-based
metric learning approaches have also gained more traction,
most notably MetricGAN-U [15] and its variants [16]. These
models extend the original MetricGAN[17] framework to an
unsupervised setting by replacing estimation of intrusive qual-
ity measures (eg: PESQ) with non-intrusive counterparts (eg:
DNSMOS). Beyond metric-based GANs, DOTN [18] and
UnSE [19] rely on Wasserstein GANs (WGANs) [20], the dual
interpretation of Optimal Theory (OT), to transform from the
noisy probability distribution to the clean distribution. DOTN
uses OT to align the joint distributions of inputs and labels be-
tween a supervised source domain and an unlabeled target do-
main, with a discriminator that constrains the transport. Al-
ternatively, UnSE aligns the distribution of enhanced speech
with clean speech via a WGAN-based dual OT formulation, and
an additional loss inspired by primal OT to minimise sample-
wise deviation. Although these frameworks outperform tradi-
tional statistical enhancement methods, the results are not as ef-
fective when compared to state-of-the-art unsupervised frame-
works such as QMixCAT, especially on perceptual metrics such
as PESQ.

Motivated by this observation, we explore strategies to
strengthen GAN-based enhancement by (i) augmenting addi-
tional adversarial losses, and (ii) enriching the information pro-
vided to the generator.
Considering (i), one strategy for the discriminator is to as-
sign a single output to an entire speech utterance. This is a
widely acknowledged approach as seen in [15], where the dis-
criminator replicates the non-intrusive DNSMOS metric, and
in [19], where the discriminator outputs a “distance” between
two probability distributions that is used to guide the genera-
tor. However, global representation can result in slower conver-
gence, and the weights learned are often insufficient to capture
local features. We propose an alternate strategy to assign an
output corresponding to local “chunks” throughout the signal.
However, if the discriminator classifies an individual chunk as
noisy with high confidence, the generator may over-suppress the
speech content, thereby silencing the entire chunk. To mitigate
this issue, it may be useful to incorporate information about the
utterance’s global structure.
In this work, we introduce a novel multi-discriminator
framework for unsupervised speech enhancement. The archi-
tecture incorporates an utterance-level discriminator to capture
global characteristics and a frame-level discriminator to provide
fine-grained temporal guidance. Each discriminator assigns a
probabilistic score within the range[0,1]where0indicates a
fake (or noisy) sample and1denotes a real (or clean) sample.
This enables enhancement without relying on any specific met-
ric.
In unsupervised GAN-based models, the generator oper-
ates with limited information since no clean reference signals
are available during training. This becomes particularly pro-
nounced under high noise conditions, where extracting speech
features from the signals proves difficult due to the dominance
of noise. To better capture long-term contextual dependencies
(ii), we propose augmenting the generator with representations
from a pre-trained self-supervised model. These models are
trained on large amounts of data and yield robust, generalised
representations that are expected to complement the generator.
Our framework operates predominantly in the time-
frequency domain, which proves advantageous as the generator
utilises pre-trained features (typically learned in spectral sub-
spaces), and the discriminators can generate an output corre-
sponding to each time-frame or for the entire utterance with ap-
propriate architectural modifications. The time-domain wave-
forms are converted to time-frequency representations via the
Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) operation.
A detailed description of the proposed framework is pro-
vided in Section 2, followed by the experimental setup, results,
and ablation studies in Section 3, with concluding remarks and
future directions presented in Section 4.
2. Proposed Methodology
Consider an additive noise model. The observed noisy speech
x[n]is represented as
x[n] =s[n] +g[n](1)
wherenis the discrete-time index, ands[n]andg[n]are clean
speech and background noise, respectively. Supervised learning
aims to learn a mapping fromx[n]tos[n]by minimizing an
objective function with the correspondings[n](parallel clean
speech).
In real-world scenarios, we do not have access to the under-
lying parallel clean speech. We propose a novel unsupervised
method to enhance noisy speech by learning the clean speech
distribution using generative adversarial networks (GANs). Fig-
ure 1 illustrates the proposed enhancement training method. In
this section, we describe the two main components: (i) Genera-
tor (G) and (ii) Discriminator (D), and (iii) the Loss Functions,
in detail.
2.1. Generator
Our generatorGtakes noisy speech signals as input and pro-
duces enhanced speech signals as output. We adopt the Neural
Time-Varying Filtering (NTVF) network [5] as our enhance-
ment block, as it demonstrates strong performance with low
computational complexity in the supervised setting. NTVF pro-
gressively refines the log-magnitude spectrogram of a speech
signal through a stack ofNidentical blocks, each compris-
ing two key components: a context-independent filtering (CIF)
block and a context-dependent filtering (CDF) block. The CIF
block consists of two dense layers with residual connections
and swish activations, while the CDF block employs convolu-
tional layers along with an adaptive multi-resolution filtering
strategy to dynamically select the context window based on in-
put features. A final dense layer is used to estimate a magnitude
mask, which is multiplied by the noisy magnitude spectrum to
approximate the clean magnitude. The enhanced time-domain
speech signal is reconstructed by applying the Inverse Short-
Time Fourier Transform (ISTFT) with the noisy phase.
To enhance the long-term contextual information of noisy
samples available to the generator, we supply representations
from the pre-trained Autoregressive Predictive Coding (APC)

2.2. Discriminator
The discriminatorDproduces meaningful feedback to the gen-
erator by distinguishing clean samples from noisy and enhanced
samples. This guides the generator’s training to fool the dis-
criminator.
We use two discriminators: (i) an Utterance-Level (UL)
DiscriminatorD u, which generates a probability score for the
entire spectrogram, and (ii) the proposed Frame-Level (FL) Dis-
criminatorD f , which outputs a score for each time-frame of the
spectrogram.
We adopt the discriminator used in CMGAN [22] for our
model. The Utterance Level Discriminator consists of four con-
volutional layers that are each followed by an instance norm and
PReLU activation. Post the convolutional layers, an adaptive
max pooling is performed over time, and the output is flattened
to pass through two linear layers. A PReLU activation follows
the first linear layer, whereas a sigmoid activation follows the
second layer to represent a score between0and1correspond-
ing to the entire spectrogram.
For the Frame Level Discriminator, the stride in the con-
volutional blocks is modified, and the max pooling operation is
skipped to generate a score for each time frame. The rest of the
architecture is identical to the Utterance Level Discriminator.
2.3. Loss Functions
The training framework primarily relies on adversarial loss
functions of Least-Squares GAN (LSGAN)[23] to promote sta-
ble training. Both the discriminators are trained to classify clean
inputs as real (target1) and noisy or enhanced inputs as fake
(target0) via Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.

3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Dataset
The proposed method is trained and evaluated on the publicly
available V oiceBank+DEMAND dataset [24]. Clean utterances
from28speakers are sourced from the V oiceBank corpus [25]
and artificially corrupted by mixing with10diverse noise types
from the DEMAND dataset [26] at four distinct signal-to-noise
ratio (SNR) levels —0dB,5dB,10dB, and15dB to create
the noisy traning data.
The noisy utterances are shuffled to generate non-parallel
training data for the discriminator.
For evaluation, a separate noisy test set is generated by mix-
ing utterances from two previously unseen speakers from the
V oiceBank corpus with five unseen noise types from the DE-
MAND dataset at SNR levels of2.5dB,7.5dB,12.5dB, and
17.5dB. All the utterances are downsampled from48kHz to
16kHz for training and testing.

3.3. Experimental Configuration
The number of NTVF blocks (N) in the generator was set to1.
The STFT operation was performed with a Hamming window
of window length25ms, hop length of10ms, and an FFT size
of512.
We employed the AdamW optimiser with an initial learn-
ing rate of0.001for all trainable parameters. The learning rate
decayed by a factor of0.5when the loss did not improve in
10consecutive epochs. All models were trained on a single
NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 Ti GPU with a batch size of32
and early stopping using a patience of30epochs.
3.4. Results
In our experiments, we benchmark our proposed method against
a diverse set of statistical filters and unsupervised deep neu-
ral network (DNN)-based approaches. The statistical base-
lines encompass classical MMSE and Wiener filtering algo-
rithms, while the unsupervised DNN models include NyTT,
MetricGAN-U, RemixIT, DOTN, UnSE, QMixCAT, and UFL-
GAN. Table 1 reports the performance of all methods on the
V oiceBank+DEMAND test set. For each baseline, we quote the
results as reported in the papers.
Table 1 demonstrates that all frameworks achieve speech
enhancement relative to unprocessed noisy inputs. However,
the effectiveness of each architecture largely varies. The re-
sults obtained using NTVF in the supervised setting justify our
choice of employing it in the unsupervised setting.
The statistical methods achieve moderate MOS scores but
perform poorly on PESQ when compared to the DNN-based
approaches, resulting in inadequate enhancement. Among the
no-reference frameworks, NyTT and RemixIT deliver moder-
ate results across metrics, with RemixIT outperforming NyTT.
QMixCAT however, surpasses them across all metrics, emerg-
ing as the state-of-the-art. In the models incorporating non-
parallel clean samples, UnSE and UFL-GAN demonstrate supe-
rior information extraction and utilisation over DOTN, leading
to improved performance. DOTN performs moderately only on
the SI-SNR metric. Notably, several models including QMix-
CAT, UnSE, RemixIT, and UFL-GAN surpass MetricGAN-U
in terms of DNSMOS, despite the latter being explicitly trained
to optimise this metric. The score reported for MetricGAN-U
(full) -3.16[15], also falls short of these models.
Our model outperforms all baseline models on the eSTOI,
CSIG, CBAK, and COVL metrics. It achieves a similar PESQ
and DNSMOS score to the front-running model, QMixCAT, and
a similar SI-SNR to the front-runner, UnSE. This demonstrates
a strong improvement over current unsupervised DNN-based
approaches, especially the GAN-based frameworks, achieving
a well-balanced performance across both intrusive and non-
intrusive metrics.2
3.5. Ablation Study
To assess the contribution of each component within our pro-
posed architecture, we conducted a comprehensive ablation
study that systematically added the key features. We first trained
the GAN-based framework in a single discriminator setting with
no auxiliary APC features. Subsequently, we combined the
utterance-level and frame-level discriminators and introduced
the contextual APC features as auxiliary inputs to the generator.
Table 2 reveals that the baseline FL-GAN marginally out-
performed the UL-GAN across all evaluation metrics. This
trend persisted across the subsequent model configurations.
The UFL-GAN combined both utterance and frame-level dis-
criminators, yielding further enhancements in PESQ and MOS
scores, but this came at the cost of a lower SI-SNR when com-
pared with the FL-GAN. Upon introducing contextual Autore-
gressive Predictive Coding (APC) representations as auxiliary
inputs to the generator, each single-discriminator variant sur-
passed its respective baseline, demonstrating the improved ca-
pability of the generator. The UFL-GAN outperformed both
the single-discriminator models across all metrics in this setting
again, further justifying that these representations provide rich
information to the generator.
4. Conclusion
In this work, we introduced a novel multi-discriminator GAN-
based framework for unsupervised speech enhancement that
provides reliable guidance to the generator, with an utterance-
level discriminator capturing global characteristics and a frame-
level discriminator offering fine-grained temporal guidance.
Furthermore, the ablation study highlights that incorporating
self-supervised features as auxiliary information substantially
reduces signal distortions and enhances overall speech qual-
ity. Experimental evaluations across both intrusive and non-
intrusive metrics demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach.
As part of our future work, we plan to extend this framework
to address additional distortions, such as reverberation and clip-
ping.
