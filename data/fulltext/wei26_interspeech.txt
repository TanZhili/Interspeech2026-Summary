Database

Abstract
Upper airway diseases can affect speech by altering nasal air-
flow and nasal-oral resonance coupling. However, most ex-
isting studies rely on general acoustic features and lack sys-
tematic analyses of vowel nasalization directly related to nasal
function. Using the CUCO database, this study analyzes vowel
nasalization in patients undergoing septoplasty, endoscopic si-
nus surgery, or tonsillectomy, and in healthy controls, at pre-
and postoperative time points. Two nasalization measures, A1–
P0 and A1–P1, are examined across pathology type, temporal
progression, and vowel category. Results show A1–P0 is more
sensitive to pathology differences, and nasalization is vowel de-
pendent, with /u/ most sensitive to resonance changes. Only the
tonsillectomy group exhibits transient early postoperative nasal-
ization changes; no long-term differences occur in other groups.
These findings support the value of vowel nasalization measures
for speech analysis in upper airway diseases.
Index Terms: A1–P0; nasal–oral coupling; acoustic measures;
clinical speech analysis
1. Introduction
Upper airway tract chronic diseases (such as allergic rhinitis
or chronic rhinosinusitis) pose global public health challenges.
Statistically, the prevalence of allergic rhinitis ranges from 10%
to 30% worldwide, while chronic rhinosinusitis affects approx-
imately 5%–12% of the population [1, 2]. By altering the struc-
ture and ventilation conditions of the nasal and pharyngeal cav-
ities, these diseases may negatively affect patients’ vocal reso-
nance characteristics and voice quality, subsequently leading to
adverse impacts on social interaction, mental health, and occu-
pational performance [3, 4, 5, 6]. Clinically, surgical interven-
tions such as functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS), sep-
toplasty, or tonsillectomy are commonly employed to restore
upper airway patency [7]. These interventions, by adjusting the
anatomical structure of the vocal tract, not only improve venti-
lation but also influence patients’ objective and subjective vocal
characteristics [8].
To quantify these vocal changes, existing studies pre-
dominantly describe acoustic differences between patients and
healthy speakers, commonly using analytical measures includ-
ing fundamental frequency (F0), formant parameters (F1, F2),
and jitter and shimmer reflecting phonatory stability [9, 10].
While these measures are widely adopted in speech pathology,
their effectiveness in upper airway disease research remains
controversial. For instance, Acar et al.’s study of 43 patients
with nasal polyposis showed no significant differences in F0,
jitter, shimmer, and NHR at 6 weeks post-surgery compared

findings generally reflect insufficient sensitivity of traditional
acoustic measures in capturing voice changes associated with
upper airway diseases. A plausible explanation is that these
measures primarily reflect vocal fold vibration characteristics
or overall vocal tract configuration differences. They do not di-
rectly address the core physiological features of upper airway
diseases—nasal airflow restriction or changes in nasal-oral res-
onance coupling. This limitation has constrained deeper devel-
opment in this research field [13].
In contrast, vowel nasalization theoretically exhibits more
direct correspondence with upper airway structural changes
[14]. V owel nasalization reflects the degree of nasal cavity par-
ticipation during vowel production, with its acoustic manifesta-
tion jointly influenced by velopharyngeal closure status, nasal
patency, and nasal-oral acoustic coupling patterns [15, 16]. In
phonetic research, nasalization has established a mature acous-
tic quantification framework, with the most commonly used
methods being amplitude-based measures such as A1–P0 and
A1–P1 proposed by Chen [17]. These measures continuously
quantify vowel nasalization degree by comparing the ampli-
tude relationship between the first formant (A1) and nasal-
related spectral peaks (P0 or P1), sensitively reflecting the en-
hancement of nasal coupling. These features have been ap-
plied to study coarticulation effects, cross-linguistic compar-
isons, speaker variation, and prosodic influences [18, 19]. For
example, Styler validated the effectiveness of A1–P0 in dis-
tinguishing oral and nasalized vowels in English and French,
while Carignan compared temporal changes in vowel nasaliza-
tion across multiple speakers using A1–P0 and A1–P1, demon-
strating good interpretability and stability [20, 18].
However, despite established measurement methods for
vowel nasalization in phonetics, its systematic application in
upper airway diseases remains limited. Existing research ex-
hibits three critical gaps: First, most work continues to focus
on general acoustic features, rarely emphasizing nasalization
measures directly related to nasal ventilation, particularly lack-
ing comparative studies of different pathological types within a
unified acoustic framework. Second, while upper airway dis-
eases often involve surgical intervention that may alter vocal
tract structure and ventilation conditions, longitudinal studies
covering pre-surgery and multiple post-surgery time points with
continuous quantification of nasalization remain scarce. Third,
different vowels vary in tongue position, lip shape, and vocal
tract configuration, potentially affecting how nasal participation
influences the acoustic signal, yet systematic evidence regard-
ing vowel-dependent nasalization effects and the existence of

Based on these gaps, this study systematically analyzes
vowel nasalization in continuous speech using a publicly avail-
able speech database containing healthy controls and multi-
ple upper airway surgery patient groups, covering pre-surgery
and multiple post-surgery time points. Focusing on two clas-
sic nasalization measures, A1–P0 and A1–P1, the research
examines nasalization differences between pathological types
and controls, trajectory changes before and after surgical inter-
vention, and whether nasalization effects concentrate on spe-
cific vowel conditions through pathological type comparison,
surgical temporal tracking, and vowel-level refined analysis.
Through this analytical framework, this study aims to evaluate
the potential value of vowel nasalization as a voice represen-
tation associated with upper airway diseases and provide more
targeted empirical evidence for subsequent phonetic and clini-
cal research.
2. Methods
2.1. Database
This study utilized the CUCO database, one of the largest pub-
licly available speech databases of patients with upper airway
diseases [21]. The database comprises 107 Castilian Spanish
speakers divided into four groups: 26 healthy controls (Contr),
29 septoplasty (Sept) patients, 27 functional endoscopic sinus
surgery (FESS) patients, and 25 tonsillectomy (Tonsill) pa-
tients. All control participants underwent clinical screening to
exclude individuals with previous vocal tract surgery, neck can-
cer, or disorders related to speech or voice.
To examine vowel nasalization, we selected the TDU sub-
set of the CUCO database. The TDU subset employed a stan-
dardized reading task: all participants read a fixed set of four
short sentences at three time points—pre-surgery, 2 weeks post-
surgery, and 3 months post-surgery. This task was deemed suit-
able for systematic analysis of vowel nasalization patterns due
to the high consistency of speech materials and time points, with
most sentences containing explicit nasal coarticulation contexts.
2.2. Target V owels and Nasal Contexts
This study selected the following three sentences containing
nasal contexts:
•Corre agua en el arroyo al crep ´usculo
•Calienta la casa el brasero y el hornillo de carb ´on
•Es h ´abil un solo d´ıa
Given that not all vowels undergo observable nasalization, this
study analyzed only vowels directly coarticulated with nasal
consonants with clearly defined sources of nasalization. These
included two types of within-syllable nasal coarticulation struc-
tures:
V owel–Nasal (VN)structures in the function wordsenandun,
corresponding to vowels /e/ and /u/, respectively.
Consonant–V owel–Nasal (CVN)structures in the content
wordcarb ´on, corresponding to vowel /o/.
All target vowels preceded nasal consonants within the same
syllable, with nasalization primarily arising from coarticula-
tion. This configuration provided clear phonetic motivation and
high predictability, establishing a stable analytical foundation
for comparing nasalization differences across participant groups
and time points.
2.3. Nasalization Metrics
To quantify the degree of vowel nasalization, we employed
two spectral amplitude difference metrics proposed by Chen:
A1–P0 (dB) and A1–P1 (dB) [17]. Both metrics reflect the
influence of nasal coupling by comparing energy related to
the first formant with energy from additional spectral peaks
introduced by nasal coupling. Specifically:
A1:amplitude of the peak harmonic closest to the expected F1.
P0:amplitude of the spectral peak in the low-frequency region
introduced by coupling with the nasal and paranasal cavities.
P0 typically corresponds to the higher-amplitude peak between
the first harmonic (H1) and the second harmonic (H2); if H1 is
too high, H2 is used as the estimate for P0.
P1:amplitude of the nasal formant located between F1 and F2,
typically around 950 Hz.
The calculation formulas are:
A1–P0=A1−P0 (1)
A1–P1=A1−P1 (2)
In terms of numerical interpretation, lower A1–P0 or A1–
P1 values indicate stronger nasalization. As nasal coupling in-
tensifies, the energy of nasal-related spectral components (P0
and P1) increases, reducing the amplitude difference between
them and A1. Conversely, higher values indicate weaker nasal
contribution, with vowels dominated by oral resonance and
lower degrees of nasalization [17].
2.4. Feature Extraction
All target sentence recordings were phoneme-aligned using the
Spanish acoustic model and lexicon of the Montreal Forced
Aligner (MFA) [22]. Subsequently, nasalization acoustic
metrics for target vowels were extracted based on a Praat script
(https://github.com/stylerw/styler_praat_
scripts/tree/master/nasality_automeasure)
developed by Styler et al., which has been validated in multiple
vowel nasalization studies [20, 19, 23]. A1–P0 and A1–P1
features were extracted for each participant’s target vowels at
three time points: pre-surgery, 2 weeks post-surgery, and 3
months post-surgery.
2.5. Statistical Analysis
Cross-sectional analysis:To evaluate the effectiveness of A1–
P0 and A1–P1 in distinguishing healthy controls from patients
with different upper airway conditions, we analyzed pre-surgery
data. For each vowel, with participants as the unit of analy-
sis, the four groups (controls, septoplasty, FESS, tonsillectomy)
were entered into a one-way analysis of variance (ANOV A) to
test for main effects of group. When the overall group effect was
significant, Tukey HSD post-hoc pairwise comparisons were
conducted. Cohen’sdwas calculated as the effect size for sig-
nificant comparisons.
Longitudinal analysis:To investigate whether vowel
nasalization changed before and after surgery and its tempo-
ral dynamics, we conducted within-group paired analyses. For
each vowel, A1–P0 and A1–P1 values were compared across
three time points: pre-surgery (baseline), 2 weeks post-surgery,
and 3 months post-surgery. Pairwise comparisons between
time points were conducted using pairedt-tests, with Holm–
Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons within the same
vowel and group. Effect sizes were reported for all significant

3. Results
3.1. Comparison of V owel Nasalization Metrics Across
Pathological Types
Cross-sectional analysis revealed that the A1–P0 metric demon-
strated higher sensitivity than A1–P1 in distinguishing healthy
controls from different upper airway pathological types. The
A1–P0 metric detected significant between-group differences
for the /u/ vowel (F= 4.287,p= 0.007), while other vow-
els (/e/:F= 0.054,p= 0.983; /o/:F= 1.583,p= 0.198)
did not reach statistical significance.
Post-hoc Tukey HSD comparisons for the /u/ vowel re-
vealed that the septoplasty group exhibited significantly lower
A1–P0 values (M=−8.81dB,SD= 5.51) compared to both
the FESS group (M=−5.31dB,SD= 4.46,p= 0.019,
Cohen’sd= 0.693) and the tonsillectomy group (M=−5.27
dB,SD= 3.49,p= 0.016, Cohen’sd=−0.759), suggesting
greater nasalization in the septoplasty group for the /u/ vowel.
However, no significant differences were detected between the
healthy control group (M=−5.79dB,SD= 4.11) and any
of the surgical groups (allp >0.05).
In contrast, the A1–P1 metric failed to differentiate between
pathological types. One-way ANOV A revealed no significant
between-group differences for A1–P1 across all three vowels
(/e/:F= 2.007,p= 0.117; /o/:F= 0.826,p= 0.482; /u/:
F= 2.082,p= 0.107).
Figure 1 illustrates the pre-surgery distributions of A1–P0

3.2. Changes in V owel Nasalization Features Before and Af-
ter Surgery
Longitudinal analysis revealed that the tonsillectomy group was
the only cohort exhibiting transient changes in vowel nasal-
ization post-surgery. In this group, the A1–P1 metric dis-
played a consistent pattern across multiple vowels, character-
ized by an increase at two weeks post-surgery followed by a
decline at three months post-surgery. Specifically, the /e/ vowel
showed a significant increase at two weeks post-surgery com-
pared to baseline (∆ = 1.75dB,t=−2.082,p= 0.047,
d=−0.408), which subsequently decreased significantly at
three months post-surgery compared to two weeks post-surgery
(∆ =−2.85dB,t= 2.151,p= 0.041,d= 0.422). The
/u/ vowel exhibited a similar trajectory, with a significant in-
crease at two weeks post-surgery (∆ = 5.29dB,t=−5.218,
p <0.001,d=−1.023) and a significant decline at three
months post-surgery (∆ =−5.58dB,t= 4.238,p <0.001,
d= 0.831). For the A1–P0 metric, only the /u/ vowel demon-
strated the same transient pattern (baseline vs. two weeks post-
surgery:∆ = 1.89dB,p= 0.012,d=−0.528; two weeks
vs. three months post-surgery:∆ =−3.18dB,p= 0.004,
d= 0.617).
In contrast, the remaining two pathological groups did not
exhibit similar post-surgical changes. Pairedt-tests with Holm–
Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons revealed no sig-
nificant differences in A1–P0 or A1–P1 metrics across the three
time points (baseline, two weeks post-surgery, and three months
post-surgery) for either the septoplasty or FESS groups (all
p >0.05). Similarly, the healthy control group demonstrated
stable A1–P0 and A1–P1 values across the same time intervals,
with no significant changes detected.
Notably, paired comparisons between baseline and three
months post-surgery did not reach statistical significance for
any group (allp >0.05), indicating that surgery did not induce
long-term alterations in nasalization features.

4. Discussion
Based on the CUCO database, this study systematically evalu-
ated the performance of two commonly used vowel nasalization
measures, A1–P0 and A1–P1, in the analysis of speech associ-
ated with upper airway diseases. The results indicate that A1–
P0 is overall more sensitive in differentiating pathological types.
Nasalization effects exhibited a clear vowel-dependent pattern,
with /u/ showing the highest sensitivity. Longitudinal analyses

In terms of metric comparison, A1–P0 showed more sta-
ble discriminatory power across pathological conditions than
A1–P1. This finding is consistent with previous studies report-
ing greater robustness of A1–P0 in characterizing the degree of
vowel nasalization [20]. From an acoustic perspective, P0 re-
flects a low-frequency spectral peak introduced by coupling be-
tween the nasal cavity and the paranasal sinuses, whose energy
variation more directly responds to changes in nasal ventilation
[17]. In contrast, the estimation of P1 relies on more complex
spectral shape characteristics and is more susceptible to inter-
ference from the first and second formants (F1 and F2) [24, 17].
The present results further demonstrate that under conditions
involving pathological structural variations of the nasal cavity,
A1–P0 maintains high measurability, extending its applicabil-
ity in pathological speech analysis beyond contexts involving
healthy speakers [25].
Nasalization effects differed significantly across vowels,
with /u/ exhibiting the highest sensitivity in both cross-sectional
and longitudinal analyses. Only /u/ was able to capture sig-
nificant differences between the septoplasty group and other
groups, and the transient postoperative changes following
tonsillectomy were also most consistently reflected in this
vowel. This finding can be interpreted from an articula-
tory–physiological perspective: the production of /u/ involves
a high tongue position and lip rounding, resulting in a relatively
constricted vocal tract configuration and higher oral radiation
impedance [26]. Under such conditions, the contribution of
nasal coupling to the overall acoustic output is amplified. In
contrast, vowels such as /e/ and /o/ are produced with more open
vocal tract configurations, in which the relative contribution of
nasal resonance is reduced. These results complement previous
analyses of the vowel /a/ based on the CUCO database and fur-
ther highlight the necessity of explicitly accounting for vowel
dependency in pathological speech research [21].
Longitudinal analyses showed that only the tonsillec-
tomy group exhibited transient nasalization changes following
surgery, characterized by reduced nasalization at two weeks
postoperatively and a return to baseline levels at three months.
This rise-and-fall pattern may reflect temporary modifications
of oropharyngeal structure and tissue healing processes during
the early postoperative period, which in turn transiently affect
resonance patterns [27, 28]. By contrast, the effects of septo-
plasty and functional endoscopic sinus surgery on nasal–oral
resonance coupling may be more subtle or may not be readily
detectable within the nasalized vowel contexts examined in this
study [29, 30, 31].
The primary contribution of this study lies in its first sys-
tematic comparison of A1–P0 and A1–P1 across different vow-
els, pathological types, and intervention conditions. Through
longitudinal analysis, it further delineates the short-term dy-
namics of surgery-related nasalization changes, clarifying the
joint roles of vowel dependency and metric sensitivity in patho-
logical speech assessment.
Several limitations should be acknowledged. The analysis
was restricted to Castilian Spanish speech materials and a lim-
ited set of vowels and nasal contexts. Future work may extend
this analytical framework to multiple languages and a broader
range of nasalization environments to further evaluate the ro-
bustness and generalizability of vowel nasalization measures
across linguistic and pathological conditions.
5. Conclusion
Using the publicly available CUCO database, this study sys-
tematically examined vowel nasalization across multiple up-
per airway pathological types and surgical time points, pro-
viding a unified evaluation of the A1–P0 and A1–P1 met-
rics for characterizing nasal–oral resonance coupling in natural
speech. Among the two measures, A1–P0 showed more sen-
sitivity in distinguishing pathological groups, while A1–P1 ex-
hibited more limited discriminative power under the patholog-
ical conditions examined. Nasalization effects also exhibited
clear vowel-dependent patterns. In particular, the /u/ vowel con-
sistently captured variations in nasalization across both cross-
sectional and longitudinal analyses, indicating heightened sen-
sitivity to changes in airway patency. Longitudinal analyses
further showed that transient post-surgical nasalization changes
were observed only in the tonsillectomy group, with no signifi-
cant long-term alterations detected in other pathological groups
or in healthy controls. These findings support the value of vowel
nasalization measures for speech-based assessment in upper air-
way diseases and provide empirical evidence for the develop-
ment of more targeted acoustic analysis protocols in clinical
speech evaluation.
