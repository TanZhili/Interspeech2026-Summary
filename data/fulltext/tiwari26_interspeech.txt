“Say That Again”: Visualizing Paralinguistic Cues with Prosody-Aware
Diffusion
Shyamji Tiwari
Independent Researcher, India
shyamji2000tiwari@gmail.com
Abstract
Text-to-image models ignore paralinguistic cues. Pitch, rate,
and emotional inflection shape how listeners visualize a scene,
yet audio-to-image methods treat speech as a single opaque
vector. NovaDiffusion conditions diffusion-based synthesis
on emotion-correlated prosodic features extracted alongside
general audio representations. It comprises (1) Prosody-
CLIP, trained on 168K human-annotated speech-emotion-
image triplets to align prosody-enriched embeddings with vi-
sual representations; (2) a 280M-parameter distilled U-Net with
multi-scale fusion; and (3) a decoupled cross-attention adapter
injecting prosodic embeddings via IP-Adapter. On RA VDESS
(8 emotions, chance 12.5%), NovaDiffusion achieves 71.3%
ECA, outperforming SonicDiffusion by 23.1pp; on held-out
IEMOCAP speakers it reaches 63.4% vs. 41.7%. ECA relies
on facial expression classification and does not capture scene-
level emotion. Scope is limited to emotion-correlated prosody;
full prosodic control remains open.
Index Terms: speech prosody, audio-to-image generation, dif-
fusion models, emotion-aware synthesis, multimodal alignment
1. Introduction
Text-to-image synthesis [1, 2, 3, 4] has reached photorealistic
quality, progressing from U-Net-based latent diffusion [1] to
Diffusion Transformers [5] and rectified flow architectures [6].
All of these models, however, condition only on written text
and discard paralinguistic information entirely. Consider the
utterance “Are you serious?” spoken with genuine curiosity
versus biting sarcasm: the transcript is identical, but the men-
tal imagery differs sharply (Figure 1). Pitch contour, speaking
rate, stress patterns, and emotional inflection form anemotion-
correlated prosodiclayer that shapes how listeners visualize de-
scribed scenes.
Recent audio-to-image methods [7, 8, 9] condition gener-
ation on audio signals but do not separate prosodic cues from
environmental sounds, speaker identity, or lexical content.
On the emotion side, EmoGen [10] aligns emotion spaces
with CLIP [11], and “Make Me Happier” [12] synthesizes
emotion-evoking images. In speech, RA-CLAP [13] captures
fine-grained speaking styles via contrastive learning [14]; Para-
CLAP [15] targets paralinguistic tasks including emotion and
speaker traits; EMOV A [16] integrates emotional perception
across modalities; emotion2vec [17] provides self-supervised
speech emotion representations across 10 languages. The pieces
needed to connect prosodic understanding with visual genera-
tion now exist, but no prior work has assembled them into a
single pipeline.
We introduce NovaDiffusion (Figure 3), which, to our
knowledge, is the first system to explicitly extract and route

2.2. Emotion-aware generation and speech representations
EmoGen [10] and “Make Me Happier” [12] condition diffu-
sion on abstract emotion spaces; EmotiCrafter [18] extends this
to continuous valence-arousal control; EmoEdit [19] manipu-
lates existing images to evoke target emotions. These meth-
ods condition on text-level emotion labels, not speech prosody.
EMOV A [16] jointly models emotional perception in vision,
speech, and language. For audio-language alignment, Audio-
CLIP [30] extends CLIP to a trimodal space; RA-CLAP [13]
adds relation-augmented pretraining for emotional style re-
trieval; ParaCLAP [15] targets paralinguistic tasks including
emotion, age, and speaker traits. emotion2vec [17] learns self-
supervised emotion representations across 10 languages. Our
ProsodyCLIP differs in that it maps prosody to vision directly.
For efficient generation, BK-SDM [23] compresses Stable Dif-
fusion via block pruning; SnapGen [31] generates 1024×1024
images in 1.4s on mobile. The OLSS scheduler [32] enables
few-step generation via QR-decomposition.
3. Dataset Curation Pipeline
Earlier tone-aware generation work relies on STT-derived text,
which strips away the very tonal variation it aims to model. We
instead build a three-stage pipeline on top of human-annotated
emotion corpora (Figure 2).
3.1. Stage 1: Emotion-annotated speech collection
We pool three corpora with validated human annotations:
RA VDESS[20] (7,356 acted recordings, 8 emotions,κ >
0.70),IEMOCAP[21] (10,039 dyadic utterances,≥3 eval-
uators), andMSP-Podcast[22] (151K naturalistic turns,
≥5 raters), giving 168K utterances that span acted, semi-
spontaneous, and naturalistic speech. Since MSP-Podcast ac-
counts for∼90% of the data, we apply frequency-based sam-
pling during pretraining to up-weight the smaller corpora and
reduce the distributional gap between clean acted and noisy nat-
uralistic recordings.
3.2. Stage 2: Prosodic feature extraction and verification
For each utterance we extract pitch contour (F0 via
CREPE [33]), energy envelope (RMS), speaking rate (sylla-
bles/s via forced alignment), and spectral features (MFCCs 1–
13, spectral centroid). A prosody-only 3-layer MLP (trained on
80% of each corpus) predicts emotion from prosodic features
alone; samples where the MLP prediction disagrees with the
human label are removed (12%, threshold: top-1 mismatch),
which served as quality control for mislabeled clips without
skewing the underlying speaker or emotional distributions.
3.3. Stage 3: Emotion-aligned image pairing
For each emotion, we retrieve candidate images from CC-
12M [34] via emotion-specific CLIP queries. Two annotators
verify emotional alignment (κ>0.65); each utterance is paired
with the highest-ranked image from 3 candidates based on their
consensus, with zero image reuse across different utterances.
The resultingProsoBenchcontains 168K triplets with speaker-
independent splits (80/10/10%). Because the images come from
CLIP retrieval on internet-scale corpora, the pairing captures
emotion-stereotypicalimagery: the model learns culturally me-
diated visual correlates of emotion, not true acoustic-visual
physical co-occurrence. This is a practical first-generation
choice; we do not claim perceptual grounding.
For U-Net distillation, we use 1M pairs from CC-12M [34]
and 200K from LAION-Aesthetics-6.5+ [35], both filtered for
quality and license compliance.
4. Method

enrichment branch (Figure 4). It has three encoders: a frozen
CLIP text encoder [11], a ResNet-based image encoder, and an
ESResNeXt [36] voice encoder with a dedicated prosodic fea-
ture pathway.

5. Experiments
5.1. Setup
We evaluate on RA VDESS [20] and ProsoBench-test. Follow-
ing AudioToken [7], we report AIS (Wav2CLIP [42] cosine
similarity), AIC (CLIP-based content overlap), IIS, and FID.
We also introduceEmotion Classification Accuracy (ECA),
defined as the fraction of generated images whose predicted
emotion matches the input speech emotion. ECA is computed
by running MTCNN [43] face detection followed by a ResNet-
50 fine-tuned on AffectNet [44] (8-class, 65.3% val accuracy;
<5% faceless exclusion on RA VDESS). ECA is biased toward
facial expressions; scene-level emotions without faces are not
captured. Note that the 65.3% validation accuracy of the Af-
fectNet classifier sets a practical ceiling on absolute ECA num-
bers, making relative comparisons more informative. Baselines
include ImageBind [28], CoDi [29], AudioToken [7], SonicD-

5.2. Main results
Results are in Table 2. We foreground ECA on RA VDESS
and IEMOCAP as primary evidence: both carry human-verified
emotion labels independent of CLIP, and the ECA classi-
fier (AffectNet ResNet-50) was trained on a separate cor-
pus, so this evaluation chain is non-circular. AIS/AIC on
ProsoBench share representational overlap with the CLIP-
based training pipeline and are supplementary. On RA VDESS
(8 classes, chance 12.5%), NovaDiffusion reaches 71.3%
ECA, outperforming SonicDiffusion (48.2%) by 23.1pp; re-
training alone (SonicDiff.†, 52.6%) accounts for only 4.4pp,
confirming prosodic enrichment drives the gain. Seeing-
Sounds [27] achieves higher AIC (0.354 vs. 0.258) via its text-
semantic bridge. FID (94.2) is 4.6pt above SonicDiffusion
(text-only U-Net FID = 91.8; gap is architectural, 280M vs.
860M). Cross-corpus IEMOCAP (held-out speakers, 4 emo-
tions): ECA = 63.4% vs. 41.7%.
