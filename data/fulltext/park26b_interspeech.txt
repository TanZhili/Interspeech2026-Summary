Accurate Source-Free Speech Classification via
Meta-Learned Target-Centric Model Merging
Ka Hyun Park∗, Junghun Kim∗, U Kang
Seoul National University, Republic of Korea
{kahyun.park, bandalg97, ukang }@snu.ac.kr
Abstract
How can we build a speech classifier for an unseen domain
when access to source-domain data is unavailable? Speech
classification is widely used (e.g., voice commands, emotion
recognition, etc.), but it is highly sensitive to domain shifts
(e.g., languages), often causing substantial performance degra-
dation. This issue becomes critical when source data cannot be
accessed and only pretrained source models with limited tar-
get data are available. Under such constraints, typical adap-
tation approaches are inapplicable, and existing model merg-
ing methods easily overfit the limited target data. We propose
MOCHEE , an accurate target-centric model merging framework
that combines multiple pretrained source models using limited
target data. To improve generalization under scarce target la-
bels, MOCHEE exploits meta-learning to resolve representation
misalignment and emphasize target-relevant sources. Extensive
experiments show that M OCHEE outperforms the baselines by
up to 14.5 points in Macro-F1.
Index Terms: speech classification, model merging, source-
free, target-centric, meta-learning, meta-reweighting
1. Introduction
Given multiple pretrained source-domain speech classifiers but
no access to the original source data, how can we build an ac-
curate classifier for an unseen target domain using only lim-
ited labeled target data? Speech classification assigns labels to
acoustic signals and supports many real-world systems, includ-
ing emotion recognition [1, 2, 3], speaker identification [4, 5],
language identification [6, 7], and fake speech detection [8, 9].
We focus on source-free setting where only multiple pretrained
source classifiers and a small labeled target set are available.
This setting is common in practice because source speech data
are often private or proprietary, whereas pretrained models can
still be shared for downstream use. For example, hospitals and
call centers release pretrained models while keeping raw record-
ings as confidential assets.
Under this constraint, many transfer learning [10, 11, 12],
data selection [13, 14, 15], and meta-learning [16, 17] ap-
proaches become difficult to apply, as they typically require
access to source-domain data. Recently, model merging [18,
19, 20] has emerged as a promising alternative under such
source-free constraints. Model merging consolidates multiple
pretrained models into a single model via averaging or heuris-
tic alignment strategies, without updating source parameters.
While some approaches [18, 21] use a held-out set, this set is
primarily used for model selection rather than learning domain-
specific relevance. Moreover, when only a few labeled target

To address the limitations of existing model merging meth-
ods for source-free speech classification, we propose M OCHEE
(TARGET -AWARE MODEL MERGING FOR SPEECH CLASSI -
FICATION IN A SOURCE -F REE SETTING ), an accurate target-
centric method that learns generalization-oriented merging
scores for each source model. Unlike prior works that opti-
mize merging weights to perform well on the available target
data, we learn target-relevance scores for each source model
with a meta-learning-based objective to promote generaliza-
tion to unseen target samples. Moreover, since independently
trained source models can be misaligned by hidden-unit per-
mutations, M OCHEE aligns them before merging. This design
ensures that the learned merging scores do not overfit to the lim-
ited labeled target instances, but instead capture generalization-
oriented source contributions that transfer to unseen target data.
Our main contributions are as follows. First, we high-
light an under-studied yet practical source-free setting for
speech classification. Second, we propose a generalization-
oriented model merging method that learns source importance
via a meta-learning objective and performs feature-permutation
alignment to reliably merge misaligned source models. Third,
on cross-corpus speech classification benchmarks, M OCHEE
consistently outperforms strong baselines, demonstrating im-
proved target-domain generalization under limited target super-
vision. Our code is publicly released at https://github.
com/snudatalab/Mochee.

2.1. Model Merging
Model merging combines pretrained models without retrain-
ing. Model soups [18] averages learned parameters but of-
ten fails under domain heterogeneity due to negative transfer.
Permutation-aware merging [19] mitigates neuron-level mis-
alignment via hard, layer-wise matching, but it can be brittle

Model merging has also been applied to disordered speech
ASR [26]. Beyond ASR, averaged pretrained checkpoints are
used in speech emotion recognition and other speech classifica-
tion pipelines [27]. However, they use model merging primarily
as within-domain checkpoint averaging to gain ensemble-like
robustness. Domain and data heterogeneity have been studied
across diverse areas, such as graph learning [28, 29, 30, 31, 32]
and semi-supervised or domain-aware speech learning [33, 34].
In our source-free setting with heterogeneous source models
and scarce target instances, existing methods often select mod-
els by directly minimizing the target training loss [35, 36] or by
heuristic interference resolution [37], which can be unstable and
overfit to the limited target supervision. Beyond model merg-
ing, efficient representation and factorization methods have also
been developed for heterogeneous or evolving data, including
spectral transforms for time series [38, 39], compact multi-way
factorization [40, 41, 42, 43, 44], streaming and irregular tensor
decomposition [45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50], and structured sequence
understanding [51]. In contrast, M OCHEE focuses on source-
free speech adaptation by aligning and merging pretrained clas-
sifiers without accessing source-domain data.

3. Proposed Method
We propose M OCHEE , an accurate target-centric model merg-
ing framework for source-free speech classification. Given pre-
trained source-domain classifiers and a small labeled target set,
MOCHEE constructs a single target-centric classifier by align-
ing hidden representation space and learning generalization-
oriented merging weights, without updating any source-model
parameters. Figure 1 illustrates the overall pipeline. We next
highlight the core challenges and our corresponding ideas:
1. How can we merge independently trained source mod-
els that are misaligned in parameter space? Even with
identical architectures, hidden units can be permuted across
source models, making naive weight averaging unreliable. To
address this, M OCHEE learns feature permutations to align
source models before merging (Section 3.1).
2. After aligning the parameter space, how can we learn the
generalization-oriented merging scores? We optimize the
per-source merging scores with meta-reweighting, which di-
rectly promotes target generalization rather than merely min-
imizing the target training loss (Section 3.2).
3.1. Permutation-based Source Model Alignment
A speech classifier consists of a frozen speech embedding
model (e.g., wav2vec2.0 [58] or Whisper [59]) followed by
lightweight MLP heads. We focus on aligning the MLP heads,
since the embedding model provides a shared representation
space while the MLP head captures domain-specific decision
boundaries. A challenge is that merging independently trained
heads is non-trivial due to permutation symmetry. Even when
all source MLP heads have identical architectures, indepen-
dently trained heads may represent the same function with dif-
ferent hidden-unit indexings; permuting the hidden units of a
layer and applying the corresponding inverse permutation to
the next layer leaves the end-to-end mapping unchanged. As
a result, the same neuron index can correspond to different se-
mantics across sources, and naive parameter averaging mixes
mismatched units. To address this, MOCHEE learns soft permu-
tations that align hidden units of each source head in a target-

4. Experiments
Through experiments, we answer the following questions:
Q1. Performance (Section 4.2). How accurately does
MOCHEE classify target-domain instances using only pre-
trained source models and limited target supervision?
Q2. Ablation study (Section 4.3). Does each component of
MOCHEE contribute to target-domain performance?
Q3. Source contribution analysis (Section 4.4). How do the
learned source weights α change during training, and do
they upweight the most target-relevant source domains?
4.1. Experimental Settings
Datasets. We use six speech-emotion datasets from the
CAMEO collection [68], each recorded in a different lan-
guage. CREMA-D [62], SubESCO [63], RA VDESS [64], and
MESD [65] serve as source domains, while CAF ´E [66] and

statistics in Table 2. Source datasets are split by 8 : 1 : 1 into
train/validation/test sets for source-model pretraining. Target
datasets are split by 1 : 9 and 2 : 8 into training/test sets for
CAF ´E and Or´eau, respectively; for MOCHEE , each target train-
ing set is split equally into target-training and target-validation
sets.

4.2. Target-domain Performance
Table 3 compares MOCHEE with existing model-merging base-
lines. Uniform averaging performs poorly, and permutation
alignment alone (Re-basin + Uni.) provides only marginal
gains, indicating that alignment without target-aware source
weighting is insufficient. Greedy Soup and TIES incorporate
more target-driven merging than uniform averaging, but their
gains remain limited. Overall, M OCHEE achieves the best ac-
curacy and macro-F1 across both target domains, showing the
importance of jointly learning permutation-based alignment and
target-aware merging weights from limited target labels.

4.3. Ablation Study
To evaluate the individual contributions of each module, we
compare M OCHEE with two variants: M OCHEE -w/o-perm and
MOCHEE -w/o-α in Table 5. M OCHEE -w/o-perm is M OCHEE
without the representation alignment and M OCHEE -w/o-α is
MOCHEE without updating the merging scores. Note that re-
moving permutation alignment causes a large drop, while re-
movingα yields a smaller but consistent degradation. Overall,
MOCHEE achieves the highest accuracy and macro-F1, indicat-
ing that both modules consistently contribute to performance.
4.4. Merging Weights of Source Domains
To analyze the evolution of MOCHEE ’s merging weightsα, we
construct a setting where English appears in both the source and
target domains by selecting RA VDESS (English) as the target
and including CREMA-D (English) among the sources. This
allows us to directly observe whether M OCHEE assigns larger
weights to sources that are linguistically similar to the target.
As shown in Figure 2, M OCHEE progressively increases
the weight of the English source (CREMA-D). In contrast,
linguistically distant sources, which are MESD (Spanish) and
SubESCO (Bengali), receive lower weights. The merging
weights stabilize toward the end of training, demonstrating that
they are learned in a stable and target-aware manner.

for source-free speech classification under scarce target super-
vision. M OCHEE jointly learns target-guided alignment and
source importance via a meta-learning objective that selects
merging parameters to improve validation generalization rather
than fit the limited target training set. Experiments on real-
