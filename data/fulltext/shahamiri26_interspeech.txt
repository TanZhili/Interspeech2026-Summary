Abstract
Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems offer immense
potential for individuals with dysarthria, yet traditional models
fail severely due to high inter- and intra-speaker variability and
data scarcity. To address this, we present BetterSpeak, a mo-
bile ASR platform powered by our novel Two-Phase Person-
alized Conformer Adaptation Pipeline. The pipeline employs
a resource-efficient transfer learning strategy, first adapting a
robust Conformer model to the general dysarthric vocabulary,
and then rapidly personalizing its acoustic encoder using lim-
ited, publicly available dysarthric speech samples. We detail
the platform’s functionalities and demonstrate how this person-
alized, data-efficient approach is crucial for providing children
with an accessible, high-accuracy medium for communication,
thereby fostering a better social and digital life.

1. Introduction
Dysarthria is a motor speech disorder that severely damages
control over speech articulators, leading to diverse and highly
variable speech patterns that significantly lower the intelligibil-
ity of speech [1]. It is caused by various neurological conditions
that could result in muscle paralysis. Dysarthria is often coupled
with physical disabilities. For children living with this condi-
tion, the inability to communicate effectively with technology
and peers can be a significant barrier to education and social
participation. A fully functional Automatic Speech Recognition
(ASR) system could serve as a medium not only to accurately
transcribe impaired speech but also to enable speech synthesis
technologies to speak on the child’s behalf, greatly enhancing
their autonomy and quality of life.
However, the performance of commercial, speaker-
independent (SI) ASR systems for severely dysarthric speech
remains unacceptably low. State-of-the-art typical ASR models,
such as the Whisper model, produced Word Error Rates (WER)
of up to 76.4% when given severe dysarthric speech [2]. This
performance gap is primarily attributable to two factors: the
vast acoustic mismatch between healthy and dysarthric speech,
and the chronic difficulty in acquiring sufficient, diverse, and
high-quality training data for this low-resource domain [3].
To address these issues, personalized ASR models, which
fine-tune a general model on a target speaker’s unique voice,
have been shown to outperform SI systems dramatically [4].
Building upon this necessity, this paper introduces the BetterS-
peak platform, which integrates a mobile data collection and de-
ployment platform with our state-of-the-art backend Dysarthric

ASR for atypical speech is uniquely challenging in the pedi-

Dysarthria impacts articulators differently across individuals,
resulting in considerably higher speech variations [7], while pe-

adaptation. Moreover, the largest publicly available corpus con-

to train Whisper [9], and no recordings of dysarthric children
currently exist. A practical solution must address both issues
while remaining accessible outside a lab setting via a mobile
application for ongoing data collection and real-time inference.
2. The personalized adaptation pipeline
The core technical innovation within the BetterSpeak plat-
form is the Two-Phase Personalized Conformer Adaptation
Pipeline [5], a Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) framework
utilizing the Conformer architecture [10]. The Conformer cap-
tures both local (CNN) and global (Transformer) dependencies,
making it suitable for the complex temporal and spectral dis-
tortions present in dysarthric speech. The pipeline starts with a
general-purpose ASR model pre-trained on a massive dataset
(e.g., LibriSpeech) to ensure strong phonetic and linguistic
knowledge, then adapts in two distinct, sequential phases.
Phase 1 (V ocabulary Adaptation).The pre-trained model is
adapted using data from healthy control speakers in dysarthric
speech corpora (e.g., UASpeech [11], TORGO [12]) to align its
lexical and phonological representations with the specific word-
set and pronunciation patterns of the dysarthric domain. This
produces a vocabulary-adapted intermediate model.
Phase 2 (Individual Adaptation).This phase is the core of
the personalization strategy. The control model is fine-tuned on
only a small amount of data from the target dysarthric speaker.
For the BetterSpeak, these samples are collected during on-
boarding. The fine-tuning is strategically constrained to a sub-
set of the model’s parameters, primarily the Conformer Encoder
layers closer to the acoustic input, effectively preventing over-
fitting on the small, personalized datasets.
Initial evaluations demonstrated average WERs of 21.5%
on UASpeech and 12.7% on TORGO, substantially outperform-
ing prior Seq2Seq approaches [13].
3. The BetterSpeak platform
BetterSpeak leverages our adaptive Conformer DSR pipeline
to construct personalized models for each dysarthric speaker.

These models are integrated with modern Large Language
Models to enhance speech transcription and synthesis. The sys-
tem then employs Text-To-Speech technologies to articulate the
intended message in clear, intelligible speech. In effect, Better-
Speak functions as a real-time translator, enabling individuals
with dysarthria to communicate effectively with others.
As illustrated in Figure 1, the platform operates as fol-
lows. Initially, users complete an onboarding process in which
we capture their speech samples, uttering phonetically rich
prompts. The platform sends the recording to our backend,
which implements the DSR adaptation pipeline to fine-tune a
DSR model for each user. Once the onboarding is complete, a
dedicated DSR model is deployed and users can utilize the app
to transcribe their speech into text. As part of our ongoing data
collection, recordings will be stored for continuous improve-
ment of the user’s DSR model. This functionality allows the
user model to be retrained or updated seamlessly on the server
side as the child’s speech patterns change or as new data is col-
lected. For private conversations, users can switch to privacy
mode, where recordings will not be collected while using the
models. Once the inference is ready, users can also utilize the
TTS function by clicking the ‘Speak’ button to facilitate daily
communication. Moreover, users have the option to correct the
transcription by tapping on it and making corrections, which
further improves the feedback.
Guardians or therapists can use the platform to record short
voice snippets from the child based on text prompts, alongside
an accuracy rating. This interactive, guided collection process
directly fuels the Phase 2 Individual Adaptation, ensuring the
model remains accurate and aligned with the child’s current vo-
cal characteristics.
4. Conclusion and future work
The integration of the BetterSpeak mobile platform with the
Personalized Conformer Adaptation Pipeline provides a scal-
able and robust solution to the complex challenge of ASR for
children with dysarthria. By employing a resource-efficient,
two-phase transfer learning strategy, the system effectively
overcomes the traditional hurdles of speaker variability and data
scarcity. This personalized approach is uniquely suited to sup-
port the rapidly developing and highly heterogeneous speech of
pediatric users, moving ASR from a generic utility to a truly
accessible assistive technology.
Having obtained ethical approval, we plan to conduct an
open trial to test the platform, targeting participants with Down
syndrome and cerebral palsy. The platform will then be publicly
available for all dysarthric speakers.
