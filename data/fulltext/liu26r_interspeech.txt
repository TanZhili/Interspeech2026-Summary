Abstract
Unlike Speech Emotion Recognition (SER), which predicts
utterance-level labels, Speech Emotion Diarization (SED) mod-
els fine-grained temporal dynamics. However, learning reliable
emotion boundaries from utterance-level labels remains chal-
lenging due to scarce frame-level annotations. We propose P-
SED, a weakly supervised SED framework based on prototype
metric learning that constructs a metric space via learnable emo-
tion prototypes and introduces orthogonal regularization to en-
hance inter-class separability. To mitigate sparse supervision
and neutral background interference, we design a Class-aware
Prototype Contrastive Loss (CPCL) combined with a Top-K
ranking mechanism to mine salient emotion segments. Fi-
nally, Total Variation Denoising (TVD) is applied during infer-
ence to preserve steep emotion boundaries. Experiments on the
ZED dataset show that P-SED significantly outperforms exist-
ing weakly supervised models, achieving an Emotion Diariza-
tion Error Rate (EDER) of 47.00%.
Index Terms: speech emotion diarization, weakly supervised
learning, prototype metric learning
1. Introduction
As a core technology in affective computing, SER plays a cru-
cial role in human-computer interaction, mental health moni-
toring, and intelligent customer service [1, 2]. However, main-
stream SER research mostly focuses on utterance-level static
classification, predicting a single label for an entire audio clip
[3, 4, 5]. In fact, emotional expressions in real-world scenar-
ios exhibit significant temporal dynamics, typically manifest-
ing as events that appear, persist, and evolve within local time
segments [6]. To capture such fine-grained dynamics, the task
of SED [7] was introduced,aiming to precisely locate the start
and end times, as well as the specific categories, of emotional
events. This task provides a more refined temporal structure for
dialogue-level emotion analysis and affective agents, holding
significant application value.
In the SED task, the scarcity of fine-grained frame-level an-
notated data is a core challenge. The only publicly available
frame-level emotion dataset, ZED [7], contains approximately
17 minutes of speech, which is insufficient for training deep
models in a fully supervised manner. Therefore, some stud-
ies treat SED as a Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) [8] prob-
lem, where models are trained directly using utterance-level la-
bels. For example, the Emotion Neural Transducer (ENT)[9]
introduces a lattice-max-pooling loss to bypass timestamp de-
pendencies, but its supervision signal is dominated by a very
few salient frames, making it too sparse for emotion events with

continuous spans. Another category of methods maps bag-level
labels directly to all frames to provide dense supervision [7, 10],
which performs well on stable emotion segments. However,
real-world speech often contains silence or transition regions,
and such global mapping struggles to adapt to local dynamic
changes, potentially introducing label noise.
To overcome the signal sparsity problem caused by extreme
pooling, proportion-based salient instance mining methods,
such as Top-K ranking [11] and max pooling [12], have been
widely applied in fine-grained localization domains like medi-
cal lesion localization and weakly supervised action detection
[13, 14, 15]. This mechanism provides denser and more stable
signals by retaining a certain proportion of high-confidence in-
stances, which aligns well with the inherent property of speech
emotions appearing as continuous segments. Meanwhile, Pro-
totype Metric Learning [16], with its interpretable metric prop-
erties, has been widely used in SER to construct highly cohe-
sive feature spaces for effectively learning the central represen-
tations of various emotion categories [17, 18, 19].
To address the aforementioned challenges, this paper pro-
poses a novel weakly supervised SED framework, P-SED. This
method overcomes the bottleneck of sparse supervision sig-
nals in traditional weakly supervised learning, achieving the
localization of emotion events in speech streams using only
utterance-level weak labels.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We construct a learnable emotion prototype metric space and
introduce orthogonal regularization to enforce prototype sep-
aration, improving intra-class compactness and inter-class
discriminability while preventing representation collapse.
• A bag-level asymmetric optimization loss and a frame-level
Top-K salient instance mining mechanism are designed to
suppress non-salient frames and neutral background interfer-
ence, providing more reliable supervision under weak labels.
• TVD [20] is applied during inference as a temporal post-
processing module to smooth local prediction jitter while pre-
serving sharp emotion transition boundaries.
• P-SED achieves the best performance among the compared
weakly supervised baselines on the ZED benchmark dataset.

3.2. Asymmetric Optimization under Weak Supervision
To address the structural imbalance problem in weakly super-
vised training where neutral frames dominate the temporal di-
mension and emotion frames are locally distributed, P-SED
constructs an asymmetric optimization framework composed of
CPCL and a Top-K ranking loss, implementing differential con-
straints at the bag and frame levels.
3.2.1. Class-aware Prototype Contrastive Loss

on the utterance-level labelY, the model divides the training

applying different contrastive strategies.

the neutral category prototype to strengthen background mod-

4. Experiments and Analysis
4.1. Experimental Setup
4.1.1. Datasets
ZED.To evaluate the model’s performance on the SED task,
this study uses the ZED dataset as the test set. This dataset
provides precise frame-level emotion annotations, objectively
reflecting the model’s continuous emotion localization capabil-
ity in real conversational flows. However, given its small scale,
which is insufficient for training deep neural models, we only
use it as a hold-out test set to verify the model’s generalization
performance and fine-grained localization accuracy.
IEMOCAP.We select the IEMOCAP [23] dataset as the
training and validation corpus, choosing interactive speech an-
notated with Happy, Angry, Sad, and Neutral as the core data.
Following a speaker-independent protocol, Sessions 1-4 are al-
located to the training set, and Session 5 to the validation set.
Since this dataset only provides utterance-level labels, it is used
exclusively as a global weak supervision signal during the train-
ing phase.
4.1.2. Evaluation Metric
We evaluate the model using EDER [7], which measures four
temporal prediction errors: False Alarm (FA, neutral predicted
as emotion), Missed Emotion (ME, missed emotion events),
Confusion (CF, emotion class confusion), and Overlap (OL,
multi-label overlap):

4.1.3. Implementation details
The model is trained on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU for 50
epochs with a batch size of 8, using a fixed random seed and
automatic mixed precision. A pre-trained WavLM-Large is se-
lected as the backbone network. To prevent initial representa-
tion shift, the backbone network is frozen for the first 5 epochs,
519
fine-tuning only the projection head and prototype layers, fol-
lowed by end-to-end optimization of the entire network. For the
optimization strategy, the model employs the AdamW optimizer
(weight decay 0.05, peak learning rate5×10 −4), coupled with
a cosine annealing schedule including a 5-epoch warmup, and a
gradient clipping threshold set to 5.0. To stabilize metric space
learning, the temperature coefficientτis linearly annealed from
0.5 to 0.1 over the first 10 epochs; meanwhile, the orthogonal
weightλ orth increases linearly from 0.1 to 1.0 over the first 15
epochs to progressively strengthen the orthogonal separation of
prototypes. The final test evaluation uses exponential moving
average weights with a decay rate of 0.999 to ensure model pa-
rameter stability. Our code is available on github1.

ments (p<0.05) over all baseline methods.

5. Conclusion and Future Work
This paper proposes P-SED, a weakly supervised SED frame-
work that integrates an orthogonal prototype space, asymmetric
instance mining, and TVD. Using only utterance-level labels,
P-SED enables fine-grained emotion localization in continuous
speech. Experiments on the ZED dataset show that P-SED sig-
nificantly reduces EDER and outperforms existing weakly su-
pervised baselines. Despite its effectiveness, the fixed-ratio in-
stance mining mechanism struggles with highly variable and
rapidly changing emotional transitions in real conversations. In
addition, the small scale of the current test set limits compre-
hensive evaluation of model generalization. Future work will
explore adaptive instance mining and context-aware sequence
modeling, as well as larger frame-annotated datasets combined
with self-supervised learning to improve robustness.
