SignMatch: Aligning Pose Latent Diffusion via Multi-dimensional Rewards for
Sign Language Video Generation
Rongjie Huang1, Weidong Chen1, Helen Meng 1, Xixin Wu1
1 The Chinese University of Hong Kong
zjurongjiehuang@gmail.com
Abstract
Sign language video generation (SLVG) aims to synthesize
photo-realistic signing videos from spoken-language text while
preserving signer identity and visual style, but remains chal-
lenging due to weak alignment between text semantics and fine-
grained spatio-temporal motion. We present SIGNMATCH, a
unified framework that couples an LLM-empowered pose-latent
diffusion planner with a motion-aware sign video diffusion ren-
derer conditioned on predicted motion latents and a reference
image. To better align motion planning with downstream ob-
jectives, we apply Flow-GRPO post-training with semantic and
visual rewards to optimize the pose-latent generator without up-
dating the video renderer. Experiments on RWTH-2014T and
How2Sign demonstrate consistent gains in translation-based se-
mantic metrics and perceptual video quality, improving overall
faithfulness and realism.
Index Terms: Sign language video generation, preference
alignment, multi-dimensional reward
1. Introduction
Sign language is the primary communication medium for many
deaf and hard-of-hearing individuals, making automatic sign
generation an important problem for accessibility, education,
and inclusive human-computer interaction. Within computa-
tional sign language research, early efforts have mainly fo-
cused on continuous sign language recognition (CSLR) and
sign language translation (SLT) [1, 2, 3, 4], while more re-
cent work has moved toward sign language production (SLP)
and sign language video generation (SLVG), aiming to syn-
thesize realistic signing videos directly from spoken-language
text [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. Given a spoken-language sentence and a
reference signer, SLVG seeks to generate a photo-realistic sign
video that faithfully conveys linguistic meaning while preserv-
ing signer identity and visual style. Such a capability has broad
potential in assistive communication systems, educational con-
tent creation, and scalable sign-language interfaces.
Despite recent progress, SLVG remains highly challenging
because the alignment between text and sign video is inherently
weak in both space and time. Diffusion and flow-based genera-
tive models have become increasingly powerful for controllable
image and video synthesis [10, 11, 12], where reinforcement
learning (RL) and preference optimization have recently shown
strong promise in aligning generative models with downstream
objectives beyond likelihood training, including semantic con-
sistency and perceptual quality [13, 14, 15]. However, these
advances have not yet been fully exploited in sign language
generation. As a result, existing methods often struggle to si-
multaneously achieve semantic fidelity and visual realism, i.e.,
producing sign videos that are both linguistically correct and
temporally coherent.
In this work, we present SignMatch, a unified framework
for sign language video generation with multi-dimensional RL
post-training. SignMatch leverages (1) an LLM-empowered
pose latent diffusion model that maps spoken-language text into
motion-condition latents, and (2) a motion-aware sign video dif-
fusion model that renders the sign video conditioned on the pre-
dicted motion latents and a reference signer image.
To align model behavior with user intent, SignMatch per-
forms RL post-training with multi-dimensional reward feed-
back, encouraging the system to improve both linguistic faith-
fulness and visual quality. SignMatch applies preference opti-
mization via Flow-GRPO, with multi-dimensional rewards that
back-translation BLEU reward captures semantic fidelity, and
SSIM reward encourages visual quality.
We evaluate SignMatch on RWTH-2014T [3] and
How2Sign [16], two representative public benchmarks span-
ning German Sign Language and American Sign Language. Ex-
perimental results demonstrate that SignMatch achieves state-
of-the-art performance across semantic fidelity and video qual-
ity. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We proposeSignMatch, a unified SLVG framework that
combines an LLM-guided pose latent diffusion model with
a motion-aware sign video diffusion renderer.
• We introduce amulti-dimensional RL post-trainingstrat-
egy for sign generation, applying GRPO to the pose latent
generator with semantic and visual reward signals.
• Experimental results on RWTH-2014T and How2Sign
demonstrate that SignMatch achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance in both semantic fidelity and video quality.
2. Related Works
Sign language generation has evolved from early Sign Lan-
guage Production (SLP) systems [17, 18] alongside CSLR/SLT
research [1, 2, 3, 4], to modern text-to-sign pipelines that trans-
late spoken-language text into pose or skeleton sequences [5]
and further synthesize photo-realistic signing videos via staged
text-to-skeleton prediction and skeleton-to-video rendering [6,
7]. In parallel, human video synthesis has progressed from con-
ditional GAN-based translation [19, 20] and pose-guided an-
imation [21, 22] to diffusion-driven frameworks that improve
sample quality and controllability [10, 23, 24]. Additional re-
lated work and discussion are provided in Appendix C.

3. SignMatch
3.1. Overview
Given a reference signer imagex ⋆ and a spoken-language sen-
tenceT, sign language video generation (SLVG) aims to gen-
erate a photo-realistic sign videoXthat is semantically aligned
with the input text while preserving signer identity and visual
style, i.e., learningp θ(X|x ⋆,T). Because the alignment be-
tween text tokens and video frames is highly underconstrained,
existing SLVG approaches [8, 9, 25] typically adopt a staged
decomposition with synchronized intermediate conditions (e.g.,
pose, skeleton, or gloss-aligned motion), factorizing the prob-
lem into text-to-condition translation and condition-to-video
synthesis.
Here, the intermediate conditionCprovides explicit spatio-
temporal structure that bridges linguistic semantics and visual
motion. In our framework, we instantiate this decomposition
with (1) an LLM-empowered pose latent diffusion model that
predicts the motion conditionCfrom text, and (2) a motion-
aware sign video diffusion model that renders appearance-
consistent and temporally coherent signing conditioned on the
reference signer imagex⋆ and the predicted motion cues.
3.2. Pose Latent Diffusion
Given an input sentenceT, the pose latent diffusion model
maps linguistic content to motion-condition latents through
pθtran (C|T), whereCencodes the spatio-temporal signing
structure. In particular,Ccaptures both body pose and hand
motion, thereby modeling the core motion patterns required for
semantically faithful sign production.
Pre-trained language models are strong semantic reason-
ers [26, 27, 28], with powerful in-context learning and under-
standing capabilities that provide semantic guidance for gen-
erative models. We therefore adopt a language model back-
bone (e.g., T5-Large or Qwen3-8B) to perceive and reason
over the input text, and use a flow-matching generative frame-
work [29, 30] to model the latent distribution of motion condi-
tions in a denoising-based latent space. The training objective
defines forward and reverse transformations that continuously
bridge noise and data, encouraging smooth (low-curvature)
transport trajectories so that the path between the two distri-
butions becomes approximately linear in the latent manifold.
3.3. Motion-aware Sign Video Generation
The sign video diffusion model generates the final sign video
Xunder the guidance of the reference signer imagex ⋆ and the
continuous motion-condition embeddingse 0:F−1 . The archi-
tecture follows a reference-guided, condition-aware latent dif-
fusion design [11, 31], with a denoising U-Net as the core gen-
erator. It is conditioned by two complementary branches:
•Condition branch.A condition encoder processes the con-
tinuous motion-condition embeddings and injects multi-scale
motion features into the denoising stream, providing explicit
control over the spatio-temporal signing dynamics.
•Reference branch.A reference encoder extracts signer-
specific appearance features fromx ⋆ through a V AE, and
passes the resulting self-attention features to corresponding
U-Net blocks to preserve signer identity and visual style.
4. Multi-dimensional RL for pose latent
4.1. Overview
Standard likelihood-based training can be misaligned with user-
preferred objectives in SLVG. We therefore perform RL post-
training with multi-dimensional reward feedback to jointly en-
courage linguistic faithfulness and visual quality, i.e., generat-
ing sign videos that match the input semantics while maintain-
ing visual alignment. Importantly, we apply RL exclusively
to the pose-latent diffusion planner via parameter-efficient
LoRA [32] adapters and keep the video diffusion renderer
frozen, since the intermediate motion latent spaceCis the key
semantic interface between language understanding and video
synthesis; aligningCdirectly improves motion plausibility and
semantic correctness, which then propagates to better down-
stream video quality through the frozen renderer (FVD↓3.4%,
see Table 3).

5. Training and Evaluation
5.1. Dataset.
We conduct experiments on two widely used public benchmarks
that provide paired spoken-language sentences and signed con-
tent: RWTH-2014T [3] offers German sign language videos
aligned with German sentences and gloss annotations, but re-
mains challenging due to its relatively low visual quality and
limited capture conditions. How2Sign [16] supplies Ameri-
can Sign Language recordings with richer timing information;
leveraging the provided timestamps, prior work commonly seg-
ments the raw recordings into sentence-level clips to increase
the number of aligned training instances.
5.2. Model Configurations
For the pose latent generator, we adopt a pre-trained language
model as the text backbone to process the spoken-language in-
put and provide semantic conditioning for latent motion gen-
eration. We experiment with both T5-Large (encoder-decoder,
770M) and Qwen3-8B (decoder-only, 8B) as text encoders, and
find T5-Large to perform better for this task (see ablation in Ta-
ble 3), which we use as the default in our main experiments.
To sample from the flow transformer, we use torchdiffeq [35]
package to implement the ODE solvers with a step size of 0.04.
For pose modeling, a standard preprocessing step is to extract
2D keypoints using OpenPose [36] with hand keypoints further
lifted to 3D using the skeletal model estimation model, ensuring
a consistent evaluation interface across methods.
We use 8 A100 GPUs for our main experiments until 200K
optimization steps, following with RL post-training where we
apply LoRA [32] with rank 64 to the linear layers of the Trans-
former. During RL post-training, we sample a group ofG= 8
candidate motion latents for each input sentence. We assign
equal weights to the two reward dimensions, settingλsem = 0.5
andλ vis = 0.5, so that semantic fidelity and visual quality are
optimized in a balanced manner. The optimal RL checkpoint is
selected at 4000 steps based on pose-level BLEU-4 on the full
test set (see Appendix for KL coefficient analysis). To mitigate
potential reward hacking from back-translation-based rewards,
we use a back-translation evaluator from a separate training run
as the reward model, which is different from the evaluator used
for BLEU scores.
For video generation, we follow SignViP [25] and adopt
the denoising U-Net and a reference network initialized from
Stable Diffusion v1.5, where the temporal attention is warm-
started from AnimateDiff [37] to better capture motion dynam-
ics across frames.
5.3. Baseline Models.
We compare against representative open-source baselines span-
ning staged pose generation, GAN-based synthesis, and
diffusion-based video generation.ProTran[5] is a pro-
gressive transformer framework for text-to-pose sign genera-
tion.MoMP[38] improves pose generation with a mixture-
of-motion-primitives design.SignGAN[6] is a GAN-based
method for skeleton-conditioned photo-realistic sign video syn-
thesis.SignGen[8] is a diffusion-based sign video generator
that improves visual fidelity and temporal consistency.Sign-
ViP[25] is a recent strong baseline that adopts compressed
multi-condition tokenization for high-quality sign video gener-
ation.
5.4. Evaluation.
To assess whether generated signing preserves the meaning of
the input sentence, we follow ProTran [5] and adopt a back-
translation evaluation protocol: an SLT model [5] translates the
generated sign video back into text, and we compare the re-
covered text with the source sentence using BLEU [39] and
ROUGE [40]. For ablation studies, we additionally train a
pose-level back-translation model that operates directly on pre-
dicted pose latents, enabling rapid evaluation during RL post-
training without rendering videos (details in Appendix D). For
video quality, we report Fr´echet Video Distance (FVD) to quan-
tify distributional realism and temporal coherence of gener-
ated videos. We additionally measure identity preservation us-
ing an identity similarity (IDS) score computed from face em-
beddings, ensuring consistent signer appearance across frames.

5.5. Main Results.
Back-Translation Comparison.To quantitatively assess
the semantic fidelity of generated sign language videos, we
adopt video back-translation evaluation by translating gener-
ated videos back into text and comparing the recovered text with
the source sentence. As shown in Table 1, SignMatch consis-
tently achieves the best performance across both datasets. On
RWTH-2014T, SignMatch improves BLEU-4 from 7.9 to 11.3
over the strongest baseline SignViP, with additional gains in
ROUGE from 25.4 to 27.1 and COMET from 0.54 to 0.62. On
How2Sign, SignMatch also improves BLEU-4 from 4.5 to 5.1,
together with higher ROUGE from 16.5 to 17.1 and COMET
from 0.54 to 0.55. These results demonstrate that SignMatch
better preserves source semantics in the generated videos, sug-
gesting that aligning the intermediate motion latent space ef-
fectively improves semantic accuracy in the final rendered sign
videos.
Video Quality Comparison.We further compare the per-
ceptual quality of generated sign videos using visual and tem-
poral quality metrics. As shown in Table 2, SignMatch achieves
the best results across all reported metrics on both datasets.
Compared with the strongest baseline SignViP, SignMatch re-
duces FVD from 967 to 914 on RWTH-2014T and from 2103
to 2009 on How2Sign. It also improves IDS from 0.56 to
0.60 and SSIM from 0.68 to 0.70 on RWTH-2014T, as well
as IDS from 0.59 to 0.61 and SSIM from 0.63 to 0.65 on
How2Sign. These consistent improvements indicate that Sign-
Match produces more visually realistic and structurally faith-
ful sign videos while better preserving signer identity. Over-
all, the results validate the advantage of our two-stage design:
the LLM-guided pose latent diffusion model provides semanti-
cally meaningful and motion-plausible intermediate conditions,
while the motion-aware sign video diffusion model faithfully
renders them into high-quality signing videos.

6. Conclusion
We presented SignMatch, a unified diffusion-based framework
for sign language video generation that improves both seman-
tic fidelity and visual realism. SignMatch adopted a LLM-
empowered pose latent diffusion model for text-to-motion
translation and a motion-aware sign video diffusion renderer
conditioned on a reference signer image. To align generation
with downstream objectives beyond likelihood training, we fur-
ther introduced multi-dimensional RL post-training for the pose
latent generator using Flow-GRPO with semantic and visual re-
ward feedback, improving the intermediate motion latent space
while keeping the video renderer fixed. Experiments on RWTH-
2014T and How2Sign demonstrate that SignMatch consistently
outperforms strong prior methods in both semantic accuracy
and video quality.
