Abstract
Wireless acoustic sensor networks (W ASNs) enable spatial di-
versity for speech enhancement, but centralized processing
is often hindered by communication and computational con-
straints. While distributed adaptive node-specific signal es-
timation (DANSE) addresses this via distributed compressed-
signal exchange, its iterative covariance-based optimization re-
mains vulnerable to low-SNR and non-stationary conditions.
This paper proposes ANDFilter, an all-neural distributed filter-
ing framework that replaces iterative optimization with end-to-
end filter synthesis. The ANDFilter decomposes enhancement
into three stages: a single-node filter module (SNFM) for lo-
cal processing, a node selection module (NSM) for adaptive
inter-node weighting, and a multi-node filter module (MNFM)
for distributed spatial filtering. Experimental results on simu-
lated W ASNs with LibriSpeech and Freesound datasets show
that ANDFilter consistently outperforms state-of-the-art base-
lines in terms of PESQ, ESTOI, and DNSMOS across all SNR
ranges.
Index Terms: wireless acoustic sensor networks, distributed
speech enhancement, neural network, noise suppression
1. Introduction
Microphone arrays are extensively employed in speech-related
applications to improve signal quality by exploiting spatial di-
versity across multiple sensors[1, 2]. However, their efficacy is
fundamentally limited by array aperture, input signal-to-noise
ratio (SNR), and the power limitations of portable devices.
Wireless acoustic sensor networks (W ASNs) address these chal-
lenges by distributing multiple sensor nodes over a large spatial
area, facilitating flexible deployment and collaborative process-
ing through wireless communication [3, 4]. By harnessing spa-
tially diverse and high-SNR observations from different loca-
tions, W ASNs can achieve substantially improved performance
over conventional fixed-array systems.
To fully benefit from this distributed sensing capabil-
ity while avoiding centralized data aggregation, distributed
signal processing has emerged as a natural paradigm for
speech enhancement in W ASNs [5, 6, 7]. Representative
works include distributed adaptive node-specific signal esti-
mation (DANSE) [8], distributed linearly constrained mini-
mum variance (LCMV) beamforming [9], randomized-gossip
beamforming [10], rate-constrained spatial filtering [11], and
synchronization-aware processing for ad-hoc acoustic sensor

networks [12]. These methods achieve communication-efficient
enhancement by exchanging compressed inter-node signals in-
stead of raw multichannel recordings, but they generally rely on
explicit second-order statistics, synchronization assumptions,
and iterative filter updates, which may become unreliable in
low-SNR or non-stationary environments [13].
Driven by these limitations, recent works have explored in-
tegrating deep neural networks into W ASN-based speech en-
hancement [14, 15, 16]. These models typically estimate
time–frequency masks or auxiliary spatial cues, which improves
robustness to non-stationary noise and alleviates the depen-
dence on handcrafted statistical assumptions [16]. However, in
many existing approaches, deep learning is introduced only as
an auxiliary front-end or side module, while the core distributed
enhancement pipeline still relies on conventional linear filter-
ing, second-order statistics, and iterative numerical optimiza-
tion. As a result, the overall system remains sensitive to covari-
ance estimation errors, numerical instability, and convergence
issues, particularly in large-scale or low-SNR scenarios. More-
over, this hybrid architecture exacerbates system complexity, by
requiring the simultaneous maintenance of learned representa-
tions and statistical solvers, which limits scalability and hinders
end-to-end optimization in distributed networks.
To overcome these challenges, this paper proposes AND-
Filter, an end-to-end neural distributed filtering framework
for W ASNs that directly synthesizes frame-wise complex fil-
ter weights in a data-driven manner. The architecture com-
prises three specialized neural components: a single-node fil-
ter module (SNFM) for local spatial processing, a node selec-
tion module (NSM) for adaptive inter-node relevance weight-
ing, and a multi-node filter module (MNFM) for network-level
spatial fusion. By synergistically modeling local observations
and selectively aggregated inter-node information, ANDFilter
achieves robust distributed enhancement under strict commu-
nication constraints. Notably, the entire framework is trained
end-to-end via module-level supervision, obviating the need
for explicit covariance estimation and matrix inversion while
maintaining structural interpretability. Extensive experiments
on simulated W ASNs demonstrate that ANDFilter consistently
outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in speech quality, intelli-
gibility, and robustness across diverse acoustic conditions.

3. Proposed Approach
3.1. System overview
Although DANSE provides a distributed solution, it still re-
lies on second-order statistics and iterative matrix inversion,
which are fragile in low-SNR and non-stationary environments.
To overcome these limitations while retaining its distributed
and interpretable structure, we propose ANDFilter, which re-
formulates distributed filter estimation as a structured function
approximation problem. Unlike the classical DANSE, which
necessitates iterative covariance estimation and optimization-
based weight updates at each node, ANDFilter employs a feed-
forward architecture that directly synthesizes both local and
global filter weights from observed signals. This design ob-
viates explicit statistical estimation and iterative refinement,
yielding a principled, data-driven approximation of the fixed-
point solution implicitly targeted by DANSE. As illustrated in
Fig. 2(a), the proposed architecture is strategically aligned with
the canonical two-stage structure of DANSE, comprising three
tightly coupled modules: a single-node filter module (SNFM),
a node selection module (NSM), and a multi-node filter mod-
ule (MNFM). In terms of communication, ANDFilter follows
the same communication protocol as DANSE, where each node
transmits one compressed signal and performs enhancement us-
ing both local observations and received compressed signals.
3.2. SNFM
The SNFM corresponds to the local filtering stage of the
DANSE framework, where each node independently derives a
node-specific filter using only its local multichannel observa-
tions. In classical DANSE, the local filterw jj is implicitly
determined by the second-order statistics of the clean signal,
which are unavailable in practice and must be estimated through
iterative covariance computation and matrix inversion. In con-
trast, the SNFM is designed to directly infer an effective lo-
cal filter from noisy observations in a fully data-driven manner,
thereby bypassing explicit statistical estimation while preserv-
ing the functional role of node-wise Wiener filtering.
To this end, the real and imaginary (RI) components of
yj are concatenated along the channel dimension and jointly
processed to preserve phase information. The SNFM adopts
an encoder–S-TCN–decoder backbone to extract discriminative
spatio-spectral representations. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the
encoder follows a U 2-Encoder [17] design composed of gated
two-dimensional convolutional blocks with instance normaliza-
tion [18] and PReLU [19] activations, enabling adaptive feature
modulation across time–frequency bins. A subsequent spatio-
temporal convolutional network (S-TCN) further enlarges the
receptive field and captures long-range temporal dependencies
as well as inter-channel correlations [20] (see Fig. 2(c)). Fea-
ture reconstruction is performed by a mirrored U2-Decoder with
symmetric skip connections, as detailed in Fig. 2(d). From the
resulting high-level spatio-spectral representation, the local fil-
ter weights are synthesized through a dedicated filter-mapping
stage, which employs a lightweight recurrent structure to im-
plicitly model frame-wise temporal evolution, in lieu of explicit
covariance updates (see Fig. 2(e)). The synthesized filter is then
applied to generate a compressed scalar signal that serves as the
node’s communicated representation within the network.

3.4. MNFM
Following the NSM, the local observationy j is concatenated
with the refined compressed signal to form an augmented in-
put vector. This augmented representation is then mapped to
a global filter vector via the MNFM, which employs the same
model architecture as the SNFM but differs in the number of
input channels. The MNFM learns to synthesize filter weights
that approximate those of a centralized SDW-MWF using only
locally available observations and the NSM-refined compressed
signal.

4. Experimental Setup
The simulated acoustic environments are generated using the
Pyroomacoustics toolbox [25], where room impulse responses
(RIRs) are synthesized via the image source method [26]. Each
simulation employs a rectangular room with dimensions ran-
domly sampled within 3–8 m (length), 3–5 m (width), and 2.5–3
m (height), and a reverberation time (RT60) uniformly drawn
from 0.15 s to 0.4 s. A W ASN withJ= 4nodes is simulated,
where each node is equipped with a four-microphone (Mj = 4)
cross-shaped array with a fixed radius of 5 cm. Each scenario
contains one target source and one noise source. Both nodes
and sources are randomly placed in the room while maintain-
ing a minimum distance of 50 cm from each other and from the
room boundaries. The heights of the nodes and sources are in-
dependently sampled from [0.7, 2.0] m and [1.2, 2.0] m, respec-
tively, reflecting typical acoustic deployment conditions. The
target speech signals are drawn from the clean subset of Lib-
riSpeech [27]. All noise signals used for training, validation,
and testing are real-world environmental recordings collected
from Freesound [28]. The training and validation sets consist
of 15,000 and 1,500 noisy–clean utterance pairs, respectively,
with each utterance lasting approximately 10 seconds. The test-
ing set contains 500 utterances of similar duration. There is no
overlap in LibriSpeech speakers or Freesound recordings be-
tween the training sets and the testing set.
All signals are sampled at 16 kHz. STFT analysis is per-
formed using a 32 ms square-root Hann window with 50%over-
lap and 512-point FFT, resulting in 257 frequency bins. The

5. Results and Discussion
5.1. Ablation study
An ablation study is conducted to analyze the contributions of
nonlinear modeling, local observations, and compressed inter-
node communication in ANDFilter. Six configurations are eval-
uated: DANSE, SNFM, ONLYZ, MNFM, SNFM+MNFM, and
the full ANDFilter framework, with all results reported as aver-
ages over all nodes. Performance is evaluated using three met-
rics: PESQ [30], ESTOI [31] and DNSMOS [32]. As shown in
Table 1, the SNFM consistently outperforms classical DANSE,
indicating that nonlinear neural filtering can effectively com-
pensate for modeling inaccuracies caused by restrictive statis-
tical assumptions, even in the absence of distributed process-
ing, thereby highlighting the advantage of data-driven model-
ing over linear, covariance-based filtering. In contrast, configu-
rations relying solely on compressed inter-node signals (ON-
LYZ) exhibit clear performance degradation, suggesting that
such signals provide only coarse global representations and
lack sufficient local spatial detail for accurate speech enhance-
ment. When local observations are incorporated, MNFM signif-
icantly outperforms ONLYZ, underscoring the critical role of
spatially localized information in discriminative speech–noise
separation. Further comparisons among SNFM, MNFM, and
SNFM+MNFM demonstrate the complementary nature of lo-
cal observations and compressed inter-node information: while
each component alone yields limited gains, their integration en-
ables effective distributed information fusion and leads to con-
sistent performance improvements. The full ANDFilter frame-
work achieves the most robust overall performance, validating
the effectiveness of jointly combining nonlinear local filtering,
node selection, and distributed fusion within the proposed ar-
chitecture.

5.2. Comparison with baseline systems
We compare the proposed ANDFilter with five representa-
tive baselines, including centralized and distributed methods as
well as classical and learning-based approaches: C-MWF [33],
DANSE [8], C-PEVD [34], D-PEVD [35], and TANGO [16].
C-MWF represents the centralized multichannel Wiener filter-
ing upper bound under full data aggregation, while DANSE
serves as a classical distributed approximation via iterative
compressed-signal exchange. C-PEVD and D-PEVD corre-
spond to centralized and distributed PEVD-based diagonaliza-
tion filtering, respectively, and TANGO is a learning-based
distributed method leveraging DNN-estimated time–frequency
masks. Quantitative results over four input SNR ranges on
the testing set show that ANDFilter consistently outperforms
all baselines across all SNR bins. Centralized methods such
as C-MWF suffer from ill-conditioned covariance estimation
due to high-dimensional channel stacking, leading to numer-
ical instability and degraded performance in low-SNR con-
ditions. In contrast, DANSE benefits from operating on a
reduced-dimensional equivalent observation, which implicitly
regularizes covariance inversion and yields more stable en-
hancement. Similar trends are observed for C-PEVD and D-
PEVD. However, PEVD-based methods rely on idealized sub-
space separability assumptions and incur unavoidable spatial
information loss during compression and iterative approxima-
tion, which limits their effectiveness under adverse conditions.
TANGO improves perceptual speech quality through mask-
based separation but remains constrained by its reliance on
explicit time–frequency masking. Overall, the superior per-
formance of ANDFilter demonstrates that replacing iterative,
covariance-based processing with structured, data-driven filter
synthesis enables more robust and effective distributed speech
enhancement.
6. Conclusions
This paper proposes ANDFilter, a deep-learning framework for
speech enhancement in W ASNs. By combining distributed
cooperative processing with data-driven nonlinear modeling,
ANDFilter achieves robust performance under limited commu-
nication bandwidth and challenging low-SNR conditions. The
proposed approach decomposes the enhancement process into
local filtering, node selection, and global filtering stages, each
implemented by a dedicated neural module and optimized end-
to-end. Extensive experiments on LibriSpeech and Freesound
demonstrate that ANDFilter consistently outperforms state-of-
the-art centralized and distributed baselines across multiple ob-
jective metrics and SNR ranges, validating its effectiveness for
distributed microphone network scenarios.
