HistoMatch: Unified Transient-Steady Assessment for Noise-Robust
Semi-Supervised Speaker Verification

1 Key Lab of Speech Acoustics and Content Understanding, Institute of Acoustics, CAS, China

Abstract
Speaker verification systems have gained widespread applica-
tions across various domains, benefiting from advances in deep
learning. However, acquiring large-scale labeled data remains
challenging, making semi-supervised learning with pseudo-
labeling a critical research focus. Current pseudo-labeling ap-
proaches predominantly rely on confidence thresholds—a tran-
sient metric vulnerable to distortions induced by random seg-
mentation and noise augmentation. To overcome this limitation,
we propose HistoMatch, a novel mechanism that leverages his-
torical prediction stability as a steady-state metric. Combined
with transient confidence, it forms a Dual-state History-based
Stability Evaluator (DHSE) for high-quality unlabeled data se-
lection. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our semi-
supervised approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on
all three V oxCeleb1 test sets, with remarkable equal error rates
(EER) of 0.91%, 1.13%, and 2.13%—closely approaching fully
supervised benchmarks.
Index Terms: speaker verification, semi-supervised learning,
pseudo-labeling
1. Introduction
Speaker verification (SV) systems have advanced rapidly in re-
cent years, propelled by deep learning. While supervised learn-
ing has become a dominant approach for building these sys-
tems [1], [2], [3], it requires substantial labeled data—a require-
ment that proves challenging to satisfy in practical applications,
particularly in sensitive domains such as finance or healthcare.
Consequently, researchers have shifted their focus toward semi-
supervised speaker verification methods. This paradigm signif-
icantly reduces resource costs for model development by inte-
grating limited labeled data with large-scale unlabeled data dur-
ing training.
In the field of image classification, researchers widely
employ pseudo-label generation and consistency regulariza-
tion techniques [4]. These methods compel models to pro-
duce consistent predictions across diverse input perturbations,
thereby mitigating performance degradation caused by annota-
tion scarcity. Subsequent approaches built upon this framework
are broadly categorized as the Match paradigm. The paradigm’s
seminal work, FixMatch [5], establishes pseudo-label reliabil-
ity via a fixed high-confidence threshold. However, this fixed-
threshold mechanism may cause underutilization of data during
early training stages and lacks adaptability to sample-specific
learning difficulties. To address this, FlexMatch [6] introduces
locally adaptive thresholds that dynamically adjust to per-class
learning difficulty, derived from a predefined global thresh-

high-confidence pseudo-labels while suppressing noise through
confidence-based weighting, thereby enhancing utilization of

We evaluate the aforementioned methods on speaker ver-
ification tasks, observing merely marginal performance gains
and limited pseudo-label utilization — conclusions aligned
with Int*-Match’s findings. Int*-Match [9] demonstrates po-
tential for enhancing annotation efficiency through enforc-
ing intra-class compactness and inter-class discrepancy con-
straints. By contrast, SpeakerMatch [10] innovatively models
confidence distributions to unify pseudo-label selection in both
semi-supervised and self-supervised scenarios, employing joint
confidence-based filtering and temporal consistency alignment.
Although the temporal consistency alignment in SpeakerMatch
tracks historical prediction stability, standard speaker verifica-
tion pipelines rely on noise-augmented training with random-
ized segment slicing [11], [12], [13]. This alignment remains
susceptible to prediction drift due to heightened sensitivity to
acoustic variability, resulting in both reduced pseudo-label ac-
curacy and suboptimal utilization of valid training samples.
To enhance both pseudo-label accuracy and sample uti-
lization rate of the *match framework in speaker verification
tasks, this work proposes the Dual-state History-based Stabil-
ity Evaluator (DHSE): a pseudo-label filtering architecture in-
corporating transient-steady fusion mechanisms. By perform-
ing statistical significance analysis on temporal predictions and
enabling collaborative decision-making with live model con-
fidence, DHSE constructs a dynamic error tolerance system.
Specifically, the main contributions of our work are as follows:
• We propose Histomatch, a framework specifically designed
for speaker verification that integrates the DHSE with
transient-steady analysis to significantly enhance robust uti-
lization efficiency of training data.
• Extensively validated on public benchmarks, our method
consistently surpasses existing approaches on EER under
identical annotation budgets. It establishes new state-of-
the-art (SOTA) performance with an EER of 0.91% on the
V oxCeleb1-O test set.

(DHSE) that jointly leverages transient-state fluctuation met-

pseudo-labels. This innovation significantly enhances model re-
silience against three disturbances: (a) data augmentation vari-
ances; (b) randomized slicing divergence; (c) intrinsic speech
variability, while improving data utilization efficiency. The
overall overview of the architecture is presented in Figure 1.

4. Experiments
4.1. Dataset Setup
We train our framework on the V oxCeleb2 [17] dataset, contain-
ing 1,092,009 utterances from 5,994 speakers. For evaluation,
we utilize the V oxCeleb1 [18] dataset with its three standard test
sets: V oxCeleb1-O/E/H. Following the experimental protocol
in [9], we designate{4, 10, 20} samples per speaker as labeled
data, with the remaining utterances serving as unlabeled data.
At the 10-sample configuration, labeled data constitutes 6% of
the total dataset.
4.2. Training Setup
We employ FixMatch, FlexMatch, SoftMatch, and FreeMatch
as baseline methods whose loss function designs are modified
from their original versions as detailed in Section 2. Both our
framework and baselines utilize an ECAPA-TDNN [19] back-
bone network with 1024 channels (ECAPA-L) to ensure eq-
uitable comparison. Notably, partial ablation experiments are
conducted using an ECAPA-TDNN backbone network with 512
channels (ECAPA-S). Training employs a labeled batch size
of 32, unlabeled ratio µ = 7 , 4 seconds input segments, and
MUSAN combined with RIRS-NOISES for data augmentation.
The 60-epoch training schedule spans approximately 290k steps
including 10 epochs of warm-up where the learning rate in-
creases linearly from 0 to 0.3, then decays via cosine annealing
to 1× 10−4. Our method initializes τt = 1

6. Conclusion
Our framework integrates Dual-state History-based Stability
Evaluator for semi-supervised speaker verification. The DHSE
synergistically integrates transient and steady-state dual thresh-
olds to screen high-quality pseudo-labels. Crucially, it leverages
the statistical distribution of class-prediction probabilities as a
steadiness metric to recover high-quality pseudo-labels lost due
to confidence prediction failures caused by random segmenta-
tion and noise augmentation. Experimental results establish our
method as the new SOTA, achieving performance competitive
with fully supervised approaches.
