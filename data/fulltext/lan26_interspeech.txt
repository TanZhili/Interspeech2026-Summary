SA-UAED: Joint Frame-Level Detection of Audio Events, Speaker Activities,
and Speaker-Attributed Paralinguistic Events

Abstract
Existing sound event detection (SED) and speaker diarization
(SD) systems typically fail to attribute paralinguistic events
(e.g., laughter, coughing) to individual speakers, primarily due
to the scarcity of fine-grained labeled data. To address this, we
propose a dedicated simulation pipeline generating large-scale
audio mixtures with precise frame-level annotations. Leverag-
ing this, we introduce the Speaker-Attributed Unified Audio
Event Detection (SA-UAED) framework for joint frame-level
prediction of sound events, speaker activities, and paralinguis-
tics. Experiments demonstrate that SA-UAED substantially im-
proves speaker-attributed paralinguistic detection without com-
promising generic SED and SD accuracy.
Index Terms: Speaker diarization, paralinguistic event detec-
tion, sound event detection, audio simulation
1. Introduction
Real-world acoustic environments are highly complex, often
comprising simultaneous multi-speaker speech, paralinguistic
events (such as laughter and coughing), and diverse background
sound events. These overlapping acoustic phenomena are ubiq-
uitous in everyday scenarios, including family homes, open-
plan offices, and clinical monitoring rooms. For instance, a
spoken dialogue system must distinguish a user’s laughter from
background music and another person’s cough to provide appro-
priate responses, while healthcare systems rely on accurate de-
tection of coughing patterns alongside speech to monitor respi-
ratory conditions remotely. Accurate, frame-level detection and
separation of these heterogeneous events are therefore essen-
tial for enabling robust, context-aware audio applications such
as intelligent meeting transcription with emotional cues, multi-
party dialogue systems, and real-time patient surveillance.
Driven by the rapid development of speech foundation
models, fine-grained paralinguistic annotations and understand-
ing of speech data have also attracted increasing research atten-
tion [1, 2]. While existing sound event detection (SED) [3, 4, 5]
and speaker diarization (SD) [6, 7, 8, 9] techniques can detect
general audio events and speaker occurrences, they often cannot
associate paralinguistic events (e.g., laughter, cough, etc.) with
individual speakers [10]. Recent efforts toward a unified frame-
work, most notably the Transformer-based Unified Audio Event
Detection (T-UAED) [10] model, have demonstrated the feasi-
bility of jointly modeling non-speech sound events and speaker-
aware speech activities by conditioning on speaker embeddings.
However, T-UAED still suffers from two critical shortcomings.
First, it completely ignores paralinguistic events that frequently
co-occur with speech. This is partially caused by the lack of
sufficient speech data with diverse speakers and fine-grained
speaker-attributed paralinguistic labels. Second, it relies on
generic speaker embeddings for speaker distinction, which are
sub-optimal for capturing the short-duration and highly variable
spectral and temporal characteristics of paralinguistic events.
In particular, our preliminary experiments suggest that existing
speaker embeddings fail to reliably distinguish the laughter and
cough of different speakers, despite their strong performance on
speaker recognition benchmarks.
In this paper, we aim to address the aforementioned limi-
tations in both data and modeling aspects, ultimately building
a unified audio event detection (UAED) system capable of de-
tecting speaker-attributed paralinguistic events, speech activi-
ties, and general audio events. Our contributions can be sum-
marized as follows:
1. We propose a dedicated audio simulation pipeline to ac-
commodate the development of UAED systems with par-
alinguistic modeling. This simulation pipeline overcomes
the prohibitive cost and annotation challenges of real-world
multi-label data while providing controlled, perfectly aligned
ground truth.
2. Exploiting the proposed data simulation pipeline, we intro-
duce LibriPara, a large-scale 500-hour simulated dataset of
16 kHz audio featuring multi-speaker speech and paralinguis-
tics, along with frame-level event annotations.
3. We present SA-UAED (Speaker-Attributed Unified Audio
Event Detection), a novel framework for joint frame-level
detection of general audio events, speaker activities, and par-
alinguistic events. It is tailored for associating paralinguistic
events with speaker identities, requiring minimal modifica-
tions to existing UAED models without compromising SED
and SD performance.
2. Related Work
2.1. Sound Event Detection and Acoustic Representations
Sound Event Detection (SED) has evolved from foundational
CNNs (e.g. PANNs [3]) to powerful Transformer-based archi-
tectures like BEATs [11] and Whisper [12] variants. While
these models excel at capturing diverse environmental sounds,
standalone SED systems typically lack the speaker-aware con-
text necessary to disentangle targeted human vocalizations from
complex, overlapping multi-talker backgrounds.
2.2. Speaker Diarization and Speaker-Attributed Paralin-
guistic Detection
Modern speaker diarization relies on robust embeddings (e.g.
x-vectors [13] or ECAPA-TDNN [14]) explicitly optimized to
capture articulated speech traits while deliberately suppress-

identity consistency during such events.
2.3. Unified Audio Understanding Frameworks
To bridge the gap between speech and non-speech analysis,
unified frameworks like the Transformer-based Unified Audio
Event Detection (T-UAED) [10] model elegantly enable joint
SED and diarization by conditioning a shared architecture on
pre-extracted speaker embeddings. However, they harbor a crit-
ical blind spot: the assumption that a generic speech-derived
embedding can generalize to all human vocalizations. Be-
cause existing models route non-verbal vocalizations through
mismatched, speech-biased embedding pipelines, they fail to
robustly disentangle speaker identities during paralinguistic
events. This architectural gap necessitates the task-specific rep-
resentation decoupling strategy proposed in our work.
3. Methodology
3.1. Data Simulation Strategies
To address the severe scarcity of multi-label audio data featur-
ing overlapping speech and paralinguistics, we design a highly-
controlled audio simulation pipeline leveraging state-of-the-art
zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) systems. As shown in Figure 1,
it consists of four phases:
(1) Audio Script Generation:As the foundational step of
our simulation pipeline, we employ a stochastic audio script
generator to orchestrate the complex temporal dynamics of the
acoustic scene. To facilitate fine-grained labeling of each sam-
ple, this generator synthesizes a deterministic, text-based recipe
(as illustrated in the lower-left corner of Figure 1) prior to wave-
form rendering. This script meticulously defines the precise on-
set and offset timestamps, event categories (e.g. speech, other
vocal sounds, and environmental events), and explicit speaker
identities for every vocal event. The audio script generator
can be configured to simulate different speech overlap ratios,
speaker counts, occurrences of paralinguistic and general au-
dio events, ensuring controllability and density. Crucially, this
pre-defined timeline also greatly reduces the effort needed for
fine-grained data labeling.
(2) Speaker-Specific Paralinguistic Generation:The
core challenge in paralinguistic diarization is ensuring consis-
tency between a speaker’s articulated speech and their spon-
taneous non-verbal vocalizations. To achieve this, we employ
an advanced zero-shot TTS model, ChatterBox-Turbo [15], for
speaker-specific paralinguistic sound generation. In this pa-
per, we considerlaughterandcoughas two representative
paralinguistic events, which can be directly generated by the
ChatterBox-Turbo model via special tokens (i.e.,[cough]and
[laugh]) in the transcript. We take the audio clips of each
speaker in the LibriSpeech [16] corpus as acoustic prompts. The
TTS model then synthesizes isolated, speaker-specific “pure
laugh” and “pure cough” tracks. To guarantee the acoustic fi-
delity and purity of these events, we apply a pre-trained SED
model, PANNs [3], as an automated quality filter to elimi-
nate samples whose detection confidence score is low. Subse-
quently, we process all retained clips through an energy-based
V AD module to trim the leading and trailing silence (or artificial
pauses) generated by the TTS engine, yielding tightly-bounded
paralinguistic sounds. These synthetic clips are then ready for
time-aligned mixing and frame-level annotation.
(3) Mixed audio generation:Guided by the pre-generated
audio script, the validated paralinguistic clips are mixed with
concatenated LibriSpeech utterances to form a meeting-style
audio session. Following [10], the background events include
nine classes of audio events from the DESED [17] soundbank.
This rendering process faithfully executes the multi-talker over-
lap protocol defined by the script, seamlessly aggregating all
isolated audio streams into complex acoustic mixtures with
well-aligned ground-truth labels.
(4) Label Generation:Because the entire multi-stream
mixture is constructed automatically through the proposed mix-
ing engine, we bypass the pervasive issue of human annota-
tion error or forced-alignment inaccuracies. The exact start and
end timestamps of every speech segment, paralinguistic burst,
and background event are deterministically aggregated to form
frame-level ground-truth labels.

Built upon the above pipeline, we create a novel multi-
label multi-speaker dataset,LibriParaby combining clean
speech from Librispeech, synthesized paralinguistic clips, and
background audios from DESED. It contains 500 hours of au-
dios with multiple speakers at an overlap ratio of 15%, speaker-
specific paralinguistic events, various background events, and
fine-grained frame-level labels. This large-scale dataset can
serve as a benchmark for developing speaker-attributed UAED
systems. The audio simulation pipeline and dataset will be
open-sourced at https://github.com/originallover/SA-UAED.
3.2. SA-UAED Architecture
Our proposed SA-UAED model is designed as a novel archi-
tectural paradigm for unified audio understanding. It builds
upon the foundational encoder-decoder structure of the T-
UAED [10] model, which was originally designed to unify SED
and SD in a single model. The auditory encoder utilizes pre-
trained slef-supervised learning (SSL) models such as BEATs
and WavLM [18] to extract rich audio representations. The
Transformer-based decoder takes as input a series of learn-
able queries (corresponding to different types of audio events)
and speaker embeddings to autoregressively generate frame-
level activities of different sound events. While effective for
its original tasks, the generic speaker embeddings in T-UAED
are sub-optimal for modeling transient, non-verbal paralinguis-
tic sounds such as laughter and coughing. As shown later in our
experiments, the direct use of speaker embeddings for speaker-
attributed paralinguistic event detection is problematic, showing
poor F1 scores across two test sets.
To address this limitation, our proposed SA-UAED incor-
porates dedicated, task-specific fully-connected (FC) layers to
project the generic speaker embeddings into distinct paralin-
guistic sub-spaces. The outputs of these dedicated FC layers are
then used as additional paralinguistic queries in the Transformer
decoder, operating in parallel with the original speaker embed-
dings and the learnable queries for audio events. The rest of the
architecture (encoder, cross-attention, dot-product prediction,
and binary cross-entropy loss) remains identical to the original
T-UAED, ensuring seamless multi-task learning and minimal
additional parameters. This design enables a single end-to-end
model to simultaneously predict sound events, speaker activi-
ties, and speaker-attributed paralinguistic events at 50 Hz frame
resolution.
4. Experimental Setup
All experiments are conducted on our proposed LibriPara
dataset, comprising a 500-hour training set and a held-out 5-
hour simulated test set generated via the pipeline described in
Section 3.1. All audio clips are standardized to 32 seconds at
a 16 kHz sampling rate, accompanied by frame-level annota-
tions at a 50 Hz resolution. To closely simulate realistic con-
versational dynamics, each clip contains up to 3 distinct speak-
ers, with the audio script generator enforcing a rigorously con-
trolled average speech overlap ratio of 15%. During the mixing
phase, 9 classes of background sound events from the DESED
soundbank are injected at a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) rang-
ing from 5 dB to 15 dB. Conversely, speaker-specific paralin-
guistics (generated via ChatterBox-Turbo with LibriSpeech ref-
erences) are mixed at a tighter SNR range of 3 dB to 8 dB to
preserve their impulsive acoustic characteristics.
4.1. Baselines
To ensure a fair comparison, we reproduce the original
Transformer-based Unified Audio Event Detection (T-UAED)
framework from scratch on our identical simulated dataset, us-
ing the exact same data splits, mixing parameters, and frame-
level labels. The original T-UAED serves as the strong baseline,
treating laughter and coughing as part of the general “speech”.
To the best of our knowledge, SA-UAED is the first unified
end-to-end framework tackling speaker-attributed paralinguis-
tic event detection. While conventional cascade systems exist,
they inherently suffer from severe error propagation in highly
overlapped multi-talker scenarios. Therefore, we deliberately
select the state-of-the-art unified architecture, T-UAED, as our
primary baseline to isolate and demonstrate the impact of our
proposed representation decoupling strategy.
4.2. Evaluation Metrics
To comprehensively assess the systems’ performance across di-
verse acoustic tasks, we adopt standard evaluation metrics tai-
lored for high-resolution audio event detection and diarization.
For the detection of background sound events and speaker-
attributed paralinguistic events, we report both the Intersection-
Based F1 (IB-F1) [19] for event-level macro-detection and
the Segment-Based F1 (SB-F1) [20], which are available
athttps://github.com/fgnt/sed_scores_eval/.
Since our proposed simulation pipeline can directly provide
frame-level ground-truth labels, we compute the SB-F1 metric
evaluated at a high temporal resolution (matched to our 50 Hz /
20 ms frame rate) as our primary metric. This allows strict and
precise quantification of the model’s frame-by-frame tracking
capability in dense, overlapping acoustic scenarios. Further-
more, to evaluate the systems’ ability of speaker diarization,
we report the standard Diarization Error Rate (DER), which
comprises the summation of Miss (MS), False Alarm (FA), and
Speaker Confusion (SC) errors.

audio features and UAED Queries. Specifically, the generic
speaker embeddings acting as initial queries are extracted us-
ing a pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN model [21]. Following [10],
the Transformer encoder and decoder each consist of 6 layers

5. Results and Analysis
To rigorously evaluate our framework and its generalizability,
we train all models from scratch solely on LibriPara (our 500-
hour simulated dataset). The evaluation is systematically di-
vided into two phases: assessing foundational multi-task learn-
ing capabilities on a held-out simulated test set (Table 1), and
verifying zero-shot generalization on a real-world test set (Ta-
ble 2) derived from the EARS [22] corpus, which provides both
speech and paralinguistic audio clips from 107 speakers.
5.1. Performance on the Simulated Benchmark
As shown in Table 1, while the Baseline demonstrates com-
petence in generic sound event detection (SED with SB-F1 of
0.984), it exhibits a catastrophic performance drop when iden-
tifying speaker-attributed paralinguistics (SB-F1 of 0.371 for
laughter and 0.282 for cough). Notably, introducing a Shared
Adapter does not resolve this, it severely degrades paralinguis-
tic accuracy below the baseline. Forcing mutually exclusive
acoustic events (rhythmic laughter vs. explosive coughing) into
a shared projection subspace causes severe representation en-
tanglement, thus greatly degrading the performance.
In contrast, our proposed SA-UAED model significantly
circumvents this bottleneck by routing identity features into dis-
tinct, decoupled event sub-spaces. This task-specific adapta-
tion achieves massive absolute improvements across both event-
level (IB-F1) and frame-level (SB-F1) metrics, boosting the SB-
F1 of laughter to 0.476 and coughing to 0.473, without notice-
ably impacting the DER.
5.2. Generalization to Real-World EARS Mixtures
To ensure the model does not merely overfit to TTS artifacts,
we benchmark the models on the EARS [22]-based test set (Ta-
ble 2), which is simulated following the same procedure as
described in Section 3.1. To strictly isolate the evaluation of
the paralinguistic sub-spaces from potential frontend speaker
enrollment errors, we extracted the reference speaker embed-
dings via a pre-trained ECAPA-TDNN model [21], using a 5-
second clean, non-overlapping speech segment from each target
speaker prior to the mixture inference.
Table 2 provides compelling evidence of SA-UAED’s zero-
shot generalization to genuine human vocalizations across both
evaluation resolutions. On the macro level, the consistent
gains in IB-F1 confirm that our decoupling strategy success-
fully transfers to spontaneous human behaviors, robustly cap-
turing “in-the-wild” paralinguistic events. More impressively,
on the micro level, the SB-F1 for EARS-based laughter detec-
tion leaps from 0.259 (Baseline) to 0.409, and cough from 0.375
to 0.470. This demonstrates that even when facing highly irreg-
ular, real-world human vocal bursts, SA-UAED maintains ex-
ceptional temporal boundary fidelity, proving the sheer robust-
ness of the proposed task-specific adaptation.
Both tables reveal an intriguing trade-off: the substantial
gains in paralinguistic detection come at the cost of a marginal
degradation in generic SED performance (e.g. dropping slightly
from 0.984 to 0.979 on simulated data). This phenomenon high-
lights a classic challenge of negative transfer. By forcing the
shared acoustic frontend to simultaneously optimize for con-
tinuous articulated speech, environmental sounds, and transient
paralinguistic bursts, the shared representation space inherently
shifts. Future unified audio frameworks may need to explore de-
coupled audio encoders to eliminate representation interference
at the feature extraction stage.
6. Conclusion
This paper addresses the limitations of existing unified au-
dio systems in speaker-attributed paralinguistic detection. We
propose a highly controlled simulation pipeline for generat-
ing complex acoustic mixtures with precise frame-level multi-
label annotations, and use it to construct LibriPara, a 500-hour
multi-talker dataset integrating continuous speech, background
sounds, and speaker-specific paralinguistics. We further intro-
duce SA-UAED for joint frame-level detection of audio events,
speaker activities, and speaker-attributed paralinguistic events.
Future work will explore self-supervised representation align-
ment and open-set vocalization detection.
