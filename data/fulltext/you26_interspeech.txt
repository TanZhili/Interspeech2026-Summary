Uncovering the Impact of G2P Precision on Korean TTS: A Large-Scale
Statistical Validation via a Novel Morphological Engine
Heejo You ID 1,∗∗, Sungwoo Moon ID 1
1 Robotics Lab, Hyundai Motor Group, Republic of Korea
hjyou@hyundai.com, sungwoo.moon@hyundai.com
Abstract
Grapheme-to-Phoneme (G2P) conversion is vital for TTS, yet the
widely used Korean g2pk library suffers from high latency and
inaccurate phonological modeling at word boundaries. Such in-
accuracies act as "label noise," degrading TTS performance and
training efficiency. To resolve this, we propose a novel rule-based
G2P engine featuring an object-oriented data structure with
syllable-morpheme connectivity and a recursive re-evaluation
mechanism for sequential phonological rules.
Rigorous benchmarking shows that the proposed engine sig-
nificantly outperforms g2pk in both speed (3.14ms vs. 14.98ms,
p <0.001 ) and accuracy ( 85.7 % vs. 27.2 %). Furthermore,
large-scale statistical validation via 96 VITS models (32 per
condition) demonstrates that our engine achieves statistically
significant improvements in intelligibility. The results confirm
that our approach enhances not only the final audio quality but
also the overall training efficiency of TTS models by providing
consistent phonetic representations.
Index Terms: text-to-speech, grapheme-to-phoneme, Korean
speech synthesis, statistic analysis
1. Introduction
Modern End-to-End (E2E) TTS models are designed to learn
complex alignments between input text and output audio [1, 2, 3].
In this process, using phonemes instead of graphemes as in-
put has become a standard approach; this method reduces the
model’s training burden by establishing a more transparent re-
lationship between input and output [ 4, 5]. However, this ap-
proach remains valid only when the Grapheme-to-Phoneme
(G2P) converter-a critical preprocessing step-guarantees near-
perfect accuracy. If the G2P converter generates inaccurate
phoneme sequences, it acts as ’Label Noise’ by providing incor-
rect ground truth to the model, serving as a critical factor that
impedes training convergence and degrades synthesis quality.
In the case of the Korean language, phonological rules are
deeply intertwined with morphological boundaries, making G2P
conversion significantly challenging [6]. The open-source library
g2pk [7], currently used as a standard in many studies, possesses
structural limitations that fail to sufficiently reflect these char-
acteristics of Korean. First, its slow processing speed creates a
bottleneck in real-time services or large-scale data preprocessing.
Second, it utilizes morphological information only superficially,
failing to accurately process liaison rules or irregular conjuga-
tions at word boundaries. Such inaccuracy in g2pk induces the
aforementioned label noise problem, consequently limiting the
upper bound of Korean TTS model performance.

To address these issues, this paper proposes a novel rule-
based Korean G2P engine that achieves deep morphological
integration of analysis data and phonological rules. By providing
a deterministic and highly interpretable baseline, this engine
resolves the structural flaws of existing open-source tools like
g2pk, which is essential for bootstrapping reliable training data
for future neural models. The proposed engine is designed with
an object-oriented structure that allows each syllable to directly
access morphological information. Furthermore, it introduces
a recursive rule re-evaluation mechanism to accurately model
the cascading phonological changes unique to Korean. The key
contributions of this study are as follows:
• Architecture: A novel high-speed, high-precision G2P archi-
tecture resolving existing g2pk bottlenecks.
• Performance: Achieves 4.7 times faster processing than g2pk
with over 85 % accuracy.
• Statistical Validation: A large-scale validation via 96 VITS
models empirically demonstrates that high-precision G2P sig-
nificantly improves TTS training efficiency and intelligibility

2. Related Works
Research on Korean G2P can be broadly categorized into rule-
based approaches and data-driven neural network approaches.
2.1. Rule-based G2P Systems
Rule-based systems typically evolve based on how they incor-
porate linguistic information. KoG2P is a representative early
model that sequentially applies phonological rules defined in a
rulebook. While it achieves high processing speeds by bypass-
ing morphological analysis, it fails to guarantee the accuracy
required for high-quality TTS, as it cannot resolve ambiguities
tied to morphological boundaries.
To address these limitations, g2pk introduced the mecab-ko
morphological analyzer [ 7]. However, its architecture suffers
from a "loose coupling" where morphological data is only super-
ficially utilized, leading to significant bottlenecks in both speed
and accuracy. Although various forks of g2pk have attempted to
improve the system, they have primarily focused on bug fixes and
compatibility issues rather than resolving the fundamental archi-
tectural flaws. Consequently, these systems remain inadequate
for processing the complex, cascading phonological phenomena
of the Korean language.

To circumvent the complexity of rule-based systems, neural
network-based End-to-End G2P models have become a promi-
nent trend. For instance, Transformer-based models like NN-

This raises critical concerns for G2P systems. First, the lack of a
linguistic foundation makes debugging difficult; neural models
are prone to unpredictable and catastrophic errors in out-of-
vocabulary (OOV) or rare combinations. In contrast, rule-based
systems offer predictability and allow for immediate, targeted
corrections by modifying specific rules. Second, the performance
of neural models is bound by the quality of the training data.
High-quality datasets often require manual labor by linguistic
experts, and in many cases, are bootstrapped using rule-based en-
gines before being refined. This implies that the performance of
rule-based G2P is a prerequisite for the successful development
of neural-based systems.
Another recent trend involves utilizing Large Language Mod-
els (LLMs) to resolve homograph ambiguities through contex-
tual and semantic reasoning [ 11, 12]. Despite their powerful
performance, these models suffer from the inherent limitations
of LLMs, including hallucinations and extreme computational
costs [13, 14]. As previously noted, employing an LLM—which
can be thousands of times larger than the TTS model itself—as a
pre-processor creates a significant "computational asymmetry,"
making it impractical for real-time, efficient TTS deployment.
3. Proposed Method
To accurately model the irregular phonological variations of
Korean, we propose a G2P engine featuring deep morphological
integration and a priority-based iterative rule mechanism.
3.1. Hierarchical Data Structure and Morphological Inte-
gration
Unlike existing methods (e.g., g2pk) that merely refer to mor-
phological analysis results as simple text strings, the proposed
engine features a Hierarchical Pointer Structure where Syllable
objects directly point to their constituent Morpheme objects.
3.1.1. Structure
Using the Kiwi analyzer, input text is parsed into a hierarchy:
Sentence → Eojeol → Syllable [15]. Each Syllable object main-
tains a pointer to its constituent Morpheme and stores both its
initial graphemes and current phonemes (see Figure 1).
3.1.2. Instant Boundary Detection
Since each Syllable links to its morphological information via
pointers, the engine can determine whether two adjacent sylla-
bles belong to the same morpheme (intra-morpheme) or lie on
a boundary (inter-morpheme) with a time complexity of O(1).
This capability serves as a critical foundation for accurately ap-
plying phonological rules that depend on morphological bound-
aries, as seen in the distinction between ’겉에’ (geot-e, ’surface’
+ locative particle ’on’; /k2.the/, liaison) and ’ 겉옷’ (geot-ot,
’outer’ + ’clothes’;/k2.tot^/, implosion + liaison).
3.2. Rule-specific Isolated Dictionaries
To handle irregularities tied to etymological origins (e.g., Sino-
Korean vs. Native Korean), we introduce Rule-specific Isolated
Dictionaries.

3.3. Priority-based Iterative Rule Engine
Korean phonology involves "chain reactions" where one change
triggers another. To simulate this, we implement a recursive
re-evaluation mechanism following the Single Responsibility
Principle (SRP).
3.3.1. Mechanism
The engine sequentially scans rules based on a predefined prior-
ity hierarchy. The priority assignment logic is strictly governed
by the Single Responsibility Principle (SRP), ensuring that foun-
dational rules (e.g., simple consonant assimilation) precede and
naturally trigger context-sensitive, downstream rules (e.g., com-
plex tensification). If a specific rule is applied to a syllable and
changes a phoneme, the engine immediately stops the scan and
resets the cursor to the first syllable of the corresponding Eojeol.
3.3.2. Handling Chain Reactions
This re-evaluation process repeats until no further rules can be
applied. For example, in the case of ’ 앞날’(/am.nal/), the im-
plosion applies in the first pass to produce /ab.nal/, and after
the reset, the nasalization applies in the second pass to finally
converge to /am.nal/. This approach perfectly simulates simul-
taneous phonological changes using a procedural algorithm.
4. Experimental Setup
To overcome the "best-case vs. worst-case" comparison fallacy
caused by deep learning’s inherent randomness (e.g., initial
weights, minibatch orders), we adopted a social science sta-
tistical framework.
4.1. Standalone G2P Benchmarking
4.1.1. Latency
Using 3,000 KSS sentences, we measured processing time 16
times per pattern to eliminate OS background noise [ 16]. A
2918
paired t-test was used to verify the significance of speed gains.
4.1.2. Accuracy
To measure the model’s accuracy and PER, we established a
ground truth dataset comprising 925 sentences. These sentences
were carefully curated from the KSS corpus to ensure a diverse
distribution of complex grapheme combinations and phonolog-
ical edge cases. All ground-truth labels were then manually
verified by three specialists in Korean literature and phonetics.
4.2. Downstream TTS Validation
To verify the impact of G2P quality, we trained a total of 96
independent VITS models, treating each instance as a participant
in a group-wise comparison.
4.2.1. Training Groups
Three groups (Grapheme, G2PK, Proposed; n= 32 each) were
trained for 500k steps. To ensure variance, we avoided fixed
seeds, allowing each model to have unique initial weights and
batch sequences. Hyperparameters followed [17] but with a re-
fined learning rate1.0e−4and batch size 48.
4.2.2. Evaluation
Intelligibility was measured via ASR (Whisper large-v3-turbo,
beam search= 1) by comparing transcribed output with original
scripts [18]. Audio quality was assessed via PESQ and WV-MOS
[19, 20].
4.2.3. Statistical Rigor
To apply the Central Limit Theorem, representative scores were
calculated per model [21]. Outliers outside the 95 % confidence
interval (|z|>1.96 ) were removed to prevent training failures
(e.g., attention collapse) from distorting the results. Finally, one-
way ANOV A and Fisher’s LSD post-hoc tests were performed.
5. Results and Analysis
5.1. G2P Performance
The proposed engine demonstrated overwhelming superiority
over g2pk in both efficiency and precision.
5.1.1. Inference Speed
The proposed model achieved an average processing time of
3.14ms, approximately 8.7 times faster than g2pk (14.98ms),
with high statistical significance ( t(2999) = 778.117, p <
0.001). Regression analysis showed that while our engine has
a steeper slope due to its recursive nature ( O(n2)), its mini-
mized initialization costs provide a significant lead for practical
text lengths. The cross-over point is estimated at 158 characters.
Based on our calculation of 118.6ms of audio per character, this
equates to 18.7s of speech—well beyond the average conversa-
tional utterance length of 7s (see Figure 2).
5.1.2. Conversion Accuracy
Evaluated on strict sentence-level accuracy—where a single
phoneme error invalidates the entire sentence—the baseline
G2PK achieved 27.2 %, whereas our model attained 85.7 %. In
terms of CER, G2PK recorded 0.021 compared to our model’s
0.002, demonstrating a reduction in error rate by more than ten-
fold. Qualitative analysis revealed that the model successfully
handles various phonological phenomena including liaison and
gemination. Nevertheless, we also identified certain limitations,
such as morphological parsing errors, which affected both accu-
racy and CER metrics. Table 1 shows representative examples
from the qualitative analysis.

5.2. Impact on TTS
ANOV A results across 96 models confirm that G2P precision is
directly proportional to TTS performance.
5.2.1. Intelligibility (CER)
The ’Proposed’ group significantly outperformed all other groups

cant difference between the ’G2PK’ and ’Grapheme’ conditions

G2P provides no benefit over raw text and acts as ’Label Noise’
that impedes early-stage convergence.
5.2.2. Acoustic Quality & Trade-off
No significant differences were found in PESQ (p= 0.265 ) and
WV-MOS (p= 0.123 ). This finding is crucial; it proves that
our engine achieves a 8-fold speedup and superior intelligibility
without any trade-off in acoustic naturalness, resolving common
concerns regarding rule-based complexity and prosodic loss (see
Figure 5).

6.2. Methodological Suggestions for Generative Models
The conventional "single-seed" comparison often falls into the
trap of accidental results, where the "best-case" of a new model
is unfairly compared against the "worst-case" of a baseline. Such
biases can mislead the entire direction of research in the field
[23, 24]. By performing a large-scale ANOV A on 96 models, this
study provides a robust framework that isolates the true effect of
the algorithm from random initialization noise. We propose this
statistical rigor as a new standard for enhancing reproducibility
and reliability in generative model research.
6.3. Extension to Multilingual TTS
In Multilingual TTS, where multiple languages share a com-
mon embedding space, the precision of each language’s G2P is
paramount. Inaccurate mappings in one language do not merely
degrade that language’s quality; they introduce interference that
corrupts the representation quality of shared phoneme embed-
dings [25]. This degrades the synthesis quality across the entire
system. Thus, our high-precision G2P engine serves as a vital

7. Limitations and Future Work
7.1. Structural and Semantic Constraints
Our engine’s precision is intrinsically tied to the underlying mor-
phological analyzer (Kiwi); parsing failures from neologisms
or spacing errors can propagate into cascading rule misapplica-
tions [15]. Furthermore, as a morpho-phonology-based system,
it struggles with semantic homograph ambiguities (e.g., rival
vs. silence) that require deep contextual reasoning beyond the
morphological level.
7.2. Future Directions
Future research will focus on developing a hybrid architecture
with lightweight contextual modules to resolve homographs. Fur-
thermore, rather than treating rule-based and neural approaches
as mutually exclusive, we plan to utilize our high-precision rule
engine to bootstrap reliable training data and explicit morpho-
logical features for future neural G2P models, mitigating their
hallucination risks. Additionally, we plan to enhance system
robustness by updating our lexical resources with contemporary
corpora to better reflect modern linguistic trends.
8. Conclusion
This study identified structural inefficiencies in existing Korean
G2P libraries and proposed a high-performance engine featuring
a hierarchical object-oriented structure and a priority-based re-
cursive rule mechanism. Experimental results showed an 8-fold
speedup and significant accuracy gains (85.7 %) over the base-
line. Moreover, a large-scale statistical validation across 96 TTS
models empirically established that high-precision G2P is vital
for enhancing both training efficiency and output fidelity. By
demonstrating how G2P precision dictates TTS training dynam-
ics, this work extends beyond a simple performance comparison.
It provides both a practical, high-speed baseline tool for the
speech processing community and a robust statistical framework
for ensuring reproducibility in generative model research.
