Abstract
Real-world respiratory sound classification faces performance
degradation from domain shifts in heterogeneous clinical sce-
narios. While Continual Learning (CL) aims to alleviate catas-
trophic forgetting, it faces challenges regarding data privacy
and the failure to handle audio-specific spectral characteris-
tics. In this paper, we propose Lung-CL, a unified framework
for buffer-free domain incremental respiratory sound classifi-
cation. It incorporates a Class-Specific Latent Replay strategy
that synthesizes pseudo-features from learned distributions, and
a Multi-Level Spectrum-Aware Distillation mechanism that dis-
entangles pathological patterns from domain-specific interfer-
ence. Extensive experiments on three heterogeneous datasets
(ICBHI, SPRSound and HF) show that Lung-CL significantly
outperforms the existing continual learning methods in terms of
classification accuracy and anti-forgetting ability.
Index Terms: respiratory sound classification, domain incre-
mental learning, gaussian mixture models
1. Introduction
Respiratory diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary dis-
ease (COPD) and asthma remain leading causes of global mor-
bidity and mortality [1]. Deep learning-driven automated respi-
ratory sound diagnosis has emerged as a key tool for early clin-
ical screening [2]. However, in real-world deployments across
different hospitals, recording devices, and patient populations,
static models struggle to generalize and frequently suffer from
catastrophic forgetting when incrementally adapting to newly
introduced domains [3, 4]. Continual learning (CL) addresses
this limitation by enabling models to evolve across heteroge-
neous environments while preserving past knowledge.
Formally, this continuous adaptation to new clinical do-
mains without catastrophic forgetting is framed as Domain-
Incremental Learning (DIL). Mainstream Continual Learning
(CL) strategies designed to mitigate this forgetting broadly fall
into three paradigms. First, replay-based methods [5, 6, 7] re-
tain past knowledge. By interleaving these retained samples
with incoming data streams during training, the model explic-
itly rehearses previously learned distributions. Second, dy-
namic architecture-based methods [8, 9, 10] structurally isolate
domain-specific components. This is typically achieved by al-
locating dedicated sub-networks or dynamically growing new
branches to accommodate novel domains. Third, regularized
continual learning methods [11, 12, 13] operate buffer-free by
relying on algorithmic constraints. These approaches typically

However, applying these general paradigms directly to clin-
ical audio faces two critical bottlenecks. First, standard re-
play approaches store real patient data, violating strict privacy
regulations like GDPR [14] and HIPAA [15]. Second, buffer-
free knowledge distillation is largely agnostic to audio spectral
characteristics [16]. Unlike natural images, respiratory sounds
are inherently time-frequency sparse, meaning critical diagnos-
tic cues like wheezes and crackles are concentrated in specific
narrow bands. By blindly aligning features, these methods
fail to disentangle domain-invariant pathological patterns from
device-specific noise. Therefore, an effective buffer-free contin-
ual learning framework for respiratory sound classification must
be spectrum-aware, enabling selective knowledge preservation
while suppressing domain-specific interference.
To address these two challenges, we propose Lung-CL,
a unified framework for buffer-free domain-incremental res-
piratory sound classification. Unlike dynamic architecture-
based methods that suffer from unbounded parameter growth,
Lung-CL maintains a constant network capacity, ensuring its
highly efficient deployment on resource-constrained portable
devices. Particularly, we introduce a Class-Specific Latent
Replay (CSLR) strategy that employs Class-Specific Gaussian
Mixture Models (CS-GMMs) as a generative memory mod-
ule. CSLR ensures robust knowledge retention by synthesiz-
ing pseudo-features for latent replay, eliminating the need to
buffer past samples. Modeling latent distributions at the seman-
tic bottleneck avoids high-dimensional spectrogram reconstruc-
tion and consequently reduces computational overhead. Ad-
ditionally, we propose a Multi-Level Spectrum-Aware Distilla-
tion (MLSAD) mechanism, which incorporates a core Energy-
Gated Distillation (EGD) module. This module utilizes a gat-
ing mechanism to encourage the student network to capture
domain-invariant pathological features from the teacher. By ef-
fectively filtering out the noise inherent to different domains,
this approach significantly enhances the model’s domain adapt-
ability.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
•A Unified Buffer-Free Framework:Lung-CL enables ro-
bust, privacy-preserving domain-incremental learning for
respiratory classification.
•Class-Specific Latent Replay:CSLR employs lightweight
CS-GMMs to synthesize past pseudo-features, mitigating
catastrophic forgetting without storing real patient audio.
•Multi-Level Spectrum-Aware Distillation:MLSAD uti-
lizes EGD to filter device-specific noise, guiding the network
to explicitly capture domain-invariant pathological features.

2. Method
As illustrated in Figure 1, Lung-CL is a Teacher-Student ar-
chitecture. Given an input spectrogram, the frozen Teacher re-
tains historical knowledge while the active Student learns new
patterns. To prevent forgetting, the CSLR module synthesizes
past pseudo-features via GMM memory, concatenating them di-
rectly into the Student’s intermediate layers. Simultaneously,
EGD uses the input’s energy distribution to adaptively weight
intermediate feature differences, suppressing background noise.
Finally, the Student is optimized end-to-end via a joint objective
of classification, EGD (L EGD), cosine similarity (L Cosine),
and knowledge distillation (LKD) losses.

2.2. Class-Specific Latent Replays
To comply with medical data privacy regulations, Lung-CL
adopts a generative latent replay mechanism based on CS-
GMMs.
It has been shown that the bottom weights of deep neural
networks tend to extract general low-level features after pre-
training, while the top layers are responsible for extracting dis-
criminative semantics for specific tasks [17]. Based on this ob-
servation, we adopt the AST [18] backbonef θ due to its great
potential in respiratory sound analysis [19], and structurally de-
couple it into two components: a fixed feature extractorG(·)
(comprising the first 6 layers) and an adaptive semantic learner
H(·)(comprising the last 6 layers and the classification head).
During the domain-incremental learning process, parameters of
Gare frozen, while onlyHremains trainable. Since the fea-
turesz=G(x)fed into the CS-GMM are derived from fixed
layers, their distribution remains stable and does not drift dur-
ing sequential training. This design thus ensures the stationarity
of the latent feature space, providing a reliable basis for density
modeling.

2.3. Multi-Level Spectrum-Aware Distillation
To ensure robust knowledge transfer in the presence of severe
domain shifts, we propose the MLSAD mechanism. In con-
ventional distillation paradigms, blindly aligning the student’s
features with the teacher’s risks transferring domain-specific

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets and Protocols
We evaluate Lung-CL on three heterogeneous datasets:
ICBHI [23], SPRSound [24], and HF [25]. These datasets intro-
duce severe domain shifts: ICBHI encompasses a broad demo-
graphic recorded with diverse equipment, SPRSound focuses
exclusively on pediatric patients with distinct acoustic features,
and HF comprises critical care adults with intense ventilator
background noise. To simulate a realistic DIL scenario, we
construct sequential learning tasks. The sequential learning set-
tings are summarized in Table 1. To ensure cross-domain con-
sistency, we map all annotations to a unified 4-class label space:
Normal, Crackle, Wheeze, and Both. Specifically, Rhonchi and
Stridor in SPRSound are merged into Wheeze, while CAS and
DAS in HF are mapped to Wheeze and Crackle, respectively.
We strictly follow the official train/test splits for all datasets.

3.2. Implementation Details
All audio recordings are resampled to 16 kHz. We fix the sam-
ple duration to 8s (798 frames) via truncation or loop-padding
with fade-in/out. Mel-spectrograms (50-2000 Hz) are extracted
as input features to capture key respiratory characteristics. We
employ the Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) as our base
architecture. The model is implemented in PyTorch and trained
on an NVIDIA A30 GPU using the Adam optimizer with an
initial learning rate of5×10 −5 and cosine annealing schedule.
We train each task for 30 epochs with a batch size of 8. For the
GMM component, we setKmax = 10.
3.3. Evaluation Metrics
Following the ICBHI benchmark, we use the Score [23] (av-
erage of Sensitivity and Specificity) as the primary metric. We
compute the CL metric based on theT×Tmatrix, including the
average performance calculated using ACC [26] and the forget-
ting metric BWT [27]. In addition, we calculated the ILM [27]
metric used in each training session.
4. Results and Discussions
4.1. Comparative Experiments
Driven by strict medical privacy demands, we prioritize improv-
ing BWT while maintaining competitive ACC and ILM. As de-
tailed in Table 2, Lung-CL is compared against non-CL upper
bounds (Naive, Joint, Cumulative) and representative CL base-
lines, including buffer-based (GEM [26], AGEM [28], ER [29],
LR [30]) and buffer-free (EWC [11], SI [13], LwF [12]) meth-
ods.
The comparative results demonstrate that Lung-CL consis-
tently surpasses Naive fine-tuning and delivers the most robust
overall performance among buffer-free baselines. Specifically,
parameter regularization methods (EWC, SI) suffer from se-
vere performance degradation when confronted with the ex-
treme domain shifts in sequence S2, yielding poor BWT scores
of -12.75% and -11.91%. In contrast, Lung-CL demonstrates
superior robustness, confining BWT to -9.6% under the same
harsh conditions. Remarkably, our buffer-free Lung-CL not
only bridges the performance gap but actively outperforms sev-
eral buffer-based models (ER and AGEM) in knowledge reten-
tion. This is highlighted by exceptional state-of-the-art BWT
scores of -4.0% in S1 and -5.05% in S3. Furthermore, consis-
tent with our design objective, Lung-CL does not compromise
plasticity for stability, as it achieves the highest overall accuracy
(ACC: 61.81%) and top-tier ILM among all buffer-free methods
in both the S1 and S3 scenarios, validating its comprehensive
adaptability.

To intuitively verify the efficacy of CSLR, we visualize the

whereas HF shows heavy class overlap. Crucially, the gen-

manifolds without mode collapse. CS-GMM accurately cap-

the high-density entanglement in HF, validating the high fidelity
of our generative replay mechanism.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we propose Lung-CL, a buffer-free genera-
tive replay continual learning respiratory sound classification
framework. To strictly preserve patient privacy, Lung-CL in-
troduces Class-Specific Latent Replay, utilizing lightweight
Class-Specific Gaussian Mixture Models to synthesize high-
fidelity past feature distributions. It also integrates Multi-Level
Spectrum-Aware Distillation that effectively suppresses back-
ground noise while maximizing consistency in both features
and outputs. Extensive evaluations confirm that Lung-CL ef-
fectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting, establishing a ro-
bust and privacy-preserving baseline for the stability-plasticity
trade-off in medical audio analysis.
