Abstract
Multimodal speech enhancement (MMSE) improves speech in-
telligibility in noisy environments by leveraging complemen-
tary strengths of various signals. However, complex MMSE
models and substantial computational demands pose challenges
for implementation on wearable devices. This motivates model
compression; however, prior approaches are often not tailored
to multimodal fusion and may fail to preserve features crit-
ical for fusion under practical noise and cross-modal mis-
match. To address these limitations, we introduce cross-modal
consistency-aware structured pruning (CCAP). CCAP estimates
channel importance using modality-wise zero masking and
ranks channels by response consistency with the multimodal in-
put to preserve modality-shared information. Experiments on a
paired air/bone microphone dataset show that CCAP improves
PESQ and STOI across multiple architectures over prior prun-
ing baselines and reduces inference latency at equivalent prun-
ing ratios, supporting embedded deployment.

1. Introduction
Speech enhancement (SE) is essential for reliable voice com-
munication in noisy environments [1, 2, 3]. However, SE based
on a single air-conduction microphone (ACM) is highly suscep-
tible to ambient interference and often degrades under highly
non-stationary noise [4, 5, 6]. Conversely, bone-conduction
microphones (BCMs), which capture vibrations transmitted
through the skin and bones [7, 8], provide superior noise ro-
bustness; but they exhibit limited frequency response below
2 kHz [9]. To leverage these complementary characteris-
tics, multimodal speech enhancement (MMSE) integrates ACM
with BCM signals, improving speech intelligibility and high-
frequency reconstruction in noisy environments [10, 11]. De-
spite these advantages, MMSE often relies on time-frequency
representations and fusion modules, which increase computa-
tional overhead and limit deployment on resource-constrained
devices [12]. For instance, deploying MMSE on edge and wear-
able devices often entails MB-level memory constraints and
tight power budgets, making even lightweight architectures with
more than 4 million parameters [11, 13, 14] difficult to exe-
cute in real time. This motivates model compression strategies
that reduce inference cost while preserving multimodal gains
[15, 16].
Channel pruning is an advantageous model compression

technique for on-device deployment [17, 18] because it re-
duces inference latency without requiring specialized sparse-
kernel support [19]. Existing pruning methods, includingℓ1/ℓ2
norm-based pruning, parameter pruning (PP) [20], and Taylor-
expansion-based pruning (TP) [21, 22, 23, 24], typically es-
timate importance from weight magnitude or gradient statis-
tics. However, these modality-agnostic criteria do not explic-
itly evaluate a channel’s contribution to multimodal fusion. In
MMSE, modality-shared channels can stabilize fusion under
noisy conditions; nonetheless, these scores may prune them be-
cause their responses can appear redundant across modalities
[25]. Modality-aware neuron unlearning (MANU) pruning has
recently been proposed to mitigate the limitations of modality-
agnostic pruning by leveraging statistical analyses of activa-
tion patterns [26]. Nevertheless, importance estimates based
on cross-modal activation comparisons may be unreliable be-
cause non-stationary noise and mismatches between ACM and
BCM signals (e.g., bandwidth and scaling differences) distort
activation patterns [27].
Motivated by these limitations, we introduce cross-modal
consistency-aware structured pruning (CCAP) for lightweight
MMSE. Instead of relying on cross-modal activation com-
parisons, CCAP estimates channel sensitivities from response
preservation normalized to the multimodal reference under
modality-wise zero masking. The resulting modality-specific
sensitivities are averaged to obtain a channel-wise importance
score, which guides structured channel pruning while preserv-
ing fusion-relevant channels. We validate CCAP on three repre-
sentative MMSE architectures [28, 29, 30] over pruning ratios
from 0% to 80%. Experiments on the throat and acoustic paired
speech (TAPS) dataset [31] show that CCAP consistently out-
performs prior pruning methods [20, 21, 26, 32] in terms of
narrowband PESQ and STOI, and achieves lower inference la-
tency at comparable pruning ratios, supporting its effectiveness
for lightweight MMSE deployment on embedded systems.

timates their sensitivity via modality-wise zero-masking. The
channel importance score is computed from the response nor-
malized to the multimodal reference, which mitigates the unreli-

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset
We conducted experiments utilizing the TAPS dataset [31], a
paired multimodal corpus that includes recordings from ACM
and BCM. The dataset comprises 60 Korean speakers, each
reading 100 unique sentences. We performed a speaker-disjoint
split, using 40 speakers for training, 10 for validation, and 10 for
testing. To simulate noisy conditions, background noise from
the DNS Challenge dataset [33] was incorporated into the ACM
signals at four signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) levels:−5,0,5, and
10dB. A total of 6,000 noise clips were randomly selected from
the 65,000 samples available across 150 noise classes.

3.2. Baselines and evaluation metrics
The CCAP method was evaluated against four baseline prun-
ing methodologies: Parameter pruning (PP) [20], which re-
moves channels using sparsity statistics; Taylor-expansion-
based pruning (TP) [21], which estimates channel importance
via gradient-based approximations; Modality-aware neuron un-
learning (MANU) pruning [26], which derives importance
scores by analyzing modality-dependent differences in activa-
tion statistics; BatchNorm (BN) pruning [32], which prunes
channels according to the magnitude of BN scaling factors. The
evaluations were performed on three representative MMSE net-
works that take two-channel inputs: an attention-based early-
fusion densely connected convolutional recurrent network (DC-
CRN) [28], multimodal involution neural network (MMINet)
[29], and lightweight accelerometer-assisted U-Net (LAU-Net)
[30]. We evaluated model performance using two widely
adopted objective metrics: narrowband PESQ and STOI. PESQ
evaluates perceptual speech quality by comparing the enhanced
signal with a clean reference [34], whereas STOI quantifies in-
telligibility based on the correlation of short-time speech en-
velopes [35]. Beyond task metrics, we further analyzed post-
pruning behavior by measuring inter-channel redundancy using
Pearson correlation [36] and quantifying representation preser-
vation using linear centered kernel alignment (CKA) [37].

3.3. Implementation details
Following the training details in the original studies [28, 29, 30],
each model was pre-trained using the hyperparameters re-
ported in the corresponding paper (LAU-Net: 200 epochs; DC-
CRN/MMINet: 30 epochs). For pruning, we compute impor-
tance scores on a calibration set of 3,000 training samples ran-
domly drawn from the training split. We perform layer-wise
structured channel pruning by ranking output channels by the
proposed scores and removing the lowest-ranked channels to
reach the target pruning ratio. Importance estimation and prun-
ing are performed once, followed by a single fine-tuning stage
using the same training configuration as pre-training (LAU-
Net: 40 epochs; DCCRN/MMINet: 15 epochs). All exper-
iments were implemented in PyTorch and run on a system
equipped with an AMD EPYC 7313 CPU, 128 GB RAM, and
two NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPUs.

4.2. Performance with respect to inference latency
Figure 4 presents the speech quality–latency trade-off by plot-
ting PESQ against inference latency as the pruning ratio in-
creases. Latency is measured as the average forward-pass time
per input segment on the evaluation platform. Although all
methods reduce latency with higher pruning, the baselines ex-
hibit larger quality degradation at comparable latency levels.
This suggests that pruning guided by modality-agnostic crite-
ria or by activation comparisons across modalities can remove
channels important for multimodal fusion. In contrast, CCAP
consistently traces the upper envelope of the curves across ar-
chitectures, achieving higher PESQ in the low-latency regime.
Overall, these results indicate that CCAP preserves fusion-
relevant cues while retaining the practical latency gains, which
improves deployability on resource-constrained platforms.

4.4. On-device demonstration
To evaluate the on-device performance of CCAP, a pruned
LAU-Net model was deployed on the MCU (STMicroelectron-
ics, STM32H753ZI). As shown in Table 3, the pruned LAU-Net
(ratio: 50%) required only 87 ms of inference time on an Arm
Cortex-M7 microcontroller to process a 304 ms input segment
transformed into the time-frequency domain using the short-
time Fourier transform. This corresponds to a real-time fac-
tor (RTF) of 0.29, defined as the ratio of inference latency to
input duration. Compared to the original model, Flash mem-
ory and RAM usage were reduced by 48.3% and 27.2%, re-
spectively, while inference latency decreased by 43.1%. These
results demonstrate that the CCAP-pruned model can be effi-
ciently executed on resource-constrained embedded hardware,
where reduced memory usage and inference latency are critical
for on-device AI applications.
5. Conclusion
This work presents a cross-modal consistency-aware compres-
sion approach for multimodal speech enhancement, prioritiz-
ing channels that reliably support multimodal fusion. CCAP
scores channels using modality-wise zero masking and response
preservation with respect to the multimodal reference. The re-
sulting importance estimates remain stable under practical noise
and cross-modal mismatch. Experiments with multiple MMSE
architectures show improved PESQ/STOI and reduced infer-
ence latency at matched pruning ratios. Post-pruning analyses
further indicate that CCAP removes redundant channels while
preserving the core representational structure. These results
support CCAP as a practical compression strategy for deploying
MMSE on resource-constrained platforms.
