Abstract
Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) suffer from Neu-
tral Bias, failing to capture subtle emotional nuances. Existing
attempts to address this through text augmentation usually in-
troduce acoustic hallucinations. To address these issues without
costly human annotation, we propose a data-centric paradigm:
Semantic Drift and Discriminative Re-ranking. Our method em-
ploys an LLM to perform multi-dimensional semantic drift, ex-
ploring diverse emotional hypotheses beyond neutral baselines.
Subsequently, a discriminative judge model filters drifted hy-
potheses via hard-negative contrastive learning, grounding them
in raw acoustic signals. Experiments confirm this hypothesize-
and-verify mechanism transforms coarse, neutral-biased labels
into precise, acoustically grounded descriptions, enriching fine-
grained emotional detail and downstream expressiveness.
Index Terms: large audio language models, emotion alignment,
data-centric AI, audio captioning
1. Introduction
The rapid evolution of Large Audio Language Models (LALMs)
has marked a paradigm shift from cascaded speech systems to
end-to-end architectures. Recent LALMs have incorporated
audio captioning data during pre-training and fine-tuning, en-
hancing their audio understanding capabilities. By jointly mod-
eling acoustic and semantic spaces, recent models such as
Qwen2.5-Omni [1], Qwen3-Omni [ 2], Step-Audio 2 [ 3], and
SALMONN [4] demonstrate strong capabilities in audio-text
understanding and cross-modal alignment, serving as foundation
models for speech comprehension and generation. These foun-
dational models leverage robust cross-modal encoders such as
wav2vec 2.0 [5], HuBERT [6], Whisper [7], or MiDashengLM’s
encoder [8] to align high-dimensional audio features with the
latent semantic space of LLMs. However, capturing fine-grained
non-semantic information such as subtle emotional transitions,
micro-tones, and implicit psychological states remains a signifi-
cant bottleneck. Consequently, model outputs often degenerate
into safe, redundant descriptions (e.g., “speaking in a calm tone”)
or demonstrate insufficient accuracy for neutral-yet-tonal audio,
a dataset-driven phenomenon we identify as Neutral Bias, as
illustrated in Figure 1.
Existing audio captioning efforts have attempted to provide
descriptive foundations through datasets like AudioCaps [9] and
WavCaps [10]. While effective for environmental sound events
and high-level scene descriptions, they primarily rely on discrete,
coarse-grained emotion labels which fail to capture the dynamic,

However, neither paradigm achieves grounding in raw acous-

text and underlying physical acoustic signals. Similarly, in multi-
modal annotation paradigms, visual and textual modalities inher-

bias compared to audio. Consequently, models systematically
prioritize visual scene understanding or lexical content from
ASR transcriptions, effectively suppressing acoustic signal pro-
cessing and causing the loss of critical non-semantic informa-
tion. Without rigorous cross-modal grounding based purely on
acoustics, the generated data fails to accurately reflect physical
acoustic reality, introducing stylistic biases and modality halluci-
nations that limit downstream LALM alignment capabilities.
Regarding evaluation methods for audio captioning, cur-
rent paradigms fail to verify acoustic fidelity directly from raw
audio signals. Traditional evaluation relies on n-gram metrics
(e.g., CIDEr, SPIDEr) [14, 15, 16, 17], which operate solely in
text space, detached from physical audio signals and failing to
capture emotional accuracy. Recent advances include Concor-
dance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) for continuous emotion
prediction [18] and emotion embedding similarity, alongside
comprehensive benchmarks like Air-Bench [19], MMAU [20],
and SD-Eval [21]. However, LLM-as-judge paradigms reveal a
critical limitation: lacking direct acoustic perception, they fall
into neutral bias, rewarding safe, formulaic descriptions. More

To break this bottleneck, we propose a novel data-centric
paradigm: Semantic Drift and Discriminative Re-ranking, il-

from neutral bias or Acoustic Hallucination, our hypothesize-
and-verify framework grounds emotional hypotheses in raw au-

emotional natural language descriptions without costly human
annotation. Our approach draws inspiration from weak-to-strong
generalization theory [23] and reward modeling in Reinforce-
ment Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) [24]. The main
contributions of this work can be summarized as follows:
• We identify and formalize neutral bias in audio understand-
ing and excessive rejection of candidates in discriminative
training, highlighting structural flaws in ungrounded text aug-
mentation methods.
• We propose the Semantic Drift and Discriminative Re-ranking
pipeline, a zero-human-annotation, data-centric paradigm that
eliminates neutral bias by refining safe, neutral descriptions
into precise, fine-grained emotional characterizations.
• Through evaluations encompassing LLM-as-a-judge prefer-
ence tests, words lexical diversity verification, and distribu-
tional analysis, we validate that our method significantly en-
hances downstream generation quality and emotional fidelity.
2. Methodology
To address the limitations of discrete emotion labels and text-
only data augmentation, we propose a data-centric automated
pipeline. This method enriches neutral-biased descriptions into
highly accurate, fine-grained emotional natural language descrip-
tions without requiring expensive human annotation. The data
construction process operates in three tightly integrated stages:
discriminative judge model construction, multi-dimensional se-
mantic drift, and discriminative re-ranking and filtering. Specif-
ically, we first construct a highly sensitive LALM judge via
collaborative multimodal annotation and contrastive learning
with hard and soft negatives, enforcing acoustic fidelity verifi-
cation. To prevent excessive rejection of valid candidates when
exposed to limited negative varieties, and to simultaneously en-
sure rigorous error exclusion, we calibrate the judge model’s
decision boundary through full-scale, multi-dimensional nega-
tive sampling. Subsequently, we employ the trained judge to
filter and re-rank diverse emotional hypotheses generated via se-
mantic drift, iteratively refining neutral-biased labels into precise,
fine-grained emotional descriptions without modifying model
architecture or requiring expensive human annotation, thereby
achieving autonomous data quality evolution. Figure 2 illus-
trates the complete pipeline, with emphasis on semantic drift and
discriminative re-ranking stages.
2.1. Discriminative Judge Model Construction
Accurately judging textual emotional descriptions of audio re-
quires a highly sensitive judge model. We build a discriminative
judge model by fine-tuning Qwen2.5-Omni. Our discrimina-
tive judge construction aligns with the Reward Model paradigm
in Preference Learning, where the model learns to distinguish
nuanced paralinguistic differences via contrastive pairs.

Based on the high-consistency subset, we utilize DeepSeek-
V3 [27] to perform semantic drift on positive examples, augment-
ing corresponding soft negatives (completely reversed emotional
polarity but identical linguistic structure) and hard negatives
(subtle emotional mismatches within the same intensity level),
thereby constructing a complete training set containing positive-
negative contrastive pairs. Specifically, for each high-consistency
audio clip, we construct a triplet containing one positive descrip-
tion, one hard negative, and one soft negative, expanding the
instances for judge model training. Table 1 illustrates this con-
struction strategy with concrete training examples.
Unlike conventional augmentation that employs single-type
negatives, our deliberate pairing leverages DeepSeek-V3’s con-
trolled polarity shifts: hard negatives discriminate subtle par-
alinguistic deviations, and soft negatives prevent over-confident
acceptance of extreme reversals. This dual-pressure calibration
ensures robust acoustic grounding, enabling the judge model
to validate diverse emotional hypotheses against raw acoustic
signals rather than merely assessing textual plausibility. This
strategy fusing multi-model collaborative annotation with auto-
mated negative sample augmentation ensures the judge model
precisely captures subtle paralinguistic deviations.

dimensional semantic drifts. Figure 2 illustrates the complete

Rather than simple paraphrasing, the generator actively ex-

semantic structure, it substitutes specific adjectives to generate a

reliability and coverage of semantic expansion.
2.3. Discriminative Re-ranking and Filtering
In the final phase, the fine-tuned judge model evaluates the
augmented candidate descriptions along with the original text.
The judge model directly ingests the raw continuous audio signal
alongside each candidate sentence to verify its authenticity. We
adopt an accuracy-first filtering approach in which the Judge
strictly rejects all hallucinatory or stylistically biased candidates.
Crucially, for the surviving candidates, we introduce a strict
strategic priority ranking: Drift → Refine → Origin. To maxi-
mize emotional richness, we prioritize accepted semantic drift
hypotheses (High/Low Arousal, Negative Shift). If aggressive
drifts fail acoustic verification, we fall back to conservative re-
finements. Finally, if all LLM-generated candidates are rejected,
we retain the original neutral text to ensure dataset scale and
safety. In the rare case where even the original neutral description
fails acoustic verification, the sample is discarded to maintain
dataset integrity. This paradigm yields a high-fidelity dataset
rich in emotional expressiveness.
3. Experiments and Results
To comprehensively validate our data-centric approach, we con-
duct extensive evaluations on a corpus of 29,530 unique audio
clips co-annotated by Gemini 2.5 Pro [25] and Qwen3-Omni [2].
Split by BGE-M3 similarity at 0.8, the 16,470 high-consistency
audio clips are expanded into 49,410 contrastive audio-text pairs
to train the judge model. Meanwhile, the 13,060 low-consistency
audio clips are reserved for semantic drift, yielding 12,000 acous-
tically grounded samples after judge model filtering for down-
stream SFT (Section 3.2).
3.1. Discriminative Probing and Ablation Studies
To prove the core necessity of our multi-dimensional negative
sampling strategy, we evaluate the judge model on a highly
challenging probing test set. We construct strictly controlled
ablation groups using 32,940 samples (1:1 positive-to-negative
ratio) trained for a single epoch to isolate the impact of different
negative sample types under constrained data regimes. We use
Balanced Accuracy as the primary metric to measure the trade-
off between recalling valid data and intercepting hallucinations.

3.2. Downstream Audio Caption Generation
To quantify the impact of our data refinement pipeline on
LALMs, we conducted Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on a base-
line model for comparison using two strictly aligned 12,000-
sample datasets: the unrefined original text (Baseline) and our
optimized descriptions (Ours).
As reported in Table 3, we evaluate the generative quality
across three dimensions:(i)human preference via the LLM-as-
a-judge paradigm (Gemini 2.5 Pro [25] blind comparison), with
a tie rate of 4.62%;(ii)semantic textual similarity via BGE-M3
embeddings to measure alignment with reference descriptions;
and(iii)lexical diversity through Unique Bigram counts and
Shannon Entropy (H2) based on bigram frequencies to assess
vocabulary richness. To address the limitations of n-gram based
metrics in capturing emotional nuances, we adopt the LLM-as-a-
judge evaluation, which has shown high correlation with human
perception in open-ended generation tasks.
It is crucial to address the evaluation results across all three
dimensions. This rich expressiveness is vastly preferred in nat-
ural conversations, as evidenced by the 66.15% win rate (vs.
29.23%) in the Gemini 2.5 Pro [ 25] blind test. Regarding the
slight decrease in BGE-M3 score (83.61% vs. 83.12%), the
baseline model tends to generate safe, dry descriptions that syn-
tactically match the coarse ground truth, yielding higher lexi-
cal similarity but failing to capture genuine emotional nuance,
whereas our model generates fine-grained emotional details that
deviate from simplistic ground truth but accurately reflect com-
plex acoustic reality. These gains are further complemented
by significant improvements in lexical diversity metrics (11%
increase in Unique Bigrams and 0.13 increase in Entropy).
3.3. Evolution of Data Distribution
To demonstrate how refinement resolves the dataset’s structural
flaws, we analyze distributional shifts before and after refine-
ment using density estimation in semantic embedding space.
LLM semantic expansion and judge filtering substantially reduce
redundant neutral descriptions while enriching affective-state
representations.
Figure 3 visualizes the t-SNE projections of text embeddings
from the baseline model, high-consistency reference annotations,
and our method. The baseline model (left panel) shows severe
mode collapse toward neutral descriptions, with high-density
regions around the neutral anchor and emotions such as Anger,
Fear, and Sadness collapsed into an indistinguishable mass. In
contrast, the ground truth manifold (center panel) exhibits a
multi-modal distribution with well-separated emotional clusters.
Our method (right panel) recovers this structure, expanding the
distribution to cover previously suppressed emotion states. This
alignment confirms our method’s efficacy in breaking neutral
bias and enriching the dataset with diverse emotional polarities
that reflect real-world speech distributions.
4. Conclusions
Our work addresses the critical bottleneck of fine-grained emo-
tion understanding in LALMs by identifying and resolving neu-
tral bias—the dataset-driven collapse toward safe, redundant
descriptions caused by ungrounded text augmentation. We estab-
lish a hypothesize-and-verify paradigm: Semantic Drift explores
diverse emotional hypotheses beyond neutral boundaries, while a
discriminative judge validates candidates against raw audio wave-
forms for acoustic fidelity via multi-dimensional hard-negative
contrastive learning. Through strategic re-ranking, the system re-
fines ambiguous data into precise emotional descriptions without
costly human annotation, outperforming conventional text-only
augmentation in emotional granularity and acoustic grounding.
This data-centric evolution enhances downstream generative
expressiveness, paving the way for the next generation of anthro-
pomorphic audio language models. Future work will leverage
these accurate captions for downstream speech synthesis tasks.
