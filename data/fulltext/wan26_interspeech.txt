Enhancing Visual Paralinguistics: Motion-Guided Spatial Denoising for
Non-Verbal Interaction Analysis
Junjie Wan
Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen
25S151090@stu.hit.edu.cn
Abstract
In Human-Computer Interaction, non-verbal cues like micro-
gestures are essential for understanding human intent alongside
speech. However, extracting these subtle movements in uncon-
strained environments is difficult due to severe visual clutter
and spatial ambiguity. We propose a signal processing-inspired
framework centered on a Motion-Guided Spatial Refinement
Module (MG-SRM). Rather than relying solely on heavy spatial
encoders, MG-SRM utilizes multi-order temporal differences to
explicitly filter out static background artifacts in pose heatmaps.
In addition, we introduce a Class-Learnable Fusion (CLF) strat-
egy that adapts modality weights based on action semantics,
prioritizing input streams according to the target micro-gesture.
Experiments on the challenging MA-52 dataset show that our
approach achieves state-of-the-art performance by directly im-
proving the signal-to-noise ratio of micro-movements. Our pro-
posed framework is highly accurate and computationally effi-
cient, providing a robust visual frontend for multimodal conver-
sational systems.

1. Introduction
Spoken communication is inherently multimodal. Visual par-
alinguistics, such as micro-gestures and postures, convey cru-
cial intent alongside speech [1]. In naturalistic human-computer
interaction (HCI), accurately capturing these non-verbal signals
is critical for building responsive and empathetic systems [2].
Despite its importance, recognizing micro-gestures in un-
constrained environments remains challenging. Like the cock-
tail party problem [3], the visual signal of interest is often sub-
merged in severe environmental interference. In pose-based
analysis, static background clutter, such as furniture resem-
bling human limbs, acts as spatial noise. This noise generates
false positive heatmaps that severely degrade the signal-to-noise
ratio. Existing methods, including Graph Convolutional Net-
works (GCNs) [4, 5] and Video Transformers [6, 7], typically
rely on heavy backbones to capture global spatial-temporal de-
pendencies. Without explicit denoising, learning representa-
tions directly from noisy data is computationally intensive and
highly susceptible to overfitting.
To address these limitations, we propose a lightweight,
signal processing-inspired framework for robust micro-gesture
recognition. Drawing a parallel to spectral subtraction in speech
enhancement, we introduce the Motion-Guided Spatial Refine-
ment Module (MG-SRM). Using temporal difference signals
as a proxy for activity detection, MG-SRM acts as a dynamic
spatial filter to suppress background artifacts. This design is
driven by the observation that while background clutter is spa-
tially structured, it exhibits significantly lower kinematic vari-
ation than active micro-gestures. This allows motion priors to
explicitly decouple the intended signal from the static environ-
ment.
Furthermore, we observe that the reliability of visual
modalities is inherently class-dependent. For instance, a“body-
touching” gesture involves occlusion and is better captured by
global RGB context, whereas “subtle finger tapping” relies on
the precise topology of pose heatmaps. Standard late fusion
with fixed weights ignores this variation. To address this, we
propose a Class-Learnable Fusion (CLF) mechanism. Unlike
instance-level attention that computes weights dynamically per
sample—which is highly susceptible to overfitting on noisy
micro-actions—CLF learns a robust categorical modality pref-
erence. This acknowledges that modality reliance is an intrinsic
property of the action itself, allowing the model to optimize a
specific fusion policy per class.
Our main contributions are three-fold: (1) We propose MG-
SRM, a plug-and-play module that spatially denoises pose fea-
tures using multi-order motion cues. (2) We introduce CLF for
adaptive modality fusion based on class-specific characteristics,
overcoming the instability of instance-wise attention. (3) We
achieve state-of-the-art performance on the challenging MA-52
dataset [8] with minimal computational overhead.
2. Related Work
2.1. Micro-Gesture Recognition
Early micro-gesture analysis relied on handcrafted features [9].
Later, deep learning models like TSN [10] and TSM [11] intro-
duced 2D CNNs for short-term dynamics. Recently, skeleton-
based methods gained traction for their robustness to light-
ing variations [8, 12]. For instance, PoseC3D [12] uses 3D
heatmaps to model skeletal topology. However, these methods
rely on clean pose inputs from robust 2D estimators. In natural-
istic settings, clutter often triggers “ghost” keypoints—spurious
detections mimicking human geometry. Our MG-SRM ad-
dresses this by filtering spatial noise using motion cues.
2.2. Multimodal Fusion in HCI
Multimodal learning is pivotal in HCI [13]. Traditional fu-
sion relies on early concatenation or late score averaging [14].
Recent works explore attention mechanisms [15] to dynami-
cally weigh modalities against noisy inputs [16]. However,
most methods use a shared attention map for all classes, forc-
ing a “one-size-fits-all” compromise. In contrast, our Class-
Learnable Fusion (CLF) optimizes specific fusion weights per
micro-action, recognizing that different gestures rely differently
on appearance versus motion cues.

3. Method
3.1. Overview
We propose a lightweight, dual-stream framework designed
for robust micro-gesture recognition in unconstrained environ-
ments. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the architecture consists of two
parallel streams: a spatial-temporal RGB stream and a motion-
aware Pose stream.
Given an input videoV, we extract RGB framesX rgb and
pose keypoint heatmapsX pose [17, 18]. The RGB stream uses
a standard ResNet-18 backbone for global appearance context,
while the Pose stream is enhanced by our Motion-Guided Spa-
tial Refinement Module (MG-SRM) to suppress static back-
ground noise. Finally, features from both modalities are fused
via the Class-Learnable Fusion (CLF) head to produce the final
prediction.
3.2. Motion-Guided Spatial Refinement (MG-SRM)
Micro-gestures involve subtle, localized movements that are
easily obscured by static clutter in pose heatmaps. To address
this, we introduce MG-SRM to model “what is moving” and
suppress “what is static” (Fig. 2).

3.3. Class-Learnable Fusion (CLF)
Standard late fusion strategies employ a fixed weight (e.g., 1:1)
for all classes. We argue that the optimal modality reliance
is semantically determined by the action category. We pro-
pose Class-Learnable Fusion (CLF) to capture this categorical
modality preference. Rather than using instance-wise atten-
tion (which infers weights from individual noisy samples and
is prone to overfitting in unconstrained HCI scenarios), CLF
optimizes a global parameterp c for each categorycvia back-
propagation, as illustrated in Fig. 3. This serves as a stable,
dataset-level regularization that reflects the intrinsic modality
dependence of each action.

4. Experiments and Results
4.1. Dataset and Implementation
Dataset.We evaluate our proposed framework on the MA-52
dataset [8], a large-scale benchmark for micro-action recogni-
tion. It contains spontaneous micro-gestures recorded in natu-
ralistic HCI scenarios with significant background noise. The
dataset is split into training and testing sets following the stan-
dard protocol.
Implementation Details.Implemented in PyTorch with Ima-
geNet weights, we train on an RTX 3090 GPU for 30 epochs.
We use SGD (momentum 0.9, weight decay10 −4, batch size
10) with an initial learning rate of 0.0075, decayed via a Multi-
StepLR schedule (epochs 10, 20). We sample T=8 RGB frames
and T=32 Pose frames per clip. Standard augmentations includ-
ing random cropping (2242) and flipping are applied.

cause instance-wise weighting infers attention from individual
noisy samples, making it highly susceptible to background clut-
ter and leading to overfitting in unconstrained HCI scenarios.
In contrast, CLF learns a robust, dataset-level categorical bias,
serving as a stable regularization that correctly reflects the in-
trinsic modality dependence of each action.
Uncertainty and Entropy Analysis.We further examine
whether CLF captures meaningful modality preferences by an-
alyzing the average prediction entropy (H=− Pplog(p))
across the test set. The baseline with MG-SRM has an av-
erage entropy of 3.9379, which remains nearly unchanged at
3.9403 after applying CLF. Unlike instance-wise methods that
might greedily reduce entropy by over-committing to noisy fea-
tures (leading to overconfidence and poorer generalization), our
class-wise CLF maintains a healthy overall entropy distribution.
This indicates that CLF improves accuracy by systematically
shifting probability mass toward the correct categories based on
stable, intrinsic modality biases, rather than inducing overcon-
fidence.
4.4. Robustness Analysis
We further evaluate the robustness of our approach by examin-
ing temporal sensitivity and architectural choices.
Sensitivity to Temporal Order.Micro-gestures possess strict
temporal causality (e.g., onset to offset). To verify that our
model captures these dynamics rather than relying on static
cues, we tested the model with reversed video sequences (“Re-
verse Playback”). As shown in Table 3, the performance drops
significantly by 1.34% (67.02%→65.68%). This degradation
confirms that our MG-SRM effectively encodes motion trends
and temporal dependencies.
Comparison with Transformer Encoder.We also compare
our signal-processing-inspired MG-SRM with a self-attention
Transformer encoder. As shown in Table 3, MG-SRM outper-
forms the Transformer variant by 0.98% (67.02% vs. 66.04%).
While Transformers are effective at modeling long-range de-
pendencies, they lack the inductive bias to distinguish subtle

5. Conclusion
We present a lightweight framework for robust micro-gesture
recognition, combining the Motion-Guided Spatial Refinement
Module (MG-SRM) and Class-Learnable Fusion (CLF). MG-
SRM suppresses static background noise in pose heatmaps via
multi-order motion differences, while CLF adaptively balances
RGB and pose modalities based on class-specific preferences.
On the MA-52 dataset, our method achieves a state-of-the-art
Top-1 accuracy of 67.02%. The framework is highly efficient,
adding only 0.072M parameters and 1.794G FLOPs to the base-
line. Our approach delivers significant gains with minimal cost,
providing a practical solution for naturalistic HCI and conver-
sational systems.
