RAISE: Resolving Ambiguity in Audio Understanding
with Imagination and Selective Extraction
Yueqian Lin1, Qinsi Wang1, Yudong Liu1, Hancheng Ye1, Hai Li1, Yiran Chen1
1 Duke University, Durham, NC, USA
yueqian.lin@duke.edu, yiran.chen@duke.edu
Abstract
Audio Large Language Models (Audio LLMs) are prone to per-
ceptual hallucination under acoustic interference or fine-grained
ambiguity. Robust auditory understanding demands active ver-
ification: even state-of-the-art reasoning models hallucinate
when forced to decode ambiguous internal representations. We
introduce RAISE (Resolving Ambiguity with Imagination and
Selective Extraction), a training-free framework that equips Au-
dio LLMs with two cognitively motivated listening strategies.
Selective Extraction isolates target signals from noise; Auditory
Imagination synthesizes candidate references for comparative
reasoning. Instead of relying on ambiguous internal represen-
tations, the model grounds every prediction in explicit acous-
tic evidence. On two challenging benchmarks, MMAR and
MMAU, RAISE delivers consistent gains of up to 12.4% with-
out any parameter updates.
Index Terms: audio understanding, large language models,
tool-augmented reasoning, source separation, audio generation
1. Introduction
Audio Large Language Models (Audio LLMs) excel at open-
ended tasks [1], yet break down when inputs are perceptually
ambiguous [2, 3, 4]. In the classic “cocktail party” scenario [5],
overlapping speakers force the model to guess which voice to
attend; when two instruments share a similar timbre, the model
has no reference tone for comparison. These failures are pre-
dictable from thetask structure: counting events in a mixture
demands source isolation, while identifying a subtle timbre de-
mands comparative references. Yet current architectures apply
the same single-pass strategy to every query, and recent bench-
marks [6, 7, 8] confirm that this one-size-fits-all approach fails
on fine-grained auditory details.
This brittleness is inherent to single-pass inference: the
model must commit to an answer with no way to verify its
perception against the raw signal. Human listeners routinely
employanalysis-by-synthesis[9], mentally simulating expected
sounds to confirm or reject hypotheses. Current Audio LLMs
have no such mechanism. When acoustic events overlap or
share spectral features, the model cannot isolate a target source
or synthesize a candidate for comparison; it guesses from a sin-
gle, unverified forward pass.
We show that off-the-shelf generative tools, audio gen-
erators and source separators, can close this gap by act-
ing as inference-time verification engines. Promptable sep-
aration (SAM-Audio [10]) and text-to-audio synthesis (Sta-
ble Audio [11]) now make on-the-fly signal manipulation
practical. Prior work has applied tool-use to modular rea-
soning [12, 13] or offline data augmentation [14, 15], but
never toverify perception at inference time. We introduce

2. The RAISE Framework
Given audiox, queryq, and answer candidatesC, standard
Audio LLMs select an answeryvia a single parametric pass
overP(y|x,q), which is brittle under high interference or fine-
grained ambiguity. RAISE transforms this passive predictor
into anactive perceiverby decomposing inference into three
stages:Semantic Routing,Evidence Instantiation, andContex-
tual Inference(Figure 1), forcing the model to ground predic-
tions in concrete acoustic evidence.

routerRto map the input tuple(q,C)to an optimal inference
strategys∈{DIRECT,IMAGINE,EXTRACT}. This decision is

By reserving computationally expensive tools for complex
queries (e.g., distinguishing subtle instruments or counting in
noise) and routing standard queries directly, RAISE balances
high-fidelity reasoning with inference efficiency.
We decouple the router from the backbone Audio LLM for
two reasons. First,independence: a model that misperceives
a mixture should not diagnose its own failure modes; an exter-
nal text-based router decides from task semantics, not corrupted
representations. Second,efficiency: a lightweight text-only call
avoids a redundant audio-encoding pass.
2.2. Stage 2: Tool-Augmented Instantiation
Conditioned on the routing decisions, the workflow adapts.
Ifs=DIRECT, the input bypasses instantiation. Otherwise,
RAISE activates a specialized grounding strategy:Auditory
Imaginationacts as anadditiveprocess that synthesizes exter-
nal references to resolve semantic ambiguity, whereasSelective
Extractionis asubtractiveprocess that distills the input signal
to mitigate environmental interference.
2.2.1. Path A: Auditory Imagination (ϕ gen)
Whens=IMAGINE, the framework adopts ananalysis-by-
synthesisapproach. This path is routed for tasks where candi-
dates describe distinct, synthesizable entities (e.g., instruments
or environmental events).
1.Candidate validation.The Audio LLM verifies that each
choicec i describes a physically realizable sound before map-
ping it to a descriptive promptp i. Choices that refer to ab-
stract or non-acoustic concepts are excluded, preventing the
generator from producing meaningless artifacts.
2.Reference generation.The generatorGsynthesizes a refer-
ence setZfrom the validated candidates. This creates a se-
lective acoustic lineup that lets the model compare the input
against tangible signals rather than latent text embeddings.
2.2.2. Path B: Selective Extraction (ϕ ext)
Whens=EXTRACT, the pipeline focuses on isolating a tar-
get signal from background interference. We employ anaudio-
conditionedproposal mechanism to bridge the gap between se-
mantic queries and physical separation:
1.Sound description.The Audio LLM performs a preliminary
analysis ofxusing an analytic prompt (e.g., “What specific
sound characteristics distinguish the target?”). This yields a
physical descriptiondderived from the actual acoustic con-
tent rather than the text query alone.
2.Source isolation.The descriptiondis fed into an open-
vocabulary segmentation model to filter the mixture:

This process results in a clean waveformx ′ where interfering

separation fails (e.g., invalid output, low energy), RAISE falls
back to the DIRECTpath so that tool errors never override a
valid baseline prediction.

In the final stage, the Audio LLMMestimates the answer. The
inference mechanism adapts to the type of evidence produced
in Stage 2.

3. Experiments
3.1. Experimental Setup
We evaluate RAISE onMMAR[6] andMMAU[7], two bench-
marks targeting complex auditory reasoning and fine-grained
understanding. Our backbone models includeQwen2-Audio-
7B[1],Qwen2.5-Omni-7B[18], andQwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-
Thinking[19]. We compare RAISE against a single-pass base-
line (DIRECT). Implementation-wise, theSemantic Router
utilizesgpt-4o-mini[20]; since it operates on text-only in-
put (question and choices), it can be replaced by any lightweight
language model.Auditory Imaginationemploys Stable Au-
dio Open 1.0 (50 steps, guidance 7.0, 5s clips) for reference
synthesis.Selective Extractionuses SAM-Audio with an en-
ergy threshold of 0.01. All experiments run on NVIDIA H200
GPUs. Our experiments use models from the Qwen family
due to their native support for multi-audio input (separate au-
dio clips, not multi-channel), which is required by the Imagina-
tion path; RAISE is architecture-agnostic and applicable to any
Audio LLM that accepts multiple audio inputs in one conversa-
tion [21].

3.3. Ablation Study
Table 2 isolates the contribution of each RAISE component us-
ing Qwen3-Omni. We ask three questions: (1) Can the model
reason its way past perceptual errors without tools? (2) Do tools
help when applied blindly to every sample? (3) Are both tools
necessary, or does one suffice?
More reasoning cannot compensate for flawed percep-
tion.A natural objection is that the model should “think harder”
and self-correct. We test two strategies: COT prompting
prepends step-by-step reasoning instructions; SELF-REFINE
gives the model a second pass to reconsider its prediction. Nei-
ther helps: CoT degrades performance (−1.0% MMAR,−1.7%
MMAU), likely because Qwen3-Omni already has a built-in
thinking mode. Self-Refine matches the baseline on MMAR
but regresses on MMAU (−2.1%), as the model second-guesses
correct answers as often as it fixes wrong ones. When the bottle-
neck is perceptual, no amount of reasoning over a misperceived
signal recovers the correct answer.

4. Conclusion
We have presentedRAISE, a training-free framework that turns
generation into a tool for perception. By routing queries toAu-
ditory ImaginationorSelective Extraction, RAISE resolves
perceptual ambiguity through concrete evidence. Experiments
on MMAR and MMAU yield gains of up to 12.4% without
parameter updates. Current limitations are tool reliance and
added latency for routed samples, which distilled generators and
batched separation can reduce. Making modelslisten by imag-
ining and extractingopens a promising direction for robust au-
dio understanding.
