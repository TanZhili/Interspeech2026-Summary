Self-Adaptive Gradient Conflict Mitigator
for Continuous-Time Diffusion Models
Takumi Hirose1, Zhiyang Li1, Nakamasa Inoue1
1 Institute of Science Tokyo, Japan
hirose.t.f8a6@m.isct.ac.jp, li.z.6a09@m.isct.ac.jp, inoue@comp.isct.ac.jp
Abstract
Continuous-time diffusion models have demonstrated strong
capabilities for modeling complex data distributions across var-
ious domains. However, these models often encounter gradient
conflicts, where parameter updates at different timesteps inter-
fere with each other, hindering effective training. To provide
theoretical insights into this phenomenon, we introduceDelta
Loss Lower Bound (DELLBO), a tractable lower bound on the
reduction in the integrated training loss. Through mathematical
analysis of DELLBO, we derive the optimal parameter update
direction as the opposite of the vector from the origin to the
nearest point in the convex hull of per-timestep gradient vec-
tors. Building on this insight, we proposeSelf-adaptive Gradi-
ent Conflict Mitigator (SGCM), a simple yet effective method
that adjusts parameter update directions during training. Exper-
imental results confirm that SGCM behaves consistently with
our theoretical analysis and demonstrate its effectiveness for
speech enhancement.
Index Terms: Continuous-time diffusion models, Theoretical
analysis.
1. Introduction
Diffusion-based generative models have rapidly evolved into
a versatile framework for data synthesis, covering diverse do-
mains including speech processing [1, 2, 3, 4]. In their
continuous-time formulation [2], the generative process is de-
fined as the reverse of a stochastic differential equation (SDE),
enabling fine-grained control over data corruption and recon-
struction. The training objective for these models is often for-
mulated as an integrated lossL=

imizing this integrated loss is intractable, training proceeds by
randomly sampling a timestept∈[0,1]and minimizing the
corresponding lossLt at each iteration.
Although this paradigm has led to state-of-the-art results,
it also exposes an important optimization challenge. Because
the gradients associated with different timesteps are generally
not aligned, updating parameters to reduce the lossL t for a
sampledtcan inadvertently increaseL t′ for othert′̸=t. Such
gradient conflictsreduce the efficiency of training, potentially
slowing convergence or preventing the model from achieving
its best performance.
This paper provides theoretical insights into this phe-
nomenon. Specifically, we introduce the Delta Loss Lower
Bound (DELLBO), a tractable lower bound on the reduction
in the integrated loss. From DELLBO, we analytically derive
the optimal gradient direction and show that it aligns with the
vector pointing from the origin to the closest point within the
convex hull of per-timestep gradients. Furthermore, building
upon this insight, we propose Self-adaptive Gradient Conflict
Mitigator (SGCM), a lightweight method that mitigates gradi-
ent conflicts during training by adaptively reweighting gradients
using a single learnable parameter. In summary, our contribu-
tions are two-fold:
1. We theoretically analyze gradient conflicts in continuous-
time diffusion models by introducing DELLBO, and derive
the optimal solution.
2. We propose SGCM, a practical method to mitigate conflicts
with only a single additional learnable parameter. We vali-
date its effectiveness on speech enhancement.
2. Related Work
Diffusion models.Diffusion models formulate data gen-
eration as the reversal of a gradual noise perturbation pro-
cess [1, 2, 5], providing principled interpretations of denoising
dynamics. In the field of speech signal processing, subsequent
works have introduced task-specific architectures and training
methods for diffusion models, addressing representative tasks
such as speech enhancement [4, 3, 6, 7], bandwidth exten-
sion [8, 9, 10, 11], speech synthesis [12, 13, 14, 15, 16], speech
separation [17, 18, 19], and voice conversion [20, 21, 22]. These
advances highlight the expanding frontiers of diffusion-based
speech processing.
Optimization perspectives.Several studies have analyzed dif-
fusion training from an optimization-theoretic viewpoint, ex-
ploring loss curvature [23], score-matching stability [24], and
projection- or guidance-based updates for improved conver-
gence [25, 26]. Gradient weighting techniques are also known
to accelerate convergence [27, 28, 29, 30]. These perspectives
highlight the potential of mathematically grounded update rules
to improve training efficiency. Our work provides an analyt-
ical formulation of gradient conflicts in continuous-time dif-
fusion models with an optimal update direction, leading to a
lightweight adaptive method.
3. Methodology
We begin with the theoretical foundations of DELLBO, which
evaluates a lower bound on the reduction in total training loss.
By applying Sion’s minimax theorem, we derive an analytical
solution that maximizes DELLBO. We then introduce SGCM,
which adjusts the parameter update direction during training,
providing a practical implementation.

4. Experiments
We conduct experiments to demonstrate that SGCM yields a
direction closer to the optimal direction on real data. Specifi-
cally, we apply SGCM to speech enhancement and demonstrate
that SGCM effectively reducesΩ(µ)in Eq. (8). As the update
rule of SGCM can be interpreted as gradient weighting, we also
compare its practical performance with other weighting meth-
ods in terms of speech quality.
4.1. Experimental Settings
Datasets.We use two speech enhancement bench-
marks: WSJ0-CHiME3 and V oiceBank+DEMAND (VBD).
The WSJ0-CHiME3 dataset consists of noisy speech utter-
ances obtained by mixing clean speech utterances from the Wall
Street Journal dataset [31] with noise signals in the CHiME3
dataset [32]. SNRs are uniformly sampled from 0 to 20 dB.
The numbers of training and test utterances are 12,777 and
651, respectively. For additional validation on a widely-used
real-recording benchmark, we also conduct experiments on the
V oiceBank+DEMAND dataset [33]. Its training split contains
11,572 noisy-clean paired utterances from 28 speakers, where
each clean utterance is corrupted by additive noise at one of
four SNR levels (15, 10, 5, and 0 dB). The test split comprises
824 utterances spoken by two speakers not appearing in train-
ing, and the noises are also disjoint from the training set. In the
test split, mixtures are generated at four SNR levels (17.5, 12.5,
7.5, and 2.5 dB).
Metrics.For speech quality evaluation, we use five metrics:
perceptual evaluation of speech quality (PESQ), extended short-
time objective intelligibility (ESTOI), scale-invariant signal-to-
distortion ratio (SI-SDR), scale-invariant signal-to-interference
ratio (SI-SIR), and scale-invariant signal-to-artifact ratio (SI-
SAR).
Baselines.We selected SGMSE+ [4] as a baseline due to its
established effectiveness. For gradient weighting, we imple-
mented three additional baselines: SNR weighting [27, 28],
Max-SNR weighting [29], and Min-SNR weighting [30].

4.2. Experimental Results
Optimality Evaluation.We analyzeΩ(µ)only on WSJ0-
CHiME3 for clarity, and use V oiceBank+DEMAND solely to
verify that the resulting quality improvements generalize to a
different evaluation protocol with unseen speakers and noises.
We evaluate the squared normΩ(µ)defined in Eq. (8) dur-
ing training on WSJ0-CHiME3. Specifically, we save a check-
point every 5% of the training progress and uniformly sample
20 points oftin the range from 0 to 1. Figure 4 summarizes
the results. As shown, SGCM consistently yields the smallest
norm value, indicating that its update direction stays closest to
the theoretically optimal one and supporting our analysis. The
norm values increase in the order SGCM, baseline, Min-SNR,
SNR, and Max-SNR, suggesting that gradient conflicts become
more severe in this order.
Test Performance.Table 1 reports speech enhancement
performance on the test sets of WSJ0-CHiME3 and V oice-
Bank+DEMAND. Across both benchmarks, SGCM achieves
the strongest overall results and improves most evaluation met-
rics. On WSJ0-CHiME3 in particular, SGCM consistently
delivers the best scores across all five metrics, yielding si-
multaneous gains in perceptual quality (PESQ), intelligibility
(ESTOI), and distortion-related measures (SI-SDR/SI-SIR/SI-
SAR). Compared with the baseline, SGCM provides clear and
uniform improvements, indicating that mitigating timestep-wise
gradient conflicts benefits not only a single criterion but the
overall enhancement behavior. Notably, the improvements are
substantial for interference-related metrics (e.g., SI-SIR), sug-
gesting that SGCM more effectively learns to suppress compet-
ing components while preserving target speech content.
Importantly, on WSJ0-CHiME3, the empirical ranking in
Table 1 aligns with the ordering ofΩ(µ)in Figure 4, indicating
that a smallerΩ(µ)correlates with better final speech quality.
This agreement supports our claim thatΩ(µ)serves as a mean-
ingful proxy of optimization progress: methods that drive the
update direction closer to the DELLBO-optimal direction tend
to converge to better solutions. In contrast, heuristic weighting
strategies can over-emphasize specific timesteps and lead to less
favorable trade-offs, as reflected by their largerΩ(µ)values and
consistently weaker test performance.
We additionally evaluate on V oiceBank+DEMAND to
probe generalization under a different data construction proto-
col with unseen speakers and unseen noise types. While the ab-
solute scores on this benchmark are lower than those on WSJ0-
CHiME3 due to its challenging evaluation setting, the same
overall trend remains: SGCM attains competitive or improved
performance relative to the alternative weighting strategies, and
yields the best overall results across metrics. Together, these re-
sults demonstrate that steering the update direction toward the
theoretical optimum translates into consistent practical gains,
while SGCM requires only a single additional parameter and
incurs negligible computational overhead, making it a drop-in
component for continuous-time diffusion training.
5. Conclusion
We presented a principled view of gradient conflicts in
continuous-time diffusion training by introducing DELLBO, a
tractable lower bound on the reduction in integrated training
loss. Its analysis gives a geometric prescription: the optimal
gradient aligns with the vector from the origin to the nearest
point in the convex hull of per-timestep gradients (Theorem 1).
Guided by this insight, we proposed SGCM, a lightweight
rescaling scheme that adapts update directions during training
with only one learnable parameter. Experiments on speech en-
hancement show that SGCM consistently reducesΩ(µ)and
improves quality metrics, with gains on WSJ0-CHiME3 and
complementary validation on V oiceBank+DEMAND. These re-
sults suggest that the convex-hull perspective offers a useful
recipe for conflict-aware optimizers in diffusion learning. Fu-
ture work includes extending the framework to curvature-aware
updates, richer proxies beyondΩ(µ), and broader speech and
non-speech diffusion tasks.
