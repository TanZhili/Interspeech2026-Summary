Abstract
Parametric array loudspeakers (PALs) exploit ultrasonic
carrier and nonlinear self-demodulation to generate directional
speech and audio sound, offering advantages for spatial audio
reproduction. While recent work extends linear-acoustic sound
zone control (SZC) to PAL arrays by optimizing sideband exci-
tations and achieves high acoustic contrast (AC), the carrier—
essential for demodulation—remains unexplored as a control
variable. We propose a carrier-aware SZC framework for PALs,
treating the carrier as a new control dimension, with an alternat-
ing optimization for sideband and carrier weights. Simulations
and experiments show that our method outperforms sideband-
only control, yielding higher AC and demodulation efficiency.
In experiments, broadband gains of 11.2 dB in AC and 7.0 dB in
target-zone SPL are achieved under identical power constraints.
The results establish the carrier as a key control variable for
more efficient, high-performance spatial audio and speech ren-
dering with PALs.

array loudspeaker, acoustic contrast control
1. Introduction
Due to the ability to generate highly directional audio beams
from a compact-size sound source, parametric array loudspeak-
ers (PALs) have gained considerable attention in various au-
dio applications such as spatial sound reproduction [1–3], ac-
tive noise control [4, 5], and private speech communication
systems [6, 7]. Compared to traditional electrodynamic loud-
speaker (EDL) arrays, PAL arrays offer advantages including
smaller source size and enhanced directivity for sound zone
control (SZC), in which the goal is to create bright zones with
desired audio content and dark zones with minimal sound leak-
age within a shared environment [8–16].
Conventional SZC with EDL arrays relies on the linear-
acoustic framework, where the sound field can be expressed as
a linear superposition of individual channel responses. How-
ever, SZC with PAL arrays is challenging due to the inherent
nonlinear demodulation process. When a PAL emits an ultra-
sonic carrier modulated by an audio signal, the audio sound is
self-demodulated due to nonlinear interactions between the car-
rier and sideband. Thus, interactions between carriers and side-
bands from different channels produce coupled inter-channel
audio components [17–20], preventing the total audio sound
field from being expressed as a direct linear sum of individ-
ual channel contributions, and making conventional linear SZC
framework unsuitable for direct application to PAL arrays.
**indicates the corresponding author.
Recent research has explored several strategies to address
the challenges of implementing SZC with PAL arrays. Zhuang
et al. developed a framework that models the nonlinear interac-
tions between any two channels using a pairwise transfer func-
tion matrix [21]. While effective in principle, this approach re-
quires characterizing all channel pairs, which becomes increas-
ingly impractical for large-scale arrays. Alternatively, Zhu et al.
proposed a linearizing framework by introducing the “effective
transfer function” for PAL arrays [19]. To measure the effec-
tive transfer function of a specific channel, unmodulated carrier
signals in other channels are emitted simultaneously. This cap-
tures the nonlinear coupling effects caused by interactions with
all channels. Thus, the “effective transfer function” of different
channels can be linearly superimposed, allowing conventional
linear-acoustics-based algorithms to be directly applied to PAL
arrays. However, this framework focuses primarily on sideband
control and treats the carrier as fixed. In PAL systems, the car-
rier plays a central role in governing the nonlinear demodulation
process that generates the audible sound field. Fixing the car-
rier therefore restricts the available control degrees of freedom,
which can lead to both degraded sound zone performance and
reduced demodulation efficiency.
We propose a carrier-aware sound zone control framework,
in which the carrier is explicitly incorporated as an active con-
trol variable alongside the sideband. An alternating optimiza-
tion strategy is developed to sequentially updates the sideband
and carrier excitation. As a proof of concept, this carrier-aware
framework is applied to acoustic contrast control (ACC) in both
simulations and experiments. Simulations are conducted using
thek-space approach [22], which is a recent work that addresses
the longstanding challenges in efficient and accurate simula-
tion of multi-channel PALs caused by nonlinear demodulation
and inter-channel coupling. The results show that the proposed
method consistently outperforms sideband-only control, yield-
ing higher zone contrast. Furthermore, better audio demodula-
tion efficiency is achieved, which is especially important given
PALs’ inherent low-frequency reproduction inefficiency.

2.3. Alternating Carrier-Aware Optimization Strategy
As the effective transfer functions are role-dependent, the pro-
posed carrier-aware control here is realized through a finite-
stage alternating re-linearization and optimization procedure, in
which the sideband and carrier are optimized sequentially:
1. Initial Sideband Optimization: Given an initial carrier config-
urationq= [1,1,...,1] ⊤, the sideband-conditioned trans-
fer functionG s(q)is measured. The sideband weightsware
then optimized using a standard SZC algorithm.
2. Carrier Optimization: With the optimized sideband weights
wfixed, the system is re-linearized by measuring the carrier-
conditioned transfer functionG c(w). The carrier weightsq
are then updated by the same SZC formulation.
3. Sideband Re-alignment: Since the ultrasonic carrier is
a single-frequency component, the carrier optimization in
Stage 2 is performed with respect to a specific audio fre-
quency. As a result, the optimized carrier weights are in-
herently frequency-specific and may not be optimal across a
wide audio bandwidth. Therefore, to ensure the sidebands
are perfectly matched to this refined carrier background, the
audio weightsware re-optimized in this final stage using the
updated sideband-conditioned transfer matrixG s(q).
In this way, we obtain bothwandqto control the sideband and
the carrier respectively, resulting the carrier-aware SZC for a
PAL array as depicted in Fig. 1(d). Since the transfer functions
are empirically measured, environmental reflections are natu-
rally encapsulated, making the framework theoretically applica-
ble to arbitrary acoustic environments including indoor spaces.
In principle, this alternating process could be repeated.
However, in practice, each stage requires a complete re-
measurement of the transfer functions (eitherG s orG c), which
imposes a significant experimental and temporal cost. There-
fore, rather than iterating to convergence, this work adopts a
pragmatic, validation-oriented perspective by employing this
finite-stage procedure that captures the primary benefits of car-
rier optimization while maintaining high operational efficiency.
3. Simulations and experiment validations
Simulations and experiments are conducted to validate the pro-
posed carrier-aware SZC framework. As a proof-of-concept im-
plementation, ACC is adopted as the sound zone optimization
method, allowing a direct comparison between conventional
sideband-only control and the proposed carrier-aware approach.
The control point configuration utilized in both simulations
and experiments is illustrated in Fig. 2, where bright and dark
zone control points are marked in red and blue, respectively.
The bright zone comprises two clusters of 9 points each, cen-
tered at(0.5 m,±0.1 m)in a3×3grid with 1 cm spacing. The
dark zone includes 25 points uniformly distributed along a line
with 1 cm spacing, centered at(1.5 m,0)and spanning a total
length of 24 cm. This configuration is designed to emulate a
typical personal audio scenario, where the bright zone corre-
sponds to the listener’s ear region in front of the PAL, and the
dark zone is the rear region intended for acoustic suppression.
Both simulations and experiments employ the same PAL
array configuration. As shown in Fig. 3, the array consists of
24 columns, each with 24 circular emitters of 5 mm radius. The
columns are arranged compactly with a center-to-center spac-
ing of 8.7 mm. Due to the symmetric control point distribution,
a symmetric channel design is adopted, pairing two columns
symmetric about the central axis (y= 0) as one channel. The
ultrasonic carrier frequency is 40 kHz, and the carrier optimiza-
tion stage is performed at an audio frequency of 1 kHz.
Experimental measurements are conducted in an anechoic
chamber as shown in Fig. 3. A condenser microphone Type
CY-407 (Hangzhou Acoustic Sensing Technology Co., Ltd.,
Hangzhou, China) is used to measure the sound pressure, which
is covered with a thin piece of plastic film when measuring au-
dio sound to mitigate spurious sound [23, 24]. The sound field
over a 2 m × 1 m plane is scanned using a stepper-motor-driven
measurement system with a spatial resolution of 3 cm.

Figure 2 presents the simulated and measured audio sound
pressure level (SPL) distributions for sideband-only ACC and
carrier-aware ACC at 500 Hz, 1 kHz, 2 kHz, and 4 kHz. For
sideband-only ACC, shown in Fig. 2(a,c), although the sound
energy is successfully steered toward the bright-zone control
points, a substantial amount of sound leakage toward the dark
zone remains observable. This leakage becomes more pro-
nounced at higher frequencies, indicating that suppressing the
demodulated audio field in the dark zone is fundamentally con-
strained when only the sideband is controlled. In contrast, the
carrier-aware ACC results in Fig. 2(b,d) exhibit a clear reduc-
tion of sound leakage into the dark zone, accompanied by a en-
hanced sound energy concentration in the bright zone across all
tested frequencies. The consistent performance improvement
is observed in both simulation and experimental measurements,
confirming that the advantage of the carrier-aware approach is
robust against practical system imperfections.
To clarify the physical mechanism underlying the perfor-
mance improvement, Fig. 4 depicts the corresponding ultra-
sonic SPL distributions of the carrier (40 kHz) and sideband
(41 kHz) at 1 kHz audio frequency. As shown in Fig. 4(ii),
by optimizing the audio-driven sideband signal, sideband-only
ACC steers the ultrasonic sideband energy toward the bright
zones and reduces its level in the dark zone, thus improving
AC of the audio field. However, the carrier remains uncon-
trolled, resulting in a nearly collimated beam pattern as shown
in Fig. 4(i), which limits the PAL array’s ability to suppress
leakage in the dark zone. Physically, the demodulated audio
sound can be interpreted as radiated from a virtual source with
the source density proportional to the spatial product of the car-
rier and sideband sound pressures. Thus, the spatial mismatch
between the carrier and sideband in sideband-only ACC not
only degrades the achievable dark-zone suppression but also
reduces the audio demodulation efficiency, leading to a lower
SPL in the bright zone. In contrast, carrier-aware ACC ad-
dresses these limitations by controlling the carrier. As depicted
in Fig. 4(iii,iv), the carrier optimization in carrier-aware ACC
aligns its spatial distribution with the sideband field, thereby
enhancing AC and improving the SPL in the bright zone.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a carrier-aware SZC framework for
parametric array loudspeakers. By explicitly leveraging the car-
rier as a control dimension rather than a fixed background com-
ponent, the proposed approach overcomes a fundamental lim-
itation of existing methods, significantly enhancing the overall
performance of PAL-based SZC. Simulations and experiments
based on ACC demonstrate that our method leads to a sub-
stantial increase in AC and demodulation efficiency, indicating
that the carrier-aware optimization is a key enabling concept for
high-performance sound zone control with PAL arrays.
