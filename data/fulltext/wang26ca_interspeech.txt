FreqGuard: Leveraging Frequency-Domain Feature Priors for Universal
Proactive Voice Defense
Yankai Wang∗, Zhipeng Chen∗, Yuxuan Du, Rong Zheng, Jing Deng
Beijing Fosafer Information Technology Co., Ltd., China
wangyankai@fosafer.com, chenzhipeng@fosafer.com, duyuxuan@fosafer.com
Abstract
The rapid development of voice deepfakes poses signifi-
cant risks to privacy and security. This paper introduces Fre-
qGuard, a universal proactive defense framework designed to
protect against malicious speech synthesis attacks. FreqGuard
leverages learnable frequency-domain feature priors to generate
imperceptible perturbations that effectively disrupt voice syn-
thesis systems. Unlike methods based on random noise, Fre-
qGuard explores frequency operations to obtain priors that de-
grade speaker embeddings while minimizing perceptual distor-
tion. These priors guide model training to target the shared sub-
space between verification and synthesized features. By jointly
optimizing multiple losses, FreqGuard achieves high impercep-
tibility and low speaker similarity. Experimental results show
that FreqGuard reduces attack success rates against black-box
TTS systems while preserving speech quality, achieving favor-
able cross-model generalization and perceptual quality com-
pared with existing methods.
Index Terms: proactive voice defense, frequency-domain per-
turbation, speaker identity prior, adversarial defense
1. Introduction
Recent advances in deep learning-based text-to-speech (TTS)
have enabled generated speech to closely resemble real human
voices in both naturalness and speaker similarity [1, 2, 3]. How-
ever, unauthorized voice cloning poses serious threats to privacy
and societal security [4], creating an urgent need for effective
speech data protection in open environments [5, 6].
Defenses against deepfake audio are generally categorized
into passive detection [7, 8, 9] and proactive defense. While
passive detection focuses on post-hoc identification, proactive
defense provides early protection by embedding imperceptible
perturbations into speech before any potential misuse, disrupt-
ing the modeling of speaker embeddings. Consequently, proac-
tive defense has become a key research focus.
Most existing proactive defense methods focus on adver-
sarial optimization in the embedding space. These approaches
either maximize shifts in speaker embeddings to reduce iden-
tity similarity [10, 11, 12, 13], or employ predictive and gener-
ative networks to improve the efficiency of perturbation gener-
ation [14, 15]. Some studies further investigate universal per-
turbations and black-box adaptation mechanisms to enhance
cross-model generalization [16, 17, 18, 19], while others de-
sign structured perturbations based on physical consistency to
improve stealthiness [20, 21]. Additional lines of research ex-
plore training-stage protection [22, 23], watermark embedding
[24], and system-level enhancements [25, 26].

Despite their diversity, these methods predominantly treat
embedding-space displacement as the primary objective. Per-
turbations are typically initialized from random noise without
explicit modeling of the structural characteristics of speech sig-
nals. Consequently, their effectiveness often depends on spe-
cific feature extractors or surrogate models, resulting in perfor-
mance degradation in cross-model scenarios.
Recent studies have further exposed this structural limita-
tion. Diffusion-based purification models can substantially re-
store the cloning success rate of protected speech [27], while
frequency-domain restoration techniques, such as filtering and
spectral subtraction, can partially reverse obfuscation effects
[28]. These findings suggest that defense mechanisms relying
solely on embedding-space perturbations fail to fundamentally
disrupt the underlying spectral statistical structures that encode
speaker identity, thereby limiting their robustness.
To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes Freq-
Guard, a general proactive speech defense framework guided
by frequency-domain feature priors. The core contributions of
this work are summarized as follows:
•Frequency-domain prior-driven structured defense:A
knowledge base of highly disruptive yet low-distortion
frequency-domain operations is systematically developed
through experiments. This guides the model in precisely
locating and disrupting the shared subspace between iden-
tity and synthesis features in the frequency domain, thereby
structurally weakening the stable expression of speaker iden-
tity.
•Multi-loss joint optimization:A multi-objective optimiza-
tion strategy is proposed, incorporating time-domain recon-
struction loss, magnitude spectrum loss, phase spectrum
loss, log-power spectrum loss, structural similarity loss, and
speaker embeddings cosine loss. This approach ensures high-
fidelity protection while reducing identity similarity in cloned
speech.
•Extensive experimental validation:Experiments on mul-
tiple public datasets and under diverse black-box synthesis
scenarios demonstrate that FreqGuard consistently outper-
forms existing methods in both cross-model generalization
and speech quality preservation.

2. Related Work
2.1. Automatic Speaker Verification System
Deep embedding learning has become the dominant paradigm
for automatic speaker verification (ASV). Representative ap-
proaches include X-vector [29], ECAPA-TDNN [30], Cam++
[31], ERes2Net [32] and ERes2NetV2 [33]. These models learn
discriminative speaker representations from large-scale datasets
and achieve strong verification performance. However, their
sensitivity to speaker-specific characteristics renders them vul-
nerable to voice cloning and adversarial attacks.
2.2. Privacy Preservation
With the rapid advancement of speech generation and recog-
nition technologies, voice privacy protection has become in-
creasingly important. Speaker anonymization aims to conceal
identity information while maintaining speech utility, typically
through acoustic feature modification [34, 35, 36] or embedding
space transformation [37, 38].
Unlike speaker anonymization, which replaces the
speaker’s identity while preserving content, audio proactive
defense embeds imperceptible adversarial perturbations to
prevent unauthorized cloning or deepfake attacks, without
altering the original identity.

3.2. Research Motivation
FreqGuard targets the shared latent feature subsetDbetween
speaker verification and speech synthesis. Unlike methods that
initialize perturbations from random noise, which may cause
imprecise disruption, we systematically evaluate the effects of
different operations on speaker identity similarity and select key
factors to build a guidance dataset that helps locate and disrupt
subsetD, improving convergence.
Experiments are conducted on LibriSpeech with 2,000 ut-
terances from 100 speakers, each contributing 20 utterances,
sampled at 16 kHz in mono W A V format. Speaker identity sim-
ilarity is measured using a pre-trained ERes2NetV2 [39], and
perceptual quality is evaluated with objective metrics.

3.3. Overview of FreqGuard
FreqGuard consists of PriorNet, a prior-driven defense network,
and GradPert, a gradient-guided perturbation module. Prior-
Net reconstructs the magnitude ˆSm and phase spectrograms ˆSp,
while GradPert predicts perturbations ˆSadv. The protected au-
dioˆxis obtained by adding ˆSadv to ˆSm and applying ISTFT
with ˆSp. PriorNet preserves quality and stabilizes perturbations,
while GradPert improves attack resistance.

network

target, which is paired with clean speech to form data pairs for

speaker identity similarity while preserving speech fidelity. The

4. Evaluation
4.1. Experiment Setup
All experiments were conducted on four NVIDIA RTX 4090
GPUs. STFT parameters were set toN FFT = 512andN hop =
256. For the ASV system, the speaker verification threshold
θ= 0.78, and GradPert usedϵ= 0.03. PriorNet was trained for
100 epochs with a learning rate of2×10 −4, using the AdamW
optimizer and a CosineAnnealingLR scheduler.
4.2. Dataset composition
The evaluation dataset comprises LJSpeech (1 speaker), VCTK
(10 speakers), and AIShell3 (10 speakers), with 200 utterances
per speaker, totaling 4,200 samples from 21 speakers.
4.3. Evaluation Metrics
•Perceptual Evaluation of Speech Quality (PESQ)[40, 41]
is an objective metric based on a psychoacoustic model
that evaluates the perceptual differences between the original
speechxand the protected speechˆx.
•Short-Time Objective Intelligibility (STOI)[14, 17, 42]
measures speech intelligibility by computing the correlation
betweenxandˆxin the short-time spectral domain.
•Speaker Recognition Similarity (SRS)[17] quantifies the
similarity between speaker embeddings, with values normal-
ized to the range [0, 1]. It is evaluated on both defended
speech(x,ˆx)and synthesized speech(x,G(ˆx,t)).
•Attack Success Rate (ASR)[10, 14] measures the propor-
tion of synthetic utterancesG(ˆx,t)that successfully deceive
the ASV system.

4.4. Performances
4.4.1. Compared with other proactive defense methods
Table 2 compares proactive defense methods across datasets in
terms of PESQ, STOI, SRS, and defense effectiveness against
four TTS systems. SRS is computed using CAM++ [39]. Since
SRS serves different purposes in the defense and attack stages,
we report SRS for both defended audio and TTS-generated
speech. A higher SRS for defended audio indicates better
preservation of speaker identity and perceptual quality, whereas
a lower SRS for TTS-generated speech indicates more effective
protection against speaker impersonation and ASV verification.
Although Attack-VC [11] achieves higher PESQ and SRS
than FreqGuard, both Attack-VC and PoP [13] yield ASR above
70% on VCTK under several synthesis systems, indicating lim-
ited cross-dataset generalization, while E2E [40] and Enkidu
[18] markedly degrade speech quality (PESQ<1.56, STOI<
0.81 on AIShell3 and VCTK).
Against large-scale TTS models such as XTTS-v2 and
CosyV oice, most methods exhibit reduced effectiveness, with
ASR reaching up to 100% in some cases, whereas FreqGuard
maintains a maximum ASR of 27.50%, indicating improved ro-
bustness and cross-domain generalization.
PoP and YourTTS do not support Chinese, while Attack-
VC’s length mismatch leads to lower STOI scores.

5. Conclusion
We propose FreqGuard, a frequency-domain proactive defense
framework that suppresses speaker identity while preserving
speech quality. Experiments across datasets, ASV systems,
TTS models, and purification attacks demonstrate superior ro-
bustness, cross-model generalization, and quality-protection
trade-offs compared with existing methods.
