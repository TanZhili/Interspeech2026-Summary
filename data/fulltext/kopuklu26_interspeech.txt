Abstract
Existing audio-visual active speaker detection methods often
rely on multi-stage pipelines or computationally expensive tem-
poral modeling, limiting their applicability in real-time scenar-
ios. In this paper, we propose RT-ASDNet, a unified, end-to-end
audio-visual architecture for real-time active speaker detection.
Our approach jointly processes raw audio waveform and video
frames through two specialized streams that are fused into an
anchor-free detection head, simultaneously localizing faces and
predicting speaking activity in a single forward pass. Unlike
multi-stage approaches that require separate face detection, fea-
ture extraction, and classification steps, RT-ASDNet performs
joint localization and classification within a unified framework,
significantly reducing inference latency and pipeline complex-
ity. Moreover, since inference is performed only once per
frame, the computational cost remains constant regardless of the
number of people in the scene. We evaluate our method on the
A V A-ActiveSpeaker benchmark and set the baseline for joint
detection and classification on active speaker detection task.
Index Terms: active speaker detection, unified architecture,
real-time processing
1. Introduction
Determining “who is speaking” in a video is a fundamental
perceptual task that humans perform effortlessly by integrating
auditory and visual cues. This task, known as active speaker
detection (ASD), involves identifying which visible person is
producing the speech at a given moment by analyzing the syn-
chrony between facial movements and the audio signal. ASD
has become increasingly important in numerous applications,
including video conferencing, human–robot interaction, auto-
matic video editing, and speaker diarization. As multimedia
content continues to grow exponentially, the demand for auto-
mated systems that can reliably identify active speakers in un-
constrained environments has grown substantially.
Recent deep learning methods have achieved impressive ac-
curacy on standard benchmarks. However, the majority of state-
of-the-art approaches follow a multi-stage paradigm: first de-
tecting faces using an off-the-shelf detector, then extracting fea-
tures from cropped face regions and corresponding audio seg-
ments, and finally classifying each candidate as speaking or not
using either graph neural networks (GNNs) [1], recurrent neu-
ral networks (RNNs) [2,3], or attention mechanisms [4]. While
effective, this pipeline introduces several limitations. The com-
putational cost scales linearly with the number of faces, making
these methods impractical for crowded scenes. Error propaga-
tion across stages can degrade overall performance, and reliance
on pretrained face detectors limits end-to-end optimization.
In this paper, we propose RT-ASDNet, a unified and real-
time deep neural network (DNN) architecture that addresses
these limitations through a fundamentally different design phi-
losophy. Rather than treating face detection and speaker classi-
fication as separate tasks, we formulate active speaker detection
as a single-stage detection problem. Inspired by anchor-free ob-
ject detectors, our approach directly predicts speaker locations
and their speaking status in a single forward pass, without re-
quiring explicit face proposals or region cropping. In general,
RT-ASDNet provides the following advantages:
• It is an end-to-end trained architecture whose performance
does not rely on external components such as face detectors.
• The inference time is fixed and does not depend on the num-
ber of speakers present in the video stream.
• RT-ASDNet is modular, and depending on complexity re-
quirements, its components can be replaced with more
resource-efficient variants.
2. Related Work
Available ASD architectures in the literature exhibit several
drawbacks that limit their suitability for real-time deployment,
which are listed as follows:
• Most existing ASD architectures are non-causal.
• Their performance depends on an external face detector, and
many require an additional tracking or linking module to con-
struct face tubes before visual feature extraction.
• Visual features must be computed for every individual person
in the frame, leading to inference times that scale with scene
complexity.
• Although a few lightweight variants have been proposed,
many approaches remain computationally heavy and are
therefore practical only for offline settings.
• Several systems optimize their components separately, pre-
venting full end-to-end learning and limiting their ability to
reach a globally optimal solution.
In practice, existing ASD architectures suffer from sev-
eral of the limitations outlined above, often combining multiple
drawbacks within a single system. Broadly, these approaches
can be categorized into two classes based on their temporal as-
sumptions and deployment goals:offlineandcausalmethods.
Offline methods.A large portion of ASD research fo-
cuses on offline architectures designed primarily to maximize
benchmark performance rather than real-time efficiency [5–18].
These approaches commonly adopt multi-stage pipelines in
which face detection, feature extraction, audio-visual fusion,
and long-term temporal modeling are handled as separate mod-
ules. Recent state-of-the-art ASD methods typically employ
GNNs [1] or attention mechanisms [4]. GNN-based approaches

sence of future context for current-frame predictions. However,
causality alone does not guarantee real-time feasibility. For ex-
ample, methods such as [19, 20] still rely on external face de-
tection and perform visual feature extraction for every detected
individual. As a result, their computational cost scales linearly
with the number of faces, easily violating real-time latency con-
straints in crowded scenes. Moreover, these approaches largely
preserve the multi-stage design of offline pipelines, inheriting
limitations such as error propagation and the lack of end-to-end
optimization. Although some recent works reduce complexity
through lighter backbones or simplified temporal modules, they
continue to operate under a face-proposal–based paradigm, pre-
venting constant-time inference per frame.
In light of these drawbacks, we have designed RT-ASDNet
to overcome the fundamental limitations of existing ASD
pipelines. Our architecture(1)eliminates the dependence on ex-
ternal detectors,(2)avoids per-face processing, and(3)enables
unified audio-visual learning in a single-stage, anchor-free de-
tection framework. This design ensures constant inference cost
per frame, full end-to-end trainability, and practical real-time
performance. To our knowledge, RT-ASDNet is the first archi-
tecture that performs single-stage joint detection and classifica-
tion on active speaker detection task.
3. RT-ASDNet Architecture
RT-ASDNet consists of three main components: (1) an audio
stream that processes raw waveforms, (2) a visual stream that
extracts spatiotemporal features from video frames, and (3) a
detection head that fuses audio-visual features and predicts ac-
tive speakers. RT-ASDNet architecture operates in a sliding-
window fashion and makes detections on the key frame, defined
as the last frame in the input video clip. The overall architecture
is illustrated in Figure 1.
3.1. Audio Stream
As the audio backbone, we employ SincDSNet [16], motivated
by its strong performance in extracting representative audio
features directly from raw waveforms using sinc-based con-
volutions [21]. This design eliminates the need for an ex-
plicit filterbank and enables direct utilization of the full au-
dio signal. Specifically, we use 80 sinc filters with a kernel
size of 251, operating at a sampling rate of 16 kHz. Follow-
ing the sinc convolution stage, log-compression is applied as
y= log(|x|+ 1). This non-linear transformation has proven
effective in prior raw-audio modeling tasks [22–24]. The result-
ing features are then passed to six Depthwise Separable Convo-
lutional (DSConv) blocks followed by Leaky-ReLU activation
functions [25]. All DSConv blocks contain 160 channels, ex-
cept for the final block, which usesC a channels. Finally, adap-
tive average pooling aggregates the temporal dimension, yield-
ing a compact audio embeddingf a∈R Ca.

match the spatial resolution of the finer one. Both feature maps
then pass through deformable convolutions and are concate-

4. Training
Dataset.We use the A V A-ActiveSpeaker dataset [29], the first
large-scale audio-visual dataset for active speaker detection col-
lected in the wild. The dataset contains 262 movie clips, each
approximately 15 minutes long and captured at 25–30 fps, split
into 120 training, 33 validation, and 109 test videos. Across
these videos, 3.65 million frames are manually annotated with
a binaryspeakingornot-speakinglabel, yielding 38.5 hours of
labeled face tracks paired with their corresponding audio.
Metrics.We use mean average precision (mAP) at a given
intersection-over-union (IoU) threshold of 0.5 as the primary
evaluation metric. The A V A-ActiveSpeaker validation set is
used for evaluation, as test set labels are not available.
Learning.RT-ASDNet is trained end-to-end using the
ADAM optimizer [30] with an initial learning rate of1×10−4,
which is reduced by a factor of 10 after 40k iterations. The
audio backbone (SincDSNet) is trained from scratch with
Ca = 32. For the video backbone, we experiment with 3D-
ResNet-18 [31], pretrained on the Kinetics [32] and Moments-
in-Time [33] datasets, as well as the resource-efficient 3D-
MobileNetV2-1.0x and 3D-ShuffleNetV1-1.0x models [34],
both pretrained on Kinetics only. The value ofC b is chosen
according to the number of channels in the finest pyramid level
of the respective video backbone.
For the loss function, we use a multi-task objective that
jointly optimizes the three prediction heads. The heatmap head
is supervised using pixel-wise logistic regression with focal
loss [35], as in [28]. The size and offset heads are supervised
withL 1 regression losses computed only at ground-truth center
locations. The total loss is a weighted sum:
L=λ hmLhm +λ whLwh +λ offLoff,(7)
whereλ hm = 1,λ wh = 0.1, andλ off = 1. For detection, we ap-
ply non-maximum suppression (NMS) on the heatmap outputs
using a simple3×3max-pooling operation, following [28].

6. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced RT-ASDNet, a unified and anchor-
free architecture that reformulates active speaker detection as
a single-stage audio-visual detection problem, enabling fixed
latency, real-time inference without reliance on external face
detectors or per-face processing. While recent offline ASD ap-
proaches have achieved near-saturated performance through in-
creasingly complex multi-stage pipelines and heavy temporal
modeling, these gains come at the cost of high latency and lim-
ited deployability. In contrast, RT-ASDNet establishes a strong
and practical baseline for the real-time ASD task by explic-
itly prioritizing end-to-end optimization, fixed computational
cost per frame, and deployment feasibility. Despite these ad-
vantages, a substantial performance gap remains between real-
time and offline methods, highlighting the intrinsic difficulty
of active speaker detection under strict latency constraints and
motivating further research into more effective audio-visual fu-
sion strategies, lightweight temporal reasoning, and improved
robustness in crowded or low-resolution scenarios.
