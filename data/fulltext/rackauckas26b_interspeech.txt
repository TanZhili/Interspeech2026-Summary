A Speech-First Character Interface for Stylized Japanese Dialogue Practice
Zackary Rackauckas1
1 AI Research, RoleGaku, USA
zackary.rackauckas@rolegaku.com
Abstract
We present Jouzu, a mobile Show & Tell demonstration of ex-
pressive persona-conditioned spoken interaction for Japanese
language practice. In Jouzu, attendees choose a fictional
character, type or speak a prompt, hear a character-specific
Style-BERT-VITS2 voice response, compare how multiple per-
sonas realize the same utterance through wording, register, and
speech, and tap unfamiliar Japanese words for in-place reading
support. The demo foregrounds speech as the primary medium
for experiencing persona, affect, and learner feedback, rather
than treating audio as an add-on to a text chatbot. This paper de-
scribes the runnable interaction loop, persona-conditioned gen-
eration, expressive TTS pipeline, speech-input path, compari-
son mode, and booth workflow that combine LLM dialogue,
character voice synthesis, and vocabulary scaffolding in a live
Japanese-practice system.
Index Terms: CALL, spoken dialogue, fluency assessment
1. Introduction
Speech interfaces are increasingly relevant for language-
practice tools because they let learners rehearse listening and
spoken interaction without waiting for a human partner. For
Japanese practice, spoken interaction is especially useful be-
cause learners must connect written forms, pronunciation, reg-
ister, and sentence-final particles during an exchange. These
aspects are difficult to experience through text alone.
Most language-learning chatbots are presented as neutral
tutors. Jouzu instead demonstrates a speech-first character in-
terface – i.e., each agent is associated with a fictional per-
sona, visual presentation, text style, and synthesized voice. The
design draws on Japanese character language, in which styl-
ized linguistic forms signal personality, role, gender, origin, etc
[1]. Related systems have explored LLM-based language prac-
tice [2] and fictional persona interaction [3]; Jouzu, however,
brings these ideas into a live mobile speech demo where char-
acter stylization is embodied through both language generation
and expressive speech synthesis.
The demonstration is designed to make persona-
conditioned speech contrast directly observable. Rather
than one agent alone, Jouzu lets attendees send the same
prompt to multiple characters and compare differences in
lexical choice, sentence-final forms, speaking style, and
synthesized voice. This comparison centers the demonstration
on stylized, persona-conditioned spoken interaction. We
intentionally scope this paper as a demonstration paper. A
separate companion paper reports user-study methods and
learning-outcome analyses [4]; those results are not repeated
here.

2. Demonstration system
2.1. Runtime interaction loop
Figure 2 summarizes the live pipeline used in the demonstra-
tion. A user selects a character and submits either typed text
or recorded speech in any language. The system converts the
turn into a dialogue request, conditions the language model on
the selected persona, synthesizes the response with the corre-
sponding character voice, and displays the Japanese response
in the mobile chat view. The user can then replay the audio,
continue the conversation, compare personas, or tap words in
the generated response for reading support. The demonstration
emphasizes end-to-end responsiveness rather than offline evalu-
ation. At the booth, a complete interaction consists of four visi-
ble steps; 1) choosing a character, 2) sending a short prompt, 3)
hearing the synthesized reply, and 4) inspecting one unfamiliar
word or phrase. Within one minute, an attendee can submit the
same prompt to two personas, hear distinct synthesized voices
and styles, and inspect an unfamiliar word from the response.

2.3. Expressive character speech
Generated Japanese responses are synthesized with Style-
BERT-VITS2. Professional Japanese voice actors recorded
character dialogue, and those recordings were used to fine-tune
character-specific speech models from the pretrained Style-
BERT-VITS2 JP Extra model. Prior evaluation found that
Style-BERT-VITS2 JP Extra can produce synthesized Japanese
speech with no significant average difference from native
Japanese ground truth [5]. In the demo, speech is the main out-
put channel through which attendees experience persona, affect,
register, and speaking style. The interface keeps playback con-
trols and the chat transcript together so that users can connect
what they hear with the Japanese text they see.
2.4. In-place word inspection
The mobile interface includes tap-based word inspection for
generated Japanese text. When a user taps a sentence or word,
the app displays support information such as furigana, romaji,
and English definitions. This avoids interrupting the booth in-
teraction with an external dictionary lookup and lets beginners
participate even when they cannot read the full response inde-
pendently. The inspection feature also keeps speech and reading
support in the same interaction loop. Attendees can hear a char-
acter response, replay it, and immediately inspect the written
form of unfamiliar words. This makes the demonstration acces-
sible to both beginners who need reading support and more ad-
vanced learners who want to compare register, style, and voice
across personas.
3. Live demonstration plan
The live demonstration is organized as a short guided interac-
tion followed by free exploration. First, the presenter asks the
attendee to choose a character. Second, the attendee sends one
of several prepared prompts or speaks a short custom prompt.
Third, Jouzu plays the synthesized response and displays the
Japanese text. Finally, the attendee taps a word to open the in-
spector or repeats the same prompt with another character.
The supported booth modes cover speech playback, char-
acter contrast, speech input, and in-context vocabulary support.
The core path requires only text input and audio playback, so
the demonstration can proceed in a noisy environment. Optional
paths include speech recording, replaying audio, and comparing
characters, allowing the presenter to adapt the demo to attendee
interest and booth conditions while still showing the full speech
pipeline.
4. Demo-specific considerations
Because Jouzu uses stylized fictional agents, the demonstration
includes a brief framing statement before attendees interact with
the system: character language is expressive and culturally rec-
ognizable, but it is not always suitable for formal real-world
conversation. The app is therefore presented as exposure and
practice with stylized speech, not as a replacement for class-
room instruction or native-speaker feedback. The live setup also
separates core and optional functionality. The core path requires
only text input and audio playback, allowing the presenter to
demonstrate the speech output reliably even under booth noise.
The optional speech-input path lets attendees speak a prompt
when conditions allow.
5. Conclusion
Jouzu shows how character voice, register, and lightweight
learner scaffolding can be combined in a speech-based
language-learning interface. The Show & Tell presentation cen-
ters on a working interaction. Attendees can select a charac-
ter, type or speak a prompt, hear persona-specific synthesized
Japanese, compare character responses, and inspect unfamiliar
words directly in the chat view.
