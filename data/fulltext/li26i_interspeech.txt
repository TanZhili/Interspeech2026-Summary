Abstract
Audio deepfake detection systems face significant challenges
in generalizing to unseen attacks and diverse acoustic environ-
ments. While raw waveform augmentation methods like Raw-
Boost show promise by modeling recording noise, they rely on
static parameters tailored for speech. This rigidity limits ef-
fectiveness in non-speech domains like environmental sounds.
To address this, we propose DAR-Boost, an end-to-end differ-
entiable augmentation framework. We reformulate traditional
convolutive, impulsive, and stationary noise injections as fully
differentiable layers governed by a lightweight auxiliary net-
work. This network dynamically predicts optimal parameters
based on the input waveform, enabling adaptive learning from
the data distribution. Experimental results demonstrate that
while DAR-Boost maintains comparable performance to Raw-
Boost in the voice domain, it exhibits superior performance
in the environmental sound domain, effectively broadening the
framework’s applicability.
Index Terms: audio deepfake detection, differentiable data
augmentation, anti-spoofing, raw waveform modeling
1. Introduction
Improving robust generalization against unseen spoofing at-
tacks remains a formidable challenge in the field of audio deep-
fake detection [1, 2]. M ¨uller et al. [3] demonstrated that the
poor generalization of detection models is often attributed to
acoustic domain shifts rather than the increased sophistication
of spoofing algorithms. To mitigate such acoustic mismatches,
standard countermeasures generally rely on Self-Supervised
Learning (SSL) [4, 5, 6] to leverage large-scale diverse pre-
training, or Data Augmentation (DA) [7, 8, 9, 10] to enrich
training data diversity. While SSL models offer robustness, they
entail high computational costs. Conversely, DA methods like
RawBoost [7] improve generalization efficiently but suffer from
static, heuristic designs.
Although Automated Data Augmentation (ADA) has rev-
olutionized computer vision through search-based paradigms
[11, 12, 13, 14] and generative approaches utilizing LLMs or
diffusion models [15, 16], these methods often incur prohibitive
computational overheads. Meanwhile, there remains a signif-
icant research gap in audio deepfake detection, where cost-
effective, automated strategies in the raw waveform domain are
still remarkably under-explored.
In the domain of audio deepfake detection, RawBoost [7]
offers unique advantages for waveform-based modeling among

egy without necessitating external datasets or architectural ad-
justments [7]. This methodology has proven highly effective,
driving state-of-the-art results in both speech anti-spoofing and
music deepfake detection tasks [17, 18].
While RawBoost remains extensively employed in speech
deepfake detection (SDD) [19, 20], its application to environ-
mental sound deepfake detection (ESDD) faces intrinsic limi-
tations due to its static, heuristic nature: (i) Its speech-centric
hyperparameters (e.g., telephony-based notch filters) fail to ac-
commodate the broadband spectral complexity of environmen-
tal audio and may induce destructive interference by inadver-
tently obliterating fine-grained high-frequency textures essen-
tial for authenticity verification; (ii) Its open-loop, stochas-
tic sampling operates independently of the detector’s decision
boundary, failing to synthesize adversarial “hard examples” and
consequently wasting training iterations on ineffective samples
that are either trivial or physically unrealistic. (iii) Its discrete,
manual selection of noise algorithms forces a reliance on expen-
sive grid searches, precluding the joint, differentiable optimiza-
tion of complex noise mixtures required to simulate real-world
degradation.
To address these limitations, we introduce DAR-Boost
(Differentiable Adaptive Raw Boost), a fully differentiable and
adaptive data augmentation framework that shifts the paradigm
from heuristic randomness to learnable optimization. Unlike
the blind stochasticity of prior methods, DAR-Boost leverages
gradient-based adversarial learning to actively probe model vul-
nerabilities, establishing a closed-loop feedback mechanism for
robust feature learning. The main contributions of this paper are
threefold:
• Fully Differentiable Architecture: To the best of our knowl-
edge, we propose DAR-Boost, the first end-to-end differen-
tiable and adaptive raw waveform augmentation framework
tailored for audio deepfake detection.
• Adversarial & Adaptive Mechanism: We design a generator-
router dual system that synthesizes boundary-seeking pertur-
bations and dynamically fuses them to prevent mode col-
lapse.
• Domain-Agnostic Generalization: We demonstrate that
DAR-Boost effectively bridges the gap between speech and

2. Method
As illustrated in Figure 1, the proposed DAR-Boost is an end-to-
end differentiable data augmentation framework designed to en-
hance the generalization of deepfake detection models against
complex channel variability. Unlike traditional methods rely-
ing on static statistical sampling, DAR-Boost models the aug-
mentation process as a learnable adversarial intervention. The
framework comprises three primary components: (1) a Differ-
entiable Boost Module, which reconstructs Linear/Non-linear
(LnL), Impulsive (ISD), and Stationary (SSI) noise injections
using differentiable operators to ensure gradient propagation;
(2) a Parameter Generator that employs a decoupling strat-
egy, separating control variables into Learnable Shape Parame-
ters (optimized via adversarial learning) and Random Intensity
Parameters (injected for stochastic regularization); and (3) an
Adaptive Router, which dynamically computes mixing weights
to fuse the augmented views with the original input based on
global signal statistics. The resulting augmented waveform is
directly fed into the downstream detector for joint optimization.

2.2. Parameter Generator
As illustrated in Figure 2 (left), the Parameter Generator utilizes
lightweight convolutional encoders to analyze the global char-
acteristics of the input waveformxand predict optimal control
variables for the differentiable boost module. To balance the
generation of “hard” examples with physical realism, we pro-
pose a Parameter Decoupling Strategy:
2.2.1. Learnable Shape Parameters
Variables determining the spectral and temporal morphology,
specifically center frequencyfc, bandwidthfbw, and impulsive
densityd, are designated as learnable. To achieve robust pa-
rameter search without manual intervention, these parameters
are predicted through a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) placed
between the encoders and prediction heads. During backprop-
agation, the GRL reverses the gradient sign, driving the gen-
erator to identify parameter combinations that maximize the
downstream detection loss. The network outputs are normalized
via a Sigmoid activation and linearly mapped to valid physical
ranges (e.g.,[0,f s/2]for frequency) before entering the differ-
entiable boost module, ensuring the adversarial perturbations
remain physically meaningful.

2.2.2. Stochastic Intensity Parameters
Variables controlling the magnitude of the distortion, includ-
ing gains (g LnL,g ISD) and signal-to-noise ratio (g SNR), are kept
strictly stochastic. They are sampled from fixed Uniform dis-
tributionsU(a,b)during training. This decoupling prevents the
adversarial generator from collapsing into trivial solutions (e.g.,
maximizing loss by simply reducing SNR to zero) and ensures
the model remains robust across a wide range of signal inten-
sities. Together with the bounded Shape Parameters, this con-
fines augmentations to perceptually natural, channel-like degra-
dations rather than unrealistic artifacts.
2.3. Adaptive Router
The Adaptive Router (Figure 2, right) performs instance-aware
fusion. It concatenates the raw inputx raw with the three aug-
mented views (x LnL,x ISD,x SSI) and processes this composite
input through a lightweight convolutional encoder with Global
Average Pooling. A Softmax head assigns adaptive mixing
weightsw∈R 3 (Pwi = 1), yieldingx aug = Pwi·x i.
This enables the framework to automatically prioritize the most
effective noise type for the current input.

3. Experiments
3.1. Datasets and Metrics
Our evaluation spans both speech and non-speech domains to
verify domain-agnostic robustness. We utilize the ASVspoof
2019 & 2021 Logical Access (LA) datasets [22, 23] for stan-
dard speech deepfake detection training and evaluation. Addi-
tionally, we employ CtrSVDD [24], a singing voice benchmark
with rich prosodic variations. We adopt EnvSDD [21], which
comprises two distinct attack types: Text-to-Audio (TTA) and
Audio-to-Audio (ATA), to assess performance in diverse acous-
tic environments. Following standard protocols, we report the
Equal Error Rate (EER) and minimum tandem Detection Cost
Function (min t-DCF) for ASVspoof, while EER is used for
CtrSVDD and EnvSDD.
3.2. Implementation Details
We implement DAR-Boost in PyTorch using an NVIDIA RTX
5090 GPU, integrated with W2V-AASIST [17] (ASVspoof),
RawNet2 [25] (CtrSVDD), and BEATs-AASIST [21] (En-
vSDD) under their original configurations. The module
(Nband = 5, filter lengthL= 101) is extremely lightweight
(26.27 K parameters), introducing negligible computational
overhead. To synchronize convergence, we adopt a backbone-

3.3. Main Results and Analysis
Table 1 compares performance across diverse benchmarks. In
the speech domains (19/21LA, CtrSVDD), DAR-Boost shows
strong robustness. It matches the best-tuned static RawBoost
configurations and consistently outperforms WavAugment-
style heuristics (e.g., Time-Drop, Band-Reject). This confirms
our differentiable reconstruction preserves channel simulation
efficacy for human voice without manual tuning.
Furthermore, results on the non-speech EnvSDD bench-
mark validate our hypothesis regarding speech-centric augmen-
tation limits. In TTA and ATA scenarios, traditional RawBoost
degrades performance (e.g., ATA EER increases from 1.42% to
1.53% with SSI), likely because mismatched spectral filtering
destroys environmental textures. In contrast, DAR-Boost adapts
to broadband environmental sounds, achieving the best ATA re-
sult (0.90% EER) and competitive TTA performance (4.82%
EER).

the Parameter Generator (reverting to random shape sampling)
degrades performance severely (TTA: 7.63%, ATA: 2.05%),
falling below the baseline. This indicates that unguided per-
turbations destroy critical acoustic cues, making active adver-

4. Conclusion
This paper proposed DAR-Boost, an end-to-end differentiable
data augmentation framework for audio deepfake detection.
By reformulating discrete signal processing steps into differ-
entiable layers and employing a decoupling strategy separating
learnable adversarial Shape Parameters from stochastic Inten-
sity Parameters, we enable joint adversarial optimization with-
out mode collapse. Experimental results confirm that DAR-
Boost surpasses static augmentation methods, maintains robust-
ness against channel variability, and significantly improves gen-
eralization in non-speech domains, bridging the gap between
speech anti-spoofing and broad-spectrum deepfake detection.
We plan to evaluate on more recent and diverse datasets in fu-
ture work.
