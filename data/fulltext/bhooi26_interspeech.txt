Refining the Latent Bridge: Superior ASR Performance via Adapter-Only
Alignment with Diffusion LLMs

1 CSE Department & Infosys Center for AI, IIIT-Delhi, India

Abstract
In this work, we validate a parameter-efficient approach to
Speech-LLM based ASR that enforces a strict adapter-only
alignment protocol. Unlike existing methods that rely on
full-parameter or partial backbone updates, we keep both the
speech encoder and the LLM frozen, focusing exclusively on
the adapter as the sole learnable interface. We demonstrate that
this restricted alignment strategy, though often underexplored,
is particularly effective for Diffusion-based LLMs. Our results
show that Diffusion models are significantly more resilient to
the adapter-only bottleneck than Autoregressive (AR) models,
yielding higher Real-Time Factors (RTF) and superior ASR per-
formance across various data size splits. While AR models suf-
fer from error propagation and drift when denied full-parameter
tuning, the global refinement mechanism of Diffusion proves
more scalable and efficient for multimodal LLM integration.

1. Introduction
The fusion of Large Language Models (LLMs) with speech
capabilities is rapidly reshaping the landscape of multimodal
intelligence. In the context of automatic speech recognition
(ASR), integrating rich linguistic knowledge from LLMs of-
fers a compelling avenue to enhance system performance and
robustness without sacrificing acoustic fidelity. By leverag-
ing the internal world knowledge and contextual reasoning of
these large-scale models, ASR systems can move beyond sim-
ple acoustic mapping toward a more holistic understanding of
spoken language.
Recent research efforts to imbue ASR with LLM capa-
bilities have explored various architectures. Approaches like
N-best hypothesis rescoring leverage LLMs to re-rank mul-
tiple ASR outputs, selecting the most linguistically plausible
one [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. While offering some improvement, this
cascaded approach can be computationally expensive at infer-
ence and is inherently limited by the quality of the initial ASR
hypotheses, failing to directly enhance the core speech-to-text
mapping. Alternatively, modality adapters have emerged as a
promising paradigm central to the SLAM vision [6, 7, 8, 9, 5,
10, 11]. These lightweight neural networks bridge the represen-
tation gap between a specialized speech encoder and a general-
purpose LLM, often by projecting speech latent representations
into the LLM’s embedding space. A key aspect of the SLAM-
ASR approach is the ability to leverage powerful, frozen pre-
trained components (both the speech encoder and the LLM),
requiring only the adapter layer to be trained.
Despite these advancements, existing SLAM-ASR frame-
works predominantly rely on autoregressive decoding, which is
limited by sequential inference latency, thus causing a bottle-
neck during inference. In this work, we propose a shift towards
non-autoregressive decoding using a diffusion-based LLM and
show that a diffusion LLM (dLLM) can achieve better perfor-
mance and faster decoding in the same setting compared to an
Autoregressive LLM.
Our primary contributions are summarized as follows. Re-
garding superior performance and efficiency, we demonstrate
that our proposed model achieves a 2.807% WER on Lib-
riSpeech test-clean, representing a 54% relative improvement
over an autoregressive (AR) baseline under the same experi-
mental setting. Simultaneously, the model improves inference
throughput by approximately 45%, reaching a 12.3x RTFx.
Through extensive architectural ablations for latent alignment,
we identify that a deep, normalized MLP adapter using man-
ual frame-stacking provides a higher-fidelity acoustic pre-
fix for dLLMs than learnable convolutional temporal models,
which appear to degrade performance in the non-autoregressive
regime. Finally, we demonstrate robustness to data constraints
by validating the stability of our approach across multiple dis-
joint 100-hour training subsets, proving that dLLMs exhibit
lower performance variance and higher deterministic compu-
tational overhead than AR models in low-resource settings.
2. Related Work
The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into ASR
is primarily explored through two paradigms. The first, n-
best rescoring, uses an LLM to re-rank multiple ASR hypothe-
ses [12, 1, 2, 4, 3]. However, this cascaded approach is of-
ten constrained by the initial ASR output quality. Second, an
emerging paradigm is using a modality adapter that bridges
a frozen speech encoder and decoder-only LLM in an end-
to-end manner, a core tenet of Speech, Language, and Audio
Modelling (SLAM) [5]. Early works like SLAM [7] demon-
strated this by using simple transformer adapters between mod-
els or via delayed fusion [13]. Recent models such as Phi-
4-Mini [8] and Kimi-Audio [11] incorporate a simple MLP
adapter. However, MLPs often lack the inductive bias for
complex spectro-temporal relationships, limiting robust cross-
modal alignment. Alternatively, some works have incorporated
the Querying Transformer (Q-Former) [9, 14, 3, 4] that has sig-
nificant computational overhead. Their query-based compres-
sion also risks discarding fine-grained phonetic details crucial
for ASR, particularly for the rich phonology of Indic languages.
The shift from autoregressive decoding to parallel gen-
eration has led to several recent advancements utilizing dis-
crete diffusion and flow-matching frameworks for speech tasks.
These works primarily focus on reducing inference latency
while maintaining the linguistic modeling capabilities of large-

Non-Autoregressive Block-wise Decoding Unlike AR models
that generate tokens sequentially, our framework employs iter-
ative parallel decoding. Inference begins with a sequence of N
[MASK] tokens. In each of the K refinement steps (K≪N):
The model predicts probability distributions for all masked po-
sitions simultaneously. Tokens with the highest prediction con-
fidence are ‘crystallized’ (unmasked). The remaining posi-
tions are re-predicted in the subsequent step, conditioned on the
newly fixed tokens. This mechanism enables the model to lever-
age bidirectional context, allowing for global error correction
that is unavailable in unidirectional AR decoding. By adjust-
ing the re-masking strategy, the dLLM effectively balances the
high-fidelity refinement of diffusion with the sequential consis-
tency of block-wise generation, significantly reducing inference
latency and improving the Real-Time Factor (RTF).

4. Experimental Setup
All experiments are conducted using various subsets and par-
titions of the LibriSpeech 960h [20] dataset. We evaluate our
models using the Word Error Rate (WER%) to measure tran-
scription accuracy and the Real-Time Factor (RTFx) to assess
decoding throughput and inference efficiency.
4.1. Backbone Models
We utilize the frozen encoder stack of the Whisper-Large-
v3 [21] model, which provides robust, ASR-specific acoustic
representations. During training, we apply SpecAugment [22]
to the acoustic features to improve the model’s robustness to
spectral and temporal variations. Specifically, we employ two
frequency masks with a maximum mask width of F = 27 and
two time masks with a maximum mask width of T = 100 .
For the decoder, we study two distinct paradigms: Llama-3
(8B) [23] for traditional autoregressive generation, and LLaDA
(8B) [24] for non-autoregressive generation via iterative dis-
crete diffusion. For the Llama-3 baseline, we employ a standard
autoregressive beam search with a beam width of 4. In contrast,
the LLaDA-8B model utilizes an iterative, non-autoregressive
denoising process where the target sequence is initially pop-
ulated with a specialized mask token. The reverse diffusion
process follows a linear noise schedule, uniformly discretiz-
ing the transition interval into a fixed number of steps (default
N = 128 ). The model utilizes a block-wise iterative approach
where Gumbel noise is applied to parallel categorical distribu-
tions, enabling the dLLM to exploit bidirectional context while
bypassing sequential bottlenecks. Using a Low Confidence re-
masking strategy, only tokens with the highest softmax proba-
bilities are committed at each step, while remaining positions
are refined through dynamic re-masking. In all experiments,
both the speech encoder and the language model parameters re-
main frozen, ensuring that the multimodal alignment is learned
exclusively through the lightweight adapter module.
4.2. Adapter Training
All models are trained for a maximum of 20 epochs or106 steps
on a single NVIDIA GPU with CUDA acceleration. We use the
AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 10−4 and a linear
warmup schedule for the first 1000 steps. The training is per-
formed with a batch size of 4 and gradient accumulation over 2
steps, effectively resulting in a global batch size of 8.
5. Experimental Results
5.1. Comparative Analysis: Diffusion vs. Autoregression
We evaluate our framework on the LibriSpeech benchmark
across two data regimes: a low-resource (100h clean) split and
a full-resource (960h) split. Table 1 summarizes the Word Error
Rate (WER) and Real-Time Factor (RTFx) performance. The
empirical results validate our hypothesis that Diffusion-based
LLMs are more resilient to the adapter-only bottleneck than
their Autoregressive (AR) counterparts.
Superior ASR Performance: At the 100h mark, LlaDA-8B al-
ready outperforms Llama-3-8B across all test sets. However,
the performance gap widens significantly at 960h. On test-
clean, LlaDA achieves a 2.807% WER, a massive 54% relative
improvement over the Llama-3 baseline (6.147%). This sug-
gests that while AR models struggle with drift when the back-
bone is frozen, the global refinement of Diffusion models effec-
tively compensates for the constrained adapter interface. Scal-
ing Efficiency: LlaDA exhibits superior scaling laws. Moving
from 100h to 960h results in a drastic reduction in WER for
LlaDA (e.g., from 6.007% to 2.807% on test-clean), whereas
Llama-3’s improvement is marginal. This confirms that the Dif-
fusion global context is better at capturing the latent acoustic-
to-linguistic mapping provided by a frozen adapter.
A critical advantage of the non-autoregressive block-wise
decoding is the improvement in inference throughput. Across
all splits, LlaDA-8B maintains a significantly higher RTFx (av-
eraging 12.3x) compared to Llama-3-8B (averaging 8.5x). By
decoupling the generation steps from the sequence length N,
our framework achieves a 45% increase in RTFx. This proves
that Diffusion models are not only more accurate in this ‘frozen-
backbone’ setup but also computationally more efficient for
real-time applications.
5.2. Robustness Under Data Constraints and Disjoint Splits
To ensure the observed performance gains were not artifacts
of specific data distributions, we conducted a robust evaluation
across multiple disjoint 100-hour subsets sampled from train-
clean-300 (LS-100c) and train-other-500 (LS-100o). The re-
sults, summarized in Table 2, reveal two critical findings re-
garding model stability and convergence.
Resilience to Acoustic Diversity: LlaDA-8B consistently out-
performs Llama-3-8B even when trained on noisy or diverse
subsets (LS-100o). While Llama-3 exhibits a WER of 7.307%
on test-clean when trained on ”other” data, LlaDA maintains
a significantly lower WER of 3.887%. This suggests that the
diffusion global refinement mechanism is better at generaliz-
ing acoustic-to-linguistic mappings from limited, imperfect data
without requiring backbone updates.

the RTFx variance for LlaDA is remarkably low (±0.11), indi-

5.3. Ablation Study
To isolate the contributions of our design choices, we evaluate
four configurations on the LibriSpeech 960h set. The results in
Table 3 highlight the critical role of normalization and temporal
representation in the adapter-only regime.
The Role of Layer Normalization (LN): Removing LN
from the MLP adapter resulted in a significant performance
degradation (e.g., test-clean WER increased from 2.807%
to 3.841%). This confirms that when both the encoder and
LLM are frozen, the adapter’s internal feature scaling is vital
for stabilizing the cross-modal projection. Without LN, the
high-dimensional acoustic embeddings likely drift outside the
expected distribution of the dLLM’s linguistic space.
Manual Stacking vs. Learnable Temporal Modeling: Sur-
prisingly, replacing the frame-stacking MLP with a CNN-based
adapter led to the worst performance across all metrics (4.154%
on test-clean). This suggests that for non-autoregressive
diffusion models, simple manual frame-stacking preserves
more raw acoustic signal than the local temporal pooling of a
CNN. The dLLM’s global refinement mechanism appears to
prefer a high-fidelity, high-resolution acoustic prefix over the
filtered representations produced by learnable convolutions.
Impact of Data Augmentation (SpecAugment): Disabling
SpecAugment led to a moderate increase in WER (3.001% vs.
2.807% on test-clean). While the dLLM shows inherent robust-
ness due to its iterative denoising nature, spectral and temporal
masking remain essential for preventing the adapter from over-
fitting to the frozen encoder’s static features.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we explored the parameter-efficient approach
to Speech-LLM integration by enforcing a strict adapter-only
alignment protocol with a frozen Diffusion-based LLM. Our
findings challenge the prevailing necessity for backbone fine-
tuning, demonstrating that the global refinement mechanism of
Diffusion models is significantly more resilient to the adapter-
only bottleneck than Autoregressive counterparts. Empirical re-
sults on LibriSpeech show that our framework, LlaDA-ASR,
achieves a state-of-the-art 2.807% WER on test-clean while im-
proving inference throughput by 45% (12.3x RTFx). Abla-
tion studies further confirm that a lightweight, normalized MLP
bridge with manual frame-stacking provides the optimal latent
representation for non-autoregressive decoding. This study es-
tablishes a scalable, efficient, and robust blueprint for integrat-
ing speech and generative diffusion models without the high
computational cost of full-parameter tuning.
