ARCHES: An Agent-Based Refinement Cycle for Hierarchical Synthesis of
Sound Effects for Variety Shows
Wentao Lei ID 1, Li Liu ID 1,∗
1 The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou), China

Abstract
Sound effects for variety shows can greatly improve viewer en-
gagement, which currently relies on a manual process. This
approach is heavily dependent on the experience of human edi-
tors. While recent advancements in audio generation methods
have shown promising performance, they struggle to meet the
unique demands of variety shows. The past methods exhibit
critical limitations in temporal precision, content diversity, and
contextual understanding. To overcome these limitations, we pro-
pose ARCHES (Agent-basedRefinementCycle forHiErarchical
Synthesis), a novel framework that leverages multi-agent col-
laboration and retrieval-augmented generation to automatically
produce high-quality, contextually appropriate sound effects.
The framework contains an iterative workflow of planning, gen-
eration, and refinement. The core components of this framework
are three modules: the Auditory Unified Retrieval Augmenta-
tion (AURA) module for content diversity, the Adaptive eXpert
Intelligent Switch (AXIS) module for specific fine-grained refine-
ment, and a Creative Experience Bank (CEB) which allows the
system to learn from past generation process. To validate our ap-
proach, we constructed the first large-scale, fine-grained dataset
for this task, the Variety Show Sound Effect Benchmark (VSSE-
Bench). Extensive experiments demonstrate that ARCHES sig-
nificantly outperforms state-of-the-art (SOTA) on both objective
and subjective metrics. The demos are anonymously available at
https://arches-bench.github.io/.
Index Terms: Sound generation, Multi-agent system, Retrieval-
augmented generation, Variety shows
1. Introduction
Sound effects are an integral component of modern variety show
production, serving to amplify humor, underscore emotional
shifts, and maintain an engaging pace. (e.g., adding a “swoosh”
sound for a quick movement, a comical “boing” for a surprising
moment, or using a laugh track to guide audience reaction.) The
current industry standard, however, is a manual, craft-based pro-
cess heavily dependent on the experience and intuition of human
editors. This reliance on manual labor introduces significant
bottlenecks, including high costs and a quality ceiling dictated
by individual skill. More importantly, this approach faces a ma-
jor scalability challenge by ever-increasing content production
demands, making it difficult to consistently produce high-quality
sound design at scale.
The emergence of automated audio generation, driven by
deep generative models [1, 2], presents a potential solution to
this efficiency problem. However, applying these general audio
generation models directly to the dynamic context of variety

comedic effects that define the variety show genre.
To address these challenges, we propose theAgent-based
RefinementCycle forHiErarchicalSynthesis (ARCHES). It
draws core inspiration from the workflow of professional human
editors. We reframe sound effect generation as a structured, hi-
erarchical process of iterative refinement, executed through a
multi-agent framework that mirrors an expert post-production
team. The architecture consists of three core components work-
ing in synergy. Firstly, we proposeAuditoryUnifiedRetrieval
Augmentation (AURA) module, which acts as the system’s cre-
ative material library. By leveraging retrieval-augmented genera-
tion, it grounds the initial sound synthesis in a vast database of
high-quality sound effects, ensuring the output is contextually
relevant and stylistically diverse from the start. Then, we design
Adaptive eXpertIntelligentSwitch (AXIS) module embodies
the critical refinement stage; functioning as an intelligent dis-
patcher, it analyzes the generated sound and routes it to special-
ized agents, which imitates an editor deciding whether to adjust
timing or alter emotional tone, to ensure precise, targeted correc-
tions. Finally,Creative Experience Bank (CEB)is presented
to enhance the entire workflow with a long-term memory system
that records successful creative decisions, allowing ARCHES to
learn from past interactions and improve its strategies over time,
effectively emulating the decision-making process of human
editors. In summary, our contributions are:
1. We propose ARCHES, a novel framework that introduces a
hierarchical, agent-based workflow of design, generation, and
iterative refinement to sound effect synthesis.
2. We design three novel modules: AURA, a retrieval-augmented
module to enhance content diversity; AXIS, a self-routing
mechanism that intelligently dispatches tasks to specialized
agents; and the CEB, a long-term memory system that records
successful creative decisions to improve future interactions.
3. We constructed and will release VSSE-Bench, the first large-
scale, fine-grained benchmark for evaluating variety show
sound generation, and a large, high-quality sound effect li-
brary with rich annotations to support the retrieval-augmented

2. Related Work
2.1. Text and Video to Audio Generation
The progress of audio generation is remarkable, driven by deep
generative models. Text-to-audio (TTA) methods like Audi-
oLDM [1] and Make-An-Audio [2] produce high-fidelity sounds
from text but lack fine-grained temporal control. To address
this, video-to-audio (V2A) generation aligns acoustic proper-
ties with visual cues. FoleyCrafter [10] and STA-V2A [6] in-
troduce semantic and temporal adapters to base TTA models.
MMAudio [11] utilizes flow matching for latent-level alignment.
Recently, industrial-scale models employing Multimodal Dif-
fusion Transformers (MMDiT), such as Kling-Foley [12] and
HunyuanVideo-Foley [13], achieve SOTA audio-visual synchro-
nization.
These models are designed for diegetic sounds (e.g., foot-
steps, collisions) originating naturally from the physical scene.
They fundamentally struggle with the stylized, non-diegetic,
and comedic effects essential for variety shows. While
ThinkSound [14] explores LLM reasoning for audio genera-
tion, the gap in generating emotion-evoking variety show effects
remains a significant open challenge.
2.2. Multi-Agent Systems in Generative AI
Recently, there has been a growing trend towards using multi-
agent systems to solve complex problems beyond the scope of a
single model. Frameworks like CAMEL [15] have demonstrated
that a team of specialized AI agents, each with a distinct role,
can collaboratively tackle multi-step tasks. This paradigm has
been successfully applied in other complex domains, such as
with AutoGen, which enables building diverse LLM applications
through multi-agent conversations [16], and ChatDev, which sim-
ulates an entire software development team with communicative
agents [17]. We are among the first to apply this paradigm to the
creative domain of sound effect generation for variety shows.
3. Methodology
3.1. ARCHES Workflow
To resolve the trade-off between generative creativity and con-
textual fidelity, we introduce ARCHES, a novel architecture with
a multi-stage framework. The framework comprises a main iter-
ative workflow and three key modules: AURA, AXIS and CEB.
The overall architecture is shown in Figure 1.
The core of our framework is an iterative process designed
as an expert editor workflow. A Planning Agent first analyzes the
input video’s visual and speech content to perform event detec-
tion, identifying key moments that require audio enhancement.
Based on the planning output, a Generation Agent performs con-
ditional audio synthesis to produce an initial sound effect. The
initial output enters an iterative loop. A Checker Agent evaluates
the generated sound effects for coherence and synchronization.
If deficiencies are found, it generates correction instructions that
are passed to our AXIS module for allocating refiner.
3.2. Auditory Unified Retrieval Augmentation
To overcome the limited diversity of purely generative models,
AURA retrieves contextually relevant sound effects to guide the
generation agents.

a scene into a queryable representation. It accepts an emotion
label, an event class label, and raw audio as inputs. These are fed
into a multimodal Encoder built upon Qwen2-Audio, projecting
them into a unified embedding space. We optimize the encoder
using standard InfoNCE loss via in-batch negatives:

3.3. Adaptive eXpert Intelligent Switch
To make refinement efficient, AXIS is a self-routing mechanism
that intelligently delegates tasks to a pool of specialized agents.
Based on proposals from the Checker Agent, AXIS automatically
routes the task to the corresponding agent for specific task such
as aTemporal Dynamics Refiner(for adjusting timing) or a
Emotion Refiner(for modifying emotion character), enabling a
precise and optimized iterative workflow.
3.4. Creative Experience Bank
To achieve long-term systemic self-evolution, we establish a
shared CEB that structurally logs successful interaction chains.
Before initiating a new task, the Planning and AXIS agents
query this repository. For recurring scenarios, the system directly
applies a Typical Template Shortcut referencing the established
workflow, significantly reducing decision-making time.
4. Experiments
We evaluate proposed ARCHES framework on newly con-
structed Variety Show Sound Effect Benchmark (VSSE-Bench).
To comprehensively assess performance, we use a set of objec-
tive and subjective metrics and compare against SOTA methods.
4.1. Experimental Settings
Datasets.We collected the first VSSE-Bench, which contains
1,000 full-length episodes of popular variety shows from major
streaming platforms, specifically YouTube and Bilibili. The
selection covers a diverse range of genres, including sketch
comedy, reality TV , and game shows, ensuring a rich distribution
of visual scenes and acoustic events. These raw episodes were
pre-processed and segmented into short, event-centric clips.
The AURA Audio Database.We built a dedicated sound ef-
fect base containing over 26k isolated, high-fidelity sound effects.
These are clean high-quality samples free from background noise
or speech. These examples match the stylistic demands of va-
riety show production and are richly annotated with class and
emotional metadata to facilitate accurate multimodal retrieval by
the AURA module.
4.1.1. Agent Model Selection
The implementation of the ARCHES framework relies on a
selection of specialized models for its different agents. For the
crucial tasks of audio generation and editing, we follow the
model structure based on MultiFoley [18]. The multimodal
retrieval model within the AURA module for understanding
and querying the audio database, is Qwen2-Audio [19]. Other
higher-level cognitive functions, including the initial analysis and
planning performed by the Planning Agent and other analytical
tasks, are handled by Gemini-2.5 Pro [20].
Implementation Details.The AURA Retriever is built upon
the Qwen2-Audio model and fine-tuned using LoRA [21] with
a rank r= 8 and α= 32 . We optimize the encoder using
InfoNCE loss with a learning rate of 1e−4 and a batch size
of 32. Audio segments are tokenized up to 2,000 tokens, while
textual metadata is limited to 512 tokens.
Evaluation Metrics.We employ a comprehensive set of
objective and subjective metrics to assess model performance.
Log-Spectral Distance (LSD):An objective metric that mea-
sures the perceptual difference between the logarithmic spectra
of the generated and ground-truth audio. Lower values indicate
higher fidelity.Onset Difference (s):This metric quantifies the
temporal synchronization by calculating the time difference in
seconds between the onset of the sound event in the generated
audio and the ground truth. A lower value signifies better align-
ment.Auto Mean Opinion Score (Auto-MOS):Here we use
an automatic MOS prediction method [22] to evaluate speech
quality.Fr ´echet Audio Distance (FAD):[23] An objective met-
ric that evaluates the quality of generated audio by measuring
the distance between the embedding distributions of the gener-
ated audio and a large set of real-world audio. It is inspired by
the Fr´echet Inception Distance (FID) in image generation and
correlates well with human perception of audio quality.
Baselines.We compare ARCHES with several SOTA video-
to-audio synthesis models, including MMAudio [11], Kling-
Foley [12], HunyuanVideo-Foley [13] and FoleyCrafter [10].

4.3. Ablation Study
To validate the effectiveness of our three key components, we
conducted an ablation study on the VSSE-Bench. We evaluated
three variants of our model: (1)ARCHES w/o AURA, where
the retrieval-augmentation module is removed and the generation
agent becomes a pure Text-to-Audio Generation Module, and (2)
ARCHES w/o AXIS, where the agent-based refinement loop is
disabled, (3)ARCHES w/o CEB, where the process does not
refer to CEB memory.
The results, presented in Table 2, clearly demonstrate the
contribution of each module. Removing the AURA module leads
to a significant increase in the FAD score, indicating a drop in
audio quality and diversity, as the model struggles to generate
less common sounds. The MOS score also decreases, suggesting
that the retrieved exemplars are crucial for contextual appropri-
ateness. Disabling the AXIS module results in a substantially
larger Onset Difference, confirming the importance of the itera-
tive refinement loop and specialized agents for achieving precise
temporal synchronization. The absence of the CEB memory also
causes a slight decline in overall quality metrics like MOS and
FAD, indicating that learning from successful historical inter-
actions helps refine the final output. The full ARCHES model
outperforms all variants across all metrics, proving that the syn-
ergy between AURA’s retrieval-augmented generation, AXIS’s
agent-based refinement, and CEB’s experience-guided learning
is essential for achieving SOTA performance.
4.4. User Study
To evaluate the subjective quality of the generated sound effects,
we conducted a comprehensive Mean Opinion Score (MOS) user
study. We have 20 participants who were asked to rate the gener-
ated audio from our model and the SOTA methods on a scale of
1 to 5 across three specific criteria:MOS-Semantic(It assesses
the correlation between the synthetic sound effect and the source
video content),MOS-Temporal(the temporal alignment of the
audio with the visual event), andMOS-Emotion(how well the
sound effect fits the context emotion and enhances the scene).
The questionnaire of user study consists of 32 test videos.
As shown in Table 1, ARCHES achieves the highest scores
across all three metrics by a significant margin. InMOS-
Semantic, ARCHES leads decisively, which can be attributed to
the AURA module’s effective retrieval of contextually appropri-
ate sounds from a high-quality, professional database. The nearly
perfect score inMOS-Temporalhighlights the effectiveness of
the AXIS module’s iterative refinement process, particularly
the Temporal Dynamics Refiner, in achieving precise synchro-
nization with on-screen events. Most importantly, ARCHES
significantly surpassed all baselines inMOS-Emotion, demon-
strating that the multi-agent framework successfully captures the
fine-grained, creative, and contextual demands of sound design
for variety shows. This comprehensive subjective evaluation con-
firms that ARCHES delivers a more engaging and contextually
coherent viewing experience.
4.5. Impact of Agent MLLM Backbones
The selection of different MLLM backbones for the Planning,
Checker, and AXIS agents significantly affects the overall per-
formance of the ARCHES framework. To evaluate this impact,
we compared several MLLMs, including GPT-4o [24], Qwen3-
VL-8B [25], Step-1o [26], and the Gemini-2.5 [20] series.
As shown in Table 3, the performance of our methods varies
clearly depending on the MLLM backbone used. Gemini-2.5 Pro
achieves the best results across all metrics. Gemini-2.5 Flash and
Qwen3-VL show comparable performance, followed by GPT-4o.
Step-1o shows the highest error rates among the tested models
in this task. These results confirm that the choice of MLLM for
the agents is a critical factor in the system’s final output quality.
But most of the models have demonstrated good performance,
especially Qwen3-VL, which shows the effectiveness of our
framework for open-source models.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we introduced ARCHES, a novel framework to
resolve the critical challenges of sound effect generation for va-
riety shows. ARCHES consists of three key modules: AURA
for multimodal retrieval of rich contextual materials, AXIS for
dynamically routing tasks to specialized refinement agents, and
CEB for recording and guiding successful shotcut workflows.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that ARCHES quantitatively
and qualitatively outperforms current SOTA methods. Future
work will focus on optimizing the iterative generation latency,
reducing the framework’s dependency on underlying model ca-
pabilities, and extending this agent-based refinement cycle to
cross-modal audio-visual generation for variety shows.
