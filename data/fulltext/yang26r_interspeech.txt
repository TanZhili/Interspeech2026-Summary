A Compact Fully-Open Cache-Aware Streaming Model for Japanese ASR
Yinchang Yang1
1 The Kyoto College of Graduate Studies for Informatics, Japan
yoinncyan2306@gmail.com
Abstract
We present a 123M-parameter fully-open cache-aware stream-
ing model for Japanese ASR with a hybrid RNNT/CTC design.
To our knowledge, no prior Japanese ASR system simultane-
ously provides full openness—open weights, data preparation
details, code, and logs—together with streaming capability. On
a 35K-hour pre-training corpus we show that CER-stratified
data curation consistently benefits underrepresented domains
but can hurt performance under multi-domain fine-tuning with
higher decoder capacity. We further study progressive fine-
tuning across five domains totaling 507 hours. Evaluated on
five test sets, our model achieves 12.4% average CER (RNNT),
outperforming the 1B OWSM-CTC v4 (13.5%) and 619M Rea-
zonSpeech NeMo-v2 (14.1%) while being 5–8×smaller, with
an RTFx of 1220 (batch) and 446 (streaming).
Index Terms: streaming ASR, Japanese speech recognition,
cache-aware attention, data curation, open-source
1. Introduction
Recent speech foundation models such as Whisper [1] have
achieved remarkable accuracy by training on massive datasets.
However, many high-performing Japanese ASR systems remain
either closed-source (e.g., commercial APIs), rely on propri-
etary training data, or operate exclusively in offline mode—
requiring the full utterance before producing output.
For on-device applications such as real-time transcription
on smartphones and IoT devices,streamingcapability is essen-
tial: the model must process audio incrementally with bounded
latency, rather than waiting for the complete utterance. Further-
more,full openness—publicly available weights, data details,
code, and logs—is critical for reproducibility and community
adoption. While several Japanese ASR models have been re-
leased in recent years, each falls short on at least one of these

We survey existing Japanese ASR systems and find that, to
the best of our knowledge, no prior model simultaneously satis-
fies both full openness and streaming capability. Whisper [1] re-
leases inference code and weights but not data details or training
recipe. NVIDIA parakeet [2] releases weights and references
public data but provides no training recipe. The espnet stream-
ing model [3] documents its training corpora but lacks complete
pipeline reproducibility. OWSM-CTC v4 [4, 5] achieves full
openness but does not provide a streaming inference recipe.
Beyond the model-level gap, building a competitive
Japanese streaming ASR system faces adata challenge: a large-
scale open Japanese corpus, ReazonSpeech [6] (∼35K hours),
is derived entirely from terrestrial TV broadcasts, while lecture
and conversational domains remain underrepresented in public
resources. To bridge this domain gap, we assemble a∼507-
hour multi-domain fine-tuning pool spanning five complemen-
tary sources—crowd-sourced read speech, TV-domain filtered
subsets, spontaneous conversation, and lecture-style talks. A
key component is anewly curated TEDxJP-20h training split:
we collect all publicly available Japanese TED talk record-
ings with human-uploaded subtitles (as of January 2026), seg-
ment them by subtitle timestamps, and apply quality filter-
ing (CER<10% against a teacher ASR), yielding∼20 hours
(21.9K utterances) that fill the previously empty lecture slot.
Similarly, MSR-86K [7] is quality-filtered with the same CER
threshold, and the BTSJ-1000 dialogue corpus [8] is segmented
via forced alignment [9] to yield∼117 hours of conversational
data. The established TEDxJP-10K evaluation set [10] is ex-
plicitly excluded to prevent train–test leakage.
To address this gap, we propose a compact 123M-
parameter model based on the FastConformer [11] architecture
with cache-aware multi-context streaming [12] and a hybrid
RNNT/CTC dual-decoder design. Our contributions are:
• To our knowledge, the first fully-open Japanese ASR model
with cache-aware streaming, releasing weights, data mani-
fests and preparation scripts, code, and logs for full repro-
ducibility.
• A2×2factorial study of CER-stratified pre-training
data curation on ReazonSpeech [6] (35K hours), iso-
lating the interaction between curation, model capacity
(pred rnn layers), and fine-tuning scope.
• A progressive multi-domain fine-tuning study across five
Japanese speech domains (∼507 hours), including a newly
curated TEDxJP-20h training split, quantifying domain-
dependent trade-offs.
• Competitive accuracy: 12.4% average CER (RNNT) across
five test sets, outperforming models 5–8×larger, with RTFx
1220 (offline) / 446 (streaming).

2. Proposed Method
2.1. Model architecture
Our model uses the FastConformer-Hybrid-Transducer-CTC-
BPE-Streaming architecture [11] implemented in NVIDIA
NeMo [13]. The encoder is a 17-layer FastConformer with
dmodel = 512and8×depthwise-striding subsampling (108M
parameters), paired with an RNNT decoder [14] (9.2M) and a
CTC decoder [15] (2.1M), totaling 123M parameters. Input fea-
tures are 80-dimensional mel-filterbanks with 10 ms frame shift.
During training, the CTC branch serves as an auxiliary objective
(weight 0.3), stabilizing encoder representations; at inference, it
provides a faster decoding path when throughput is prioritized
over accuracy.
2.2. Cache-aware streaming
We adopt cache-aware multi-context attention [12] with
att context style=chunked limited, preventing ef-
fective look-ahead from accumulating across encoder lay-
ers. During training, we uniformly sample from four context
configurations[L,R]∈{[70,13], [70,6], [70,1],
[70,0]}, whereLandRdenote left and right context frames
after8×subsampling (1 frame = 80 ms). Algorithmic look-
ahead isR×8×10ms:[70,13]yields 1.04 s and[70,0]
yields zero for fully causal streaming. A single model thus
supports multiple latency–accuracy trade-offs at inference time
without retraining.
2.3. Pre-training data curation
We pre-train on ReazonSpeech v2 [6] (∼35K hours of Japanese
TV broadcast speech) and compare two strategies:
Baseline: all utterances without filtering.
CER-stratified (CER-strat): (1) transcribe all utterances
with parakeet-0.6b-ja [2] and compute CER against original
transcripts as a quality proxy; (2) discard utterances with
CER>20% (∼41% of data); (3) sample from three quality
bands—[0%,1%],(1%,5%],(5%,20%]—with weights 0.65,
0.25, 0.10, retaining∼59% of the original data.

2.4. Multi-domain fine-tuning
All models are pre-trained on ReazonSpeech with AdamW (lr
= 2.0, 15K warmup steps, min lr= 5×10 −6), exponential
moving average (EMA, decay 0.9995), SpecAugment, and fast-
emit regularization (λ= 10 −4). Fine-tuning uses the same
optimizer with lr= 0.6.
We fine-tune progressively on five Japanese speech do-
mains (∼507 hours total). Table 3 lists the data sources.
TEDxJP-20h and MSR-86K are quality-filtered by comparing
pseudo-transcriptions from parakeet-0.6b-ja against original la-
bels and retaining utterances with CER<10%; BTSJ is force-
aligned via NeMo Forced Aligner [9]. Sampling weights are
rebalanced at each step to prevent domain collapse. The full
training pipeline completes in∼200 hours on 4×NVIDIA RTX
6000 Ada (48 GB) GPUs.

3. Experimental Results
We evaluate on five domain-representative Japanese test sets:
JSUT basic5000 [16] (read speech), MCV 16.1 test [17]
(crowd-sourced), TEDxJP-10K [10] (lecture), FLEURS-ja
test [18] (read), and SPREDS-D1 [19] (simulated meeting).
These five sets span read, spontaneous, lecture, and conversa-
tional domains, providing a broad evaluation of robustness.
CER is computed with greedy decoding, no external LM;
text is normalized by NFKC, num2words (Japanese), and non-
letter removal (retaining CJK, kana, and Latin characters).
Throughput is reported as batch RTFx=total audio duration /
wall-clock time (higher is better); each model is evaluated with
its native inference pipeline on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada
(48 GB).

The five baseline systems cover four decoder architectures
(AED, CTC, RNNT, TDT) and range from 150M to 1.55B pa-
rameters, including both offline and streaming configurations.
This comparison isolates the contribution of our compact 123M
model against systems with up to12×more parameters. We ad-

4. Conclusion
We presented a 123M-parameter fully-open cache-aware
streaming model for Japanese ASR based on FastConformer-
Hybrid-Transducer-CTC. To our knowledge, this is the first
Japanese ASR system that simultaneously provides open
weights, data details, code, logs, and streaming capability. A
2×2factorial study of CER-stratified pre-training data curation
on 35K hours of ReazonSpeech showed that curation consis-
tently benefits domains absent from fine-tuning, but degrades
TEDxJP-10K only when combined with both multi-domain
coverage and increased decoder capacity—implicating a three-
way interaction rather than curation alone. Progressive multi-
domain fine-tuning across five domains (∼507 hours), includ-
ing a newly curated TEDxJP-20h training split, yielded 12.4%
average CER (RNNT) on five test sets, outperforming fully-
open baselines up to8×larger. With RTFx 1220 (offline)
and 446 (streaming), the model confirms real-time viability on
widely available workstation hardware. We publicly release all
weights, data manifests, code, and training logs to support re-
producible research.1
